Great Ways To Find Room For Everything In Your Harlem Home

Harlem World · November 25, 2026

By HWM

Every home in Harlem has the same quiet crisis. The things keep coming, and the closets stay the same size.

If you keep saying goodbye to belongings because there’s nowhere to put them, don’t downsize. Get better storage. Before you buy anything, donate or sell what you no longer use. Then try these six ideas.

1. Use Your Outdoor Area

Your yard can do more than hold a lawn chair. Many homeowners now build outdoor structures, including DIY pole barn kits, to store tools, sporting gear and seasonal items. Suppliers of metal post-frame buildings advertise garages, RV barns and other residential structures, and some offer free planning tools and quote comparisons. Ask any contractor for a license number and proof of insurance before you sign.

Check local zoning and permit rules first, because New York City’s can be strict. A backyard shed or sturdy shelving on exterior walls also protects your belongings and frees up the basement, attic and closets.

2. Put Your Walls to Work

A bare wall is storage you haven’t used yet. The design editors at House Beautiful say any empty wall is untapped space. Shelves, hooks, over-the-door hangers and wall organizers keep things off the floor, and many cost little. A curtain over a shelving wall hides clutter, and a shelf above a window adds display room.

3. Find the Forgotten Spaces

Walk through your home and look for spots that aren’t pulling their weight:

• Corners

• Attics and basements

• Space under the stairs

• The gaps behind furniture

• Your outdoor area

With some planning, these spots become storage, and you skip the addition. That saves money too.

4. Add Chests

A chest is furniture that also holds your things. It’s affordable, it warms up a room, and it comes in plenty of sizes and colors. Use it for blankets, toys or keepsakes.

Shop secondhand. Goodwill, thrift stores and consignment shops are good places to look for chests, trunks, baskets and storage benches. You can often find sturdy, well-made pieces with real character for far less than retail. Check that the lid and hinges work, smell the inside for mustiness, and look for wobbly joints before you buy. A fresh coat of paint or new hardware can make a find match your décor.

5. Look Up

Overhead storage uses vertical space that usually goes to waste. It suits laundry, holiday decorations, wrapping paper and office supplies, and it gives shelves, cabinets and closets a cleaner look. Hire a reputable Harlem installer who uses quality materials. Ask them to anchor racks to studs, and stay under the weight limits.

6. Reclaim the Garage

Better Homes & Gardens recently updated its garage organization guide, and its advice is to go vertical:

• Hang bikes on hooks or a ceiling pulley system.

• Store ladders near the ceiling.

• Keep seasonal clothes and holiday decorations in labeled, lidded bins on high shelves.

• Use pegboards and slatted wall panels for tools.

• Hang a curtain to hide clutter.

Some homeowners turn part of the garage into a playroom or workspace. Keep boxes out of direct sunlight, and choose plastic bins over cardboard.

Start Small

Organizing doesn’t have to be a production. Start with one wall, one corner or one shelf. Over time, those small wins can turn a cluttered house into one with room to spare.

Before you buy anything new, check your local Goodwill or thrift store for shelves, bins, baskets and chests. Secondhand finds add storage and keep costs down. Then tell us your best storage hack on our social pages.
