Blue Jays sign former Yankees second baseman Jazz Chisholm Jr.

By Andrew Mearns · Pinstripe Alley · November 27, 2026

Chisholm found a new home today and it’s still in the AL East: Toronto. The Blue Jays came to terms with the second baseman on a one-year, $18.5 million deal for 2027 ($12.5 million in salary plus a $6 million signing bonus) that also has a funky option for 2028. It’s a $16 million player option, but if Jazz declines it, then the Jays still have a chance to bring him back on a pretty big $40 million club option. If they decline that and give him a $1.5 million buyout, then he’s a free agent next offseason as well. So if Jazz has a great year for Toronto and thinks he could beat $16 million, then Toronto still has a chance to bring him back on a hefty price tag, albeit hefty for just one year. The bottom line is that he’s locked into $34.5 million even if he repeats his up-and-down 2026 or regresses.

FanSided’s Robert Murray was the first to report the deal, and The Athletic’s Ken Rosenthal had the specifics on the terms.

Chisholm’s 2026 campaign with the Yankees was a bit of a mess and we don’t need to go back into it in great detail. It was a disappointing walk year and we subsequently gave it a poor grade in our report card series. In a vaccum, Jazz was still productive in 2026 (a 98 wRC+ and good defense per FanGraphs still gave him 3.5 fWAR), but his slugging tumbled from a 30/30 guy in 2025 with .491 SLG to 19 homers with a .399 SLG. That looks bad when mixed in with his worst strikeout rates since 2023, and his highest in any season of at least 400 plate apperances. Still, Chisholm stole 41 bases as well and it’s not hard to envision a scenario where he becomes a pest again next year. It’s a reasonable gamble from Toronto’s perspective, and even if you’re tired with Jazz’s bit, the prospect of “Anthony Volpe, 2027 Yankees second baseman” isn’t particularly enthralling, either.

We’ll learn the answer from a distance — but not too much of a distance. I’ve already made my guess.
