Queens Public Library restores most services nearly 3 weeks after ransomware attack

By Giulia Heyward · Gothamist · November 23, 2026

“Thanks to the extraordinary efforts of our IT team and colleagues throughout the organization, we are back online and have restored virtually all our services,” Queens Public Library President and CEO Dennis Walcott said in a statement Monday.

Walcott said library staff kept all locations open throughout the outage, sometimes recording patrons' names and borrowed materials by hand to keep books circulating.

Demand for QPL's services has continued to rise, with attendance at library programs increasing 11% from 964,018 in fiscal year 2025 to 1,071,432 in fiscal year 2026, according to the mayor's management report.

Electronic visits to QPL's website also rose 52% to 7.5 million in fiscal year 2026, although the library reported an increase in bot traffic over the last fiscal year, according to the same data.

And while most QPL services are back up and running, not every section of the library's website has been fully restored, including its online contact forms and interlibrary loan feature.

Some previous book requests and library calendar entries could also be out of date or missing altogether, officials said.

QPL also said it's been working with cybersecurity experts to investigate the attack, restore its remaining systems and strengthen its technology infrastructure. The investigation includes determining whether hackers accessed or stole patrons' personal information.

“There are encouraging indicators that no personal information was compromised, but it is too soon to draw any definitive conclusions,” the library said in its announcement Monday. “If we learn that personal information was accessed or taken, we will provide appropriate notifications directly to any impacted individuals.”
