Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: Luis García Jr.

By Joshua Diemert · Pinstripe Alley · November 13, 2026

It’s very tempting to want to love Luis García Jr. His lefty swing reminds me so much of Robinson Canó, and indeed my own swing is modeled after the former Yankee All-Star. At the time the Yankees dealt for him, desperate to get some kind of thump in an Aaron Judge-less lineup, he led the National League in slugging. When he came to New York, he clubbed a long home run in his second game, a worthy introduction to the Bronx faithful. After that, he was fine in the regular season, dreadful in October, and now is the source of a bit of a logjam on the roster.

Grade: B-

2026 Stats (With Yankees): 43 G, 169 PA, 21 R, 42 H, 6 2B, 7 HR, 16 RBI, 1 SB, .264/.302/.434, 102 OPS+, 104 wRC+, 0.5 fWAR, 0.6 bWAR.

2027 Contract Status: Arb eligible

To Brian Cashman’s credit he made more than one deal for bats at the deadline, but neither Heliot Ramos nor García Jr. really had the impact I’m sure he planned. Luis came in in grand style:

Will forever love you, Ruocco.

That the Yankees went on to lose that game, one of the stupider of the year and what a list that makes, maybe says something about García Jr. as a Yankee. Getting him was the right move, it just never really paid off. I was surprised to see that he actually managed an 87 wRC+ in the postseason, because I genuinely would have thought he posted half of that.

To be fair, he was On One against the Red Sox in the Wild Card Series, hitting .500, but then kind of everyone did that against the Red Sox in those two games. Facing the Rays in the ALDS Luis went 1-for-10, a -56 wRC+. The Yankees only who played three games against the Rays, if García Jr. went 1-for-10 in a random series in July we probably wouldn’t even notice, but it happened when the lights were brightest.

I’ve said this a lot about the 2026 Yankees, but Luis didn’t really do himself many favors. Yes, he has that big home run in his second game against the Cardinals, then he went two weeks without another dinger. He slugged .560 in Washington, .434 in New York. His strikeout rate jumped five points.

I do wonder how much the dimensions of Yankee Stadium worked against him. Luis was on pace for 33 doubles across 600 PA while with the Nationals, down to 22 over 600 PA in the Bronx. Yankee Stadium is a tremendous ballpark to hit home runs in, and not very friendly to other kinds of extra-base hits. The short right field wall was absolutely a reason why the club brought Heliot Ramos in, and maybe they believed that smooth lefty swing would turn some of those doubles into dingers for García Jr., but that didn’t happen, at least in 2026.

Moving into 2027, the Yankee have once again assembled a roster with so many first base/DH types. Giancarlo Stanton will be on the roster, Ben Rice is not going to be a catcher, Aaron Judge will almost certainly see less time in the outfield than we’re used to, if for no other reason than the game’s best hitter needs to sometimes be saved from himself. Then there’s García Jr., who on paper is possibly a better hitter than Stanton but not better than Rice or Judge, but also probably isn’t good enough at second base to merit uprooting Anthony Volpe.

Maybe that’s the project for this winter, making Luis García Jr. a functional second baseman and stick him in some kind of first base/second base platoon role. This gets complicated if there’s a lockout and teams can’t communicate with players, and is further complicated by the fact that the very bad Washington Nationals gave Luis 0 innings at the keystone last year.

I want to love Luis García Jr. I don’t think his performance in 2026 merits that love. I am hopeful, perhaps foolishly, that it will in 2027.
