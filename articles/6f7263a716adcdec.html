Digital skills are an increasing requirement in NYC jobs beyond tech, report finds

By Walter Wuthmann · Gothamist · November 9, 2026

A new report finds that nearly half of all job postings in New York City now seek digital skills, including home health care, construction and retail jobs.

Forty-four percent of job postings in the city now seek applicants proficient with things like productivity software and AI, compared to 35% a decade ago, according to a study released earlier this month by the Center for an Urban Future, an independent think tank.

“ These are jobs that have long provided a foothold in the economy, and they're becoming harder to access,” said Eli Dvorkin, the center’s editorial and policy director. “The danger is that there is a new barrier to opportunity for New Yorkers who are already struggling, in many cases, to get ahead.”

Researchers analyzed hundreds of thousands of public job postings between 2015 and 2025. The report found that the share of job listings asking for digital skills increased in nearly every sector the study tracked.

For home health care services, where over half of workers have a high school diploma as their highest level of education, the report found nearly a quarter of job listings now ask applicants for digital proficiency.

“For years as a city, we've thought about the digital divide largely in terms of who has a computer or an internet connection,” Dvorkin said. “Those things still matter, but the new digital divide is about who has the skills to succeed in an increasingly digital economy.”

Data shows the top five in-demand digital skills were Microsoft Office, Data Analysis, Python coding language, Microsoft Outlook and familiarity with AI software.

“ If Mayor Mamdani is to succeed in making New York more affordable, it's not going to be enough to only focus on lowering the cost of living. Policymakers are also going to need to help more New Yorkers qualify for better-paying jobs and building digital skills across our economy,” Dvorkin said.

City Hall and the city Economic Development Corporation did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
