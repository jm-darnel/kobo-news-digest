Yankees 2026 Roster Report Card: Spencer Jones

By Matt Ferenchick · Pinstripe Alley · November 18, 2026

Coming into 2026, Spencer Jones was in a weird spot as a prospect. Throughout most of his minor league career, Jones had produced good numbers, including slugging 35 home runs across Double-A and Triple-A in 2025. He had cracked various top prospect lists throughout his time in the minors. When Jones made contact, good things tended to happen.

However, the issue was that Jones often did not make contact. All three of his full seasons in the minors saw him strike out 150+ times. He’s had a strikeout rate near or above 30 percent every season since he got above Low-A ball. There was certainly reason to worry that would be a thing that big league pitchers could exploit, rendering the power he has mostly meaningless.

Injuries precipitated Jones getting his call up to the bigs a little earlier than we might have guessed, leading to him appearing in 86 major league games in 2026. While Jones didn’t end up looking like a second version of Aaron Judge in those games, there’s reason to hope that he can carve out a role for himself in the majors.

Grade: B+

2026 Statistics: 86 games, 289 plate appearances, .245/.313/.429, 106 wRC+, 11 home runs, 40 RBI, 36.3 K%, 8.7 BB%, -4 OAA, 0.7 fWAR, 0.9 rWAR

2027 Contract Status: Pre-arbitration

Following an early May injury to Jasson Domínguez, the Yankees called up Jones. He starting by facing about as tough a task as you can in 2026, as his debut came against the eventual champion Brewers and NL Cy Young winner Jacob Misiorowski. Jones did go 0-for-2 with two strikeouts for the game, but did manage to work a walk off Misiorowski. His first major league hit came later that series, when he singled off Logan Henderson. After 10 games where he OPSed .426, Jones was sent back down to the minors.

However, he wouldn’t stay there long. After Judge suffered his first injury of the season, Jones was recalled. His return featured a three-hit performance against Sonny Gray and the Red Sox, and he crushed his first MLB home run a few days after that.

After slumping a bit after that, Jones was again sent down in early July. However, yet another injury — this one to Cody Bellinger — led to him getting recalled once again. After that, Jones remained with the Yankees for the rest of the season.

Following that initial stint, Jones actually hit decently enough. From his June 5th recall until the end of the regular season, he put up a respectable .774 OPS. He still struck out a lot, but that’s probably just going to be something he’ll have to work through even when he’s hitting well. Jones will likely always have at-bats where it looks like he’s never played baseball before, but he showed somewhat of an ability to adjust to major league pitching, which was a worry when he was striking out a ton in the minors.

One thing that wasn’t as good as hoped, though, was his defense. Jones got decent enough grades around his defense throughout his time in the minors, having regularly played in center field. However, both on advanced numbers and on the eye test, his defense left a little to be desired. His numbers in right field graded out okay, but there’s a certain other guy who plays over there.

If Jones can figure out defense at the big league level and put up a .750+ OPS, he could end up as a very useful player. Should he regress, he would hardly be the first player to ever fall short of expectations after a decent start, but there’s certainly reason to be hopeful.
