Homeless New Yorkers’ healthcare costs plummeted after getting housed, study finds

By Caroline Lewis · Gothamist · November 12, 2026

An NYC Health and Hospitals initiative to help homeless patients find housing is paying off in the form of fewer emergency department visits and lower Medicaid costs, according to a new study by the public hospital system and its insurance subsidiary, MetroHealthPlus.

At the same time, the group’s spending on doctors’ visits and other non-emergency services such as substance use treatment increased, suggesting they were getting more preventive care.

Under the Housing for Health Initiative, started by the Adams administration, the hospital system has been helping patients find permanent housing, while also using its land to build supportive housing developments and creating new transitional housing options for people leaving inpatient hospital stays who have nowhere to go.

“We have to think about our robust patient population that experiences homelessness,” said Leora Jontef, who leads NYC Health and Hospitals’ Housing for Health initiative. “This patient population is sicker than housed New Yorkers.”

NYC Health and Hospitals serves more than 60,000 patients a year who are homeless or unstably housed and many of them are what those in the healthcare field refer to as ‘frequent flyers’ — patients who visit the emergency department or get admitted for inpatient care over and over.

Among the study cohort, emergency department visits declined by about 40% in the first two years after getting housing placements, while inpatient stays dropped 36%.

However, healthcare spending among those that got housed was not lower than the broader homeless population enrolled in MetroHealthPlus overall, the study found.
