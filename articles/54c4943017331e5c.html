NYC Juvenile Lockups Packed Beyond Capacity as Overtime Soars: Audit

By Reuven Blau · The City · November 13, 2026

“New York City is spending more money to keep young people in detention, and they are staying there longer,” state Comptroller Thomas DiNapoli said in a statement.

“The city needs to address staffing shortages and overcrowding and also invest in prevention efforts, including after-school programs, mentoring and summer jobs, to give young people more opportunities and help keep them out of the juvenile justice system.”

Overcrowding at the two facilities has gotten so bad the city has been forced to use classroom space as housing units. Earlier this month, a state oversight agency stopped issuing waivers to allow ACS to use those common areas for housing.

Among the initiatives is a new intensive community monitoring program through the Department of Probation that will combine closer supervision with clinical assessments, family engagement, educational and employment assistance, and other community-based services. The program is intended to serve 150 young people who are transitioning out of secure detention or at risk of being placed there.

ACS also announced plans to hire 56 additional youth development specialists and 85 employees dedicated to emergency response at Horizon and Crossroads.

In New York City, that meant transferring them from Department of Correction-run adult housing units on Rikers Island to the two secure detention centers operated by ACS.

But the department has struggled to staff and operate the centers as the number of young people facing criminal charges has ballooned over the past several years, according to a series of oversight reports and court filings.

In October 2024 the city’s Department of Investigation concluded that ACS’s point-based system to reward and punish the behavior of incarcerated youths, known as a behavioral management tool, had failed to adequately deter violence and contraband smuggling.
