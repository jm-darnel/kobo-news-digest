Yankees sign Jonathan Loáisiga to two-year deal

By John Griffin · Pinstripe Alley · November 20, 2026

So far this winter, the bullpen has been a clear priority for the Yankees, who saw no fewer than four important relievers from the 2026 season become free agents. After re-signing Tim Hill and bringing back David Bednar on the qualifying offer, the team has now made its third addition to the bullpen, coming to terms with old friend Jonathan Loáisiga on a two-year deal with a team option for 2029.

Canal 4 News in Loáisiga’s native Nicaragua had the news late last night, the man himself hinted at it with a picture of the NYC skyline on his Instagram story , and Jorge Castillo of ESPN confirmed it this morning. MLB.com’s Mark Feinsand reported the total outlay of the contract as $18 million across the two years and buyout.

Yankees fans are, of course, very familiar with the man nicknamed Johnny Lasagna. Signed as a minor-league free agent in 2016 after a couple years in the Giants’ organization, Loáisiga spent parts of eight seasons with the big league club, and served as the bullpen’s ace fireman/setup man in 2021 and the second half of 2022. Injuries and ineffectiveness plagued the right-hander for the next three seasons, however, and after a lackluster 2025, the Bombers declined his team option for 2026.

It turned out to be the best thing for him. After sitting on the market for most of the winter, Loáisiga signed a minor-league free agent deal with the Diamondbacks last January. He made the team out of spring training, stayed healthy for a full season for the first time in five years, and put together one of the best seasons of his career, posting a 2.42 ERA in 67 innings. Perhaps more importantly, his advanced metrics were some of the best of his career, and helps explain why there were so many suitors for the recently-turned 32-year-old (the Mets, Phillies, Dodgers, and Blue Jays were all reportedly in on him).

Despite the small time apart, though, Loáisiga is not exactly the same pitcher who Yankee fans remember. Gone are the days where he threw the sinker like Mariano Rivera threw the cutter, i.e., pretty much every pitch. Although it’s still his main offering (he threw it 35 percent of the time last year), that is a far cry from the 54-to-69-percent usage rates from his first Yankees stint; in its stead, he’s upped his changeup, fastball, and cutter.

While nobody would ever consider Loáisiga a strikeout pitcher, these changes oversaw a slight bump in strikeout percentage to 18.4 percent from his 16.6-percent rate between 2022-24 and a slight drop in walk rate to a career-best 5.0 percent, all without sacrificing ground-ball rate. The question will just be how his body holds up after an actual full, healthy season.

Even with Loáisiga, Hill, and Bednar back in the fold, the Yankees are probably not yet done looking at bullpen arms — assuming they ever truly feel like they have enough bullpen arms. At this point, though, the Yankees have successfully turned it from a “need” to a “want,” as they’re no longer so thin out there that Yerry De los Santos and Angel Chivilli were listed as two of the team’s higher-leverage relief arms on the FanGraphs Depth Chart .

One could, of course, absolutely still argue that they still need a big swing-and-miss arm in the ’pen, as they’re still lacking in that regard and not everyone should be a pitch-to-contact guy. As we wait for the league to come to a screeching halt in 10 days due to the owners’ insistence on a lockout, getting some things crossed off the winter to-do list is certainly not a bad thing, and Loáisiga checks another box.
