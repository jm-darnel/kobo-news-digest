Are we bearish on a salary cap?

By Joshua Diemert · Pinstripe Alley · November 25, 2026

My father spent 40 years with his union, Sheet Metal Workers International Association Local 562. More than half of that time was spent organizing the union and bringing newer unions into being in the often burnt-out industrial sectors of Southwestern Ontario where I grew up. He served on the executive board of the local so often that formative years of my life were spent listening in on bargaining strategy, pension concerns, or dangerous bosses as dusty men sat around my parents’ kitchen table.

For each and every restaurant or bar I worked at in high school and later college, he grilled me on the joint’s health and safety standards. He took me with him to get his regular fall arrest certification once “just in case you need to know”. For my entire life, my father has been my idealized version of a labor hero. Until Sunday.

On Sunday, Tarik Skubal became my new labor hero. He inked one of the most ridiculous contracts you could possibly imagine, one that I think is already underwater, but that rising tide made Freddie Peralta and Kaima Taira more money. Tarik Skubal sucked every possible dollar out from one of sports’ more ghoulish figures, and in doing so upped the bids for every one of his brothers. Labor. Hero.

We’ve had quite a few deals signed since Skubal, with Peralta getting $25 mil a year for the next five years, Taira signing with the Blue Jays for $80 million, Trever Rogers will hurl for the Cubs while Teruacki Satō, who I wanted very badly will patrol a corner outfield behind him in Wrigley Field for $120 million. The Yankees have been a little busy themselves, bringing back Tim Hill and inking reliever Brent Suter.

Now, we have Jazz Chisholm Jr. and Randy Arozarena that will sign deals, and honestly I would be on the low end of those contract expectations, but it’s a hot market. This is not the behavior of players scared of future economic conditions, nor is it really the behavior of management that feels all that strong about a salary cap. They’re giving out more money, nobody is really settling for anything, and if some kind of a cap is brought in there’s no grandfather system in the world that would save the Mets.
