Percy Jackson, the Helm of Hades, and Annabeth Chase’s Yankees hat

By John Griffin · Pinstripe Alley · November 27, 2026

Earlier today, the third and fourth episodes of Percy Jackson and the Olympians season three premiered on Disney+. Adapted from the Rick Riordan books of the same name, the series centers on the titular son of Poseidon as he and his friends grappled with the gods and monsters of the ancient Greeks and Romans, alive and well and living in North America. Two years ago, in the aftermath of the season one finale (and our Top 100 Yankees series here at Pinstripe Alley), I dove into Yankees history, asking the rather amusing question, “ Which legendary Yankees figures were secretly the gods of Mt. Olympus? ”

As the new season of the television show approached, I found myself thinking about another place where Percy Jackson and baseball intersect: Annabeth Chase’s Yankees hat. A gift from her mother, the goddess Athena, the Yankees hat is more than a simple baseball cap, also functioning as a magical item that turns the wearer invisible. For those who know Greek mythology, it is a clear reference to the Helm of Hades (also known variously as the Helm of Darkness, the Cap of Hades, and the Cap of Invisibility), a magical item normally associated with the God of the Underworld but which others use sporadically throughout Greek mythological history.

Within the Percy Jackson universe, items and places from Greek myths are reimagined all the time: Mt. Olympus is in the Empire State Building, Charon the ferryman is an elevator operator, and Apollo’s sun chariot is a sports car. And so I began to wonder, why did the author decide to make a magical item inspired by the Helm (but which is itself not the helm — that specific item appears elsewhere in the story) out of a Yankees hat?

To answer that question, we first need to dive a bit into the mythical helm. While it’s hard to find an ancient depiction of it, here’s how Renaissance and Enlightenment era artists tended to depict it, as seen through this statue of Perseus holding the head of Medusa:

Sure, this doesn’t help us understand what the ancient one looked like (that’s a bit harder to determine), but it does show us that… well, it’s not some big special thing, just a normal everyday item that is imbued with magical properties. This, in fact, reflects most things in mythology: modern depictions of Cerberus, the guard dog of Hades, depict him as a big, scary monster, but in antiquity he was just a simple dog that happened to have three heads; see, for example, this statue group of Hades, Persephone, and Cerberus from the first century BCE:

With that in mind, it’s clear that, for a modern day invisibility cap, Riordan was looking not for something extraordinary and noticeable, but rather something incredibly mundane. And in the United States in the 21st century, what headwear is more mundane than a baseball cap? I mean, baseball caps with the logos of basketball, football, hockey, and soccer teams are everywhere. Colleges and high schools plaster their school logo on them. Even the US military and veterans’ organizations employ baseball caps, both as part of certain uniforms and as non-uniform gear. The baseball cap has spread well beyond its original use on the diamond, and has become one of, if not arguably the, American fashion accessory.

So it makes sense why Riordan would make the modern-day cap of invisibility out of a baseball cap. But why, specifically, a Yankees hat? It could simply be narrative convenience — the Percy Jackson series centers on New York, and when people think of baseball hats in New York City, they tend to think of Yankee hats (apologies to any Mets fans reading this, but it’s true). Narrative convenience, though, is not in my mind a sufficient answer, even if it hints at the real answer: the author immediately jumps from baseball cap to Yankee hat because, ultimately, the Yankee hat is the default baseball cap not just in New York, but within public consciousness. Check out, for example, the Wikipedia page for “ baseball cap; ” at the time of writing, the top image on the page, normally designed to give the reader an understanding of the topic, is a Yankee hat. And so, when trying to create the most mundane, unexciting, run-of-the-mill modern hat? Well, in truth, there’s only one option.
