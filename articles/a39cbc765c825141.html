Today, Speaker Julie Menin and the New York City Council announced the beginning of the 2026-2027 participatory budgeting cycle. New Yorkers are encouraged to submit project ideas they’re interested in seeing funded.

Over $22 million will be available for disbursement across 22 participating Council districts. This year, three new districts, Council District 4 (Upper East Side, East Midtown, Kips Bay, Turtle Bay, Murray Hill, Sutton Place, Tudor City, Peter Cooper Village, and Stuyvesant Town); Council District 17 (Mott Haven, Melrose, Morrisania, Longwood, Hunts Point, Bronx River, and Crotona Park East); and Council District 21 (Corona, East Elmhurst, Lefrak, and Jackson Heights) are joining the participatory budgeting process as new participants. The deadline to submit ideas is December 4th.

“For 15 years, the Council’s participatory budgeting program has given New Yorkers even more access to their government and given them a voice in how their dollars are put to work in their communities,” said Speaker Julie Menin. “Whether it’s expanding access to meals at a local food pantry to investing in performing arts education programs to planting more trees, we’re giving New Yorkers a real stake in shaping the future of their neighborhoods. I encourage everyone to get involved in this year’s cycle, think big, and help make our city an even better place to live.”

“After several years, I’m thrilled to bring participatory budgeting back to District 4,” said Council Member Virginia Maloney. “No one knows our neighborhoods better than the New Yorkers who live and work in them, and I’m excited to put $1 million directly into their hands to shape the future of our community. I encourage every resident to get involved, share their ideas, and help us build a stronger District 4.”

“The best ideas for the Bronx come from the people who live here! For too long, the people of the South Bronx have felt unheard, unseen, and left out of the decisions that impact their lives,” said Council Member Justin Sanchez. “Participatory budgeting is flipping the script by giving our community a direct say in how their money is being spent. I’m looking forward to seeing this initiative ignite a new wave of civic engagement that lasts long beyond the budgeting process.”

“Good government meets people where they are, and that starts by giving our neighbors a real voice in how their tax dollars are spent,” said Council Member Shanel Thomas-Henry. “For the first time in over 5 years, District 21 neighbors have the opportunity to participate in deciding how we invest in our community. From our schools and parks to the public spaces we share, the people who know our neighborhoods best should help shape their future.”

New York City’s participatory budgeting initiative began in 2011. The process allows New Yorkers over the age of 11 years old, regardless of immigration or incarceration status, to be civically involved and decide how part of the City’s budget is allocated. Since its inception, participatory budgeting has funded over 1,200 capital projects in New York City, distributing over $250 million.

Following the ideas collection phase, City agencies and staff will work to transform ideas into official ballot projects that meet the needs of each community. Constituents will then select their favorite projects during a nine-day voting period taking place in Spring 2027. Winning projects will be included in the upcoming Fiscal Year budget that is finalized in June 2027.

To learn more about the New York City Council’s Participatory Budgeting initiative, visit council.nyc.gov/PB.
