It's officially Christmas season in NYC. Can you afford a tree?

By Karen Yi · Gothamist · November 27, 2026

As long as her fresh Christmas tree costs under $100, Nicole Norris said she can stomach it.

“ Anything under $100, we're like, ‘Oh my God. OK, great,’” she said as she picked a 7-footer at a Manhattan Whole Foods with her family for $99.99 that was “not too full” and had a top branch perfect for her Donkey Kong topper.

Gothamist is once again tracking the price of a fresh pine and asking readers to help us report the cost of Christmas trees in their neighborhoods. To participate, fill out this Google form, tell us what price you paid for your tree, and share photos of your shopping experiences.

Last year, after more than 400 submissions, we found the average price for a tree was $116. The cheapest tree our readers spotted was a footlong mini from Trader Joe’s going for $10. On the other end was a 9-foot-tall Fraser fir on the Upper West Side for $633.

Some tree sellers told Gothamist they’re raising their prices slightly this year because higher gas prices have pushed up transportation costs and inflation is just making everything more expensive.

“ We don't have no choice. The market's so hard,” said Ruibel Diyarza, who runs Diyarza Landscaping in Brooklyn. He said he’s raising prices by about 5% this year. ”We don't want to raise the prices, but they just keep raising everything else.”

While tree prices tend to be lower at box stores like Home Depot and Whole Foods, according to crowd-sourced data Gothamist analyzed last year, independent sellers said you don’t get the same level of customer service, or sometimes, quality.

“Even though we're more expensive, you know you're getting a good tree,” said Karl Wiesner, who sells trees at Wiesner Bros. Nursery on Staten Island, a family-run business that’s been around for 98 years. Wiesner said they’re raising their price by $5 on their larger trees that come from Canada.

“If you shake the tree, you don't want any needles to come off. Also, if you grab a branch and pull your hand down it, you shouldn't have any needles in your hand.”

Buying at his location, also comes with good advice: “My big tip would be to always make sure you use warm water in the stand, and it's typically gonna be the first few days that matter the most for watering the tree,” he said.

Even at Whole Foods, 6-foot to 7-foot trees are selling for $10 more than last year, when the national grocer sold them for $89.99.

Pricier pines were more common in Manhattan and were cheaper in the outer boroughs. Last year, the median price for a tree in Manhattan was $120, compared with $99 in Brooklyn and $75 in the Bronx.

“ I feel like it's par for the course with what's been happening. The big economic dirty word is inflation,” said Antonia Bats as she browsed the $99.99 trees at Whole Foods in Lower Manhattan.

“Compared to the other vendors, it's still a pretty good deal,” she said.

Diyarza said New Yorkers looking to save some money can opt for a balsam tree that tends to be cheaper, or, just get a smaller tree. And if you get your tree early in the season, opt for a Fraser that lasts longer.

“We got so many sizes, so we just try to make sure the customer will be OK and enjoy Christmas,” he said.
