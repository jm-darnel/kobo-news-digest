Stevie Wonder celebrates 50 years of ‘Songs in the Key of Life’ at Madison Square Garden

By Johnny Knollwood · Amsterdam News · November 26, 2026

You can feel the heat in the world right now. The socio-political climate is tense and fiercely divided. Not a whole lot has changed since Stevie Wonder wrote “ Songs in the Key of Life ,” Fifty years ago, his landmark epic that reflected on topics like race, class divide, love and beyond, and one thing in particular still remains clear: Stevie Wonder is for the people. He’s always been for the people — and the people let it all out on Nov. 19, at the historic Madison Square Garden to celebrate 50 years of an album that changed the landscape of music and culture forever.

Little Stevie Wonder started his career as a child star, a Motown hitmaker with classics like “Uptight (Everything’s Alright)” and “Fingertips,” under his belt. But by the early 1970s, Wonder’s music became increasingly introspective and equally progressive. Wonder experimented with synthesizers and reached deep into the recesses of contemporary music — soul, jazz, funk, and rock and roll — to create something brand new. This culminated in “Songs in the Key of Life,” perhaps Wonder’s most lauded work that feels as relevant and fresh today as it did five decades ago.

Wonder swept the audience off their feet and on an incredible journey with great fervor at MSG on Thursday, opening with the album’s initial track, “Love’s in Need of Love Today” and setting the tone for the evening with a power that felt greater, stronger than all of us. The unity in the room was apparent — we sang together, we danced together, we wept together as Wonder belted out every classic on the record, including the four tracks from the “A Something’s Extra” EP that was included with the original release of the album, as well as a couple of bonus tunes. Fusion masterpieces like “Contusion” were played alongside classics like “Sir Duke,” that celebrate the rich history of music.

Songs like “Pastime Paradise,” that boasted shades of hip-hop to come, contrasted against sweet moments like the rendition of “Summer Soft.” Wonder took time to interject excerpts of classics outside the record like “Uptight (Everything’s Alright),” and “Living for the City” throughout the night, and at one point included renditions of The Beatles’ “She Loves You,” and “Love Me Do,” and Bruce Springsteen’s “Born in the USA.” It later surfaced that these were nods to Springsteen and Paul McCartney, who were both in the audience.

Stevie’s voice was angelic, his piano and harmonica playing unmistakably him. Wonder also featured special guests throughout the evening, including jazz harpist Brandee Younger, and daughter Zaiah Morris, who joined her dad for an emotional rendition of John Lennon’s “Imagine.” Wonder’s touring band was led by longtime musical director Greg Phillinganes and featured longtime bassist Nathan Watts, alongside a slew of incredible players that included a string and horn section.

While much was troublesome just outside the walls of MSG, inside, feelings of reflection, of hope and possibility flooded the audience at Wonder’s celebration. “We are the ones who make the difference,” he told the crowd to rousing applause. Later Wonder dedicated “Saturn” to victims of war. “I hope that we can all come to our senses and learn that peace is the only way for everyone to be,” Wonder said. “And I mean that.” Stay up to date with Stevie at steviewonder.net and stream the new EP, “Songs in the Key of Life – the EP,” wherever you listen to music.
