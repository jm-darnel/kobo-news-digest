Ms. Rachel, Brad Lander meet with children facing immigration court alone

By Kelly Mena · NY1 · November 24, 2026

Ms. Rachel is a world-renowned YouTube creator and Netflix star of children’s educational content, and now, she's lending her star power to bring attention to children at immigration court.

Kids like 9-year-old Angel, who faced an immigration judge on Monday alongside his mom, baby brother and unexpected guest, Ms. Rachel. His family didn't give out his last name.

“It’s heartbreaking. I care so much about every child, and I just want to see every child thriving and in school," Ms. Rachel said. "My heart goes out to anyone going through this."

Also there was Congressmember-elect Brad Lander.

He’s since been reunited with his mother, but has no lawyer — a wish Ms. Rachel says he’s hoping she could grant him.

“He came and gave me this, and it says, the first thing he wants is an immigration lawyer," she said, holding up a letter he wrote. "And then Nikes that are blue, and a TV."

"And he'll be getting all of these things from me, don't you worry," she added. "He just stole my heart."

Lander, for his part, said White House chief of staff Stephen Miller "is doing everything he can to be as cruel as possible to kids, and that’s not the country that we want to be in."

Miller is widely considered to be the architect of President Donald Trump’s immigration agenda.

"So this new Democratic majority is going to stand up for these kids,” Lander said.

Angel is just one of dozens of kids who face immigration judges daily, many without legal representation.

Making the issue worse, advocates say, is that a $250 million grant to the Acacia Center for Justice and their legal services network of over 100 organizations expired on July 31.

“Since the expiration of our federal contract as of Aug. 1, we are not exactly sure what is going on in custody," said Battina Schlegel, chief of staff at Acacia Center for Justice.

"We understand that some children may be shown a video that could be outdated about what to expect, but we are very concerned that children have much less understanding of what to expect in court than they did previously,” Schlegel added.

NY1 reached out for comment to the federal Administration for Children and Families, which administers the grant, and has not heard back.

To fill the void, advocates and faith leaders say they have stepped in to support families.

“We will continue to show up day after day after day, so that every person, even if it's one person at a time, can know that they are not alone,” said Rev. Amanda from Middle Church.

Angel is due back in court next month.
