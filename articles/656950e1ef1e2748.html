This nation’s founders believed that a democratic society depends on citizens having access to information, different viewpoints, and open debate. Therefore, a free press serves as a watchdog over government, business, and other powerful institutions.

Freedom of the press is not a special privilege. It is a right that protects the public’s ability to receive information so that citizens can make informed decisions, hold leaders accountable, and participate meaningfully in civic life.

FREE PRESS: What Does “Free Press” Really Mean?

Ebony Magazine was a lifestyle magazine launched in 1942 by John H. Johnson and Johnson Publications and sold to Black readers for an annual subscription of $2. That $2 request is equivalent to $41.81. Ebony Magazine’s 1948 offering at 25¢ per copy is likewise equivalent to an estimated $4.55 cost to the reader today. Jet Magazine’s reporting of the murder and funeral of Emmett Till is intrinsically tied to the start of the Black Civil Rights Movement. Without accurate reporting of certain events and the nuanced backstory, there wouldn’t be a record of history to be told from the perspective of Black freemen, the formerly enslaved, or those with critical insight into the Black experience.

New York City has recently structured a Black History curriculum despite nationwide rollbacks. At a time when literacy rates are dropping, the term “free press” is often misunderstood. Paid subscriptions and subscription fees, though historically reserved for newspapers and magazines, are now being assessed for Wi-Fi services, digital music, gaming platforms, mobile data applications, software applications, cloud storage, and other online platforms.

In the information age, thanks to the Information Superhighway, many now assume Free Press means that a news organization publishes information at zero cost. Even if the content is free for the public, media still cost money to produce. And these costs are exorbitant for media companies, publications that independently provide local news or other media content through print or digital means, television, or radio.

Monthly service charges become monthly expenses for media companies, which, at the business level, must also maintain web hosting, supplies, equipment, and other tools of the trade to disseminate accurate information fast and effectively. Still, as a civic duty, media companies produce and archive content, maintain the bandwidth to host data-intensive content, and acquire cloud storage and adequate IT support to protect data and stave off cyberattacks by ‘unscrupulous tech companies.’

What Is Independent Press?

What Free Press actually means is an independent press, independent from government control, political pressure, and undue influence from political parties, corporations, or other powerful interests that might seek to influence news coverage. An independent press protects the people’s right to receive information free from censorship and government propaganda. Producing quality media is expensive. Yet an independent press makes editorial decisions based on facts, journalistic standards, and the public interest rather than on demands from government officials, political parties, corporations, wealthy donors, or special-interest groups. Independence allows journalists to investigate and report stories even when those stories are uncomfortable or unpopular.

Why Independent Press Matters

Without an independent press, citizens would have to rely solely on whatever information those in power want us to know. Journalists and media organizations can dig for the truth, research the backstory, fact-check, and better serve the public by reporting comprehensive information without being controlled by government power or political interests. History shows that when governments control the media, criticism is often suppressed and public accountability suffers.

An independent press:

• Reports facts without government censorship.

• Investigates wrongdoing wherever it occurs.

• Maintains editorial control over its content.

• Serves the public rather than a political agenda.

• Provides multiple viewpoints and encourages public debate.

• Holds the powerful accountable regardless of political affiliation.

An independent press also helps ensure there’s someone in ‘the room’ you’d trust, asking the hard questions for:

• Government transparency

• Public accountability

• Informed voting

• Protection of civil liberties

• Exposure of corruption and abuse

• Stronger democratic institutions

Independent journalism protects the public’s right to know. It asks difficult questions, examines public records, attends government meetings, investigates corruption, and informs communities about issues affecting their lives. For publishers, journalists, and community media organizations such as Harlem World Magazine, independent press means maintaining the ability to tell important stories, elevate community voices, question authority, and inform the public without fear of censorship or political retaliation.
