DOT commissioner makes progress on Mayor Mamdani's transportation agenda

By Samantha Liebman · NY1 · November 20, 2026

New York City transportation officials unveiled center-running bus lanes on Flatbush Avenue this week, a project Transportation Commissioner Mike Flynn said is already helping buses move faster while traffic adjusts to the new layout.

“The buses are flying, you know, that’s clear,” Flynn said. “Traffic seems to be flowing. I think, yeah, as folks adjust to it, it should continue to improve as well.”

Drivers on the corridor have been reduced to one lane in each direction, a change Flynn said he expects people to adjust to over time.

Flynn said early data from the MTA shows buses are moving faster there.

“Some of the initial data we’ve gotten from MTA shows that buses are going a lot faster in that section,” Flynn said. “Traffic seems to be flowing.”

Grand Army Plaza is also expected to get a complete overhaul.

Flynn said the city wrapped up community engagement earlier this year and heard from residents about which design concept they preferred.

“So now basically it moves into the process as a capital project,” Flynn said.

In Astoria, a roundabout at a five-way intersection has drawn mixed reactions, with some people supporting it and others opposing it.

Flynn said the Astoria roundabout is not the first in the city and that roundabouts are known to significantly reduce the frequency of T-bone crashes.

“Every time you propose a roundabout around the country, it’s the same concerns,” Flynn said. “So you have to take those concerns seriously. But also, there is an adjustment period. So the one in Astoria, it’s part of that basically discouraging through traffic along that corridor, making it more walkable, making it safer for cyclists.”

More changes could be coming. In the coming weeks, DOT is expected to release a new city streets plan that will set benchmarks for progress for the next five years.

Flynn said those benchmarks may not focus only on a set number of new bus and bike lane miles.

“Quality can be just as important as quantity,” Flynn said. “You may have a bike lane where thousands of cyclists use it every day. Should we be widening it? Should we be improving it? That may not count towards new mileage, but it may be just as impactful.”

Flynn also provided an update on the 34th Street busway, which City Hall has indicated will move forward even after interference from the Trump administration.

According to the commissioner, the city will start installing some elements of the busway later this fall.
