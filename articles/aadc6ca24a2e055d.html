A rush to rent control in Jersey City ends in a setback for DSA council members

By Michael Sol Warren · Gothamist · November 22, 2026

A fight over rent control rules erupted in Jersey City this month, after progressive city council members pushed legislation to protect tenants of a downtown development from a rent hike — a move that ended with the bill getting tossed before it could be formally introduced.

But the measure proposed by Councilmember Jake Ephros, a democratic socialist who took office in January, was financially unsound, according to critics and other officials. With only Ephros and fellow democratic socialist Joel Brooks supporting, the nine-member council declined to entertain the bill. Now pro-development groups are claiming a win against rent control in a city that is rapidly gentrifying.

An early payoff and a warning

It’s been 40 years since ground broke on Newport, a master-planned community developed by the LeFrak Organization and the Simon Property Group. The neighborhood sprawls over hundreds of acres on Jersey City’s waterfront, right above the Holland Tunnel. It’s land that was a derelict railyard bolstered by landfill. Today there’s a mall, a row of high-rise hotels and office buildings, restaurants, parks and even a heliport.

“ This year, we were in a position where we could prepay the mortgage. We wanted to refinance it so that we could pay for and finance building renovations,” said Charles Burton, LeFrak’s head of government and community relations.

After paying off the mortgage, LeFrak informed tenants in September that below-market rents would only be available for one more lease. Rents would rise to market rate in subsequent leases.

Newport tenants said they feared being priced out of their homes and they called on city leaders to help them.

Ephros, a political newcomer who ran for council last year as a democratic socialist, responded to the Newport situation with an ordinance that would change Jersey City’s rent control law to eliminate a lifetime exemption for developments built in a designated redevelopment area.

“This is about more than Newport,” he said. “This is about tackling skyrocketing housing costs and moving just a step closer to winning housing as a human right.”

There are dozens of redevelopment areas across Jersey City, all designated by the Jersey City Redevelopment Agency, that are places where builders are incentivized to invest in struggling neighborhoods. Newport is built in one of those areas, but has long since ceased to be an area in need of investment.

About 100 municipalities across New Jersey have some form of rent control. The state law that allows for rent control exempts new construction for 30 years. Jersey City’s local rules give new construction in redevelopment areas a lifetime exemption.

“ The deal was that if developers come in and build housing, they would in return get the ability to have rent control exemption indefinitely,” said Ben LoPiccolo, the CEO of the development firm BLDG-UP and the president of the Jersey City Apartment Owners Association. “That was what gave them the incentive to take the risk to invest the money in a city like Jersey City.”

Nicholas Kikis, the vice president of legislative and regulatory affairs for the New Jersey Apartment Association, said the lifetime exemption was critical for allowing developers to recover the costs of high-rise construction.

“ When you're talking about redevelopment projects, they are significant projects, often significantly vertical,” Kikis said. “We're talking about luxury towers, and this is not the type of project that recovers its entire cost basis in a thirty-year time period.”

Mayor James Solomon said he was skeptical that the ordinance was financially sound. He worried about potentially discouraging development at a time when Jersey City faces a deep fiscal crisis.

“ We're not at a place right now as a city where we can afford tens of millions of dollars of loss to our tax base, particularly from some of the biggest developers in the city,” Solomon said.

But Ephros noted that other cities and towns which don’t offer the lifetime exemption have been able to sustain their own redevelopment efforts.

“ We have had decades and decades, 40-plus years of rent control in New Jersey, and we have not seen evidence of a collapse in property taxes or a collapse in property values that leads to this massive shift in the burden of property taxes,” Ephros said. “We just haven't seen that.”

Ephros’s ordinance was not the first time the redevelopment area exemption was targeted. A similar measure was introduced by the city council in 2019, but never received a final vote.

Thomas Zuppa, the member representing Journal Square, called the measure a potential “monumental shift” in city policy.

“ I live in a redevelopment zone in Journal Square with a landlord who raised my rent,” Zuppa said. “I have to set aside my own personal interest for the betterment of the city. Journal Square's had some lean years there for about four decades, and we're finally seeing some growth and development infrastructure built. So I wouldn't want to put us in a position where we're hampering that at this point without the appropriate studies.”

A new deal for Newport Towers

Though the proposal that would place the Newport towers under rent control is stalled, new affordable housing protections for tenants at the properties could be on the way.

Solomon announced Thursday that city hall had reached a deal with LeFrak for a new payment-in-lieu-of-taxes (PILOT) agreement. As part of the deal, LeFrak would increase the number of affordable units in the Newport towers from 272 to 299, with some offering rents at $1,000 a month.

“ We want to protect our residents. We want to keep them in our homes,” Burton said. “They're very much a great part of our community, but affordable housing is a public-private partnership, and we just hope that the city government works with us to accomplish this goal for everyone.”

Burton said LeFrak and the Solomon administration began negotiating the deal in October, the same time that Newport tenants were pressuring city leaders for help.

“Families at Newport were told this fall that their rents were going to market rate,” Solomon said. “Many of them had no idea where they would go. Because of these agreements, they get to stay, and get to stay at affordable monthly rents.”

“ It gives me no joy to offer LeFrak a tax abatement,” Ephros said. “But my duty is to protect these tenants who are fighting for their homes. And if this is the only way because we are not able to, this time, bring the building under rent control, I'm willing to entertain it if the deal is right.”

LeFrak had been on the verge of a similar deal with the city last year, after negotiations with former Mayor Steve Fulop. But the city council rejected it by a large majority.

Ephros and Brooks said they intend to bring back the proposal to end the redevelopment area exemption as expanding rent control remains a core part of the democratic socialist platform. It’s unclear when, or how it might be amended to win over skeptical council members.

Developers pledged to fight any future version of the ordinance.

“ I don't think there's anything you can change about the proposal to make me feel comfortable,” LoPiccolo said. “I think that when you really dig deep into any proposal that eliminates the rent control exemptions and you truly do an impact study on what will happen if that happens, it will really create a disaster for real estate here in Jersey City.”

Ephros cast the ordinance’s defeat as a learning lesson. He noted the recent win for rent control advocates in Elizabeth came after years of organizing and campaigning in that city.

“ I see this as a first step,” Ephros said. “We understand a lot more of the terrain that we're going to have to deal with.”
