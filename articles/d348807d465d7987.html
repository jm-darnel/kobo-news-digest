Federal judge rules Mamdani administration must give ICE names of Bronx shelter residents

By Willow Higgins · Gothamist · November 7, 2026

A federal judge has ruled that the Mamdani administration has to give immigration officials the names and birthdays of certain people living in a large, city-run homeless shelter in the Bronx.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement claims they need the information as part of an investigation into residents that may be subject to deportation. But City Hall, which has barred city agencies from cooperating with ICE, has fought the subpoena in court and advocates are urging Mamdani to keep up the fight.

“The City has not shown that producing this list would be unduly burdensome,” the ruling by U.S. District Judge Lewis Kaplan reads. “Under the applicable case law, the Court therefore has no choice but to compel the subpoena’s enforcement.”

ICE had asked for the names of all the residents, but under Friday's ruling, the agency has appointed a special master to identify only residents with “serious criminal histories,” according to the ruling.

Friday’s ruling comes after several failed attempts by the federal government to file similar subpoenas. Beginning in August 2025, ICE submitted four separate subpoena requests for personal information about residents of New York City shelters and the city fought them.

What “ICE has been trying to do for some time now is continuously launch these horrible campaigns of mass deportation against our community members,” said Murad Awawdeh, the president and CEO of the New York Immigration Coalition.

“ We believe that the city must appeal and that City Hall and Albany must act also on the simple principle of making sure they are also protecting everyone that's in their care.”

Awawdeh said that the city should not have to comply with the order because of the city’s sanctuary law policies, which limit cooperation between local government and ICE.

“Providing data on civil immigration enforcement…that's illegal as per our local law,” Awawdeh said.
