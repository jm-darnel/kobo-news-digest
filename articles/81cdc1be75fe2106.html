Queens drivers give thanks as Van Wyck work wraps ahead of busy season at JFK Airport

By Stephen Nessen · Gothamist · November 25, 2026

“I thought I was never going to see this,” Sandy Contreras, 36, who works at JFK Airport said on a recent afternoon. Contreras said the new roadway has shaved 30 minutes off his hour commute. “I'm glad I finally got to witness this.”

A new HOV lane on the highway open only to buses, taxis and cars carrying at least two people could reduce driving time by up to 25 minutes, Gov. Kathy Hochul declared at a ribbon cutting last week.

Ramnauth Kush, who lives in South Jamaica, was a child when construction began. The 18-year-old commutes to college on Long Island.

“ I would say there's not as much traffic anymore,” he said as he filled his gas tank up at a station right off the highway. “I mean, rush hour's still always going to be a mess, but I do feel like it has changed for the better, and it's finally done.”

But critics of the project said the wider highway will actually make traffic worse. The research group TransitCenter noted that Hochul’s victory lap over the Van Wyck ignored the well-documented phenomenon of “induced demand,” where expanded road capacity creates more traffic.

“Those benefits will likely be temporary because of induced demand, which isn’t accounted for in the project’s environmental impact statement,” TransitCenter wrote in a blog post.

“Rather than sink more than $1 billion for at best, temporary time savings, New York State DOT should cancel the Van Wyck widening and apply over $500 million of flexible funding to improve nearby transit instead,” the TransitCenter report argued.

Rolando Infante, a spokesperson for the state DOT, wrote in a statement the department "stands by the integrity of the data outlined in the EIS."

Hochul quipped at last week’s ribbon-cutting about the Van Wyck highway’s terrible reputation. She cited a 1991 episode of “Seinfeld” where Elaine Benes drove recklessly to the airport after oversleeping so an unwelcome house guest could catch a flight, only to be thwarted by a five-car pile-up on the Van Wyck. Hochul said Benes’ guest would have made their flight if they had driven on the new Van Wyck.

“ It's been a long time in coming, but Elaine, if you're out there, and you need to get to JFK, try it again. It's gonna be incredible,” Hochul said, making a double Seinfeld-entendre.
