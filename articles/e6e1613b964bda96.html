TPP Applauds SRG Disbandment Plan, Urges CURB Act For Free Speech

Harlem World · November 16, 2026

Disbanding the SRG, a unit notorious for brutalizing and intimidating New Yorkers exercising their First Amendment rights, is an important step toward protecting New Yorkers’ right to protest. The coalition called on the City Council to hold a hearing on and pass the CURB Act so these protections last beyond any one administration.

Members of The People’s Plan Steering Committee responded:

“Mayor Mamdani promised to disband the SRG, and he is following through,” said Grace Mausser, Co-Chair of New York City Democratic Socialists of America (NYC-DSA). “Counterterrorism and protest response should never have been housed in the same unit. Separating them, banning the NYPD’s most abusive anti-protest policing tactics, and backing Council Member Chi Ossé’s CURB Act are real wins for New Yorkers’ right to protest. They come out of years of organizing by advocates and New Yorkers who were harmed by the SRG. Disbanding the SRG is a significant step, but it is only a start to the work needed to make our city truly safe. Now we need the City Council to pass the CURB Act to enshrine First Amendment protections in law.”

“We are heartened to see Mayor Mamdani disband the SRG, civilianize first response to protests, and support the CURB Act, but hope for ongoing dialogue with the Administration about protest policing. We will be watching the Patrol Support Division closely, because New Yorkers have seen the NYPD rename a unit and keep its practices before,” said Fahd Ahmed, Executive Director of DRUM – Desis Rising Up & Moving. “This policy is still taking shape and implementation will take time, which makes it more important than ever that the City Council pass the CURB Act. The CURB Act would protect New Yorkers’ First Amendment rights and guard against the worst of the SRG’s abuses in protest policing in a way that is permanent and lasts beyond any one administration. A future mayor or police commissioner should not be able to undo these changes on their own.”

“Since it was created in 2015, the SRG has used kettling, mass arrests, and force against New Yorkers exercising their First Amendment rights, and it has cost the city tens of millions of dollars in legal settlements,” said Audrey Sasson, Executive Director of Jews For Racial & Economic Justice (JFREJ). “The Mayor’s plan disbands the unit and makes several positive policy changes: it moves the NYPD’s counterterrorism work out of protest policing, bans some of the most abusive tactics the NYPD has used against protesters, including kettling, tear gas, and the use of bicycles as weapons. While we remain concerned about the creation of any new NYPD units, we appreciate the Mayor’s commitment to not increase NYPD headcount or budget in this plan. The changes announced today are a step in the right direction, but banning tactics does not mean the NYPD will stop using them; chokeholds were already banned when Daniel Pantaleo murdered Eric Garner in 2014. It is vital that the City Council pass and enact the CURB Act to codify these reforms into law.”

“Protesting is a fundamental right in the United States of America – so much so that the Founding Fathers wrote it into the First Amendment. Across this country, and at home in NYC, our right to protest has been increasingly under attack. That is why NYCC is thrilled to see the NYPD’s Strategic Response Group disbanded. This is an important step toward protecting New Yorkers’ right to protest,” said Olivia Leirer, Executive Director of New York Communities for Change. “We also know that disbanding the SRG alone is not enough to ensure that New Yorkers are protected from abusive and aggressive policing. That is why we also welcome Mayor Mamdani’s endorsement of the CURB Act introduced by Council Member Chi Ossé and urge the City Council to bring it to a vote.”

“We thank Mayor Mamdani for prioritizing the public safety and the First Amendment rights of all New Yorkers through today’s action. For too long, protesters exercising their right to make their voices heard were met with abusive tactics and violence on our city streets at the hands of the SRG,” said Murad Awawdeh, President & CEO of the New York Immigration Coalition. “Their training and skills were designed to fight terrorism, not New Yorkers marching and chanting for justice. The era when New York police officers can trample on our rights with impunity is finally over, for now. We look forward to seeing this new civilian-led approach in action, and call on the New York City Council to pass the CURB Act to ensure meaningful and lasting protections.”

Today’s announcement cannot be the end of protest policing reform, and disbanding the SRG alone is not enough to protect New Yorkers from abusive protest policing. The Mayor made these changes through executive action, and a future mayor or police commissioner could reverse them. The Mayor has said these reforms “accomplish the key changes called for by [the CURB Act],” and the City Council should make them permanent and enforceable by passing it. New Yorkers should be able to exercise their constitutional rights without fear of police violence, no matter who the mayor is.
