New natural history museum exhibit explores science of color

By Roger Clark · NY1 · November 17, 2026

“One of the scientific purposes of this show is to show how diverse reactions to color are in nature,” said Rob DeSalle, who curated the exhibition. “The things [our exhibitions department] have come up with in the show certainly are big wows. They are a lot of fun.”

A light lab lets visitors experiment with light and learn about mixing colors of light. Hint: It’s not like mixing paint.

There’s plenty for visitors to learn, including why leaves are green and why they change color.

“They are green when they are alive, and they are making chlorophyll,” DeSalle said. “They are processing sunlight and they are making chlorophyll, which is green, we observe as green, but then when they disconnect from the tree and fall to the forest floor, they are not absorbing sunlight anymore and what’s happening is pigments in the leaf are taking over and covering over the green.”

“Kids are going to have a blast with the lights and the colors and just playing around, but people who are interested in the concept of color vision and color perception are going to have a pretty good time in here too,” DeSalle said.

It’s hard to not get caught up in all the color.
