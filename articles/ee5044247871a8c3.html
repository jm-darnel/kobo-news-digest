Public Housing Community Fund And Hoopbus Launch ‘Every Court Counts’ Initiative

Harlem World · November 17, 2026

“New York didn’t learn and fall in love with basketball in stadiums and arenas, it happened on the concrete courts across public housing, and with this campaign we’re bringing life back to these incredible spaces,” said Alex Zablocki, Executive Director of The Public Housing Community Fund. “‘Every Court Counts’ honors the city’s basketball roots and will bring joy and community back to residents across all five boroughs. No one should have to play on a cracked court or put shots up on a rusty hoop with no net. By investing in these courts, we’re not just fixing basketball infrastructure, we’re restoring the places where generations of New Yorkers have played and connected with each other and the game.”

“These places hold lifetimes of memories.”

“Every neighborhood has a court that means something to somebody. One where summer games stretched past curfew, where block parties brought out the whole community, where you hit the shot you still replay in your head twenty years later. These places hold lifetimes of memories. When we let these courts crumble, we’re telling kids they don’t matter. When we rebuild them, we’re telling them they do,” said Tobias DiMarco, Executive Director of Hoopbus.

“NYCHA’s basketball courts have long been focal points where residents gather to stay active, build community, and develop friendships,” said NYCHA Chief Executive Officer Lisa Bova-Hiatt. “The ‘Every Court Counts’ initiative ensures our basketball courts will once again serve as beautiful public spaces for residents of all ages to recreate. We extend our gratitude to the Public Housing Community Fund and Hoopbus for their generous support in making this $20 million revitalization possible.”

Due to decades of disinvestment and competing capital needs, the vast majority of NYCHA’s basketball courts have fallen into disrepair, with uneven, cracked surfaces; unusable play spaces; broken or missing rims and backboards; and play spaces that are in need of investment. “Every Court Counts” aims to bring new life to these basketball courts across public housing communities. In partnership with Hoopbus, PHCF will work to redesign and renovate the more than 160 courts that have helped build New York’s basketball culture and keep them open, safe, and accessible for generations to come.

Renovations are expected to cost an average of $100,000 per court, ranging from approximately $25,000 to more than $500,000 depending on each court’s condition, size, and specific needs. With basketball buzz across the city following the New York Knicks’ 2026 championship run at the highest level in decades, now is the time to reinvest in both the courts that have shaped the city’s basketball culture and the NYCHA residents who use them every day.

“…bringing new life and energy to public housing communities.”

PHCF and Hoopbus previously partnered to revitalize three basketball courts across Brooklyn and The Bronx, bringing new life and energy to public housing communities. The partnership began in September 2025 at Patterson Houses in The Bronx, with a restored court featuring a community-inspired mural motif and new backboards and rims. Located beside a mural honoring neighborhood legends, including NBA Hall of Famer Nate “Tiny” Archibald, the project was part of PHCF’s Green Space Connections program and a broader renovation that also included two new playgrounds and an accessible adult fitness zone.

In 2026, the partnership continued with the repainting of “Seven Stars Court” at Tilden Houses in Brownsville, Brooklyn, and the revitalization of a court at Saratoga Village Houses in Ocean Hill, Brooklyn. The Saratoga renovation featured a resurfaced court, new art, backboards, and nets, in partnership with Metro by T-Mobile. Together, these projects bring basketball back to the heart of public housing communities and show the possibility of “Every Court Counts.”

Additional information about the initiative can be found at http://ourcourts.nyc.

About Hoopbus

Hoopbus is an international 501(c)(3) nonprofit rooted in the belief that basketball has the power to transform lives, build bridges, and uplift communities. It refurbishes courts, paints them as works of art, and brings the game to the places that need it most, often with its fleet of basketball buses signed by more than 100 NBA and WNBA legends. hoopbus.com
