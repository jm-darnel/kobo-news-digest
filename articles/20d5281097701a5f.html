Next on the Park Slope Food Coop agenda: lifting a COVID-era mask mandate

By Walter Wuthmann · Gothamist · November 27, 2026

Members of the Park Slope Food Coop are wrestling with whether it’s time to finally lift a pandemic-era policy that requires shoppers to wear masks two days of the week.

On one side are members who argue the rule, which is in place on Wednesdays and Thursdays, is outdated and results in thousands of discarded surgical masks. Supporters of the mask mandate say the policy helps older and immunocompromised members feel safe, especially during cold and flu season.

“It's stupid. It's wasteful,” co-op member Meredith Davis said Wednesday as she put on her mask. “It's just a holdover. It feels, like, slightly woke. It's bad.”

But many shoppers on the mask-mandatory day said they supported the policy.

“There's a lot of diseases in winter to worry about,” said Zach Gage, a Park Slope local, as he loaded Thanksgiving groceries onto his bicycle. “I have two kids. We're always thinking about dodging the flu. It just doesn't seem like a big deal to have masks two days a week.”

In fact, “ we should probably mask more places,” he said as he pedaled away.

Like many businesses, co-op leaders required staff and shoppers to wear masks in 2020. The co-op lifted the policy in March 2023 after surveying about 5,000 members. The poll found 68% of respondents favored making masks optional, while 32% wanted to keep the mandate in place.

But some members pushed back, arguing for a mask requirement during specific days or shopping hours. At the co-op’s October 2023 general meeting, a majority of attendees voted to mandate masks on Wednesdays and Thursdays to support “immune-compromised or medically vulnerable members.”

Free surgical masks are now provided at the door. As shoppers leave, they discard the blue masks in a cardboard trash bin marked “mask recycling."

But that practice has raised concerns among some co-op members about the amount of litter generated through the mandate.

“There’s a massive environmental impact,” said Larry Linsey, a retired lawyer who’s one of the sponsors of the measure to make masking fully optional. He said the term recycling is inaccurate, and that the masks are disposed of like any other medical waste. “This single establishment throws away almost a quarter-million masks a year,” he said.

In addition to concerns about waste, Linsey argues recent studies have challenged the efficacy of universal masking as a public health measure. Studies also show that N95-style masks provide much better protection than the thin surgical masks provided by the co-op.

Linsey said he and his co-sponsors filed their proposal eight months ago and it’s just coming up for discussion. Under the co-op’s governing structure, policy changes require a discussion period before an official vote.

“I personally don't think that encouraging a policy that doesn't really protect anybody is being kind to them,” Linsey said. “I just think it's kind of pointless, honestly.”

Others disagree. On a Reddit thread that’s generated hundreds of comments, some users chided those advocating for ending the mandate.

“For most people it’s a slight inconvenience,” said Redditor cheesus_christ. “For disabled people, it’s an important and effective accommodation.”

Katrina Dodson, who joined the co-op this summer, agreed.

“I think it's nice to have at least one fixed period when people who are more sensitive can be guaranteed that everyone's going to mask,” she said.

Elijah Sivin, who was working a co-op shift, said he doubted the policy’s efficacy as a public health measure but supported it in spirit.

“I think what they're doing is an awkward and not very effective compromise,” he said. “It probably annoys more people than it helps.”

Retired member James Schlefer counts himself among the annoyed. “ It's unnecessary. The pandemic was three years ago,” he said.

Schlefer said he doesn’t usually go to co-op meetings but was planning to attend next week to voice his opinion.

“It is an imposition. I don't like wearing a mask unnecessarily, and if somebody is worried about that, they should wear a mask,” he said. “Or maybe not come to the co-op.”
