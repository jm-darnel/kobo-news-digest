Yankees Birthday of the Day: Todd Erdos

By Jake Devin · Pinstripe Alley · November 21, 2026

Every season, every team in the league needs arms to fill in the gaps. Sure, the 2026 Yankees had a league-leading, star-studded pitching staff, but even they needed players to mop up occasionally, to shoulder the load when the lights had dimmed a bit. These are the kinds of arms that are tough to name in the season-ending Sporcle quizzes, players like Kervin Castro or Justin Topa this year, or guys like Chi Chi Gonzalez and Anthony Banda in prior years.

If there were Sporcle quizzes back in the 1990s, the feature figure in today’s Birthday series, Todd Erdos, would’ve been one of those deep cuts, a background character on a few championship teams.

Todd Michael Erdos Born: November 21, 1973 (Washington, PA) Yankees Tenure: 1998-99

Erdos was born on Thanksgiving Eve of 1973, and would go on to star on the baseball team for Meadville Area Senior High School in northwest Pennsylvania. We don’t have much in the way of scouting reports from almost 40 years ago, but by the sounds of it, Erdos threw hard, and later Baseball Prospectus reports compared his command of the strike zone to eventual teammate Jeff Nelson. Erdos evidently threw hard enough to convince the Padres to take him in the ninth round of the 1992 MLB Draft.

Erdos had a stellar introduction to pro ball, posting a 2.65 ERA in 57 innings in rookie ball in 1992, and earning himself a cameo in Low-A at the end of the year, where he gave up just one earned run in 13 frames. He’d return to Low-A in 1993 and continued to excel, before hitting a series of roadblocks in Single-A. After a midseason promotion, Erdos got crushed for a 8.31 ERA in 11 starts in Single-A, and he would miss the entire 1994 season due to a disease called empty sella syndrome , a rare condition which leads to the pituitary gland being flattened. Erdos returned to the game in 1995, but it took him until 1997 to break through to Double-A, where he posted a 3.36 ERA in 59 innings as a reliever, prompting the Padres to give him a run at the big-league level, where he ran a 5.27 ERA in 13.2 innings.

Then came one of the more interesting twists in Erdos’ story. Erdos was taken 41st overall in the 1997 Expansion Draft, as the Arizona Diamondbacks and Tampa Bay Devil Rays came into being. Arizona drafted Erdos, but he never made it to camp with the D-backs. They traded him to the Yankees for Andy Fox, the Yankees’ second round pick in 1989 who became a utilityman and frequent late-inning defensive replacement on the 1996 World Series championship team. Fox got into 10 postseason games as a member of the Yankee, never taking an at-bat.

It would be a similar story in 1999, with Erdos getting called up to join the Yankee bullpen in June and making a smattering of appearances. His finest game as a Yankee, and perhaps his MLB career, came in his season debut on June 6th. Roger Clemens got the doors beaten off him in a Sunday night, primetime matchup with the Mets at Yankee Stadium, Mike Piazza taking Clemens deep as he was chased in the third inning having allowed seven runs. Erdos relieved Clemens and pitched four shutout, no-hit innings, giving the Yankees a chance to get back in the ballgame. It was a chance they failed to take, with Al Leiter holding them to one run over seven innings as the Mets won 7-2, but it was a noble performance from Erdos nonetheless.

Erdos appeared in three more games in mop-up duty in June, finishing his season with seven innings pitched and three earned runs allowed. He wouldn’t see action for the rest of the year, but once again got himself a championship ring, as the Yankees swept away the Braves in the 1999 World Series.

In the 2000 season, Erdos finally got some significant run. He was up and down with the big league club for the first half of the season, appearing in 14 games and tossing 25 innings with a 5.04 ERA (just about league average at the peak of the steroid era!). Erdos earned his first save on April 17th, and in memorable fashion too. The Yankees won in an 11-inning thriller , with Joe Torre turning to Erdos in the final frame of extras with two on and none out. Around the midpoint of the 2000 season, Erdos ended up on waivers and was plucked off by the Padres team that drafted him. He wouldn’t finish the season with the Yankees, but he’d earn a third championship ring for his efforts. His final numbers as a Yankee were unspectacular, but respectable: 20 appearances, 34 innings, a 5.03 ERA (97 EA+) and 4.87 FIP.

Erdos struggled in his return to San Diego and became a free agent after the season. He latched on with the Red Sox in 2001 and tossed 16.1 innings for them before hitting free agency again. He bounced around the minors with a few organizations but wouldn’t make it back to the bigs again.

Erdos’ career concluded with a few stops in independent and foreign league ball, including a stint with the Brother Elephants of the Chinese Professional Baseball League, as well as the Long Island Ducks. Endearingly, Erdos describes his time with the Ducks on his Linkedin as such: “I was trying to get back to the Major Leagues.”

Erdos never would get back to The Show, and according to said Linkedin, he’s fashioned for himself a career as a financial advisor with Edward Jones. But he was fond enough of his time in the Bronx that he returned on Old-Timers’ Day in September 2023 for the 25th anniversary celebration of that powerhouse 1998 squad.

You may not remember his time in New York, unless you can recall that memorable night in Texas where earned his only save (or his presence on the Yankees’ rosters in the All-Star Baseball video games of that era). But Erdos had himself a solid career, and careers like his ultimately are essential to the game; there will always be a need for the journeymen who are happy to take the ball when few others are — and it doesn’t hurt to participate in a dynasty along the way.

Hats off to Todd today!
