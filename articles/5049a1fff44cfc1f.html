How one Brooklyn cat avoided the building collapse and was rejoined with his human

By Catalina Gonella · Gothamist · November 16, 2026

When 13-year-old Zariah Coleman came back to the apartment she shared with her mom, one entire wall had been shorn off, leaving the wreckage of a deadly building collapse exposed outside.

But what scared her the most, was that she couldn’t find her cat Isaac.

“The first thing I thought about was my cat,” she said during a press conference last week that quickly went viral. “They said they went inside the house to try to find him but I don’t think they really did… he’s hiding from them and I think he’s scared.”

Among the many who watched videos of her tearful plea was Will Zweigart, executive director of local cat rescue non-profit Flatbush CatsHe said he had to do something.

Zweigart went to the site the next day, but rescuing Isaac would be more complicated than just showing up and looking around.

For two days, Zweigart worked with cat lovers within the city government, including Allie Taylor, the director of the Mayor's Office of Animal Welfare, to get the safety clearance needed to enter the site.

“ We had to go through more city agencies than I can count,” Zweigart said.

Finally, on Sunday, the Department of Buildings granted him approval, and First Deputy Commissioner Yegal Shamash, the department's senior-most engineer, escorted Zweigart into Coleman’s apartment. Hers is one of the apartments with its interior now exposed, missing an entire side wall where its neighboring building once stood.

Armed with a glasses, hard hat, trap, carrier, bait, lights and, towels (for claws), Zweigart made his way inside. One video shows the precarious steps, covered in debris, that he had to make his way up to Coleman’s home.

“ I circled the entire space three times and I saw no cat,” he said. “All the regular places you might look on a hide and seek… Under the sink, under the couch,, in the bathroom, in the closets, underneath clothes, no cat.”

But he decided to check one more time, dreading having to tell Coleman, who was waiting outside, that he hadn’t found Isaac. It was in that last sweep of the kitchen, when he looked in the cabinet under the sink, that he heard “a little meow.”

“ Hearing that meow was a relief, but also even more special because I knew that he knew that we were here, and he knew that we were gonna help even though it was scary,” he said. “That's my favorite sound that I've heard in a while.”

Isaac was scared, and it took some work to get him into the carrier — clearing everything from the cabinet and nudging him into it, Zweigart said. But he was finally safe, and on his way to reunite with Coleman.

In a heartwarming video of their reunion, a smiling Coleman greets Isaac still inside the carrier, reaching her fingers through the holes in it. Isaac meanwhile, meows repeatedly.

“Isaac! Isaac! Isaac! Hi!,” she says. “Aw, he looks so stressed out… Isaac, it's ok!”

It was an outcome people all over the city, and even across the world were waiting for after seeing Coleman speak at the press conference Friday.

“ I think the internet was looking for a little bright spot, because I've had people from Australia — you can't get any further away — I've had people from the farthest reaches of the world asking about this cat, 'What's the latest?' [and] celebrating in the last couple hours,” Zweigart said in an interview Sunday.

Taylor, with the Mayor’s Animal Welfare Office, waited with Coleman while Zweigart searched and said the rescue took about half an hour but “felt like forever.” Isaac was taken to Flatbush Cats’ clinic for evaluation through Sunday night, Zweigart said.

“Seeing how incredibly relieved Zariah and her mom are was really moving,” Taylor said, adding that Isaac is an approved emotional support animal. “It's incredibly important that they have their pet during one of the most traumatic experiences of their lives.”
