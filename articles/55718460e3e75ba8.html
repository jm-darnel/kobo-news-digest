“…investigation revealed that a process responsible for establishing secure connections had become overwhelmed…”

Further investigation revealed that a process responsible for establishing secure connections had become overwhelmed, preventing servers from properly handling incoming requests.

Initial recovery efforts encountered additional complications. Microsoft acknowledged that some corrective changes were not producing the expected results before reporting early stabilization and improved system capacity.

By approximately 9 a.m. Friday in New York, the company said email queues had decreased significantly and were approaching normal levels. However, users of more than 20 Microsoft products continued experiencing errors, according to Bloomberg.

Governments, Businesses and Public Services Affected

In the United Kingdom, police forces experienced problems accepting crime reports online, while pharmacists warned that prescription processing could be delayed.

Japan’s shared government IT platform was also affected. Courts in Los Angeles and Singapore experienced disruptions, while Australian intelligence and federal police agencies reported operational impacts.

Financial markets faced communication challenges as Euronext instructed listed companies to submit corporate information by telephone.

In NYC, Harlem World Magazine staff reported “no work for two days,” in Washington, U.S. House staff reported interruptions to their work. One congressional aide told Politico, “There goes a day of legislative work.”

Schools, medical facilities, and transportation services also experienced disruptions.

“…an update that has gone awry.”

Australian Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said Microsoft did not believe the incident involved a cyberattack. He compared the situation to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage, describing it as “an update that has gone awry.”

As Microsoft completes its investigation, customers and organizations will be watching for its promised report and assurances that similar network failures can be prevented.
