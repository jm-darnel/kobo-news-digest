MTA will test body-worn cameras on bus fare enforcement teams

By Stephen Nessen · Gothamist · November 16, 2026

New York City Transit President Demetrius Crichlow said members of the so-called EAGLE team will also be retrained on de-escalation and emotional intelligence. The body-worn cameras will begin as a pilot program. New uniforms will be issued to team members, and the MTA is also creating a dedicated fare inspector customer incident hotline to report bad interactions.

Last month, members of the EAGLE team stopped the dean of a Brooklyn public school, Kyle Taylor, who said he paid his fare using a paper bus transfer. Video captured the fare enforcement agents wrestling him to the ground. Taylor appeared at a news conference a week later with his arm in a sling, saying he couldn't return to work and was still in pain.

“ It is not what we expect of them, nor what they have been delivering to date,” Crichlow said Monday. “It's a shame again that we have to address it, but this is not indicative of the level of performance or customer service that we give to our customers.”

Crichlow added that the agents involved in that incident could be fired.

“Charges will be drafted against select individuals and those charges could end up in discipline up to and including termination,” Crichlow said. “These actions are not what we expect or have come to expect from our fare inspectors.”

“This is a step in the right direction. However, these so-called EAGLE team members need to be held to the full extent of the law, meaning criminal charges should be brought on these individuals for using excessive force on a passenger,” Rev. Kevin McCall, a civil rights activist who has been speaking on behalf of Taylor. “The MTA has a duty, so I hope after they bring charges internally, they refer to the case to the DA for criminal charges.”

“Putting a more gentle squeeze on riders isn't going to fix the bus or raise the several hundred million dollars in lost revenue we need for better operations and maintenance,” Danny Pearlstine with the advocacy group Riders Alliance said. “The governor and mayor need to find sufficient, reliable funding to deliver the bus service New Yorkers deserve.”
