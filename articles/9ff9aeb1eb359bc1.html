Mamdani signs bill to end city's horse-drawn carriage industry

By Spectrum News Staff · NY1 · November 19, 2026

Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Thursday signed a bill into law that will put an end to New York City’s 150-year-old horse-drawn carriage industry.

Romanch’s Law requires carriage horse operations to cease by June 1, 2028.

“For 150 years, horse-drawn carriages have been a fixture of Central Park. Today, we are bringing that chapter to a close,” the mayor said in a statement. “This industry will come to an end, and we have a responsibility to the workers whose livelihoods depend on it.”

“We will work alongside them to ensure they have the financial support and career opportunities they need to build their next chapter,” he added.

Support for the ban picked up momentum after 18-year-old tourist Romanch Mahajan died this past summer when a horse pulling a carriage he was riding in ran away.

In her own statement, City Council Speaker Julie Menin said the bill signing marked “a truly historic day for New York City, nearly two decades after the Council first considered ending the horse-drawn carriage industry.”

“Today, I’m thinking of Romanch Mahajan, who should still be with us and whose tragic, untimely death reminds us why this legislation was so urgently needed,” she said.

“This law will protect the welfare of horses while ensuring that the hardworking New Yorkers who depend on this industry have the support they need to transition to new employment opportunities,” she added.

"This bill is an unconstitutional abomination," TWU International president John Samuelsen said in a statement. "It strips horse-carriage owners of the city-issued medallions that they purchased for hundreds of thousands of dollars — without mandating or guaranteeing compensation. It prohibits carriage drivers from selling their horses to carriage-ride operators in another city, or even another state where rides are permitted. And that's just the tip of the iceberg."

"Despite the good intentions of the mayor, the protections he outlined recently should have been written into the legislation," he added.
