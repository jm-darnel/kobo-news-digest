Giants face a perilous road to NFC East title as the Jets hold optimism

By JAIME C. HARRIS AmNews Sports Editor · Amsterdam News · November 26, 2026

With six regular season games remaining, the 3-8 Jets have no chance. But they did end a five-game losing streak with a spirit lifting 27-24 road win over the Los Angeles Chargers on Sunday. Progress is measured using various metrics. Last season, the Jets ended the NFL’s 17-game regular season schedule 3-14. Optimistically, with the 1-9 Dolphins up next in Miami this Sunday, the Jets are in a favorable position to have back-to-back wins for the first time since they won two in a row in Weeks 8 and 10 last season (they had their bye in Week 9), which is over one full calendar year.

A strong finish in which the Jets can snatch two or three more victories and generally be competitive in their losses is compelling evidence for supporters of head coach Aaron Glenn that the Jets are building a sturdy base and he should be afforded another year by team owner Robert “Woody” Johnson to move the team toward vying for the playoffs. Glenn is currently in the second year of a five-year, $50 million deal with the Jets signed in January 2025.

Powered by a superlative defensive effort, the Giants had an impressive and statement win over the Jacksonville Jaguars at MetLife this past Sunday. Under the direction of laudable scheming and playcalling by defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson, and a signature performance by second-year linebacker/edge rusher Abdul Carter, the Giants defense held the Jaguars — who came into the game 7-2 and in total control of the AFC South with the league’s top five offense — to 18 points.

They also handed the now 7-3 Jaguars just their third regular season loss in their past 18 games. Carter registered four tackles, two sacks, two tackles for loss and five hits on Jaguars quarterback Trevor Lawrence, along with 12 pressures, the most recorded by any player in the league this season.
