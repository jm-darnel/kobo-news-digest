How the presidential election of 1860 is eerily echoed in this year's elections

By Spectrum News Staff · NY1 · November 19, 2026

Historian Jonathan Earle is the author of “He Has the People: Abraham Lincoln and the Election of 1860,” a new book arguing that Lincoln’s rise and victory were far from inevitable in an election that redefined American democracy.

Earle joined NY1’s Errol Louis to discuss the fractured politics of 1860 when Democrats split, the Republican Party campaigned to stop the expansion of slavery and John Brown’s raid on Harpers Ferry deepened sectional mistrust. They also explored how Lincoln, a self-taught lawyer and former one-term congressman, beat front-runner William Seward for the Republican nomination at Chicago’s Wigwam convention hall.
