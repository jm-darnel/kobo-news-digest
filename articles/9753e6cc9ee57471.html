Mamdani unveils $25M expansion of NYC winter safety plan

By Erica Brosnan · NY1 · November 9, 2026

In the wake of a deadly winter in his first months in office and with the next winter approaching, Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Monday announced a $25 million plan to expand the city’s emergency response program during freezing weather.

“We have spent the past year preparing for any challenge winter throws our way,” Mamdani said in a statement. “No New Yorker should face the dangers of winter alone, and no part of our city should be left behind.”

More than a dozen homeless New Yorkers died last winter, in which there was a nine-day period of sub-freezing weather. Some critics questioned whether the mayor should have done more to involuntarily remove people from the streets to ensure their safety.

Up to 55 NYC Health + Hospitals WARM mobile units will also provide medical care and transportation to warming centers, hospitals and other heated locations.

Additionally, the city will add more than 600 single- and double-occupancy rooms, creating 800 shelter beds during the winter months.

On street cleaning preparedness, the Department of Sanitation says it has more than 2,200 vehicles ready to be outfitted as snowplows, more than 700 salt spreaders, 47 pieces of specialized equipment for protected bike lanes and 7,000 uniformed workers trained for snow removal.

New preparations include mapping every bus stop, sidewalk and crosswalk into the agency’s snow-removal routes and establishing better forecast triggers to pre-position equipment.

Meanwhile, after Staten Island was hit particularly badly during snowstorms in early 2026, the city is planning to add eight plow routes in hilly areas of the borough.
