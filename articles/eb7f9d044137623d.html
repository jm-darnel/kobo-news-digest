Mamdani's plan to wind down carriage horse industry takes shape

By Brittany Kriegstein · Gothamist · November 17, 2026

Mayor Zohran Mamdani has unveiled some basic details on how his administration plans to help carriage horse drivers transition to other careers once the industry’s ban takes effect — but the union representing the drivers remains highly skeptical that they won’t be left high and dry.

According to City Hall, the plan would give the industry’s 200 carriage drivers and stable hands access to career training programs at LaGuardia Community College, offering entrepreneurship support, connections to employers and tuition assistance. The city would also facilitate medallion buybacks.

Mamdani has repeatedly pledged not to leave the workers behind, but Transport Workers Union International President John Samuelsen said all of his proposals could “quickly be abandoned” if the city faces a fiscal crisis and needs money for other things.

“That would leave hundreds of horse-carriage workers, owners and family members on the curb with nothing at all,” he wrote in a statement, adding that the union still planned to file a federal lawsuit against Romanch’s Law — the bill that would shutter the industry.

Mamdani said his administration would continue working with TWU Local 100 to develop a full transition blueprint by April 2027. He also planned to commission a feasibility study to see whether electric carriages and pedal-assist bikes could be potential alternatives for Central Park tours.

Romanch’s Law — named after the teen tourist who died this summer after he was thrown from a runaway horse carriage — also stipulates rules for the horses as the industry shutters, barring owners from selling or transferring them for slaughter or for use in other horse-drawn carriage businesses.
