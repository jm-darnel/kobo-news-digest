Flip your ceiling fan to keep rooms warmer this winter

If you have a ceiling fan, turn it on and look up to see which way the blades are spinning. If they’re turning counterclockwise, it’s time to switch the direction for winter.

You may have noticed that the blades are slightly angled. That’s by design. The tilt moves air differently depending on which way the fan spins. Counterclockwise, the blades push air down, creating a cooling breeze. Clockwise, they pull air up and push warm air near the ceiling back down along the walls.

In winter, a ceiling fan spinning clockwise on low speed pulls cool air up and pushes the warm air that has collected near the ceiling back down along the walls, mixing the air in the room. Because warm air rises, the fan puts that warmth to use.

In summer, a ceiling fan spinning counterclockwise pushes air down and outward, creating a gentle, cooling breeze.

To change your fan’s spin direction, check the manual first. In most cases, the switch is on the fan’s housing or inside the light fixture. If your fan has a remote or wall control, look for a direction button there.

Running your ceiling fan the right way can also help lower your energy bill.

One more thing: make sure your ceiling fan is the right size for the room. Fans work best when the blades hang 10 to 12 inches below the ceiling, 7 to 9 feet above the floor and at least 18 inches from any wall.

For rooms up to 225 square feet, a fan 44 inches across or smaller is ideal. Larger rooms need a 52-inch fan or bigger.
