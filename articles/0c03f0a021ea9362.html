Yankees news: Loáisiga back; Gil, Cruz cut loose

By Nick Power · Pinstripe Alley · November 21, 2026

MLB.com | Bryan Hoch : As John covered yesterday morning , the Yankees have brought Jonathan Loáisiga back into the fold, securing another deal with a familiar face in the early days of free agency to augment their bullpen. The 32-year-old agreed to a two-year, $18 million contract with a club option for 2029.

Loáisiga spent eight years in pinstripes, making 193 appearances while struggling to stay healthy. He returned to form in a major way in his sole season in Arizona last year, posting a 2.42 ERA in 67 innings. The right-hander pitches off an exceptional sinker, which sat at 97.9 mph last year and helped him to an elite 55.8 percent ground-ball rate (though he uses the sinker a little less than his peak Yankees days). Loáisiga joins the re-signed David Bednar and Tim Hill as bullpen fortifications, with fellow free agent Paul Blackburn another name linked to New York.

MLB Trade Rumors | Anthony Franco : The offseason giveth, and the offseason taketh away. The Yankees parted ways with a pair of pitchers Friday, choosing to non-tender Fernando Cruz and Luis Gil. Each was projected to earn $2.3 million in arbitration. Cruz was a top late-inning option the past two seasons but suffered a partially torn UCL down the stretch last year. For the Yankees, that injury and the fact that Cruz will turn 37 in March made him expendable. For Gil, who won the AL Rookie of the Year in 2024, it was a precipitous fall from grace. Shoulder injuries and ineffectiveness limited the right-hander to 20 appearances over the past two years and, despite the fact he’s still only 28, the Yankees felt Gil no longer had a future in pinstripes.

FanGraphs | Davy Andrews : The signings of Loáisiga and Hill continue the Yankees’ trend of prioritizing ground balls over strikeouts from their bullpen arms. Hill’s is an extreme case — the southpaw comfortably led baseball with a 68.8 ground-ball rate but got torched when opponents did put the ball in the air, allowing homers on nearly 31 percent of fly balls. Loáisiga’s arsenal was tweaked by the Diamondbacks last year; his four-seamer, previously an afterthought, became a viable third pitch against which opponents hit .148. He also regained speed on his fastballs, perhaps the most critical element in his resurgence.

MLB.com | Bryan Hoch : For the 15th year, Brian Cashman participated in the Sleep Out, spending a cold November night outside in a sleeping bag to raise money for Covenant House, a non-profit that provides support for unhoused youth. He does this despite the many memes it inspires of the Yankees GM sleeping on the job, telling the Daily News , “I’ll take all those shots for a good cause.” During the event, Cashman gave some updates on the free agency process, which he equates to “drinking out of a fire hose right now,” due to the rush to make acquisitions before the looming lockout. He celebrated the re-signing of Bednar and Hill to fortify the bullpen, acknowledged he’s “having conversations” about upgrading at third, and declined to anoint Spencer Jones as the presumptive starter in center.

MLB Trade Rumors | Charlie Wright : Another topic of conversation at the Sleep Out was Jasson Domínguez. Cashman noted that the Martian is “pounding the ball in winter ball,” adding that he’s been fielding calls about the 23-year-old former phenom. It remains to be seen what the Yankees GM would be looking for in an exchange for Domínguez, who has disappointed early in his MLB career but still features an enticing raw skill set. The outfielder has a 1.069 OPS with seven extra-base hits and 12 RBI in 12 Dominican Winter League games.

FanGraphs | Eric Longenhagen : FanGraphs released their updated rankings of the Yankees’ top 37 prospects Friday. The upshot is that the system is below average overall and particularly threadbare among position players. The Yankees have more promising pitching talent, as eight of the team’s top 10 prospects are hurlers. Topping the list, though, is shortstop Dax Kilby. Evaluators love the 20-year-old’s swing and defensive profile; his ascent has been slowed by injury but Longenhagen suggests a “Gunnar Henderson-esque turn” is within the realm of possibility. Carlos Lagrange and Elmer Rodríguez follow as the top pitching prospects.

FanGraphs | David Laurila : The number-28 prospect on the list, Antonio Gomez, made a fortuitous decision before the start of the 2026 season. A strong defensive catcher whose offensive profile had stalled out, the Yankees farmhand flipped to the other end of the battery. The early returns from the mound have been strong; the 25-year-old features a four-seamer sitting in the high 90s, a sinker, and a slider, with encouraging control for a recent convert. Gomez’s 16.1-inning audition between A-ball and the Arizona Fall League are enough to encourage some evaluators to now consider him a legitimate prospect.
