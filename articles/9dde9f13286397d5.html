E-Bike Battery Safety Bill Heads to Trump After Bipartisan Senate Approval

By Greg B. Smith · The City · November 20, 2026

Batteries that are not safety certified are susceptible to bursting into flame, a phenomenon that’s resulted in 406 fires and nine deaths in New York City so far this year. The City Reporter has been reporting on this problem since 2022.

On Thursday Sen. Gillibrand, the bill’s lead sponsor in the Senate, requested a bipartisan unanimous consent on bringing it to the floor for a vote. The bill then passed with support from both Democrats and Republicans.

“We have lost far too many New Yorkers due to underregulated lithium-ion batteries,” she said. “Passing this bill is an important step in preventing future fires. I urge the president to immediately sign this bill into law and for CPSC to meet its requirements.”

Sen. Schumer said the bill’s passage “brings us one step closer to protecting the public and firefighters from the growing hazards of these low-quality and dangerous batteries.”

As the proliferation of e-mobility devices took off with the growth of delivery drivers during the pandemic, the danger of unsafe batteries increased annually. Since 2019, more than 1,600 e-bike battery fires have resulted in 46 deaths and 628 injuries in the five boroughs.

In 2023, after an alarming wave of battery fires, the city passed a law barring the sale of uncertified batteries. But since then, enforcement of this law has wavered, records obtained by The City Reporter have shown.

Last month a battery fire at a public housing development in The Bronx resulted in four deaths. Then earlier this month a fire in a Sunset Park apartment building resulted in six fatalities, including an 11-year-old boy. The cause of that fire has not yet been announced but fire officials confirmed two charred e-bikes were removed from the building following the blaze.
