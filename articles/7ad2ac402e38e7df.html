NYCEDC Releases New Data On Economic Boom Of Holiday Season From Hollis To Harlem

Harlem World · November 23, 2026

New York City Economic Development Corporation (NYCEDC) today released new data highlighting the economic boom and significant increase in shoppers, tourists, and visitors that New York City experiences annually in November and December.

In preparation for what is primed to be a busy time of year for New Yorkers and their businesses, NYCEDC analysis found that the holiday season generates $500 million in economic impact each year, with the city’s largest shopping neighborhoods from Bryant Park to Downtown Flushing to Metrotech together attracting nearly 1 million visits per day on Black Friday and December weekend days. In addition, this season supports the creation of nearly 2,000 temporary small businesses across sectors such as retail, hospitality, and entertainment. Through employing seasonal workers, stimulating local economies, attracting visitors to new neighborhoods, and giving a boost to small businesses and storefront entrepreneurs across all five boroughs, the holiday season creates opportunities for many New Yorkers.

“New Yorkers are what make our city such a special place and an international destination during the holiday season. They’re the seasonal workers stocking shelves, the vendors setting up stalls, and the small business owners decorating their stores and staying open late so the rest of us can finish our holiday shopping,” said Mayor Mamdani. “With New Yorkers and visitors set to fill our streets in all five boroughs, we’re thankful for the workers across our city who are doing the heavy lifting this holiday season.”

Economic Impact

• The holiday season generates roughly $500 million in economic impact for New York City.

• Retail adds roughly 12,400 seasonal jobs (2021–2025 average).

Entrepreneurship

• The holiday shopping period is important to the city’s storefront businesses and more than 40,000 storefront entrepreneurs. These storefront entrepreneurs are much more likely to identify as BIPOC (62 percent compared to 47% for non-storefront entrepreneurs), women (43% compared to 32% for non-storefront entrepreneurs), and to have been born outside of the United States (56% compared to 43% for non-storefront entrepreneurs).

• Each holiday season, at least 1,900 New York City businesses open in November or December and close by January. Most are one-person ventures, with many concentrated in consumer-facing sectors like retail, hospitality, entertainment, and personal services.

Foot Traffic

• Ten of the city’s largest Business Improvement Districts (BIDS) attract a combined total of roughly 1 million visits per day on Black Friday and December weekend days, 35% above a typical weekend day. These neighborhoods are Times Square, Bryant Park, Downtown Flushing, 5th avenue, Metrotech (Downtown Brooklyn), Fordham Road, Fulton Mall, Jamaica Center, South Shore, and Hub 3rd Avenue.

• Outer-borough hubs are major draws. Downtown Flushing draws roughly 125,000 visitors on Black Friday and more than 110,000 on an average weekend day in December. (2023-2025 average) MetroTech draws more than 96,000 on Black Friday and more than 93,000 on an average weekend day in December. (2023-2025 average)

• Black Friday draws roughly 3.5 million visits across the city’s BIDs, including nearly 1 million in the 10 primary shopping areas above. (2023–2025 average)

• On an average weekend in December, the city’s BIDs combined welcome roughly 3.8 million people, about 18% above a typical weekend day. The ten largest neighborhoods combined welcome roughly 1 million visits, up 35%. (2023–2025 average)

• In the city’s largest shopping and tourist areas, December weekends are busier than typical weekends. Bryant Park runs roughly 2.5x, Fifth Avenue roughly 2.1x, and Times Square roughly 1.4x. (2023–2025 average)

• The data underscores the importance of the holiday shopping season for New York City’s tourism sector. On Black Friday 2025, 70% of Times Square visitors were from outside the city and more than half were from outside the metro area, highlighting the city’s continued draw as a global holiday destination.

METHODOLOGY

Foot traffic and visitation estimates are based on NYCEDC’s analysis of anonymized mobile device location data from Placer.ai for BIDS across New York City. Figures for the city’s ten largest shopping areas reflect average daily visits on Black Friday and on December weekend days, averaged over 2023–2025, and are compared with a typical weekend day. Visits are counted by BIDs and may include repeat visits by the same person, so they should not be read as unique visitors. BID boundaries do not cover all retail activity in the city. Visitor-origin figures for Times Square reflect Black Friday 2025.

Storefront entrepreneur estimates are based on NYCEDC analysis of ACS PUMS 2024 5-Year data.
