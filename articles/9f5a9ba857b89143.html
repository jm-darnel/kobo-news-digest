Air travel in NYC slowly returns to normal after FAA ground stop

By Carly Haynes · NY1 · November 16, 2026

Flights were slowly getting back on schedule Sunday following Friday's nationwide ground stop, but many travelers were still dealing with delays and cancellations.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in an interview with ABC on Sunday morning that the worst of the disruptions was over, though it could take another day for flights to fully return to normal.

"We are going to see things come back to normal today but probably fully normal until tomorrow," Duffy said. "We had airplanes and crews out of position because of Friday, so it takes a little time to do that."

At LaGuardia Airport on Sunday, some travelers were finding things running smoothly.

"So far, so good" for Mike Philips, whose flight was on time.

"I run into delays all the time, so you gotta go with the flow and take it as you can," Philips said.

But not everyone was as lucky.

One group of travelers had already spent 24 hours at the airport and wasn't sure they would make it to Dallas on Sunday.

"Stressful, it's tiring," said Maximi Liaono.

"Now we're here, we spent the night here, woke up trying to get a flight, but still nothing. So now we're trying for Monday," Liaono said.

Federal officials had said they expected issues to be resolved by the end of Sunday.

Some travelers were making backup plans in case their flights remained delayed.

"We started to come up with a plan B of, OK, if our flight gets delayed, how long are we willing to allow that delay before we just get a rental car and drive home?" said Emma Mitchell.

Federal officials said a router overloaded the communications system with data during routine contractor work, causing the outage.

"I wish they'd get the air traffic control system fixed, because it's way overdue," Philips said.

Since the ground stop was lifted Friday, Duffy has criticized the aging infrastructure of the nation's air traffic control system and called on Congress to increase funding for updated equipment.
