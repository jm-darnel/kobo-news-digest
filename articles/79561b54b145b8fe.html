Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: Carlos Rodón

By Jonathan Farrar · Pinstripe Alley · November 27, 2026

Of all the pitchers on the Yankees’ roster, Carlos Rodón was the hardest for me to grade. His traditional numbers, a 7-3 record, 2.67 ERA, and 91 strikeouts, look great in the context of playing only half a season. However, he’s paid like an ace, with a $27 million average annual value, just a quarter million less than Max Fried, and only pitched half a season. The metrics tell a story of a pitcher who ground through the year despite finishing with a dazzling ERA. I was on the low end of the Pinstripe Alley staff, personally issuing a B. The staff average ended up giving the veteran an A-minus, or, as I like to think of it in this situation, an “Ace Light.”

Grade: A-

2026 Stats: 16 games (16 GS), 84.1 IP, 2.67 ERA (158 ERA+), 3.44 FIP, 26.8 K%, 11.2 BB%, 1.8 fWAR, 2.2 rWAR, 7–3 record

2027 Contract Status: $27 million salary (six-year, $162 million contract); full no-trade clause; free agent in 2029

Rodón’s 2026 season really got underway in October 2025, when he underwent surgery to remove loose bodies and shave down a bone spur in his left elbow. This caused Rodón to miss spring training and the first six weeks of the 2026 regular season. The team activated Rodón from the 15-day injured list on May 10th to officially start his 2026 season.

As one would expect, Rodón was rusty initially, and his command was off, to the tune of a 16.0 percent walk rate in May. Rodón needed 354 pitches just to record his first 19 innings of work on the season as he worked on his mechanics and tried to find a consistent release point. He looked better in June but was still issuing free passes at a high rate. Pretty much every start seemed to be a grind, resulting in six innings or fewer and around three earned runs. At the end of June, Rodón’s left elbow flared up and landed him on the shelf. This first stint of his season ended with a 4-2 record and 3.30 ERA across 46.1 innings.

It was not until mid-August that Rodón would return. He made three starts over the rest of the month, and his command looked a lot better. From the first stint to the second, his walk rate was cut nearly in half, from 13.4% before the IL stint (26 BB in 194 TBF) to 8.2% after his return (12 BB in 146 TBF).

Now, if you’re like me, after seeing all that positive, you might be thinking, that’s not the pitcher I remember watching. That could be due to nearly every underlying indicator suggesting Rodón should have been giving up more runs. Over a larger sample, predictive models peg his true performance closer to the mid-3.00s in ERA (3.29 xERA, 3.44 FIP, 3.73 xFIP, and 4.61 botERA).

Rodón also benefited from some favorable batted-ball results in 2026, posting a .220 BABIP that ranked in the 98th percentile and sat roughly 70 points below league average. There was also a big 50-point gap between opponents’ actual .254 wOBA and their .304 xwOBA, suggesting that hitters were not getting the results their contact quality might have warranted.

To his credit, Rodón kept the ball in the yard this season, allowing just 0.75 HR/9, and that elite mark, especially for a fly ball pitcher, helped his numbers significantly. Rodón lost command at times, contributing to some difficult innings, but he also found ways to limit the damage and keep opponents off the scoreboard. He did not allow more than three runs in any start and yet only registered six quality starts on the year.

Rodón’s 2026 season had a dash of batted-ball luck, a run down the stretch where he looked like an ace, and underlying peripherals that look more like those of a solid third option. One last little nugget: Rodón was the Yankees’ Roberto Clemente Award nominee for the second straight season, a commendation on his terrific philanthropic efforts through the foundation he runs with his wife, Ashley. Here’s to hoping 2027 brings a year of health and a continuation of outperforming computer projections.
