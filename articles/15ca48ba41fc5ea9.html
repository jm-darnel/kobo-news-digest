Fare hikes on yellow taxi cabs could keep drivers out of poverty, city report finds

By Ramsey Khalifeh · Gothamist · November 24, 2026

Cab drivers could better avoid poverty through regular hikes to New York City’s yellow taxi fares, the city Taxi and Limousine Commission wrote in a report published Tuesday.

But even with that fare hike, the average yellow cab driver now takes home from $40,000 to $58,000 a year before taxes, according to the report.

Over the summer, at least four yellow cab drivers died on the job. Gothamist reported that all of them suffered with poor health and were falling behind financially — and many were renters who couldn’t meet their weekly lease payments.

In issuing the report, TLC officials didn’t actually propose fare hikes. They merely noted it would be a good way to help the drivers.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani campaigned on improving the lives of working class New Yorkers, including taxi workers.

“Taxi drivers get up before dawn and work long hours to keep this city moving. They should be able to support their families on what they earn,” Mamdani said in a statement.

Bhairavi Desai, executive director of the New York Taxi Workers Alliance, said yellow cab drivers are struggling even more than what the report suggests.

“I think what the TLC’s painted is actually just a partial picture and the conditions are even more dire,” Desai said. “The yellow cab drivers are subject to more regulated expenses.”
