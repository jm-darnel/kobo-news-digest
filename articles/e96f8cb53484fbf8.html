Eric Ulrich says he expects to be 'fully vindicated,' could testify in corruption trial

By Bernadette Hogan · NY1 · November 23, 2026

Embattled ex-city Buildings Commissioner Eric Ulrich could testify in his own corruption trial as soon as next week, according to his attorney.

“I pleaded not guilty. I expect to be fully vindicated. I wanted my day in court. I waited four years to get here,” Ulrich told NY1 before returning to Manhattan Criminal Court on Monday.

“The government has failed to show that beyond a reasonable doubt that I’ve committed those crimes. So in this system and in America, you are innocent until proven guilty — not the other way around,” he added.

Ulrich resigned from the Department of Buildings in 2022, before Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg charged him with felony bribery and conspiracy charges.

Prosecutors allege he took cash bribes for favors while working as a top official in former Mayor Eric Adams’ administration. The men allegedly paying him, Queens business owners and brothers Anthony and Joseph Livreri, pleaded guilty to conspiracy and bribery in September.

“I think it’s absolutely clear as day that they were out to target not only members of the administration, but I believe the mayor himself. And they were successful in doing that and at great expense to people like me,” Ulrich said.

Monday, Ulrich’s defense team called Republican City Councilwoman Joann Ariola to testify in a bid to show actions taken by the ex-commissioner were nothing more than business as usual.

Lawyers for the Manhattan DA’s office instead argue Ulrich used her.

Representing the council district once held by Ulrich, Ariola said Ulrich asked her to hire Joseph Livreri temporarily until he could find a job for him at the Department of Buildings.

“I have nothing to do with this case, and I only appeared because I was subpoenaed, and I respect the court, and I came in to answer questions honestly,” Ariola said in an interview after giving testimony.

Ariola has not been accused of wrongdoing.

“I had no knowledge of Eric Ulrich’s dealings with Mr. Livreri other than he was once an employee, and certainly there was no play-to-play between myself and Council Member Ulrich or Mr. Livreri,” she added.

Though a federal corruption case against Adams was dismissed, Ulrich is one of several former Adams officials facing criminal charges with cases going to trial.

This trial is expected to resume Tuesday.
