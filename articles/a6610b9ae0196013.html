Mamdani Administration Plans To Close 2 Small Harlem Schools, Move UWS School

Education officials are proposing to shutter two of New York City’s smallest schools, the first formal closure plans this academic year as Mayor Zohran Mamdani grapples with a growing number of underenrolled schools.

Two Harlem schools are on the chopping block: The Urban Assembly School for Global Commerce, a high school that serves 112 students, and School of Earth Exploration and Discovery Harlem, which serves just 40 children, making it the city’s smallest middle school. Both schools would close at the end of this school year.

Officials also plan to move The Center School, an Upper West Side school serving grades 5-8, to address overcrowding on a campus it shares with a popular elementary school. A plan to relocate the school to a different site last school year fell apart amid intense backlash.

All three Manhattan proposals are expected to come before the Panel for Educational Policy for a vote on Dec. 16.

It remains to be seen whether these initial proposals represent the beginning of a larger push to merge or close underenrolled schools. Chancellor Kamar Samuels has indicated that addressing small campuses is top of mind and doing so could help reduce costs and expand academic offerings for students on campuses that are increasingly struggling to provide the basics.

“If a school is more underenrolled, it’s more likely not to offer the advanced classes that you need,” Samuels said earlier this year. “It may not offer the level of enrichment that you want to see.”

But Samuels has not shared a detailed roadmap for addressing the problem and has instead pointed to working groups in five of the city’s 32 local districts that are charged with coming up with community-driven plans on enrollment among a host of other issues.

Here’s what to know about the Education Department’s three proposals.

Closure Proposal: School of Earth Exploration and Discovery Harlem

City officials say SEED Harlem has gotten too small to be sustainable. Enrollment has plunged 68% since the 2022-23 school year, and it enrolled just six sixth graders this year.

It is also underperforming, with just 21% of students proficient in reading and in math, according to state tests, roughly half the average in District 5.

Closure Proposal: The Urban Assembly School for Global Commerce

Launched 13 years ago as part of former Mayor Michael Bloomberg’s push to open small themed high schools, the campus has been forced to end its career and technical education programs in supply chain management and marketing. With few staff on hand, its principal and assistant principal have stepped in to teach special education and some of its remaining career and technical programming, city documents show.

Like SEED, the school serves a high-need population: Nearly all of the school’s students come from low-income families. More than 90% of the school’s students are Black or Latino.

Despite the school’s challenges, the closure proposal has been gut-wrenching for students and staff, physical education teacher Gilbert Schuerch said.

“The amount of students I’ve held in my arms today,” he wrote on Substack after the closure was announced to the community, “and the number of tears shed — from me and the kids — has only reaffirmed how special this place has become to me and the kids.”

Relocation Proposal: The Center School

Facing overcrowding in its current building on West 84th Street, city officials are proposing to move the school 1.5 miles north to a campus at West 109th Street that houses P.S. 165. Officials say the move will help The Center School and P.S. 9, the coveted elementary school that currently shares its building, shrink class sizes under a state mandate.

Officials plan to upgrade the theater space at the new campus, though they acknowledged the stage is smaller and shallower.

Last year’s proposal to move The Center School also involved eliminating the middle school grades at P.S. 191. Separately, the city proposed to eliminate the middle school grades at Manhattan School for Children and close the Community Action School. Those proposals were withdrawn last school year. An Education Department spokesperson did not say if they are still under consideration.

“NYCPS remains committed to meaningful engagement with communities before issuing any proposals,” Education Department spokesperson Dominique Ellison said in a statement.
