NYC’s true Thanksgiving Day heroes: Greyhound bus drivers

By Ramsey Khalifeh · Gothamist · November 26, 2026

This column originally appeared in On The Way, a weekly newsletter covering everything you need to know about NYC-area transportation.

And with airlines facing so many issues — like this month’s nationwide ground stop by the Federal Aviation Administration due to tech issues — more people are turning to the trusty old bus to get home for the holidays.

Greyhound reports its bookings are up 17% this holiday week compared to last year. And that means the company needs to make sure it has enough drivers for the busy season.

Earlier this month, drivers-in-training were at the Midtown bus terminal learning the rules of the roads in the labyrinthine facility. One of them, Shakari Mahfood, said he was ready to hit the road. He felt a sense of pride and responsibility ahead of his Thanksgiving shift.

“I honestly haven’t thought about that before this job, how I might be missing my own events with my own family, but then again, I have 50 passengers in my bus,” Mahfood said, adding, “So I’m making a whole bunch of other people happy and making sure they get off at the destination in a safe manner so they can be with their families.”

During training, drivers practice steering 45-foot buses around the loops and ramps of the Midtown bus terminal. Drivers also perfect parking at one of the 183 gates.

Daouda Kone, a new hire at Greyhound, had to navigate the facility and park at a gate while his instructor, Carlos Santiago, sat right behind him. After Kone put the bus in park, Santiago checked the alignment of the wheels.

He gave Kone top marks.

“This is part of their practice technique. The more time behind the wheel and they’re going to get better and better,” Santiago said. “This is actually really good for a start.”

Kone, 32, was jubilant. Asked if he was ready to drive for the holiday season, he replied with a smile: “Honestly, yes.”

Greyhound’s training process for new bus operators can take up to eight weeks, according to Matt Maglio, the company’s northeast regional vice president who works out of the Port Authority Bus Terminal. In addition to handling the bus, trainees get schooled on customer service and safety rules.

In order to be hired, drivers need a commercial drivers license that allows them to operate large vehicles like buses, trucks and trailers.

“We want people who like to work with people, people who enjoy driving the bus,” Maglio said. “And it’s a pretty rigorous training program. We do a lot of in-classroom instruction, followed by a lot of instructor-led, almost one-on-one instruction, and then a lot of road time with a senior driver instructor.”

Mamdani inks new deal with Lyft to cap Citi Bike member rides. Under a new agreement between Mayor Mamdani’s administration and Lyft, Citi Bike members will soon pay less for electric bike rides. Jon from Brooklyn asks

Will NYC buses and busways ever be free? Why can’t congestion pricing and other funding streams be used to pay the fare?

Answer

We can’t say for sure whether the city’s buses or subways will ever be free, but we can say it’s unlikely to happen any time soon. While Mayor Zohran Mamdani campaigned on eliminating the bus fare, the leaders of the state-run MTA have pushed back against the idea. The agency collects about $600 million a year from bus fares, and more than $3 billion in bus fares. Making either service free would require lawmakers to sign off on a source of revenue to replace all that money, which would likely mean more taxes for everyone else. The MTA’s congestion pricing tolls, which rake in more than $500 million a year, are required by state law to finance the MTA’s construction budget, not its day-to-day operations — so that money isn’t available to subsidize a free fare.
