New York State Assembly to investigate AI’s impacts in K-12 classrooms

By Nicole Neuman · NY1 · November 24, 2026

A hearing is set for Dec. 1, 2026, where lawmakers will hear testimony from educational leaders.

“Our children only get one childhood, right? We don’t want them to spend it participating in some experiment that Silicon Valley is running,” said Melinda Person, president of the New York State United Teachers Union.

NYSUT is preparing to testify in front of the Assembly Standing Committees on Education, Libraries and Education Technology, and Science and Technology.

“Every decision that’s made about every product that goes onto a Chromebook in a school or that kids get access to via their school technology should be a really intentional conversation and deliberated with educators and parents at the table," said Person. "It should not be a decision that’s being made by tech companies."

NYSUT has “taken the position” that children pre-K through second grade should not have access to AI.

From the third through eighth grades, it believes students should learn AI literacy, and only in high school should students be allowed to use AI with adult supervision.

NYSUT is also calling on tech companies to protect students’ privacy and that any AI programs be tested before they’re deployed in classrooms.

Democratic Assemblymember Robert Carroll is chair of the Committee on Libraries and Education Technology.

“What we shouldn’t have and New York state is rare in this. New York Sstate is too often allowed for complete local control,” said Carroll. “We have 750 school districts. Which means it’s the Wild West. Sometimes, you have schools in the same district doing totally different things.”

Carroll is hoping for a more mainstreamed approach when it comes to AI.

Last legislative session, he introduced a bill that “prohibits the use of most artificial intelligence in classrooms prior to high school.”

It was referred to the Assembly Committee on Education.

He says he intends to reintroduce it this upcoming session.

Right now, Carroll says the one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI in New York City schools for grades K-8 is based on his proposal.

Student-facing generative AI refers to AI tools including chat and tutoring bots and writing assistants.

“The mayor of New York City, Zohran Mamdani, I sit on his AI task force in schools and in many ways his moratorium models my bill,” said Carroll. “There’s a basic moratorium for kids K-8, and then there are exemptions inside high school where they delineate specific programs that can be used.”
