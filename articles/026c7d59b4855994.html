Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: Ben Rice

By Joshua Diemert · Pinstripe Alley · November 25, 2026

Whenever a player underperforms his expected metrics, there’s always a little fear that he’ll be the type to permanently does so. There are a few of those guys around the league, they should be better than their production but for one reason or another, usually a reason we can’t figure out, they don’t. In 2025 Ben Rice should have been one of the best hitters in baseball based on his expected metrics. In 2026, he was.

Grade: A+

2026 Stats: 154 G, 667 PA, 105 R, 151 H, 24 2B, 4 3B, 41 HR, 97 RBI, 3 SB, .264/.363/.534, 146 OPS+, 149 wRC+, 4.2 fWAR, 3.7 bWAR.

2027 Contract Status: Pre-arbitration eligible.

It’s quite something that the brightest sparks in the Bronx this year came from two boys who grew up in the heart of Red Sox country. For all the annoyances the 2026 Yankees gave us, Cam Schlittler and Ben Rice kept us coming back, with both receiving MVP votes.

Nobody hits all year long, and Rice did have two cold spots in June, right when Aaron Judge went down, and again in the dog days of August, but he did the three things that impress upon a fan: he started the season red hot, ended it red hot, and clubbed the ball in the postseason. A 212 wRC+ in March and April, a 195 in September/October as the club locked up a playoff spot, and a thunderous 329 in what was otherwise a dead and dull postseason run sealed up one of the best non-Aaron Judge seasons of the “Baby Bomber” era — and being the only player to even show up against the Rays helps too:

Indeed that postseason, albeit just five games, is a perfect representation of what the Yankees were like all year. Rice hit in each of the top four slots in the lineup, and while the Yankees are probably at their best with Judge in the two-hole, Rice cleaning up and someone actually competent in the leadoff role, they had no Judge for so much of the season and no competent leadoff hitter save Rice himself. We all know the flaws in looking at RBI seriously, but Rice barely missed driving in 100 with these guys “getting on base” ahead of him down the stretch:

If I have one concern with Rice it’s what the hell the Yankees do with him positionally. He’s not going to catch anymore, that much is clear and I’m okay with it. He cannot play 95 games at DH next year. If Giancarlo Stanton sees the field in 2027 he’s the primary DH, Aaron Judge also needs to spend some time in that slot because BOY the team needs 150 games of Judge’s bat. Ben Rice will be 28 and I’m sorry to have to tell everyone this but first base defense just doesn’t matter that much, especially if you have a 150 wRC+. Keith Hernandez on his best day wouldn’t be able to corral some of the throws we saw from Ryan McMahon and Jazz Chisholm Jr. this year anyway, stick Rice there and use the DH spot more strategically.

Homegrown Kids Made Good was the story of the 2026 Yankees, at least so much as it concerned Rice and Schlittler. For Rice in particular, to follow a good-but-should-have-been-better season with proof he can be one of the sport’s best bats, being the Juan Soto Replacement the Yankees have been looking for since Soto went across town, Benny Barrels becomes one of the central storylines in whatever version of a baseball season 2027 turns out to be.
