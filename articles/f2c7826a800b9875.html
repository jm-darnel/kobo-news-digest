MTA falls further behind on $2B in upgrades to NYC subway signals, consultant finds

By Ramsey Khalifeh · Gothamist · November 16, 2026

MTA officials last year blamed delays in signal upgrades on contractors the agency hired and a lack of engineering expertise in the field of signal work. The agency earlier this year said the delays in the G line work were in part due to leaks that workers found in the Greenpoint Tube under Newtown Creek, which took a year to repair.

MTA construction chief Jamie Torres-Springer said Monday that riders were already seeing benefits from the disruptions to G train service, noting the agency has added new elevators and modern subway cars to the line.

“I just want to be clear that as we’re doing this work, the benefits to riders accrue incrementally even before we get to turning on the CBTC program and it’s a good new story in that way,” Torres-Springer said.

He said the delays to the Eighth Avenue project were “primarily due to software issues” and the "unavailability" of time to test the technology on subway tracks.

Dr. Nabil Ghaly, a signal technology expert who issued the report for the MTA consultant at the agency’s committee meeting, said it was difficult to upgrade New York City’s subway signals because the system runs around the clock.

“The [independent engineering consultant] recognizes that implementing [communications-based train control] on an existing transit system that operates 24/7 presents operational, technical and physical complexities,” Ghaly said.

Modern signals are already operational on the entire lengths of the L and 7 lines. The MTA also completed an upgrade to the signals on the F line in Brooklyn last year.

Officials said they plan to release a new report on Wednesday that lays out the MTA’s vision of implementing the new signal technology on every subway line.
