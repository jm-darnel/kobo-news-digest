Melba Wilson Brings Harlem Together For A Thanksgiving Tradition With Heart

Harlem World · November 24, 2026

In Harlem, a Thanksgiving table can extend well beyond the dining room.

At Melba Wilson’s Annual Thanksgiving Giveaway, that spirit found its expression in an afternoon devoted to local students, followed by an evening helping families prepare for the holiday. Approximately 1,000 free turkeys were distributed to members of the greater Harlem and New York City community, while a partnership with Timberland brought gifts of footwear to students from two neighborhood schools.

With Harlem native and recording artist FERG joining the volunteers, the gathering paired a familiar name with a deeply local purpose: showing up for neighbors.

An Afternoon for Harlem’s Next Generation

Before the public turkey distribution began, the day’s attention turned to young people. From 3 to 5 p.m., students from PS 161 and Wadleigh Secondary School for the Performing & Visual Arts received special Timberland footwear through the event’s partnership with the brand.

FERG helped distribute the footwear alongside other volunteers, bringing his connection to Harlem into the day’s activities. His role was hands-on: joining the effort, handing out gifts, and helping spread holiday cheer.

Here, the connection was straightforward: a Harlem artist participating in an event for Harlem students. The focus remained on the exchange itself, with volunteers and young people sharing an afternoon organized around generosity.

A Thanksgiving Table, Extended

From 5 to 7 p.m., the gathering shifted into Wilson’s annual public turkey giveaway, underwritten by Bank of America. Approximately 1,000 free turkeys were distributed, helping households across Harlem and the wider city prepare their Thanksgiving meals.

Their contributions formed part of a shared effort, with Bank of America underwriting the public turkey giveaway and Timberland partnering on the student footwear initiative. FERG joined the volunteers distributing gifts, adding a hometown presence to the afternoon.

Across its two parts, the day connected support for local youth with preparations for a family holiday. Students received footwear; households received turkeys. The offerings were different, but the intention carried through from afternoon to evening: to put something useful and welcome into the hands of community members.

That continuity gave the event its shape. It was not simply a distribution window, but a gathering with room for different generations and different needs.

Melba’s Commitment, Close to Home

At the center was Wilson, whose annual giveaway continues to make the Thanksgiving season an occasion for community care.

This year’s turnout underscored the reach of that commitment. The afternoon welcomed students from two Harlem schools; the evening drew families from the neighborhood and across New York City. Together, those encounters made the event both distinctly local and open to a broader community.

Thanksgiving, after all, is built around the act of making room. Wilson and her partners extended that invitation beyond any one dining table, bringing together resources, familiar faces, and neighbors preparing for the holiday.

In Harlem, the result was an afternoon of gifts, an evening of giving, and a tradition sustained by the people who keep showing up.
