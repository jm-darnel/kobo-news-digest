600 Harlem families receive free Thanksgiving meal fixings

By Emma Barnett · NY1 · November 24, 2026

“People are suffering, the need is bigger than ever,” Fernandez said. “People have to choose between paying for rent or food. At least on Thanksgiving, don’t worry about the food. We have it.”

Fernandez said the Thanksgiving meal is about more than food.

“It’s about the hope and the respect and the dignity that every human being deserves,” he said. “It’s the love that you have on Thanksgiving that you want to spend with your family, your parents, your siblings. That’s what Catholic Charities wants to do.”

Harlem resident Hattie Middleton said the distribution makes a difference for her.

“It’s very good and is helpful. And you don’t have to buy all the ingredients because they give you a lot of ingredients. And I thank everyone for helping us,” Middleton said.

Alfred Reese, another Harlem resident, said the food helps people who are facing financial strain.

“Some people have bills, and some people have setbacks, so this really helps out,” Reese said. “And I appreciate it. That’s why sometimes I help out by volunteering here.”

Gov. Kathy Hochul and Mayor Zohran Mamdani helped hand out ingredients to people in Harlem. “

“Today, I’m grateful that we can all come together to help make sure that those who call Harlem home will be able to set their Thanksgiving tables without worrying if they can afford both turkey and stuffing,” Mamdani said.

New Archbishop of New York, Ronald Hicks, blessed the food and made remarks during the event.

“We all look at each other and see the face of God in each other,” Hicks said. “See each other as true brothers and sisters because when we treat each other as true brothers and sisters, then this world is a better place.”

Catholic Charities of New York serves more than half a million New Yorkers each year and distributes more than 13 million meals.
