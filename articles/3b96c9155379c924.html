The Great Borough Bake-Off returns for fifth year

A depiction of a fun Coney Island day: A ride on the Cyclone Roller Coaster, a Hot Dog from Nathans,and maybe some games too — all made from gingerbread.

"I love the history. I love that there's people there from every walk of life every age, every background. It's just a very inclusive welcoming fun place — very whimsical."

It's the fifth year for the competition. A panel of judges selected almost two dozen gingerbread landmarks for display, out of more than 60 entries.

"Every one of these creations does tell the story of a neighborhood, an idea, a people, a person or a place or an idea that shapes our city's iconic character," said Stephanie Hill Wilchfort, the Ronay Menschel director and president of the Museum of the City of New York.

Mario DiBiase of SottoVoce Restaurant in Park Slope teamed up for a third time with Susanna Caliendo of Something Sugared to craft the Williamburgh Savings Bank Tower in Fort Greene.

"It's like the premier gingerbread installation in New York now. It's a great thing for the museum," DiBiase said.

Other landmarks include the Little Red Lighthouse near the George Washington Bridge, a bodega with the resident cats, a Bronx boom box and an award-winning depiction of Ariemma's Italian Deli on Staten Island. It features tiny details like meats, cheeses and salads.

Chiara Klein and Diggle paired up for the project.

"We wanted to kind of give a real feast for the eyes and something to discover in every corner," Klein said.

While visitors to the museum benefit from the sights and gingerbread scents, the bakers love it too, having been challenged to make a structure to last at the museum through mid-January.

"It's our third year, and we love competing in it. It's an honor, and there are so many great exhibits," Caliendo said.

Loretta Bricchi Lee is participating in her fourth bake-off, with a big Gingerbread version of Times Square including dozens of people there.

"There is a very good sense of comradery with people helping each other, so it's great," she said.

Some of the bakers say they are already thinking about what they are making out of gingerbread next year, for what has quickly become a New York holiday tradition.
