The number of vacant NYC storefronts is growing, report says

By Rosemary Misdary · Gothamist · November 28, 2026

New York City has an empty storefront problem.

According to a new City Comptroller’s Office report, 1 in 9 storefronts citywide was vacant as of September, with Manhattan and Brooklyn exceeding the citywide average. The city has launched a dashboard to track street-level vacancies.

“Thriving commercial corridors are essential to the health of New York City’s economy and neighborhoods,” City Comptroller Mark Levine said. “In many ways, New York City has recovered since the pandemic, but the comeback has been uneven, with storefronts sitting empty in many communities that deserve bustling activity.”

Last summer, between July and September, nearly 142,000 storefronts were shuttered, according to the findings. The number represented a slight increase from the previous quarter, and was above pre-pandemic levels. Since summer 2023, the citywide storefront vacancy rate has remained above 11%.

Across the five boroughs, Manhattan had the highest vacancy rate at just over 13%. Brooklyn was the second-highest at about 12.7%. Queens’ vacancy rate was nearly 10%, but the borough had the largest quarterly increase in shuttered retail spaces. The vacancy rate was 8% on Staten Island and slightly higher in the Bronx.

But city neighborhoods including the Financial District, Tribeca, Ocean Hill, East New York and Old Astoria-Hallets Point have some of the highest vacancy rates, all around 20%. Overall, the city recorded a net loss of more than 200 small businesses between spring and summer of this year, the report said.

Levine said the goal of the storefront vacancy dashboard is to assist policymakers, small businesses and community organizations track the economic status of shopping districts across the five boroughs. The dashboard will be updated quarterly.

“Keeping an up-to-date accounting of the severity of this problem is essential for us in government to develop solutions,” Levine said.
