Yankees add Carlos Lagrange, two others to 40-man roster ahead of Rule 5 deadline

By Peter Brody · Pinstripe Alley · November 17, 2026

Carlos Lagrange headlines that list of rostered prospects, and he is joined on the 40-man by pitcher Kyle Carr and outfielder Garrett Martin. It was part of a busy day for the Yankees in terms of roster additions. We had previously learned that David Bednar accepted the qualifying offer to return in 2027 and that the Yankees were reuniting with former top pitching prospect Chase Hampton via waiver claim from the Orioles .

Adding Lagrange was a foregone conclusion given the team’s plans over the summer to get him a look in the big league bullpen, those plans scuppered by a shoulder injury that derailed the middle of Lagrange’s season. The six-foot-seven righty is a consensus top-two pitching prospect in the Yankees organization. He has touched as high as 103 mph with his fastball and put in considerable effort to hone a secondary package of cutter, sweeper, and changeup. He made 19 appearances (11 starts) at Triple-A in 2026, going 1-4 with a 4.48 ERA and 4.68 FIP in 64.1 innings. Lagrange did well to rein in the walks to a more manageable 11.6-percent while still striking out just shy of 30-percent of batters faced. There are still long-term questions over Lagrange’s command as a starter, which coupled with the Yankees’ needs in relief motivated the move to the ‘pen. The Yankees still have a lot of open spots in the major league bullpen and Lagrange has a chance to break camp with the team in 2027.

Kyle Carr is the Yankees’ 12th-ranked prospect according to MLB pipeline, selected in the third round of the 2023 MLB Draft out of Palomar College. The 24-year-old lefty split time between Double-A and Triple-A in 2026, going 6-8 in 20 starts with a 4.58 ERA, 4.82 FIP, and 105 strikeouts in 90.1 innings. That being said, he experienced some serious growing pains upon making the jump in level, seeing his walk and home run rates spike while the strikeout rate plummeted. His fastball operates in the low-90s and is better at generating weak contact than missing bats. However, Carr showed signs of gaining velocity after joining the organization, though his sweeper and changeup are now considered average at best. Given the Yankees’ needs for left-handed relief, you wonder whether this addition was made with an eye toward stockpiling southpaw depth at Triple-A.

Garrett Martin is not ranked among the Yankees’ top-30 prospects by MLB Pipeline nor FanGraphs. The 26-year-old right-handed outfielder joined the organization as an undrafted free agent in 2023. He played most of 2026 at Double-A before being called up for eight games at the end of June at Triple-A; he missed the second half on the IL. Between the two levels, Martin slashed .270/.337/.570 with 24 home runs, 64 RBIs, 18 stolen bases and a 135 wRC+ in 326 plate appearances. His power righty bat and and speed are an intriguing combination, though he showed clear signs of needing more time to adjust to Triple-A pitching.

There are several notable names that the Yankees chose not to protect. They did not add Roderick Arias to the 40-man, the 22-year-old infielder failing to show appreciable progress in his five years in the minors after signing for $4 million as the No. 1 ranked prospect of the 2022 international class. Left-handed pitcher Allen Facundo rocketed up to seventh on the organizational prospect list in 2026 after posting eye-popping strikeout numbers thanks to his triple-digits fastball — perhaps they feel it is worth the gamble to leave him exposed considering he finished 2026 at High-A. Brock Selvidge was among the Yankees’ top pitching prospects as recently as 2025, but missed all of 2026 after undergoing an internal brace procedure on his pitching elbow — his floor is higher than many of his fellow pitching prospects and it’s possible he could go overlooked in the Rule 5 Draft.

All of this being said, there is a strong likelihood that the Rule 5 Draft will not be held in 2027. It was originally scheduled for the Winter Meetings in early-December, but with the CBA expiring at 11:59 pm ET on Dec. 1, it is largely expected around the league that the major-league portion of the draft will not occur.
