Legislation to prevent lithium ion battery fires heads to the president's desk

By Shannon Ryan · NY1 · November 25, 2026

Legislation to regulate lithium-ion batteries is headed to the president’s desk.

There have been 46 deaths and 628 injuries caused by lithium-ion batteries in New York City in that same timeframe, according to fire officials.

Earlier this month, a lithium-ion battery fire killed a 6-year-old boy and his grandmother in Colonie. A fire caused by a lithium-ion battery also killed six people in a Sunset Park rowhouse this November.

“When these batteries are faulty or poorly made, especially in China, they can turn deadly,” Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand explained.

In the Senate, legislation sponsored by Gillibrand and Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer just passed unanimously, with initial legislation from Bronx Rep. Ritchie Torres having already passed out of the House.

“This is a common sense, bipartisan bill that could save American lives by preventing lithium ion battery fires,” Gillibrand said. “The Setting Consumer Standards for Lithium-Ion Batteries Act would prevent fires by requiring the Consumer Product Safety Commission to implement more robust regulations for lithium-ion batteries used in micro-micromobility devices.”

Those regulations must be set within 180 of the bill, which is currently awaiting the president’s signature, becoming law.

Gillibrand told Spectrum News it also will allow federal regulators to halt the sale and distribution of non-compliant batteries — a critical tool, as most are manufactured abroad

“These fires are entirely preventable,” she said.
