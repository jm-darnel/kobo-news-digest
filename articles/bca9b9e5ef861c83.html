NYPD unit details search through rubble after Flatbush building collapse

By Carly Haynes · NY1 · November 17, 2026

Some of the first people to arrive at the building collapse on Flatbush Avenue last Wednesday shared their personal accounts of what they experienced.

Members of the NYPD’s Emergency Services Unit navigated darkness, heavy rain, unstable rubble and the threat of a secondary collapse as they searched for signs of life.

“We tried to size up the scene as best as we could, but the magnitude of that building collapse was just enormous,” Lt. Keith Hum said. “We knew it was going to be multiple days trying to find all the people within the buildings.”

On Tuesday, the unit demonstrated how they used technology and other resources to locate survivors.

Crews used thermal imaging and telescopic cameras to search hard-to-reach voids within the wreckage.

They also brought in both rescue and cadaver K9s.

“They can smell things that we can’t. And you know, no tool can do what they could do,” said Det. Joseph Brayuha, an NYPD K9 handler.

In the days that followed the building collapse, responders came across clothes, pictures, cell phones and toys — grave symbols of the homes that had been destroyed.

“Some of the places smelled like the kitchen, you know? You knew when you were in the kitchen or when you were in the bathroom,” NYPD Officer Xavier Mercedes said.

Within the first minutes of arriving, crews focused on rescue. They listened for voices and used their hands to dig victims out.

“It’s a sense of relief to actually find someone,” Mercedes said. “And then the first thing is to look for signs of life.”

Not every person pulled from the wreckage survived. According to the FDNY, eight people were killed in the collapse. Six people died at the scene, and two others died at the hospital. Fourteen civilians and four firefighters were injured.
