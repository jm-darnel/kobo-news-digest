Service upgrade coming to On The Way: An hour-long radio show on all things NYC transit

This column originally appeared in On The Way, a weekly newsletter covering everything you need to know about NYC-area transportation.

For three years, this newsletter and weekly radio segment on All Things Considered have dug into the nitty gritty of the subways and streets.

Now, much like an old A train getting upgraded to a sleek R211, we’re improving service for our audience. On The Way is launching an hour-long pop-up radio show on Thursdays. Starting next week, on Nov. 12, tune into WNYC 93.9 FM from 1 to 2 p.m., listen on the WNYC app, or check out our YouTube channel for the show hosted by me, Stephen Nessen. I’ll be speaking with my fellow transportation reporter Ramsey Khalifeh and editor Clayton Guse, bringing you analysis of the latest transportation news.

We’ll have special guests, including decision makers and the sharpest thinkers in the transit world. We’ll take your calls and welcome questions about your daily commutes, as well as your hopes and dreams for mass transit. Write me at [email protected].

It’s going to be a gas — consider this your invitation.

As we do in this newsletter each week, we’ll discuss the most important transportation topics of our time, from the redevelopment of Penn Station and the antiquated elements of the subway system, to the new projects like the Interborough Express and Penn Access. We’ll look at the ongoing issues in the transit system like fare evasion, subway signals, and the future of the taxi industry.

We’ll also look at the subway bottlenecks that slow your commutes, and share tips on how to legally avoid paying for the AirTrain, or navigate Exit 31 on the BQE without pulling your hair out.

Speaking of the BQE, you can bet we’ll get into that too.

NYC transportation news this week

Bus and bike lanes galore to come. Advocates say that a ballot measure that passed on election night will reform the process for the construction of bus and bike lanes and means more street safety projects throughout the five boroughs. The measure will amend the city charter to “simplify the review of street safety projects to reduce construction timelines,” according to the text of the ballot question.

“This really just defangs some of the opponents who have sought to slow down and stop these street safety projects,” said Ben Furnas, executive director of Transportation Alternatives.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s office estimated construction timelines on major bus and bike lane projects could be reduced by at least 33%. But the measure still requires the city to solicit input from community boards before moving forward with any street redesign.

SoHo basketball court set to leave MTA construction purgatory. The refreshed Grand Canal Court at Sixth Avenue, set to open at the end of November, will feature new benches, a water fountain, upgraded lighting and fencing. The courts were originally ripped up in 2022 so workers could build a new substation that provides electricity to trains.

Scary good ridership for the MTA over Halloweekend. On Halloween Saturday, 3.27 million riders got on the subway, the most on a Saturday since 2016. And during the New York City Marathon, the subway saw its highest Sunday ridership on record.

NYPD seizes e-bikes along a clogged East Village street. Eleven e-bikes parked along East 11th Street and First Avenue were confiscated by police last week. Neighboring business owners and residents have been complaining for months about idle delivery bikes clogging sidewalks and curbs, sometimes by the hundreds.

Watchdog says MTA should eliminate some jobs to prepare for budget woes. The budget watchdog Citizens Budget Commission recommends the MTA should make sweeping cuts to positions that it argues technology has made obsolete as the agency could be looking at a nearly $900 million funding shortfall as spending outpaces fare revenue.
