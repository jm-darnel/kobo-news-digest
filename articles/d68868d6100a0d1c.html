Yankees 2026 Roster Report Card: Heliot Ramos

By Matt Ferenchick · Pinstripe Alley · November 24, 2026

As part of the Trade Deadline moves this past season, the Yankees picked up outfielder Heliot Ramos from the Giants. While outside an All-Star campaign in 2024, he has never been an outstanding hitter, he’s generally been good enough that it seemed like a move that could at least lengthen the Yankees’ lineup with Aaron Judge out. Plus, he’s genuinely been outstanding against lefties over the course of his career, so there was potential for him to be tough out for opposing lefty pitchers.

Well, none of that particularly happened.

Grade: D+

2026 Statistics (with Yankees): 38 games, 136 plate appearances, .198/.309/.379, 4 home runs, 12 RBI, 11.0 BB%, 28.7 K%, 93 wRC+, -8 OAA, -0.5 fWAR, -0.4 rWAR

2026 Statistics (overall): 112 games, 442 plate appearances, .245/.305/.411, 13 home runs, 46 RBI, 7.0 BB%, 27.4 K%, 99 wRC+, -10 OAA, 0.2 fWAR, 0.5 rWAR

2027 Contract Status: First year arbitration eligible

Having come up as a prospect regularly on Top 100 lists, Ramos had a decent enough career with the Giants. However after the aforementioned All-Star appearance in 2024, he had never really kicked on. While he was never truly bad, his offense didn’t match 2024 and — as we saw at times with the Yankees — his defense often graded out as sub-par. With Ramos about to enter his arbitration years, that made San Francisco decide to make him expendable.

However, for the reasons mentioned in the introduction, the Yankees and Brian Cashman thought he might be a useful piece to have around as they dealt with having some large holes in their lineup. On deadline day, the Yankees and Giants made a deal, with prospects Kaeden Kent and Henry Lalane going the other way. It was a bit of a hefty price, but that can often be the case for a decent player who still has a couple years left before free agency.

Ramos made his Yankees’ debut on August 4th against the Cardinals. He recorded his first Yankee hit the next day and another with two walks in the game after that. However, Ramos’ early games with the Yankees were notable for him just… not doing much at the plate. Through the end of August, he had gone just 9-for-61, with even that helped by a two-hit performance in his last appearance of the month. One of those hits was his first Yankee home run, which came in his 21st game with the team.

Over the course of September, Ramos was much better, posting a .813 OPS in 17 games. Despite that, the Yankees only used him once in the postseason. Ramos got the start in right field in Game 1 of the AL Wild Card Series against the Red Sox. He went 0-for-3, was replaced in the late innings for defensive reasons, and didn’t appear again in that series or the ALDS.

Ramos’ September got his hitting numbers with the Yankees to only a bit below league average, but that was still far from what the Yankees were hoping for. With a 1.028 OPS, he generally did do his job against lefties. However, his defense — which was already questionable — was generally pretty poor after the trade, often making him unplayable in situations like the postseason. Part of that probably was due to having to often play in the sneaky tough Yankee Stadium left field, but he was still pretty bad.

Going forward, Ramos is arbitration eligible this year and will be under contract through 2029. Considering that the Yankees have been connected to Randy Arozarena when they already have something of a crowded outfield, it would hardly be shocking if Ramos ends up getting moved before the offseason is over. However, despite his struggles after the trade, he still hits lefties well enough that there’s a good chance his name is getting announced on Opening Day 2027.
