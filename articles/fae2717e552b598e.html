Yankees Birthday of the Day: Randy Velarde

By Jonathan Farrar · Pinstripe Alley · November 24, 2026

There are many players who have worn professional baseball jerseys but never won a championship. However, there are very few who played for the Yankees in both the 1990s and 2000s and came so close to postseason glory without getting to celebrate a title. Randy Velarde played parts of 10 seasons for the Yankees, but somehow found his time in pinstripes bookending the dynasty’s championship run.

Randy Lee Velarde Born: November 24, 1962 (Midland, TX) Yankees Tenure: 1987-1995, 2001

Growing up in Texas, Velarde was a bit of an under-the-radar prospect. After graduating high school, he attended Lubbock Christian University, which competed at the NAIA level. Though it was not the highest level of competition, Velarde made the most of the opportunity to play every day and would go down as one of the best players in the school’s history. For his collegiate career, he posted a .358 batting average in 1,027 at-bats with 65 doubles, 25 triples, and 39 home runs. Velarde helped the team win the NAIA national championship in 1983 and was named an All-American in both 1984 and 1985.

Following his final collegiate season, Velarde was selected for the first time in the 1985 MLB Draft by the Chicago White Sox in the 19th round. He spent the rest of 1985 and all of the 1986 season in the White Sox’ system. However, before the 1987 season, Velarde and Pete Filson were traded to the New York Yankees for Mike Soper and Scott Nielsen.

Velarde’s bat took off with the Yankees, and he quickly worked his way up to the big league club. He got his first cup of coffee in the Bronx in 1987, playing in eight games and collecting four hits. This started a series of yo-yo years as Velarde spent the next two seasons bouncing between the Bronx and Triple-A Columbus.

In 1990, Velarde was finally able to break camp and stick with the club as a super-utility man. Playing second base, shortstop, third base, and left field, Velarde appeared in 95 games, batting .210 with five home runs and 19 RBI. This performance was good enough to keep him in that role heading into the 1991 season, and Velarde improved at the plate, hitting .245 with a .322 OBP in 80 games.

Despite the success, Velarde stayed in the utility role and had another respectable season in 1993. In 85 games, he hit .301 with an .829 OPS, seven home runs, and 24 RBI. By 1994, Velarde was the second-longest-tenured Yankee behind only captain Don Mattingly. Before the players’ strike ended the season in August, he played 77 games, hitting .279 with a then-career-best nine home runs and 34 RBI.

Velarde put together his best season in pinstripes in 1995, appearing in 111 games and batting .278 with a .375 OBP, seven home runs, and 46 RBI. The Yankees reached the postseason for the first time since 1981, but Velarde went just 3-for-17 in the ALDS against Seattle as the team lost in five games. Following the season, Velarde became a free agent and signed a three-year, $2.45 million deal with the California Angels.

Away from the Yankees, Velarde became a productive everyday player for the Angels, Athletics, and Rangers. From 1996 through 2002, he appeared in 615 games, batting .286 with 57 home runs and 237 RBI. He had his best season in 1999, splitting the year between Anaheim and Oakland while recording 200 hits and batting .317.

In 2000, while playing for Oakland against the Yankees, Velarde turned the 11th unassisted triple play in major league history on a soft line drive hit by Shane Spencer. Velarde caught the ball, tagged Jorge Posada running from first, and stepped on second base before Tino Martinez could return.

On August 31, 2001, the Yankees traded two players to be named later to Texas for Velarde. Down the stretch, he appeared in 15 regular-season games, hitting .152. He also appeared in the postseason for the Yankees’ American League pennant-winning squad, playing in the ALDS, ALCS, and World Series. Unfortunately, New York fell to the Arizona Diamondbacks in seven games, leaving Velarde without a World Series championship.

Velarde played the 2002 season with the Athletics and retired following the year. After his playing days, Velarde became linked to baseball’s performance-enhancing drug scandal and was named in the 2007 Mitchell Report. The report stated that Velarde had admitted through his lawyer to receiving performance-enhancing substances from Barry Bonds’ trainer, Greg Anderson.

During the 2011 federal trial involving Bonds, Velarde also testified that he had purchased human growth hormone from Anderson and received injections during the 2002 season. He described meeting Anderson in parking lots and receiving injections. No rings and forever immortalized in one of modern baseball’s greatest scandals. Such was the fate of many such players from the 1990s and 2000s.

All the same, we wish Randy Velarde a happy 64th birthday.
