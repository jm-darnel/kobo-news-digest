40 years later: Larry Davis shoot out with NYPD

By Dean Meminger · NY1 · November 19, 2026

His story, for some, has taken on legendary status. Referenced in rap songs, a gun battle with NYPD officers and threats he did not back down from.

“We told you, if you ever crossed us, you’re going to die,” Davis said. “And now you’re going to die.”

During a prison interview with NY1 in the early 2000s before he died, he described his side of the story saying cops wanted to kill him and they started shooting on Nov. 19, 1986 when they entered his South Bronx apartment on Fulton Avenue.

“He fired the first 12 gauge shot after the woman had told him to fire,” he said.

David, who was 20 years old at the time, fired back, injuring six cops during the chaos.

At the time, police said more than two dozen officers were trying to arrest Davis for killing drug dealers. But during an interview with NY1, he insisted cops were coming to silence him because he knew about police drug corruption.

He claimed they were also upset that he planned to keep money from drugs he said he sold for NYPD officers. Something Davis immediately said to reporters when he was arrested after being on the run for 17 days.

“They made me sell the drugs and made me do it,” he said after his arrest.

A Bronx jury acquitted Davis for attempted murder of the officers, but he was found guilty of a much lesser charge of weapons possession.

“It’s hard to imagine that much time has passed,” attorney Ron Kuby said.

Kuby was on Davis’ defense team four decades ago.

“So not only did he beat the cops with their tools, firearms, but he also beat the cops through the legal system, the criminal legal systems which is very, very rare,” he said.

“There was no question in my mind that he would be convicted,” he said. “When a trial would be held., the shock was when he was exonerated.”

At the time, many felt he had toppled an unfair and corrupt system. Meanwhile many hip-hop artists used his name in their songs.

“He inspired so many people in different areas, you know, particularly music. And particularly rap and hip-hop with its then intense desire for authenticity. And there was no one was more authentic than Larry Davis,” Kuby said.

“Whether it be a cop, president, or the governor, or whoever, if they are coming to hurt you, you defend yourself,” Davis told NY1.

Davis was stabbed and killed by another inmate in an upstate New York prison in 2008. This was while he was serving time for the murder of a drug dealer.

He has been gone now for nearly two decades, but part of New York City’s history.
