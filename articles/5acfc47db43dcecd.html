Retired NYPD detective's bail revoked in George Washington Bridge shooting case

By Alyssa Paolicelli · NY1 · November 23, 2026

Victor Romero pleaded not guilty to the upgraded charge. A judge revoked his previous $1 million bail and remanded him to Rikers Island.

Prosecutors said the case began after a near collision on the Henry Hudson Parkway.

They said Romero aggressively followed and tailgated Koran Irvine onto the upper level of the George Washington Bridge.

According to prosecutors, surveillance video shows Romero pulling up alongside Irvine’s car and firing a gunshot through the back passenger window, killing Irvine, a father of two.

Romero had initially pleaded not guilty to manslaughter and was released on $1 million bail before prosecutors secured the second-degree murder indictment.

Prosecutors said a shell casing recovered in Romero’s car, along with other ballistic evidence, helped build a stronger case to support the upgraded charge.

They also cited new surveillance video of the shooting and video showing Romero cleaning one side of his car in two different locations after the incident.

Romero’s attorney said keeping the company car clean was part of Romero’s job as an executive chauffeur.

Prosecutors argued Monday that Romero was a flight risk and should be held without bail. Irvine’s family packed the courtroom and applauded when the judge ordered Romero remanded.

Outside the courtroom, Irvine’s family, friends and supporters cheered the decision.

“Everybody’s celebrating, and everybody’s happy, and I’m ecstatic, but it’s not going to bring K back,” said Zeria Irvine, Koran Irvine’s sister.

“It’s what should have been done,” added Vanessa Miller, a family friend.

Keith White, the family’s attorney, said the family had asked prosecutors to increase the charge from manslaughter to second-degree murder and to seek Romero’s remand.

“That has happened,” White said.

Gale Forde, Irvine’s aunt, said the ruling brought a measure of fairness for the family.

“How he expect my nephew Koran to have a change of address, the Cypress Hills Cemetery, and he stays in his nice home?” Forde said. “How unfair, how unfair that would have been. But thank God to the judge. She used fairness and understanding.”

He is due back in court on Feb. 22.
