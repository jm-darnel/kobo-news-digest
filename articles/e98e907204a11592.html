Trump administration may fund white South Africans to document ‘hateful rhetoric and violent acts’

By SUSAN APPE and DAVID CAMPBELL · Amsterdam News · November 26, 2026

This article is republished from The Conversation under a Creative Commons license.

Supporting democracy by funding NGOs

For decades, the U.S. used foreign aid to support efforts to build democracy abroad, enhance governance, and strengthen human rights protections . Until the second Trump administration, the primary government agencies distributing this assistance were the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID), State Department, and National Endowment for Democracy.

Past administrations called this assistance “ fundamental ” to the goals of U.S. foreign policy. By the end of President Joe Biden’s term, funding for democracy promotion had reached almost $3 billion annually . Some of this money has supported NGOs engaged in civic participation and media independence. These efforts had been particularly important in fragile states and fledgling democracies, where civil society is especially at risk .

NGOs can also play an important role in reducing risks of violence between different ethnic and religious groups. By raising awareness about past atrocities, they can help stave off future violence .

Slashing foreign aid

In addition, democracy assistance has been severely cut. USAID’s Democracy Fund , which funded programs dedicated to building democratic institutions and promoting fair elections and respect for human rights, was eliminated. Funding and staffing for promoting democracy were also slashed for the Department of State and the National Endowment for Democracy.

Claiming a ‘white genocide’

In our research about NGOs that work on promoting democracy and preventing violence, we have seen and documented the benefits of these efforts . The organizations we have studied emphasize how people in South Africa and other countries with a history of identity-based violence can learn to live together across racial, ethnic, and religious divides.

By contrast, AfriForum seeks to lift up Afrikaner identity and history , without any evident effort to bridge the divide between Afrikaners and other racial groups in South Africa. It champions causes that have contributed to what South African President Cyril Ramaphosa, scholars, and other analysts call a false narrative of a “ white genocide ” in that country. In fact, researchers have found that Afrikaners and other white people in South Africa are less likely to be victims of crime and other forms of violence compared to other ethnic and racial groups in South Africa.

By funding AfriForum, the State Department would be elevating the dubious claims the group makes about the oppression of Afrikaners in South Africa. T he Trump administration’s embrace of this false narrative goes beyond this grant for AfriForum. It explains why the U.S. has allowed thousands of white South Africans to come to the U.S. as refugees while denying admission to nearly all other refugees from the rest of the world.
