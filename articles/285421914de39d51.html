Builder facing bankruptcy will lead environmental review of controversial NJ ICE warehouse

By Mike Hayes · Gothamist · November 20, 2026

Residents of Roxbury, New Jersey, have expressed alarm because the Trump administration selected a company run by a Boston real estate developer with a troubled financial record to lead a court-ordered environmental review of plans to convert a local warehouse into one of the country's largest immigration detention facilities.

Gothamist confirmed that developer James Grossmann’s company Rudiarius, Inc. has been subcontracted to conduct the environmental review, according to a company spokesperson. The firm’s selection has not been previously reported and may indicate that the White House remains committed to opening the facility, despite Republican losses in this month’s midterm elections and a lawsuit from New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill to stop it.

Grossmann ran a separate development company in Boston, Rise Construction Management, that is currently facing a $27 million bankruptcy and several lawsuits claiming he failed to pay development partners and subcontractors.

Rudiarius is a partner firm of Nashville, Tennessee-based SK2, LLC, which received a $31.8 million contract to convert the warehouse into an ICE detention facility if the environmental assessment clears the path for the project to proceed, according to USAspending.gov, posing a potential conflict of interest.

Grossmann managed at least one other environmental assessment for the Department of Homeland Security at a similar warehouse in Surprise, Arizona, that ICE plans to use as a detention facility. The review found that the facility would have no significant impact on the surrounding area. Critics called the review lax and said it ignored serious environmental concerns. Local lawmakers have also pushed back against the plan.

Brian Kiernan, a Roxbury resident, said he feared the environmental review would not be done properly because of the connections between Rudiarius and SK2.

“Is the study going to be a true study, or is it going to be lip service to get the results that his client desires and to get a fee?” he said.

David Broderick, an attorney representing Project No ICE North Jersey Alliance, a local activist group formed last year to oppose the Roxbury project, said he’s concerned about Grossmann’s financial troubles.

“It sounds like [DHS officials] have picked someone and a company which is uniquely unqualified to perform this,” Broderick said.

In an interview, Rudiarius’ spokesperson Jessica Tocco defended Grossmann’s track record and Rise Construction’s bankruptcy proceedings. She said the company is a “completely different LLC” from Rudiarius.

“Some businesses work, and some don't,” she said, adding that the environmental review in Roxbury is “not officially underway.” She did not provide a timeline for the review to start.

In a House Homeland Security Committee hearing Wednesday, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin also said he could not provide a timeline for the environmental review or the administration’s plans to begin construction work at the Roxbury site.

SK2’s representatives did not respond to multiple requests to be interviewed or provide comment on its connection to Grossmann and Rudiarius.

In a court filing Tuesday, the New Jersey attorney general’s office said that after “repeated requests over 11 days,” DHS provided it with a copy of the $31.8 million contract with SK2, LLC. Attorney General Jennifer Davenport said in the filing that she “continues to be concerned” that DHS is violating the judge’s court order by “pressing ahead with steps” to convert the warehouse.

A copy of the contract with SK2 does not mention Rudiarius or any other subcontractors. It states that DHS intends to spend $650,000 on the environmental review.

Roxbury Township Mayor Shawn Potillo said in an emailed statement that the township council plans to contact Roxbury’s legal counsel, the New Jersey attorney general’s office, and their federal representatives about the selection of Rudiarius to manage the environmental review.

“The Township has no authority or involvement in the federal government's decisions regarding the qualifications or suitability of contractors retained to perform federal work,” the statement said. “Regardless of those decisions or the contractors involved, our position remains unchanged; Roxbury Township opposes the proposed ICE facility.”

$27 million in bankruptcy debt

By his own account, Grossmann rose through the ranks at nationally renowned Suffolk Construction for more than two decades before starting his own company, Rise Construction Management, in 2019.

Court records show the company became mired in legal disputes within two years of being founded.

In 2020, Parks Plus, Inc., a New Jersey-based company that specializes in manufacturing and installing elevated parking systems, entered into a nearly $1 million contract at a new condo development under construction by Grossmann’s company in the Brighton section of Boston, according to Parks Plus’ Senior Vice President Ryan Astrup.

He said Rise Construction was “difficult from the get go” and failed to pay more than $50,000 it owed Parks Plus.

“It kind of fizzled out from there. We made several attempts to collect, and they went unanswered,” Astrup told Gothamist.

His company is one of more than 50 firms with debt claims against Rise Construction, according to an August 2026 Chapter 7 bankruptcy filing. According to the filing, several claims — including the one from Parks Plus — are in dispute due to litigation.

Two developers who worked on projects with Rise Construction are suing the company for more than $2 million. Each claims that Rise failed to pay subcontractors. The Lever previously reported Grossmann’s financial troubles.

Tocco said Rise and Grossmann’s troubles can be attributed to market headwinds and a “very tough time for developers.”

“He's doing the right thing,” she said. “He's closing that company with his partners through a legal bankruptcy process.” She added that Rise is one of “dozens” of companies that Grossmann has started during his career in the construction industry.

“To be out there for thirty years and have as many businesses as he had and had one not work out, that's a pretty good track record,” she said.

‘Uniquely unqualified’

News that the federal government signed the $31.8 million contract with SK2 to renovate the warehouse has sowed confusion about the project’s status.

Kiernan, the Roxbury resident, and his wife live about a three-minute drive from the warehouse site off Route 46 in the Ledgewood section of the township. He said he had not gotten clarity from his local elected officials on whether the project is still on hold as the court ordered. And he said he was alarmed that Grossmann’s company would conduct the environmental review.

“This is the individual through a new company who's supposedly going to perform this environmental study, that raises real concern,” he said.

Rudiarius’ environmental assessment of the proposed ICE facility in Arizona has also faced local scrutiny. DHS released the report on November 9.

Brent Peak of Northwest Valley Indivisible, a local grassroots political organization opposed to the facility, criticized the 81-page report for only briefly mentioning a nearby warehouse storing hazardous materials.

“It is completely ridiculous, negligent and derelict that this is one of the primary concerns brought up in the attorney general’s lawsuit,” Peak told AZFamily.com. “And they give it one paragraph out of those 81 pages.”
