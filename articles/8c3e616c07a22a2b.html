Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: Luis Gil

By Estevão Maximo · Pinstripe Alley · November 16, 2026

Even the most successful of teams in what they do best will not bat 1.000, and it usually has nothing to do with skill set—it’s just the way things go. The Yankees, who work so well with pitchers of different levels, have not been able to develop Gil the way they’d liked following his Rookie of the Year-winning campaign back in 2024—culminating in a disastrous, injury-riddled 2026 season, one of the most forgettable among all Yankee pitchers.

Grade: F

2026 Stats: 9 games, 4 starts, 27.1 IP, 4.61 ERA (92 ERA+), 7.12 FIP, 6.31 xFIP, 13.7 K%, 14.5 BB%, -0.5 fWAR, 0.0 rWAR

2027 Contract Status: After making a bit over $2.1 million in his first year of arbitration eligibility in 2026, Gil is set for a minimal raise by the nature of the system alone (assuming the Yankees choose to retain him)

Few players had quite as big an opportunity to insert themselves in the Yankees’ early-season plans during spring training as Gil, but the right-hander was unconvincing, allowing 11 runs in 19.2 innings. Although the strikeouts that had plummeted back in 2025 regained reasonable levels in spring, punching out 24, Gil certainly didn’t generate much confidence in his ability to retain that fifth starter spot before the return of established injured arms.

A scheduling break allowed the Yankees to begin the year with a four-man rotation, sending Gil to the minors and delaying his season debut until April 10th. The hard-throwing righty took the ball against the Rays and got hit with the loss, allowing three runs in four innings. Gil’s third outing sparked a bit of hope, holding the Red Sox scoreless in 6.1 innings, but even that game created doubts about the sustainability of his performance, doing it with three free passes and only two punchouts. (Indeed, Boston was so inherently lifeless at this point that within days, they would part ways with longtime skipper Alex Cora and several coaches.) The Astros knocked him around in his next outing and he was demoted on April 27th.

Luckily for the Yankees, the period between Gil’s demotion in late April and Rodón’s activation in early May was minimal, limiting the negative impact of this lack of a reliable fifth starter. To make matters worse, Gil didn’t even have the opportunity to work on his craft in the minors—shortly after his demotion, he was placed on the minor-league IL with shoulder inflammation, returning only in mid-August, making this an absence of over three months. While Aaron Boone went on the record saying that this injury was unrelated to any problems Gil might’ve been having in the major leagues, it’s difficult to separate the two — especially since his stuff was hardly crackling in a manner resembling the way it did during his breakout 2024.

A little short on bullpen arms, the Yankees quickly recalled Gil at the end of August after he got healthy, handing him the role of mop-up duty as a low-leverage arm—one he fulfilled reasonably well, finishing three straight games in September. While he kept opponents off the board, allowing just one earned run in eight innings out of the bullpen, any thoughts of rejoining the circle of trust for a postseason role, however small it might be, were long gone—the walks remained a problem, allowing seven free passes.

While his salary isn’t an obstacle, acknowledging any raise on the $2.1 million he might get in arbitration will be minor; Gil will be out of options next season, and while the Yankees may be hesitant to commit to a guaranteed roster spot for him over a full season (even in the bullpen), other pitching-strapped teams might not be so picky. It’d be one thing if the results weren’t going his way, but something under the hood made you feel confident in another approach.

From loss of velocity to a complete inability to locate consistently without the strikeout stuff to back up or alleviate a more erratic spray pattern, there is just not a lot of optimism to be had here. Gil’s future may very well be with a different uniform in 2027.
