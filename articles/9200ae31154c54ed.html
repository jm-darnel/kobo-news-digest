Westchester: New York’s Cannabis Office Convenes Medical Leaders For Third Annual Symposium

Harlem World · November 23, 2026

Never miss new stories in Harlem

“…Real progress is … measured by how well we care for patients and protect our communities…”

“Progress in medicine does not come from everyone agreeing; it comes from asking better questions. This symposium brought science, medicine, and public health together to challenge assumptions, follow the evidence, and rethink how we approach cannabis. Real progress is not measured by what we learn in the conference room—it’s measured by how well we care for patients and protect our communities,” said Dr. June Chin, Chief Medical Officer of the New York State Office of Cannabis Management.

“New York is not simply participating in the national conversation on medical cannabis; we are helping lead it,” said John Kagia, Executive Director of the New York State Office of Cannabis Management. “Through initiatives like our Center of Excellence for Cannabis Care and Health Equity, we are bringing together leading medical institutions, researchers, and public health experts to advance the science, strengthen clinical education, and expand opportunities for equitable care. This symposium reflects our ambition to make New York a national model for how thoughtful policy, scientific innovation, and meaningful collaboration can shape the future of medical cannabis.”

“We are honored to bring together regional and national leaders at the symposium with the common goal of advancing rigorous evidence-based clinical care, research, public health and policy that moves our field forward, said Dr. Deepika Slawek, Associate Professor of Medicine at Montefiore Einstein. “Montefiore Einstein has been a leader in state-regulated medical clinical research, since 2017 and we continue our mission to better understand how cannabis interacts with and can aid human health.”

“There is still a great deal we do not know about cannabis…”

“There is still a great deal we do not know about cannabis, and that uncertainty is exactly why gatherings like this matter. This symposium brings the current evidence and the open questions into the light, so clinicians across disciplines can approach the topic together. Meeting patients with curiosity instead of judgment keeps the conversation honest and the care safer. I’m excited to be a part of the dialogue at this much needed event.” said Brooke Worster, MD, FACP, Professor and Enterprise Director of Supportive Oncology at the Department of Medical Oncology.

OASAS Commissioner Dr. Chinazo Cunningham said, “Over the past several years, we have strengthened the steps we are taking to educate New Yorkers about the potential adverse effects of cannabis use, especially for those under 21, and the risks of addiction. And have also increased resources for those seeking treatment and other supports. As New York State’s cannabis landscape continues to evolve, events like this give us an opportunity to come together and evaluate what is working, and what may need adjustments, and we are grateful for this collaborative opportunity.”
