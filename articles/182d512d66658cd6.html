Prospect Park forest makes miraculous recovery 2 years after fire

By Liam Quigley · Gothamist · November 28, 2026

Two years after a brush fire ravaged a secluded enclave of forest in Prospect Park, the area known as the Ravine is bouncing back to its former glory — and serving as a symbol for Brooklyn’s resiliency.

But thanks to an extensive restoration project, parkgoers these days can hardly tell the area was scorched by the inferno just two years ago.

Howard Goldstein, a senior forest ecologist for the Prospect Park Alliance, said the recovery has been striking.

"If you looked at the scorching that was [there] in November after this was done, you would never necessarily know it by looking at the site today," he said.

Crews started working last winter to seed the area, planting nearly 3,000 native plants and shrubs. Researchers from Cornell and Rutgers have conducted fire ecology studies in the burn zone.

Now, the Ravine is once again a place for New Yorkers to find peace. It’s an area where birdsongs are louder than the wailing sirens on Flatbush Avenue.

"I actually didn't know such places were so close to the center of the city," said David Smith, a 40-year-old painter visiting the Ravine for the first time. "It does remind me of an actual active living forest."

Ena Da — a comedian who uses the Instagram handle @park_slope_arsonist — was taking in the Ravine's beauty on a recent weekday afternoon.

“To think that there was a fire here is kind of amazing, 'cause it has recovered really well,” she said.
