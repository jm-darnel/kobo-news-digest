Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: Aaron Judge

By Kevin Winterhalt · Pinstripe Alley · November 19, 2026

In retrospect, it’s really surprising the 2026 Yankees won 93 games. Because if you’d told me a year ago that Aaron Judge would miss 96 games and that, when healthy he would be closer to great than to historically great (his 2026 OPS is the lowest of his career, not counting his 2016 cup of coffee), I would have penciled the Yankees in to win something like 83 games, if not 73.

So, I guess the good news is the Yanks showed themselves able to withstand a prolonged absence of the greatest hitter on Earth (perhaps the greatest right-handed hitter modern baseball has seen?). The bad news is that, after transcendent 2022, 2024, and 2025 seasons (when he was healthy in 2023, he was nigh-unstoppable as well), we might have to start grappling with the idea that Judge’s superhuman peak has passed.

One of my 2027 storylines to watch will be whether Judge’s 2026 was an anomaly or the onset of his decline phase, which has to arrive eventually, after all. Father Time is undefeated. Judge will play next season at 35 years old. And we don’t exactly have a robust sample size of players of a Judgian stature from which to forecast how he’ll age. But his floor is just so damn high that when decline comes, if it’s gradual, he’ll still be a superstar for a while.

I can’t stress enough how much I am rooting for anomaly to win.

Grade : I 2026 Stats : 66 G, 18 HR, 41 RBI, 45 R, .241/.360/.511, 140 OPS+, 140 wRC+, 1.9 rWAR, 2.0 fWAR 2027 Contract Status : Under contract through 2031 ($40 million 2027 salary)

Lest I be mistaken as a downer, I want to be clear. Even an injury-plagued Aaron Judge was really, really good in 2026. We’ve been spoiled by the back of his baseball card in recent seasons, so numbers that 99% of major league hitters would take in a heartbeat look disappointing when they belong to Judge.

His post-rib injury seven game September cameo also drags down his final line. That week was ugly. One home run, a .174 batting average, and a 13:1 K:BB ratio in only 24 plate appearances. It was bad. But Judge was determined to get back and try to help the Yankees down the stretch.

Unfortunately, his season ended in disappointing fashion when he suffered a calf strain against the Twins that ended his season and kept him off the postseason roster – though not without much Shakespearean discussion about his circumstances. “To roster Judge, or not to roster Judge: that is the question.”

Rewinding the clock, Judge had a strong start to the season, though his batting average never reflected that. Considering his 2026 BABIP plummeted 87 points from 2025 when he won his batting title, that shouldn’t be a surprise.

Everything else was still there. He was drawing his walks and hitting home runs. Heading into what turned out to be a fateful game against the Astros on April 26 th , Judge had nine home runs and 17 RBI in 27 games. That night, he hit a solo shot to put him in double digits. He also dove for a ball in right field. Little did we all know that defensive effort was the beginning of the end of his season.

Judge gave us no reason to suspect anything. On the contrary. He went on his best offensive tear of the season in the games that followed. Over the next two weeks (13 games), Judge hit .348/.483/.804 with half a dozen dingers and 12 RBI.

But he knew he was hurt. He revealed in early June he’d been playing through pain since the dive in Houston. Crashing into the outfield wall against Baltimore on May 3 rd didn’t help anything either. Finally, it became too much. He played the series against the Athletics at the end of May, at which point he finally told manage Aaron Boone he’d been playing hurt. On June 5 th , the club placed its franchise cornerstone on the IL.

There’s a conversation to be had here about Judge keeping his injury from the club for as long as he did, and the level of deference the club seemingly shows him. But that’s for another day. He returned for a hot minute late in the season before that aforementioned calf strain ended his campaign for good.

If you’re looking for reasons to have faith that Judge can bounce back in 2027 and put together another one of his fantastic seasons, he’s still doing many of the things he does better than almost anyone on Earth. Unfortunately, he’s not doing them as well as he used to.

A couple of examples illustrate this. First, Barrel%. If he’d qualified in 2026, he would have been among baseball’s best at barreling baseballs. Compare that metric to his 2024 and 2025 seasons, however, and there was a clear dropoff – 20.9% versus 27.1% and 24.8%.

HardHit% is another metric that saw a falloff, especially compared to 2024. Though again among baseball’s best, his 57.5% was down from 58.2% in 2025 and from 61.0% in 2024.

It’s hard to know the extent to which any of Judge’s suppressed numbers (outstanding though they are) are due to him playing through a fractured bloody rib for more than a month, or from coming back and struggling for a week before his season ended. Hopefully, any and all decline was injury-related, and we get Historically Unstoppable Aaron Judge back for 2027.

Looking forward to 2027 (and beyond), there is another conversation waiting to be had about Judge’s defensive future, given how important his offensive presence is to the club. But that too is a chat for another time. As far as Judge’s 2026 goes, ultimately it was… Incomplete. And the Yankees were inarguably worse for it.
