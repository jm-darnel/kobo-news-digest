Performers scramble to stay in New York for postponed Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade

By Annabelle Childers · NY1 · November 27, 2026

NEW YORK — New York City officials, thousands of performers and the Macy’s team are spending Friday preparing for the postponed Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, which is set for Saturday morning.

High school and college bands worked hard to apply and be accepted for the parade. They also had to fundraise to cover hotels, meals, and air, bus or train tickets.

Lafe Cook, band director at Dobyns-Bennett High School in Kingsport, Tennessee, told Spectrum News he immediately went into fix-it mode when he learned of the postponement. He wanted to make sure his students still got the opportunity.

“We had to figure out whether we could find hotel rooms in Manhattan on a holiday Friday night — and a lot of hotel rooms,” Cook said. “And then we had to figure out if the bus company could extend their contract to get us home when it was all over. … Then we had to figure out how to come up with about $170,000 in 24 hours. That was the scariest part of it all.”

Cook said the band put up a donation link, and overnight the funds grew to more than $200,000.

Not all bands were as lucky. The University of Alabama band, for example, had to return to perform at the Iron Bowl against Auburn on Saturday.

Videos posted on social media on Thanksgiving have led many people to say Macy’s and city officials made the right call by postponing the parade. The footage shows messy, wet conditions and the high winds that were the biggest safety concern.

Now that the weather has calmed, there’s much work to do to prepare the balloons, all of which had to be deflated Wednesday evening.

City officials say it typically takes a week to get all of the parade’s balloons ready. This time, crews have 24 hours so the parade can start on time at 8:30 a.m. EST.
