Mayor Mamdani Nominates Three Worker Advocates to Fight For Taxi Drivers Facing Rising Costs

Harlem World · November 19, 2026

Today, Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani nominated Ligia Guallpa, Lorelai Salas and Felicia Singh to serve on the Taxi and Limousine Commission (TLC) Board, subject to City Council confirmation.

“Taxi drivers get up before dawn and work long hours to keep this city moving. They should be able to support their families on what they earn,” said Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani. “Ligia, Lorelai and Felicia have spent their careers fighting for working people. Together with Commissioner Valdivia, they will lower costs and secure fairer pay for the people behind the wheel.”

“Drivers have been squeezed by higher vehicle and insurance costs and by app companies that keep per-trip pay out of view,” said Deputy Mayor for Economic Justice Julie Su. “Ligia helped win a minimum pay rate and pay transparency for delivery workers, Lorelai led the City’s consumer and worker protection agency, and Felicia grew up watching her family work these roads. They know how to lower costs and win fairer pay, and the people who drive our taxis will feel the difference.”

“Being a professional driver in New York City is always going to be hard work, but it is harder than it needs to be,” said TLC Chair and Commissioner Valdivia. “Hundreds of thousands of families have built good lives through driving, put food on the table and sent kids to college. We will make sure this industry works for them, for passengers and for our streets. I look forward to working with Ligia, Lorelai, Felicia and the rest of our board to make changes drivers can feel.”

“The City Council should move swiftly on these appointments so that we can have a full and functioning TLC board without further delay,” said Bhairavi Desai, Executive Director of the New York Taxi Workers Alliance, the union that represents over 28,000 NYC yellow taxi, green cab, Uber, Lyft, black car, and livery drivers. “The TLC has power over 180,000 licensed professional drivers and their families and has authority to regulate billionaire corporations that loom large over drivers’ lives, but it can’t do that without a full board. We look forward to working with this new class of pro-worker board members, who are unafraid to hold corporations accountable.”

TLC Board Nominees

“It is an incredible honor to be nominated by Mayor Zohran Mamdani to serve on the New York City Taxi and Limousine Commission Board under the leadership of Chair and Commissioner Midori Valdivia,” said Ligia Guallpa. “As a longtime labor advocate, I have dedicated my career to fighting for living wages and worker-centered policies that advance economic opportunity and prosperity for the people who keep our city running. I look forward to the City Council’s consideration of my nomination and to bringing my experience, commitment, and worker-centered perspective to the TLC Board. Our city works because of its workers, and they deserve policies that recognize their contributions, protect their livelihoods, and ensure they can live and work with dignity.”

“I am honored to be nominated to the Taxi and Limousine Commission’s board under Mayor Mamdani and Commissioner and Chair Valdivia’s leadership,” said Lorelai Salas. “The TLC has broad regulatory authority over many of the companies that operate on NYC roads, employing thousands of New Yorkers, and providing transportation services to many more. I look forward to working with my colleagues on the board to support the development of policies that improve the lives of NYC workers and residents, and that provide opportunities for economic mobility for the TLC licensees. Thank you to the City Council for considering my nomination.”

“I am honored to be nominated by Mayor Mamdani to the Taxi and Limousine Commission board,” said Felicia Singh. “As a proud daughter of a taxi driver, I’ve seen and felt the direct impact corporations have had on families like mine in maintaining self-employment and a livelihood in New York City. From watching my parents advocate in Albany with taxi workers to fighting together against the medallion crisis, it’s about time the TLC board includes more voices of those with lived experiences in the industry. I will honor this role by centering drivers and their families through championing policy that improves the lives of the many who serve this city.”

About the Nominees

Ligia Guallpa is the co-founder and executive director of the Workers’ Justice Project (WJP) and Los Deliveristas Unidos. For more than 16 years, she has organized immigrant and app-based workers to advance labor rights. Under her leadership, WJP launched Los Deliveristas Unidos, a worker-led collective representing 70,000 delivery workers across New York City. Their organizing secured a $22.13 minimum pay rate, pay transparency, protections against unjust deactivations and the country’s first Deliverista Hub.

Lorelai Salas is a senior fellow with The Century Foundation and Protect Borrowers and an adjunct professor at the Colin Powell School for Civic and Global Leadership at City College of New York. She served as supervision director at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau until February 2025. From 2016 to 2021, she served as commissioner of the New York City Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP). Before that, she led the legal services departments at Make the Road New York and Catholic Migration Services.

Felicia Singh is the director of external affairs at the Coalition for Asian American Children and Families (CACF), the nation’s only pan-Asian children and families advocacy organization. The daughter of a taxi driver and working-class immigrant parents, she has worked in organizing, policy and politics. She taught for 10 years and served in the United States Peace Corps. She lives in Queens, where she grew up.
