Exit Polls From Harlem To Hollywood Show Most Voters Blame Trump For High Prices

Voters went into the 2026 midterm elections in a sour mood.

Surveys released as polls closed on Tuesday, November 3, showed most of the electorate unhappy with the country’s direction and blaming President Donald Trump for high prices. Roughly three-quarters of voters said they feel “angry” or “dissatisfied” with the country’s trajectory, and only about one-quarter said they were “satisfied” or “enthusiastic,” according to the AP Voter Poll.

The exit polls, run by SSRS for a media consortium that included the major TV networks and the Associated Press, came out before any results. They found that two-thirds of voters blamed Trump for high prices and that nearly 9 in 10 called the cost of living a crucial or major factor in their vote, Reuters reported. The AP Voter Poll asked a different question and got a somewhat lower number: about 6 in 10 said higher-than-usual prices are “more because of Trump’s policies” than because of factors outside his control.

Costs and corruption lead

About 4 in 10 voters named the economy as the country’s most important issue, and about 3 in 10 named the state of democracy. Roughly half said government corruption and the cost of living were “crucial” to their vote for Congress. About two-thirds said Trump’s policies, including his tariffs, had made the economy worse. Fuel prices have climbed since the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran began in February.

Voters want checks on Trump

About 7 in 10 voters said Congress is doing “not enough” to check the presidency. About 6 in 10 disapproved of Trump’s job performance, and about three-quarters disapproved of Congress. The exit polls put Trump’s disapproval at 62%, which Reuters called one of the highest unfavorable rates in modern times. Half of voters said they saw the election as a chance to rebuke the president.

Iran, immigration and health care

Majorities also disagreed with several of Trump’s signature policies. About 6 in 10 voters said the U.S. made the “wrong decision” in taking military action against Iran. About 6 in 10 said the administration’s immigration enforcement has gone too far, and a similar share disapproved of how ICE is doing its job. On health care, about half said Trump’s policies had worsened access and costs.

In the final weeks of the campaign, Trump urged supporters to vote as if he were on the ballot. According to Reuters, the exit poll results suggest that push did little to improve his standing with voters, reports Perplexity.
