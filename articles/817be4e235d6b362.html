How To Apply for the NYC Housing Lottery

By Samantha Maldonado · The City · November 16, 2026

Want to secure an affordable, rent-stabilized apartment through New York City’s housing lottery? Step right up and try your luck!

As The City Reporter has shown, your chances of winning a subsidized apartment through the affordable lottery are a long shot, but not unheard of. In recent years, there has been one apartment available through the lottery for every 510 applications.

Those lucky New Yorkers have a fabled, enviable opportunity: Moving into (typically) brand-new buildings with stabilized rent that’s likely cheaper than the market.

But even when your number comes up in the lottery, there are a lot of hoops to jump through before you secure a lease. We spoke to leasing agents, city officials and some winners to find out the inside scoop on the lottery.

Here’s our guide for locals on how to access those opportunities, tips to navigate the process and how you can prepare — with insight from people who won the lottery and professionals who screen applicants for lottery apartments.

I’m Ready to Apply! Where Do I Start?

You can sign up for and apply to housing lotteries on Housing Connect, a web-based system run through the city’s Department of Housing Preservation and Development that lists and manages all available subsidized apartments.

Think of Housing Connect as the portal through which you enter lotteries for individual buildings.

First, create a profile.

You’ll include details about all sources of income, including from jobs, government assistance, investments or workers’ compensation, as well as the value of your assets in bank and investment accounts. You must include this income information for the people you’d live with.

You’ll also indicate your current residence, whether it’s a rental, NYCHA building or a homeless shelter, and whether anyone you’re applying with receives rental assistance.

Make sure this information is complete, or else you won’t be able to submit an application.

You can also include details about your preferences, such as interest in a ramp or elevator, number of bedrooms and a pet-friendly policy.

One thing to keep in mind: “Anything you put on your profile, you will have to prove that it’s true,” said Wout Stienaers, a founder of Homey, a paid service that helps people apply to the housing lottery. “Don’t lie about anything.”

For instance, Stienaers said applicants might be tempted to say they have a disability because there are apartments set aside for those who do (more on that later). But you’d be asked to provide proof of that disability, like a letter from a doctor.

You can get free help to apply for the housing lottery through HPD’s Housing Ambassador Program.

I Made a Profile on Housing Connect. Now What?

Once you’ve made a profile, check buildings for which you’re eligible and click “Apply Now.” You’ll know if you’re eligible if your household income falls within a range based on the number of people you’ll be living with. (How is that income range determined? Read our guide on that here.)

Keep the information about your household and income updated as you go along. For example: If you or your partner get a raise or lose a job, make sure to log that information. If you become a parent, add your child as a member of your household!

One caveat: don’t change your household size after you apply to a lottery.

“If a household is a single mom and a teenager, and then they find out they only qualify for a studio, they can’t then add the grandmother with Social Security income to see if they qualify for another unit,” said Christine Wendell, CEO of Pront Housing, an affordable housing leasing company. “If they try to do that, they will be rejected, so they should just pick their household size, and stick with it.”

Make sure to check back on Housing Connect frequently, as new opportunities come online often.

Application periods for each building are open for two months. It doesn’t matter when you submit your application within that period, as long as you apply before the lottery closes.

“No matter at what point in time you submit your application, you have the same chance to win as everybody else,” Stienaers said.

Don’t be shy about throwing your hat in the ring! You can apply to as many lotteries as you’re eligible for.

“You can apply to an unlimited number of buildings,” Wendell said. “Just statistically speaking, you will have a greater chance of getting an affordable housing unit if you apply to a lot of buildings, which everyone is completely allowed to do.”

How Can I know if I’m Eligible?

To be eligible for affordable housing through Housing Connect, your household income must fall within a specific band. Your household’s assets must also fall under a certain amount.

Can I Apply With a Voucher or Rental Subsidy?

Yes! If you get rental assistance through programs including Section 8, CityFHEPS or Medicaid Waiver, you’re good, and then you’re not subject to minimum income requirements. But the amount of rent of the apartment you’re applying to must be within the limits of what your voucher covers.

“If you have a voucher, you should apply for everything because your income isn’t your limiting factor anymore,” said Michelle de la Uz, executive director at the nonprofit Fifth Avenue Committee. “It’s basically whatever your voucher is approved for.”

That means as a voucher-holder, you may be eligible to apply for more housing lotteries than you think. Even if you don’t make enough money, you can apply for apartments based on the amount of rent — as long as it’s no more than what your voucher is worth.

Can People Apply to the Lottery if They Don’t Live in New York City?

Yes, they technically can, but applicants who are New York City residents get priority.

“While any and everybody can apply for Housing Connect lotteries, it first focuses on folks that reside within the five boroughs of New York City before it can move someone that does not reside in New York City into one of its apartments,” said Shaheem Taylor, a marketing outreach coordinator at HPD, in a webinar.

It’s very rare for outside applicants to secure an apartment through a lottery. A New York City resident who applies to the lottery is over 10 times more likely to sign a lease for an affordable apartment than someone who lives outside the city, according to an analysis of HPD data by The City Reporter.

Having set his sights on someday moving back to his birthplace of New York City, Grayson Sparrow applied to housing lotteries from his home in Virginia. He lucked out when he moved back to the city in April 2024 and was asked to submit his documents for a housing lottery building in East New York just three months later.

“I’d been applying just in the event if I move back someday and maybe it lines up with something,” Sparrow, 30, said. “My info went in and by the time they pulled it, I had New York residency.”

Can I Apply to the Lottery for a Rental Apartment if I Own Property?

If you or a member of your household own property within 100 miles of New York City, then you can’t apply through the lottery. (And you can’t try for an affordable homeownership lottery if you own, or ever owned, property anywhere.)

Can I Apply for One Lottery Multiple Times?

No! One entry per household.

Your household means all the people who will live in the apartment you’ll be applying to. Only one member of the household should apply for the lottery. And you can’t apply for the same lottery twice, even if, for example, you’re applying with a roommate and then on your own.

If more than one member of a household applies to the lottery, the application is automatically disqualified.

Christina Harsch, director of leasing and compliance at Wavecrest Management, recommends keeping track of what you’re applying to and the information you’re sending in, if you get your number called.

“Keep a journal of what you’re applying to and when you apply, keep copies of the [Housing Connect] ad if you can and even copies of applications so you remember what you’re doing,” she said.

That way, you know your household only applies to one lottery and you can follow up with the agents if need be.

Need more help? Connect with a housing ambassador to get assistance preparing for and applying to the housing lottery.

What Else Might Improve My Chances of Being Selected?

If you work for the city government, are a military veteran or live in the same neighborhood as the building you’re applying for, you might get a boost in the lottery. That’s because a percentage of the available apartments are set aside for people who fall under specific categories. In all new buildings, 5% of apartments are for households where a member has a mobility disability and 2% for households where a member has a hearing or vision disability.

In addition, 10% of apartments are set aside for city employees and military veterans, and 20% for residents of the community district where the building is located. Some projects offer preferences for seniors or formerly homeless individuals, as well.

OK I Entered a Lottery! How Long Will It Take Before I Hear Back?

Once you apply, you get assigned a random number called a log number. The lower it is, the better chance you have of being contacted for the development.

And then it could be weeks, months or years before you hear back if your number has been selected. Some people never hear back, even if they are eligible! Others don’t hear anything for a long time, but all of a sudden, will be notified their number was chosen for multiple buildings.

Pro tip: Make sure to stay on top of your Housing Connect account so you don’t miss any notifications. Taylor, of HPD, recommends applicants check their Housing Connect account three times a week.

Jumelia Abrahamson, director of programs at University Neighborhood Housing Program, said she tells people to log into Housing Connect “as often as you’re logging into your social media.”

She also tells applicants to check their email on a daily basis.

“Leasing agents are not calling, they’re not texting, and if these communications are going into the junk mail or if people don’t check their emails on a daily basis, they’re missing a lot of communication,” she said.

(If you applied on paper, you can choose whether to get notifications via email or in your actual mailbox — but ideally, you’ve applied through Housing Connect! Otherwise, it can be a slower process.)

What’s the Status of My Lottery Application?

Housing Connect will indicate where your application is in the process. Here’s what those statuses mean, according to experts:

• Pending just means you submitted your application. Nothing else!

• Action required means there is a request for documentation, indicating you moved to the next stage in the process — your log number was pulled — and you need to respond to a request from the lottery administrators. (If you happen to be rejected, that will also appear under “action required.”)

• In process could mean one of two things: either that you submitted your documents and they are under review, or you missed the window to submit the documents.

• Completed indicates the end stage of the application process. It can mean that you signed a lease and received your keys. Or it could mean there are no more apartments available in a development. Or it could mean you were rejected in the process and did not appeal the rejection within the required timeline.

My Number Got Picked! Now What?

Once you’re notified your number was called in a lottery, get ready for a long road. This is where the process can get tricky, as you must provide a pile of paperwork to verify your eligibility, including identifications and proof of income. You have 10 business days to submit those documents.

And if the leasing agent requests more documents beyond what was in your original application, an applicant has five business days to respond.

“Five days is really not a long time if you’re not checking, and once that five days is gone, your place in line has been passed by,” said Charmaine Marizan, the affordable housing marketing and leasing manager at Fifth Avenue Committee. “If you don’t get back quickly, there’s a bunch of people who need it.”

It’s good to get as many of your documents in order as you can before you’re selected, experts say. Keep a digital file of PDFs of important information so you can send it off quickly if your number is called. Here is a list of the documents you may need to provide.

In sum, you must provide IDs — such as IDNYC, drivers’ license, or state ID card — for all members of the household, plus birth certificates for minors.

You’ll need to show all sources of income so the agent can verify you qualify within the income bands. That includes interest from assets, student loans, child support payments and even regular checks or Venmo payments from family members or others. Applicants employed by jobs must submit a month of pay stubs, and self-employed applicants must provide tax returns.

If you get benefits through SNAP, TANF or Social Security, you can simply submit the income determination you got through those programs.

You’ll also have to show your assets, if you have more than $51,600 saved and in investment accounts. That means providing bank account and retirement account statements, among other records. If you have less than that $51,600 in assets, you can just self-certify that that’s the case — no need to prove it.

Once it’s confirmed that you’re eligible for an apartment — and there’s a vacancy available — you can either show you’ve paid rent for the past year, or you can opt for a credit check.

There’s no application fee, but you might be charged $20 for a background or credit check.

You might have a back-and-forth with the listing agent who represents the development. They may ask for more information or clarification. You can also ask them questions.

Amira Willingham, 25, who won her Chelsea studio through the lottery, said the agent asked her about specific transactions on her bank account.

“I had to explain to them every transaction,” she said. “I bought a lot online so I had lots of receipts to show.”

On one occasion, Willingham had to get a copy of a friend’s bank statement with everything blacked out except a transaction showing Willingham sending the friend money for a dinner.

“There’s no privacy in the process,” Willingham said. “I wish I had known to have a folder of everything I’d ever done put together and up to date.”

When she wasn’t sure about how to move forward, Willingham turned to an online forum she found helpful to chat with others navigating the process.

Dan B., a musician who lives in a Greenpoint apartment he won through the lottery, advised following up with the agent to get clarity on requirements and to check in for updates: “You really have to advocate for yourself because no one’s going to care more about you getting it than yourself.”

And when you advocate for yourself, it’s good to stick to email when contacting agents in order to maintain a record, said Mildred Flores, director of affordable housing marketing and compliance at Settlement Housing Fund.

“Everybody’s going to do things differently. There are some marketing agents that terminate applications as quickly as they can, and then there are others that you know will work with applicants,” Flores said.

She said she typically tells applicants to wait to contact agents for a status update until 30 days have passed since the start of tenant selection. Have patience, she cautioned, as agents often are working to lease up multiple buildings at a time and handling hundreds of applications.

For those who inquire, Flores said she will give applicants a sense of their chances and the timeline based on their log numbers — again, the random number assigned to you when you enter a lottery.

I Got Turned Down. What Can I Do?

You can appeal the decision. Within five days of the decision, write to the agent to say why you think you should not have been rejected, or why you think you should still be moving forward, and send in supporting documents.

“An applicant can appeal at any part in the process, except during the initial qualification period,” Flores said. If an applicant successfully appeals, they will not lose their place in the queue.

HPD is not involved in this process — it’s the agents associated with the building who handle the appeals.

Often, applicants face rejection because they don’t meet income or household eligibility requirements, because a single household submitted more than one application for a building or because there was insufficient information provided to the leasing agent.

Willingham said she appealed at least three times during the process when agents disqualified her based on insufficient information or not providing the right documentations.

“I had to kind of be a Karen about it,” she said. “This is one of the only paths to affordable housing in the city. Doing anything you can to fight to get it is really important.”

I Got Approved. What’s Next?

You’ll likely take a tour of the apartment — or multiple open apartments in one new complex — and if you like what you see, you can decide to move forward. You’ll have 30 days to accept a lease offer or complete a purchase, if it’s a lottery homeownership opportunity.

You’ll have to pay the first month’s rent and a security deposit (equal to a month’s rent) upfront, so make sure you have savings!

You’ll also have to figure out how to get out of your current lease, sometimes with limited notice. That requires negotiating with your landlord.

How Long Will This Whole Process Take?

It can vary a ton! The answer has to do with how fast you as the applicant are able to mobilize, and what kind of obstacles may pop up in the screening process.

Between July 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026, the median time it took for an applicant to get approved for an apartment after the lottery ended was 136 days (about 4.5 months).

For Sparrow, who had applied to the housing lottery from outside New York, a year passed between when he applied for the building and when leasing agents reached out to ask for documents. Three months after that, he signed the lease for his affordable apartment.

“It is a little surprising how my process was seamless,” he said. “I’ve seen other people where they get asked for documents and it takes months for them to move into the apartment.”

WIllingham, on the other hand, applied for her building’s lottery in February of one year, received a request for documents by November, and moved in the following March.

What Happens if My Life Changes Between When I Get a Call for the Lottery?

That can happen — and it’s why you should keep your Housing Connect profile updated.

If your life changes between when you apply for a lottery and your number gets picked, you get screened for your current situation. Maybe you got married and had a family, or maybe you got promoted. Either way, you may or may not qualify for the apartment any more. The agent will work with you to figure it out.

But be careful! You don’t want your situation to change during the screening process. Marizan, for instance, cautions against earning too much overtime, which can alter your income.

“If you’re currently in the screening process and you’re due a promotion, hold back on it,” she said. “You cannot be over-income by a penny.”

Any Other Way to Secure Affordable Housing?

Yup: look for apartments that people won through the lottery but are now vacant.They’re called re-rentals (or re-sales, for homeownership). These kinds of apartments don’t go through the lottery — at least until April 30, 2027. That means you can apply for these affordable apartments almost like any other apartment — essentially, first-come, first-served. You can find listings here and here, or on the sites of individual property management companies. StreetEasy, Zillow and other listings sites may also feature vacant affordable housing re-rentals.

Harsch, of Wavecrest Management, suggested reaching out to affordable management companies by cold-calling or emailing to ask about their re-rental stock. (She mentioned even if there’s nothing to offer, she may add people who get in touch to a mailing list and send them info about new housing opportunities.)

If you apply for a re-rental opportunity, you still must be eligible for the apartment based on your income and household size — details that the leasing agent will need to verify. So it takes time! And they may add you to a wait list that they’ll pull from if another applicant doesn’t work out.
