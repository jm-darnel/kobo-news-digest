Veterans Day Parade steps off in Manhattan

By Jillian Jorgensen · NY1 · November 11, 2026

Fifth Avenue was filled with veterans and their supporters Wednesday for the city’s annual Veterans’ Day Parade. It was a chance for New Yorkers to thank them for their service, and for many of them, to reconnect with one another.

“We like to say we’re not just the biggest parade in the country that honors veterans. We’re also the largest reunion for veterans in the country,” said Mark Otto, president of the United War Veterans Council, which produces the parade.

Otto says the parade welcomes between 20,000-25,000 marchers each year, from across the country and from 10 foreign allies. The event begins with a wreath laying ceremony before the marchers step off. Then, thousands gather along the parade route.

“It gives them the opportunity to say thank you for your service and welcome home. It’s probably more meaningful to the veterans in particular when you think about some of the generations that had a hard time transitioning,” Otto said. “I always think about the Vietnam veterans, the way they were treated, when they came home. So so, you know, we're sort of making amends in some way for that.”

George Arnone was among the first Marines to land in Vietnam in 1965. He said he lost even more friends to post-traumatic stress disorder after the war than he did during the fighting.

“Personally, I think the war was a big mistake for the United States. But, having said that, the people that went and died and the people that are in wheelchairs and suffered, we all thought we were doing the right thing for the country. And we don't ask for a lot. You don't have to give us money, or accolades, just don’t put us down,” he said.

He comes to the parade every year with his son, and gets the opportunity to see old friends.

"Veterans Day is a good day for me because it’s the day that we celebrate and memorialize all veterans, those that didn’t make it and did make it,” Arnone said.

Along the route, other veterans turned out to show their support.

“This country was built on the sacrifices of people’s lives to give us the freedoms we have and it’s a great honor to be veteran,” said Todd Cumberbatch, who served in Desert Storm.

“I'm a veteran and it's very, very important that we support our veterans and all that, especially the Vietnam veterans,” said Navy vet Bruce Zitkus.

Paul Rieckhoff, a veteran and founder of Independent Veterans of America, said it was important to celebrate the city’s veterans, and to garner support for them as new ones come home.

“We need support from everybody of all backgrounds, especially now with the ongoing war in Iran and a new generation of veterans coming home. And the military has become divisive and political for many people because of this president. We need to know that that shouldn't extend to our veterans,” he said.
