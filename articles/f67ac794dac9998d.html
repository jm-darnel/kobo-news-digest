Astoria board eyes plans for 3 high-rise towers on Steinway Street

By Arya Sundaram · Gothamist · November 17, 2026

Three proposed high-rise apartment buildings on the southern end of Astoria’s Steinway Street, set to be reviewed by the local community board, have become a lightning rod over the neighborhood's redevelopment future.

While several other high-rise buildings are planned for the same stretch of Steinway Street, home to many one- and two-story shops and shuttered businesses, these three are among the few that require public review and government approval to move forward.

Evie Hantzopoulos, the chair of Queens Community Board 1, said she’s received over 100 letters detailing concerns about the projects ahead of the board meeting, at 6:30 p.m. Tuesday at the Marquee Astoria, 25-22 Astoria Blvd.

“ People are concerned, No. 1, that the majority of these apartments that are being built are not going to be accessible to working-class and immigrant communities,” Hantzopoulos said in an interview. “ I think (there’s) just concern that it is gonna become a neighborhood only for the wealthy.”

One project includes two apartment buildings—one nine stories and the other 12—on the corner of 34th Ave at 34-14/22 Steinway St., by the developer Schuman Properties. Together, the buildings would total 243 units, about 61 of which would be designated as affordable. Commercial space would occupy the first floor.

Details about specific rental prices were not immediately available. Schuman Properties declined to comment and ZD Jasper Realty did not respond to a request for comment.

Critics of the projects worry rents will be too high, and that even the required affordable housing will be out of reach for many of the neighborhood’s working-class immigrant residents.

Rana Abdelhamid, the founder and executive director of Malikah, a neighborhood anti-violence group, said the projects don’t “necessarily guarantee true affordability for our communities.”

Malikah is among several local organizations, including Chhaya Community Development Corporation and the Astoria Tenants Union, that have signed onto a letter to the community board asking them to withhold support for the proposals, pending an investigation of the displacement impacts of the projects and “binding, enforceable commitments to deeply affordable housing.”

Other projects are on tap in the area. Just north of 34th Ave., another developer submitted an application to the Department of Buildings in March to construct a nine-story building with 74 units at 32-73 Steinway St., according to the Real Deal. Next door, at 32-75 Steinway St., another developer submitted an application for an eight-story building with 65 units.

In July, another developer submitted an application for a 19-story building with 99 apartments at 35-13 Steinway St., just south of 35th Avenue.

Those developments don’t require community board consideration, since they don’t require changes to the city’s current zoning rules.

But to move forward, the three proposals at 34-14/22 and 34-49 Steinway St. will need to be reviewed by the community board and borough president before the city’s planning commission and City Council consider them. The proposals do not need community board approval to move forward in the rezoning process.
