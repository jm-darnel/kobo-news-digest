Yankees Mailbag: The DH question

By Madison Pavich · Pinstripe Alley · November 20, 2026

Good afternoon everyone, it’s time to dive back into the mailbag and answer some of your questions. Remember to send in your questions for our bi-weekly call by e-mail to pinstripealleyblog [at] gmail [dot] com .

jmack175 asks: With Judge getting older and needing to stay healthy, do you see a scenario where he gets more at bats at DH this season, and at some point when Stanton has his annual muscle strain from an intentional walk, Judge is fully transitioned to DH and Stanton is released? If that doesn’t play out, how many more seasons do you see Judge playing RF?

Not right away, at least not with how the roster as constructed stands. The Yankees have their designated hitter spot locked up between Stanton, Ben Rice, and Luis García Jr. at the moment, though the team’s future with Stanton is currently cloudy at best. For now, the team is going through with bringing Stanton to spring training and seeing what he’s got left in the tank, but even removing him from the equation still leaves the position filled up with a hitter that the team wants in the lineup more often than not. There’s also been some speculation that the Yankees could turn around and flip García after acquiring him at the deadline as part of their effort to find a quality third baseman to complete the infield, shifting Rice back to first base. Rice admittedly wasn’t that good of a defender despite working with Paul Goldschmidt though, and the team certainly won’t risk moving him back behind the plate at this point with how valuable he is to the team’s offense.

Personally, I don’t think it’s worthwhile to lose García’s bat unless there’s no other avenue to upgrade third, and with the team’s incredible starting pitcher depth as well as the prospects they could part with I think there are plenty of other routes they can consider. As for Judge, while it is concerning that the last two major injuries he’s sustained have been on maximum-effort plays in the outfield (I’m still willing to consider the Dodger Stadium wall collision as more of a freak accident, but c’est la vie) he’s still a tremendous outfielder when healthy and the team would do well to maximize the value he can bring by playing him out there as long as they can. Cody Bellinger provides excellent defense in left field, but we saw how much of a struggle the other two positions were for the team on a regular basis last year, and they may not be able to find a true center fielder this offseason to improve the defense with most options for the outfield being bat-first players. I don’t know if Judge can still be Judge out there if he’s giving 80-90 percent effort out there, but his cannon arm shouldn’t be too affected by telling him to avoid a full-out dive for the ball and that change alone could be enough.

jws85 asks: Forget rumors of Seager, Bohm, Skubal, Jeffers, etc. Boone has one year left and to spend money on long term contracts to get the same old results — start the year on fire, sleep through the summer, make the playoffs, and get bounced early — makes no sense. Why hamstring a new manager with contracts that jam the roster, let the current roster with the addition of kids or one year cheap players get through 2027, and then go crazy for 2028.

We’re making a lot of assumptions here, so let’s break this down. Aaron Boone is on a lame-duck contract, yes, but there’s no guarantee that means they’ll move on from him at the end of next year — or replace him with someone fundamentally different. If the team can’t accomplish more than another ALDS flameout three years in a row, I do think it’s unlikely that Boone gets handed another contract, but why set yourself up for failure just to move on from Boone when he’s at worst a minor part of the problem? The organization should be in win-now mode with their superstars getting up in age, and while they shouldn’t take on a bloated contract for a player that’ll only have one or two more positive years at best they should certainly be aiming to put together a World Series-caliber team instead of trusting that the kids can fill the gaps. They developed Rice and Cam Schlittler from unheralded prospect status into stardom, sure, but they made it to the majors by making the team unable to ignore them much like George Lombard Jr did this season.

Dax Kilby is a rising star in the organization but he’s still years away from the majors, and after him the rest of their top 10 prospects are all pitchers. The best-case usage of the kids in this scenario is finding smart trades to fill out the lineup, as they simply can’t all play. The only argument I’ll make in favor of not adding too much to the payroll for 2027 is that the team likely won’t be in the Tarik Skubal sweepstakes anyway and the other big ticket names don’t impress me as much, but if they were I wouldn’t hate it. Hand him a bag and the Yankees would instantly have the best rotation in baseball bar none, and still have depth to trade away to improve around the margins.
