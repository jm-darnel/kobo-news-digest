Chambers Street station overhaul moves forward with congestion pricing funds

By Samantha Liebman · NY1 · November 17, 2026

Gov. Kathy Hochul is promoting transit upgrades funded by congestion pricing, including a planned overhaul of the Chambers Street J/Z station, where the MTA is set to award a repair contract at its November board meeting on Wednesday.

In an exclusive TV interview, Hochul acknowledged its reputation, and that it is in need of repair.

“I think it’s haunted, personally. No, I’m rather convinced this is a scary place,” Hochul said. “No more ghosts, you know, Kathy Hochul: Ghostbuster.”

Hochul said congestion pricing — which requires drivers to pay a toll to enter Manhattan below 60th Street — is helping fund subway improvements, including the Chambers Street project.

“About time a governor actually cared about the subway and said that we can restore this to its grandeur,” Hochul said. “I mean, we have the ability. We have the resources now thanks to congestion pricing.”

Hochul first paused the toll before it went into effect and later lowered the price. She said that the planned increase from $9 to $12 will go ahead in 2028. She said she can’t lower it because of the agreement with the federal government.

“I don’t want to take it to Donald Trump because I think his desire is to abolish it entirely,” Hochul said. “And so, it’d be risky to open this issue up to them.”

Hochul said she is ready to speak with President Donald Trump about other transit priorities.

“I will be talking to the president. I keep track of his schedule,” Hochul said. “I want to catch him on a good day. And he hasn’t had a good day in a while.”

Asked whether pressure from groups such as the Democratic Socialists of America, which plans to recruit a candidate to primary her in 2030, would change her position on fare enforcement, Hochul said pressure does not work on her and that the MTA is taking corrective action.
