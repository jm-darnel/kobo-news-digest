Dad, girl killed in Brooklyn building collapse stayed home in fear of ICE, cousin says

By Brittany Kriegstein · Gothamist · November 13, 2026

Juan Daniel Sabala and his family increasingly felt like home was the safest place they could be.

When they weren’t working, the Colombian father of two and his wife preferred to stay in their Brooklyn apartment as much as possible — terrified that federal immigration agents would stop them on the street — according to cousin Juan Carlos Zabala, who spoke to Gothamist from his home in Spain. Juan Carlos Zabala clarified to Gothamist that he and his cousin spelled their family name differently.

On Wednesday, the illusion of safety in the Flatbush Avenue home came crashing down when the building collapsed. Juan Daniel Sabala, 31, and his 9-year-old daughter Nicole were later identified among the dead, along with 10-year-old Kevin Santiago Reyes Gonzalez, another cousin.

“We have no words,” Juan Carlos Zabala told Gothamist by phone early Friday, adding that he had just heard the news earlier in the day. “They were following the American dream.”

Juan Carlos Zabala said they had arrived in the country three or four years ago, and Juan Daniel Sabala had been working for a cleaning company. But the last time they spoke, he said, his cousin sounded anxious.

“Everything with ICE was really bothering them,” he said. “So they stayed shut inside, from work to home and home to work because they were afraid of being deported.”

“I have a lot of nostalgia and a lot of sadness … because I am also in another country that’s not mine,” he said. “We came with a dream, hoping for a better life. And at the end, we don’t know what will happen to us.”
