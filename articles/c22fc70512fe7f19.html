Investigators Swarm Construction Sites Tied to Contractors in Fatal Flatbush Collapse

By Greg B. Smith · The City · November 18, 2026

Soon after the collapse of a Flatbush apartment building that killed two children and six adults, Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced that building inspectors had launched an emergency review of conditions at 160 locations citywide where contractors working at the collapse site have been doing work.

While the Department of Buildings has yet to announce the results of the sweep, The City Reporter has found several of the job sites visited so far by the team assigned to the task, the Construction Safety Enforcement (CSE) unit. And records reveal a troubled history at these sites, including accusations that contractors performed work that wound up damaging neighboring properties.

That’s similar to the circumstances that unfolded at 2625 Bedford Ave., where contractors were performing excavation at a site where the owners had sought permission but not yet obtained approval to erect a nine-story building. Investigators are now looking into whether the digging triggered the collapse of an adjacent three-story apartment building at 1321 Flatbush Ave.

CSE inspectors visited at least two Queens sites where LMW Engineering was doing work and issued partial stop-work orders, including one for scaffolding not being up to code at 131-28 40th Rd. in Flushing and two at 56-12 Roosevelt Ave. in Sunnyside Gardens for a worker without a proper harness. Calls to LMW were not returned.

Two more contractors had been cited previously within the last two years for allegedly wreaking havoc on their neighbors, records show.

Take Elite Concrete, the Williamsburg-based company that was performing excavation at the Bedford Avenue site when the adjacent building caved in. Three laborers working in the pit were killed in the collapse, including one who was employed by Elite.

On Friday the construction safety enforcement team showed up at 2167 Arthur Ave. in The Bronx where Elite earlier this year was performing excavation.

Buildings Department records show that in March someone called 311 to report the excavation work there had caused cracks to appear in the foundation of his house next door. “Please contact me so that we can arrange for you to enter my house to see the damage,” the caller said.

Inspectors arrived two days later and issued no violations, but in May the state Public Service Commission cited Elite for damaging a 6-inch gas main at the Arthur Avenue job site. The agency determined Elite had performed excavation without first properly notifying a call-in system that would have flagged the gas main.

Several calls seeking comment from Elite on Monday were not returned.

Another key player at the Bedford Avenue project was N Tech Design, hired to do the demolition of a one-story commercial structure at the site. Last spring, inspectors slapped N Tech with violations and fined the firm $10,000 for doing work without a permit there and then ignoring a stop-work order issued after they got caught. N Tech paid the fine and in May the Buildings Department granted them a permit to finish the job.

Over the weekend, construction safety enforcement inspectors showed up at two other Brooklyn work sites where N Tech had been doing demolition recently. Both have a checkered history.

At 285 and 291 Eastern Parkway, adjoining structures the firm took down over the last year, the Buildings Department has issued seven violations since February. Twice they ordered work halted there, including once for N Tech’s alleged failure to protect an adjacent building during demolition by not providing adequate protection from falling debris.

On Monday, N Tech representative Aramis Brown insisted in an interview that his firm’s work at Bedford Avenue had nothing to do with the deadly collapse of the neighboring structure. Brown said his demolition work ended in May and that the building he took down rested on a slab and had no basement. Therefore, he said, he had nothing to do with the excavation that followed.

A Brooklyn jobsite of Townhouse Builders, the general contractor overseeing the Bedford Avenue construction, also got visits from the CSE team on Saturday and then again on Monday, records show.

Mordechai Waisbrod, the licensee for Townhouse, did not return a call seeking comment.

On Tuesday, Ryan Degan, a Department of Buildings spokesperson, said the sweep of the 160 sites was continuing and results would be revealed soon. Meanwhile the DOB, the city Department of Investigation and the Brooklyn District Attorney continue to investigate what triggered the collapse.

Probe Targets Excavation

Among other things, investigators are looking at whether Elite was performing work that had not yet been approved by the Buildings Department.

Last spring DOB approved plans by the developer, a limited liability company called W6 Facilities X LLC, to erect a three-story apartment building at the site, but in the fall the developer submitted new plans to build a nine-story structure that would have required much deeper excavation. At the time of the collapse, the nine-story plan had not been approved.

Investigators are trying to determine whether the excavation had gone beyond what was approved and if workers had removed too much soil without properly supporting the adjacent apartment building.

On Tuesday, Buildings Department spokesperson Ryan Degan said that “in the interest of public safety” the agency’s engineers have issued orders “to replace some of the removed dirt from portions of the excavation site in order to fill in the hole along the property lines.”

That operation will begin on the north side of the site, opposite the location of the collapse. “The south side of the site, where the collapse occurred, is not being touched at this point,” Degan said.
