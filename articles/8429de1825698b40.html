Jeffries poised to be nominated speaker as Schumer's push for Senate leadership intensifies

By Shannon Ryan · NY1 · November 17, 2026

On Wednesday morning, U.S. House Democrats are set to meet behind closed doors to begin electing their leadership team.

While House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries is believed to be on a glide path to the speakership, his fellow Brooklynite, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, is facing a tougher fight.

Though Jeffries, who is running unopposed, is all but certain to be nominated as House speaker, he suddenly switched plans Tuesday. He confirmed that he plans to force members to take a tallied vote instead of a vote by acclamation — a ballot-free process that would have made clear that Jeffries faces no substantive opposition.

“It’s the same approach that was taken the last time that we transitioned from the minority in 2018 into the majority, as a result of Democrats taking back control of the House in 2018. It certainly seems to me to be appropriate that we take exactly the same approach to give every single person the opportunity to weigh in,” Jeffries explained.

Jeffries spent Tuesday courting the Congressional Progressive Caucus and the Blue Dog Coalition. The groups house the bulk of his detractors, many of whom are seeking to secure agenda compromises in exchange for their support.

“Right now, I’m in conversation with members across the House Democratic Caucus,” Jeffries said. “The caucus is going to be able to work its will, and we’ll see what the outcome is.”

On the Senate side, Sen. Chris Van Hollen insisted Tuesday that his effort to challenge Schumer for Senate majority leader is gaining traction.

“I think we have the momentum,” he said.

Van Hollen is the first lawmaker to challenge Schumer in his more than a decade in his leadership post.

Meanwhile, Schumer held his first press conference in Washington, D.C., following Van Hollen’s announcement, where he stressed that the Democratic Caucus is united behind him.

“I’m confident I’ll be the next leader. I have the votes, and we’re all going to roll up our sleeves, and we are going to focus on lowering costs, ending corruption,” he said.

Senate Democratic leadership elections are currently scheduled for Dec. 1.

Van Hollen has repeatedly accused Schumer, and his team of attempting to move the elections up.

When pressed about the accusation, Schumer’s office would only reiterate that he is “confident [he will] be the next leader” and that he “has the votes.”
