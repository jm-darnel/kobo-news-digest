Dubai: Emirates SkyCargo Brings Aid To Malawi

Harlem World · November 24, 2026

By APO Group

Emirates SkyCargo is underlining its commitment to global communities and humanitarian relief by donating upcycled first aid kits.

“…supporting access to essential first aid supplies in communities where healthcare resources can be limited.”

As a first step, the ‘Aircrafted First Aid kits’ were produced by a team of upholsterers at a dedicated upcycling workshop at Emirates Engineering Centre using 97% upcycled materials including the AOG bags, leather offcuts and other materials from Emirates’ retrofit programme. In parallel, the team from Emirates worked closely with Dubai Humanitarian and The Sparkle Foundation to identify and source the medical supplies for the first aid kits. The finished kits provided by the Engineering team were then packed with essential medical supplies by employee volunteers before being transported on an Emirates SkyCargo freighter to Malawi, where they were received and distributed by The Sparkle Foundation to support community outreach and healthcare programmes in Zomba, Malawi.

Badr Abbas, Divisional Senior Vice President, Emirates SkyCargo, said: “The Aircrafted First Aid Kits project perfectly captures our dedication to minimise our environmental footprint through responsible consumption while delivering tangible impact and support to global communities. The original AOG ‘Must Go’ bags were developed for visibility and speed for urgently needed aircraft components. In their second life as first aid kits, they carry the same sense of urgency to medical and humanitarian care. Working in coordination with Dubai Humanitarian, the largest humanitarian hub in the world, and with Sparkle Foundation, we are able to deliver the first aid kits to communities who need the essential care.”

Giuseppe Saba, CEO and Board Member of Dubai Humanitarian Authority said: “This initiative reflects the strength of the ecosystem we have built at Dubai Humanitarian, where we bring together nonprofit organisations, private sector partners, and other stakeholders to turn opportunities for collaboration into tangible impact, creating practical solutions that benefit communities in need. By transforming retired operational equipment into valuable resources, Emirates has demonstrated how sustainability and humanitarian action go hand in hand. This is precisely the role we seek to play: fostering meaningful connections, enabling innovative solutions, and maximising the impact of the international humanitarian community that we host and support.”

“…flying around the world are now carrying urgent supplies to save lives.”

Sarah Brook, Founder and Global CEO, The Sparkle Foundation, said: “Bags that once carried urgent parts to keep aircraft flying around the world are now carrying urgent supplies to save lives. Seeing Emirates’ AOG bags transformed into 2,100 medical kits for vulnerable communities in Malawi is a powerful example of sustainability meeting humanity. We are incredibly grateful to Emirates for this generous donation and, even more, for the opportunity it creates to reach more people, deliver vital healthcare and change even more lives across Malawi. We hope this is just the beginning of a partnership with extraordinary potential for impact.”

In addition to the community outreach in Malawi, approximately 4,000 upcycled first aid kits were also distributed to Emirates Group employees.
