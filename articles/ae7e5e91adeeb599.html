NYC Council looks for colleges to testify about sexual assault procedures

By Catalina Gonella · Gothamist · November 24, 2026

Council Speaker Julie Menin said the lawmakers will be taking an investigative approach at the Dec. 8 hearing, questioning school leaders on sexual assault reporting procedures, how schools investigate allegations, what disciplinary actions are taken against those found responsible and other issues. The Council is also inviting New Yorkers to testify at the hearing about their experiences.

“The point of the hearing is to help inform our decision-making in terms of legislation,” she said. “We believe legislation is needed, but in terms of what those legislative fixes are, that is the point of the hearing, to basically hear relevant testimony, to take that back and then work on some legislative solutions.”

There were 1,100 sexual assault complaints reported in across the city's 10 largest schools in 2024 — a figure Menin called “unacceptable” — according to data gathered under the New York State “Enough is Enough” law, which requires that colleges adopt standardized policies regarding sexual assault, dating violence, domestic violence and stalking. The 10 largest schools are Columbia University, NYU, City University of New York, Fordham University, St. John's University, Pace University, Touro University, The New School, Mercy University and Yeshiva University.

“This is an urgent issue,” Menin said, citing state data. “The fact that there are 1,100 complaints just at these 10 largest universities alone in one year, is shocking. And so it's very deeply concerning, and so we really wanna get to the bottom of what can be done about this.”

Menin said there’s been an “unbelievable amount of sign-up and interest from current students, former students, women’s groups,” and others. A finalized list of which school presidents will be testifying will be published next week, she said.

Spokespeople for the presidents of the New School and Mercy University confirmed to Gothamist they plan to testify. Messages to spokespeople for the other schools had not yet returned messages sent Tuesday afternoon asking if they plan to participate in the hearing.
