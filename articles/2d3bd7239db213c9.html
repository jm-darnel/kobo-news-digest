“Ms. Blakk For President” — an important slice of LGBTQIA+ history

By Linda Armstrong · Amsterdam News · November 26, 2026

You only have until this weekend to experience an important part of LGBTQIA+ history in this country at the Vineyard Theatre with “Ms. Blakk For President,” a play based on a true event: When Black drag queen Terence Alan Smith ran for President of the United States during the AIDS epidemic in the early ‘90s, when the queer community was dying by the tens of thousands and was being ignored by the government under President Reagan and then Bush. Smith decided to run in drag and took on the persona of Joan Jett Blakk to make sure that the public knew what the queer community was facing.

Tarell Alvin McCraney and Tina Landau wrote this production in a way that touches the soul. They interviewed Smith at length to put this piece together in a way that resonates with everybody. You are introduced to Smith, gloriously played by Ato Blankson-Wood. When the queer community is being overwhelmed with HIV/AIDS fatalities and know that they need to take a stand with the government and local politicians to bring attention to the plight of the community, they form Queer Nation Chicago and take the country on a very informative journey about what was happening to people with AIDS. — things like being dropped from health coverage and having to pay for medical services and medications out of pocket; and the ways in which members of the queer community were shamed, shunned and discriminated against by society in general.

Blankson-Wood embodies this character with all of his being, delivering a performance filled with respect, dignity and determination. He is joined on stage by a marvelous group of actors, often in drag and looking fabulous. This company of delightful thespians includes Nic Ashe, Gideon Click, Kelvin Moon Loh, Sawyer Smith and Adina Verson. For the most part the actors play multiple roles. The runway struts that are a pleasure to see.

Before the show begins there is a pre-show drag show. When you come to the theater on a different floor there is a screen where you can view some of the interview with Terence Alan Smith and be touched by his deep experiences as a early champion for the queer community. There is also a timeline and article that relate to the HIV/AIDS epidemic and what was happening to members of the LGBTQIA+ community.

There are stunning costumes by Ari Fulton, scenic design by David Zinn, lighting design by Cha See, sound design by Lindsay Jones, projection design by Rasean Davonte Johnson, props supervisor by Andrew Diaz, wig and hair design by Ashley Wise, makeup design by La Sonya Gunter. The production also has fierce dance choreography by Darrell Grand Moultrie and music direction by Steven Cuevas.

Please make it your business to strut down to the Vineyard Theatre and see this stirring part of history. For info and tickets visit vineyardtheatre.org .
