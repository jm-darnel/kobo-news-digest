Tenant in collapsed Brooklyn building says he warned landlord of cracks in lawsuit

By David Brand · Gothamist · November 17, 2026

A Brooklyn musician made homeless when his apartment building collapsed into an adjacent construction site is suing his landlord and several construction firms, claiming he warned them about cracks near the Flatbush Avenue building's entrance.

In a complaint filed in Brooklyn Supreme Court late Monday, tenant Dengane Omari Jack said he “photographed those cracks, and reported the condition to the landlord or management responsible for the building” in October, weeks before the disaster claimed eight lives, including two children, and injured several others.

Jack claims the owner, Olufemi Falade, shrugged off the complaint, responding “that the condition was not the landlord's fault or could not be fixed” and that he “failed to arrange a timely and adequate structural investigation, obtain necessary protection or repairs, or take appropriate steps in response to the reported condition and the ongoing adjoining excavation.”

Falade owns the property through a limited liability company called Isolag Realty Corp. The city’s Department of Housing Preservation and Development documented 98 violations at the four-unit building, including unsafe electrical wiring and an inadequate water supply to the sprinkler system, before its collapse.

Falade did not immediately respond to a phone message seeking comment Tuesday.

“Our client obviously sends condolences to the families of all those killed, and best wishes to all those injured,” said Jack’s attorney Scott Epstein in a phone interview Tuesday. “The loss of his residence and the entirety of his worldly possessions has traumatically affected him.”

LMW Engineering, RI Consulting Services, Townhouse Builders, Elite Concrete, Fischman and Swimer did not respond to requests for comment about the collapse.

Fischman and Swimer received approval to construct a three-story apartment complex on the lot after demolishing a single-story commercial building. They sought approval to build a nine-story complex with a first-floor grocery store in September, but the city had not yet signed off on the plan before the collapse.

Building records show the apartment building next door was in bad shape before it collapsed. A preconstruction report documented the “poor” condition of the side facade overlooking the construction site, though the engineering firm that prepared it noted it could not access the building.

Excavation safety plans prepared by LMW Engineer’s Jieming Wang called for steel support beams, known as rakers, at every location along the perimeter of the lot except at the collapse site.

Tenants living at 1321 Flatbush Ave. dealt with problems in the building long before the fatal collapse. The building cycled through the city’s tax lien sale process for unpaid municipal debt at least five times in the last decade, court and property records show.

Falade also has a dubious track record as a landlord.

A judge sent him to jail for 12 days in 2006 for violations at a different building.
