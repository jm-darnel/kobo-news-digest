How Our Newsroom Estimated NYC Housing Lottery Odds

By Kennedy Sessions · The City · November 16, 2026

Over the last few months, The City Reporter reviewed data from the Department of Housing Preservation and Development’s Local Law 217 Report, summarizing housing lottery outcomes from Jan. 1, 2022, through Oct. 31, 2024. Our analysis calculated the approximate odds of the more than 10.9 million applications submitted through the city’s Housing Connect portal.

HPD said it has redacted the number of approved applications and leases for privacy reasons in cases where fewer than 10 applications have been approved.

To test our methodology, we spoke with an expert who previously worked with HPD data. They confirmed our methodology and agreed that Local Law 217 data provides the best historical basis for analyzing applicants’ odds. In our previous 2020 housing lottery analysis, we analyzed two HPD datasets of affordable housing lotteries and more than 20 million lottery applications. We didn’t use this methodology in this analysis because HPD declined to give us the data, despite multiple requests.

For this analysis, we calculated the win rate by dividing the number of signed leases by the number of applications.

Win Rate = # of Signed Leases / # of Applications

Then, to find the odds rate by community board, we divided the win rate by 1. These numbers are estimated odds per application.

Odds = 1 / Win Rate

There are some other important points:

• Each household can make one application to each lottery. Members of the same household cannot make separate, individual applications.

• Area Median Income bands are defined every year by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.

• This analysis does not include the various preferences that factor into the housing lottery, including those for people with a disability, municipal workers, and NYCHA residents, among others.

To calculate the number of units available in housing lotteries from July 30, 2020 to November 2, 2026, we used HPD’s database, Advertised Lotteries on Housing Connect. With this dataset, we calculated the number of advertised lotteries based on their designated income band.
