Interview: In Harlem, Six Decades Of Turning Entrepreneurial Ambition Into Lasting Ownership

Harlem World · November 18, 2026

By HWM

In Harlem, the story of a business does not begin and end with an opening night.

It unfolds in the daily act of turning a key, paying a supplier, setting a price, and making enough to return tomorrow. Behind those transactions lies a consequential question: Who gets the knowledge to make ownership last?

A Harlem Beginning, a Lasting Mission

For Dr. Stacie N. C. Grant, the answer is central to WIBO, the organization that began at the YWCA on 125th Street in 1966. Six decades later, its mission remains rooted in an unglamorous but powerful proposition: Entrepreneurial ambition needs an education in the numbers. More than 16,000 New Yorkers have passed through its classroom, Grant says. The measure that matters to her is how many keep opening their doors long after graduation.

It is a perspective that shifts the conversation away from the romance of starting something and toward the discipline of sustaining it. A compelling idea may bring a business into being; understanding costs, cash flow, and profitability helps an owner keep it alive. In Grant’s telling, financial fluency is not a finishing touch. It is the foundation on which independence, and eventually a family’s future, can be built.

Consider pricing. Rather than borrowing a number from the shop down the block, WIBO asks aspiring entrepreneurs to look inward, calculating what their own business costs to run, including compensation for themselves. Cash flow requires another adjustment in perspective: A company can appear profitable and still find itself without the money it needs on an ordinary Tuesday. These are practical lessons, but the stakes extend beyond a ledger.

For Grant, the promise is generational. When children watch a parent build an enduring business, ownership becomes something familiar rather than something reserved for other people. The classroom, in that sense, reaches beyond the person enrolled. It can alter what a household imagines is possible.

Keeping the First Step Within Reach

That ambition also explains WIBO’s commitment to keeping its business education free. Grant describes access not as an act of charity, but as a matter of who gets to participate. Talent and determination, she argues, are already present. What too many prospective owners lack are the training, tools, and support to translate them into something sustainable. Alumni connections, free WIBO Decide sessions, and cohorts in Manhattan and on Zoom offer ways into that community.

From the arithmetic of a fair price to the possibilities a child sees across a parent’s counter, she makes the case for an education designed to keep opportunity close to home.

Questions for the Conversation: The Five Ws and How

Who: Who are the Harlem entrepreneurs who came through WIBO and are still behind the counter, and whose stories best capture the organization’s impact?

Dr. Stacie N. C. Grant: WIBO was born in Harlem, at the YWCA on 125th Street in 1966, so Harlem owners are part of our DNA. Over 16,000 New Yorkers have come through our classroom since then, and the ones I think about most are the owners still opening their doors every morning years after graduation. What their stories have in common is not luck. It is that they learned their numbers before they needed them.

What: What does WIBO teach about pricing, cash flow, and profitability that aspiring business owners most often overlook?

Dr. SNCG: Most aspiring owners price by looking at the shop down the block. We teach them to price by looking at their own costs first, including paying themselves. The second thing people overlook is cash flow: a business can be profitable on paper and still run out of money on a Tuesday. WIBO puts the numbers first, because when owners in our neighborhoods learn the numbers, the money stays in our neighborhoods.

When: When did you recognize that access to business education could change not just an entrepreneur’s prospects, but a family’s future?

Dr. SNCG: I have watched it happen again and again: a parent learns to build a lasting business, and the children grow up seeing ownership as normal rather than out of reach. That is how communities build wealth that lasts.

Where: Where do you see the greatest need for WIBO’s work in Harlem today, and how do you stay connected to entrepreneurs there?

Dr. SNCG: The need is greatest where people have the talent and the drive but have never been handed the tools: the skills, the training and the support to start and sustain a business. That is the barrier WIBO exists to remove. We stay connected through our alumni network, our free WIBO Decide sessions, which any New Yorker can join, and our cohorts in Manhattan and on Zoom.

Why: Why has keeping this business education free remained essential to WIBO’s mission?

Dr. SNCG: Because a business of your own should not be a privilege reserved for a few. WIBO was founded on the belief that every person, regardless of background, education, or access to capital, deserves the same quality of business education. Not charity, not handouts: education, tools, and community. The moment you put a price on the first step, you lose the very people who need it most.

How: How will you turn the momentum of the November 19 anniversary gala into sustained support for entrepreneurs over the next decade?

Dr. SNCG: Sixty years is a milestone, not a finish line. The gala puts WIBO-trained owners from across the five boroughs in the same room as the funders, partners, and neighbors who make this work possible, and gala proceeds go straight to our programs. We have established a legacy fund to honor our late director, Stephen Jackson. We will announce it at the gala and are building our year-end campaign around the Stephen Jackson Legacy Fund. Our job now is to make sure the next 60 years of owners get the same chance the first 16,000 did.
