A new day arrives on Brooklyn's Flatbush Avenue as redesign gives bus riders priority

By Carol Chen, Ramsey Khalifeh · Gothamist · November 17, 2026

City officials on Tuesday unveiled an overhaul to a stretch of Flatbush Avenue designed to speed up commutes for tens of thousands of bus riders.

Crews also added 36 new bike racks to the street, as well as fresh bus shelters, benches and leaning bars at every single bus boarding island, according to the city transportation department. The changes removed 41 parking spaces, but also added new loading zones for trucks and deliveries on side streets.

“Flatbush Avenue is the foundation for the future of bus service in New York City … Bus riders are tired of waiting — waiting for the bus that never comes, waiting for the bus to crawl through traffic and waiting for government to care about their precious time,” Mamdani said in a statement.

Bus riders on the B41 bus said they already noticed a slight improvement in their commutes

Monica O’Neill, 30, who rides the route every other day to get to work, said “traffic goes a little bit faster than usual” since the buses no longer have to stop in front of restaurants and shops.

Some locals were still getting used to the new street design, having never seen a bus island where riders wait to board in between lanes of traffic instead of the sidewalk.

Nadine Barry, 45, worried that cars would crash into the boarding islands.

“I think pedestrians…are the first priority,” she said. “Because living in New York, New York is always congested.”

Workers at Antonio’s Pizza on Flatbush Avenue were up in arms about the bus lane, claiming it’s already pushed customers away from the slice shop.

“This place used to be full around 12 o’clock with all the construction guys, plumbing guys,” said Arlind Kirkuti, 20, who’s worked at the shop for five years. “Now they can’t even find parking, so they don’t even stop by no more.”

Sy Hannan, who runs a painting business in the area, said he might have to charge more for jobs in the area because it takes longer to find parking and haul his equipment to the location.

“If I had to work for one of these people, I'd have to park several blocks away and haul my gear, and that costs the client more,” Hannan said.
