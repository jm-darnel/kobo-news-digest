David Bednar returns to Yankees on qualifying offer

By Nick Power · Pinstripe Alley · November 17, 2026

David Bednar accepted the Yankees’ qualifying offer on Tuesday, as first reported by The Athletic’s Tyler Kepner . The deal reunites the Yankees with their lockdown closer on a one-year, $23.15 million deal.

Bednar has excelled during his year and a half in pinstripes, posting a 2.39 ERA along with 45 saves in 52 opportunities across 86 appearances. His 2.7 fWAR ranked eight-best among all relievers during that period. Before joining New York, Bednar made two All-Star teams as Pittsburgh’s closer. The veteran turned 32 last month.

With Bednar returning to the Bronx, none of these implications will come into play. Instead, alongside Twins catcher Ryan Jeffers, he becomes the 19th player and first reliever (not counting swingman Nick Martinez) to accept the qualifying offer. Critically, a player can only receive a qualifying offer once in their career, meaning Bednar’s services would not be tied to draft pick or international bonus pool compensation should he hit free agency again after the 2027 season. It’s unclear if the Yankees would entertain an extension before that point or whether they will be content to let Bednar play out his new, one-year deal. If he keeps his current deal, Bednar will certainly be among the game’s highest-paid relievers on a per-year basis; the most richly rewarded bullpen arm in baseball, Edwin Díaz, is making an average of $23 million annually over the course of his three-year deal with LA.
