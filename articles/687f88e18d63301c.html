NYC Council Votes To Expand Access And Affordability For Reproductive Health Care Services From Harlem To Hollis

Harlem World · November 20, 2026

Today, the New York City Council approved a package of legislation focused on expanding access to and information on fertility treatments, doula care, contraception, and other reproductive health services.

“For many New Yorkers, the dream of starting a family feels increasingly out of reach,” said Speaker Julie Menin. “Between the rising costs of essential health care services and the challenges navigating available sources, New Yorkers face too many barriers to getting the reproductive care they need. No one should be left in the dark about the services, benefits, and protections available to them, or face unnecessary financial obstacles to accessing care. This legislative package takes important steps to empower our city’s families with the information and resources they need to make informed decisions about their reproductive health and their futures.”

Expanding Access and Affordability for Reproductive Services

Introduction 211-A, sponsored by Council Member Shahana Hanif, would require the Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH) to conduct a public education and outreach program on fertility treatment services, including information about insurance coverage, Medicaid coverage, and the benefits provided by New York City or New York State that may assist individuals in accessing fertility treatments.

“For New Yorkers hoping to start or grow their families, fertility treatment can be expensive, physically demanding, and emotionally overwhelming,” said Council Member Shahana Hanif. “Too many New Yorkers are unaware that they may qualify for low-cost or even free fertility care. No one should miss out on reproductive healthcare simply because they don’t know what resources are available to them. By passing Intro 211, we’re helping New Yorkers better understand their fertility treatment options, available coverage, and rights. I’m proud to help ensure that New York City remains a leader in reproductive freedom and healthcare access.”

Introduction 200-A, sponsored by Council Member Jennifer Gutiérrez, would require the Commissioner of the Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH) to develop materials to inform the public about options for obtaining doula care, including information about State laws regarding access by a doula to hospital spaces and Medicaid reimbursement for doula services. DOHMH would be required to develop a submission form to receive feedback from doulas working in New York City, to be reported annually, and would establish a doula advisory council to study the work of doulas in the city and advise the Citywide Doula Program.

Resolution 89-A, sponsored by Council Member Jennifer Gutiérrez, would call on the New York State Assembly to pass, and the Governor to sign, legislation requiring each institution within the State University of New York (SUNY) and the City University of New York (CUNY) to have at least one vending machine making emergency contraception available for purchase.

“I am proud to stand alongside my colleagues to celebrate advancing maternal health,” said Council Member Jennifer Gutierrez. “Two of my bills in today’s package, Intro 200 and Res 89, strengthen maternal and reproductive health in hospitals and on campuses, and ensure that we are giving a voice to those giving birth and the doulas supporting them.”

Resolution 108-A, sponsored by Council Member Frank Morano, would call on the New York State legislature to pass, and the Governor to sign, legislation creating a refundable tax credit for up to three cycles of in vitro fertilization (IVF) not covered by insurance.

“For too many New Yorkers, the dream of becoming a parent comes with a price tag of tens of thousands of dollars,” said Council Member Frank Morano. “I’ve spoken with friends, family members and constituents who have made enormous financial sacrifices just for the chance to have a child. No one should have to choose between starting a family and maintaining their financial security. A refundable state tax credit covering 75 percent of eligible out-of-pocket expenses for up to three cycles of IVF would provide meaningful relief, particularly for working- and middle-class families. I’m especially proud that this resolution has brought together Democrats and Republicans from across the ideological spectrum. Helping people who desperately want to become parents is something that should unite us all. With today’s passage, we are sending a clear message to Albany: New York can and should do more to make the joys of parenthood accessible to more people.”

Resolution 447, sponsored by Council Member Kayla Santosuosso, would call on the New York State Legislature to introduce and pass, and the Governor to sign, legislation to ensure insurance companies cover the cost of Preimplantation Genetic Testing for Aneuploidies (PGT-A). PGT-A is an optional in vitro fertilization (IVF) procedure that screens embryos for aneuploidy, a genetic disorder where the total number of chromosomes does not equal 46. The condition can affect birth outcomes, often leading to miscarriage.

Resolution 637, sponsored by Council Members Kayla Santosuosso and Amanda Farías, would call on the New York State Legislature to pass, and the Governor to sign, legislation requiring insurers to cover all lactation consultation and counseling services without restrictions and reimburse providers accordingly.

“Starting a family is a stressful time in anyone’s life, especially parents struggling with infertility,” said Council Member Kayla Santosuosso. “Services such as PGTA testing can dramatically shorten the time to conceive and services like lactation counseling are essential for mothers with difficulty breastfeeding. Right now, these services are often not covered by insurance, forcing parents to suffer unnecessarily. We must fight for the health and wellbeing of future parents by ensuring that these services are accessible and require that they are covered by insurance.”

Notifying Property Owners of Open Permit Requirements

Introduction 717-A, sponsored by Council Member Yusef Salaam, would require the Department of Buildings (DOB) to notify property owners with open permits that the permits must receive DOB sign-off. DOB would be required to email an initial notice within 7 days of the issuance of a permit. The notice would inform property owners of the consequences of failing to obtain sign-off and provide instructions for accessing DOB’s online self-service portal. DOB would also be required to send follow-up notifications if such permit has not been renewed and yet to receive sign-off within 30 days of expiration.

“An open permit should never be a surprise you discover at the closing table,” said Council Member Yusef Salaam. “Too many property owners finish a project without ever being told that the permit has to be signed off with the Department of Buildings, and they pay for it later in reinstatement fees, stalled permits, and delayed sales. Intro 717-A directs DOB to inform owners what is expected within seven days of issuing a permit, to follow up when an expired permit is still open, and to publish a clear guide to the process. For many families in Harlem and across our city, a home is the most valuable thing they own, and a simple email from the City can help protect it.”

Providing an Electronic Option for Property Registration

Introduction 1023, sponsored by Deputy Leader Chris Banks, would give property owners that need to register their property with the Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) the option of doing so through an electronic registration system.

“For too long, New York City’s property registration process has remained stuck in the past, creating unnecessary burdens for responsible property owners trying to comply with the law,” said Deputy Leader Chris Banks. “This legislation, Int. 1023, will modernize that process by allowing electronic registration, bringing both City government and the property registration system into the 21st century while making compliance more accessible and efficient.

Banks continued, “I was proud to collaborate with the Small Property Owners of New York (SPONY), whose members helped shape this legislation through their firsthand experiences navigating the current registration process. Their advocacy made clear that this change was long overdue. By reducing administrative hurdles and improving access, this bill will make a meaningful difference for property owners across our city while helping ensure that critical housing information remains accurate and up to date.”

Clarifying and Eliminating Certain City Agency Local Law Reporting Requirements

Introduction 1127-C, sponsored by Deputy Speaker Dr. Nantasha Williams, would amend and repeal sections of the New York city charter and the administrative code of the city of New York to eliminate certain reporting requirements and change the dates of when specific reports are sent to the Council.

Introduction 1128-A, sponsored by Deputy Speaker Dr. Nantasha Williams, would establish a default reporting due date system for agency reports due to the Council for reports that do not have a deadline specified in law.

Introduction 1129-A, sponsored by Deputy Speaker Dr. Nantasha Williams, would establish a procedure, to be prescribed by the Speaker of the Council, for how a City agency is to transmit reports and completion of an operational milestone to the council.

“Effective oversight requires more than passing legislation. It requires the ability to track implementation, evaluate progress, and hold city agencies accountable for delivering on their responsibilities,” said Deputy Speaker Dr. Nantasha Williams. “Agency reporting is an essential part of that process, but the system must be clear, consistent, and structured to produce information that is both timely and useful. This legislative package strengthens that framework by improving reporting requirements, establishing greater consistency, and reinforcing accountability for compliance with local laws. Ultimately, this is about ensuring that the Council has the information it needs to exercise meaningful oversight and that New Yorkers can have greater confidence in how their government operates.”

Expanding Educational Materials on Dental Care

Introduction-259-A, sponsored by Council Member Shekar Krishnan, would require the Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH) to collaborate with the Department of Education (DOE) to develop materials for students and parents on the importance of dental care and fluoride.

“If the federal government will not provide the public health information we need to keep our communities healthy, our New York City Council will do so,” said Council Member Shekar Krishnan. “Our country is in an urgent public health crisis fueled by misinformation spread by our federal government and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. My legislation to educate New York City about fluoride chips away at that misinformation. I’m thankful to our New York City Council for standing up for accurate health information.”

Calling for Haiti to be Redesignated for Temporary Protected Status

Resolution 626, sponsored by Council Member Farah Louis, would call upon the United States Senate to pass, and the President to sign, S.4814, to require the Secretary of Homeland Security to designate Haiti for Temporary Protected Status.

“The passage of Resolution 626 sends a clear and powerful message from New York City to Washington: Congress must act now to restore Temporary Protected Status for Haiti,” said Council Member Farah N. Louis. “More than 5,000 Haitian TPS holders in New York City are facing profound uncertainty about their ability to remain in the country, contribute to the economy, support their families, and continue building their lives here. These are our neighbors, our coworkers, our parents, and valued members of our communities, and the federal government has failed this population who deserves stability and the ability to plan their futures without the constant fear of losing their protections. As the largest city in the nation, New York City has an important responsibility to speak up for the communities that make this city what it is. I am proud to have sponsored this resolution and grateful to Speaker Julie Menin and my Council colleagues for supporting its passage. Now, Congress must hear this message and take action.”

Supporting Local Organizing Efforts in Puerto Rico

Resolution 494, sponsored by Council Member Alexa Avilés, would stand in solidarity with the people of Puerto Rico to stop the construction of Project Esencia, a luxury development in Cabo Rojo.

Resolution 28, sponsored by Council Member Alexa Avilés, would call on the United States Congress to establish a date certain for ending the Financial Oversight and Management Board for Puerto Rico.

“I am a proud Boricua. That means I know how deeply the Puerto Rican diaspora of our city feels the effects of exploitation from back home,” said Council Member Alexa Avilés. “PR suffers under the weight of several forms of colonial rule. These resolutions address two. One calls on an End to the Financial Management and Oversight Board known as ‘La Junta.’ This is the unelected body of people that have delivered crushing austerity policies, including defunding the public university system by 50%. The other demands a stop to the mass displacement project known as Esencia. I am proud that the Council is standing up for the Rights and dignity of Puerto Ricans, ensuring these heinous forms of exploitation be put to bed. We stand proudly with the People of Puerto Rico to say Esencia No Va and Que Viva Puerto Rico Libre.”

Designating November 28 annually as Albanian Independence Day

Resolution 186, sponsored by Council Member Amanda Farías, would designate November 28 annually as Albanian Independence Day in the City of New York.

Declaring November 25 as International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women

Resolution 441, sponsored by Council Member Amanda Farías, would declare November 25 as the “International day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women” in honor of the Mirabal sisters.

“The Mirabal sisters hold an extraordinary place in Dominican history, but what makes their legacy so consequential is how it helped shape an international movement to confront violence against women. The movement they inspired brought generations together to confront the many ways violence is used to control, intimidate, and deny women their freedom, safety, and independence,” said Council Member Amanda Farías. “As a Dominican and Puerto Rican woman, I take tremendous pride in the passage of my resolution, ensuring that New York City formally recognizes November 25 in honor of three women whose courage continues to resonate far beyond the Dominican Republic. This declaration reflects the significance of their contributions to our shared history and reinforces our collective responsibility to address gender-based violence wherever it occurs. My sincere thanks to my co-prime sponsors, Council Members Carmen De La Rosa and Pierina Sanchez, our fellow sponsors, and the advocates and community organizations whose work to protect women, support survivors, and prevent violence gives this observance continued purpose and meaning.”

Land Use:

100-12 Queens Boulevard Rezoning – will facilitate the development of a new 21-story mixed-use building with approximately 389 residential units, 97 of which will be permanently affordable, as well as ground floor commercial space in Council Member Lynn Schulman’s district. The Council is modifying this application to strike MIH Option 2 and add MIH Option 3.
