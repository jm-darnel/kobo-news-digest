Council report shows stores selling e-cigarettes close to schools despite ban

By Kelly Mena · NY1 · November 18, 2026

City Council Speaker Julie Menin and her fellow lawmakers are calling for greater enforcement against shops selling illegal flavored vapes.

“Vapes are a risk to our children and to the health of our future,” she said.

In 2019, the City Council passed legislation banning the sale of any flavored e-cigarette.

A new council report found that, despite a seven-year ban, 32% of public middle and high schools surveyed had a shop within 500-feet offering the product.

“It is our responsibility to make good on that agreement, to enforce the laws we passed and to deliver on our vision of a safer city for every New Yorker who calls it home,” the speaker said.

“The investigators of our team also found unlicensed stores brazenly selling vapes despite not being licensed to sell any tobacco,” Councilman Shekar Krishnan, who chairs the Committee of Oversight and Investigations, said.

Of the more than 1 million penalties that have been issued to businesses without active e-cigarette licenses, less than 1% have been paid.

“They are breaking the laws, and their store should not be open because they’re getting our kids hooked,” she said.

“What are the tools we need to give to DWP or any other city agency to ensure that our young people are protected? This is a crisis moment for our young folks, and while we’ve reduced smoking, I, Council Member Krishnan, you know, for noting that this problem can be worse. This is addictive,” Councilman Harvey Epstein said. “We need to hold these businesses accountable to make sure they start harming our young people and have a plan.”
