Lithium-ion battery deaths rise as City Council reviews safety efforts

By Louis Finley · NY1 · November 20, 2026

Nine people have died from lithium-ion battery fires so far this year, up from one at the same point last year, according to officials. One of the nine died from injuries connected to a 2023 fire.

As of Nov. 16, FDNY had investigated 423 lithium-ion battery fires this year, compared with 252 at the same point last year.

FDNY First Deputy Commissioner Chief Daniel Flynn said some of those fires have started in unexpected places.

“One hundred of these fires this year were in sanitation in the back of sanitation trucks,” Flynn said.

Council Member Susan Zhuang, who represents part of the neighborhood, said the city needs to invest more in prevention.

“We have to push the administration to invest more for doing [a] prevention job,” Zhuang said.

Zhuang said she wants the city to be able to shut down shops sooner. Under her bill, a store caught selling uncertified e-bikes or batteries twice in two years could be ordered to close.

A separate bill could fine someone who owns an uncertified e-bike or battery. Fire Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore raised concerns about that proposal.

“There are a lot of individuals that have these things that may not even know that they are not certified,” Bonsignore said. “And we’re worried about penalizing normal people, whether it be civil or criminal.”

Bonsignore said FDNY will work with the council on the wording. She said enforcement should focus on where uncertified batteries are sold, before riders take them home.

“It really does come down to education and collaboration throughout all communities and all different languages, so that we can all keep each other safe,” Bonsignore added.
