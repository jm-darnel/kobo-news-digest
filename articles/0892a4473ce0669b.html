FAA Halts U.S. Departures Nationwide After Air Traffic Communications Outage Friday

Harlem World · November 13, 2026

According to reports from The Associated Press, the communications failure affected all 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers responsible for directing aircraft traveling between airports. The FAA ordered airlines to hold departing aircraft on the ground as officials worked to assess the situation and restore normal communications.

Aircraft already in the air were allowed to continue flying and land safely. International arrivals were evaluated on a case-by-case basis. The FAA’s decision was driven by safety concerns. Air traffic controllers depend on reliable communications to coordinate aircraft movements, maintain safe separation between planes and manage the nation’s complex airspace system.

When those communications are disrupted, restricting additional departures helps prevent more aircraft from entering an already affected air traffic network.

Telecommunications Failure Under Investigation

Federal officials described the incident as a telecommunications outage. The precise cause remained under investigation Friday. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication at the time that the disruption resulted from a deliberate cyberattack. Officials were working to identify the source of the communications failure and restore affected systems.

Transportation officials and FAA technicians were also investigating the incident. Authorities emphasized that aircraft were accounted for and that the immediate priority was maintaining aviation safety while communications systems were evaluated. The outage also raised questions about the reliability of the telecommunications infrastructure supporting America’s air traffic control network.

Airlines Warn Passengers of Delays and Cancellations

Airlines faced the challenge of managing grounded departures, delayed schedules and passengers waiting for information at airports nationwide. Travelers were encouraged to check directly with their airlines before leaving for the airport or making connecting travel arrangements.

Even after communications are restored and a ground stop is lifted, airlines can experience additional delays while aircraft, flight crews and passengers are repositioned.

For passengers traveling through major hubs, including New York, Chicago, Atlanta, Los Angeles and Dallas, the incident underscored how interconnected the nation’s aviation network has become.

What Travelers Should Know

Passengers with scheduled flights should monitor airline websites and mobile applications for cancellations, revised departure times and rebooking instructions. Travelers should also check the FAA’s National Airspace System Status website for official aviation updates.
