Mamdani shuts Bronx migrant shelter at center of ICE fight over residents’ data

By Karen Yi · Gothamist · November 20, 2026

Lawyers for the city filed a letter in court late Thursday night arguing the South Bronx shelter’s closure rendered the subpoena moot since there are no current residents to identify, a copy of the filing shared with Gothamist shows.

“There will be no more residents living inside of Bruckner, and the site is officially closed,” Mamdani said in a statement. “Our administration believes that every homeless New Yorker should not only have access to shelter but to spaces that are safe, humane and truly livable. We cannot accept a system that treats people without dignity or stability.”

A federal judge earlier this month ordered the Mamdani administration to hand over the personal information of certain residents staying in the shelter. U.S. Customs and Border Protection argued the information was needed to investigate whether anyone inside the facility was subject to deportation.

Under the judge’s November ruling, the city and ICE agreed to the appointment of a special master who would identify shelter residents with serious criminal histories, and arrange for their information to be turned over.

Under the Trump administration, ICE had tried at least four times to obtain information about shelter residents. But the city fended off those requests.

It’s not clear how many residents were living in the facility in the weeks before it closed. But as recently as this summer, the shelter was still home to 1,500 residents, many of whom were migrants from Latin America or West Africa.

City officials said some of the residents had left and found housing or were staying with friends. Others were relocated within the city’s municipal shelter system.

Many of those sites skirted stringent rules imposed on traditional homeless shelters. For example they did not provide kitchens for homeless families with children, and they ignored rules about how far apart the beds should be. The migrant population has declined in recent years, allowing the city to close these sites,

Several residents inside Bruckner had previously complained about poor conditions, like bad food and bug infestations.
