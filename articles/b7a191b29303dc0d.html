Dangerous winds, rains expected in NYC and NJ for Thanksgiving Day

By Rosemary Misdary, Louis C. Hochman · Gothamist · November 25, 2026

New York and New Jersey are in for potentially dangerous winds and flooding on Thanksgiving, with officials in both states urging people to stay off the roads when possible — as residents try and scope out their options for holiday travel.

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul warned on social media New York City, Long Island and the lower Hudson Valley could experience wind gusts of as much as 60 mph, in addition to heavy rain and coastal flooding. The winds could bring down tree limbs and power lines as well, Mayor Zohran Mamdani warned in his own posts.

New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill declared a state of emergency for all of the Garden State's 21 counties ahead of the storm, anticipating as much as 4 inches of rain along the Jersey Shore. Non-coastal areas in New York and New Jersey are expected to see about 1 to 3 inches of rain, forecasters say.

"Damaging winds, heavy rain, and major flooding will threaten communities across our state as families gather for Thanksgiving, and our coastal and bayside communities will face the greatest danger," Sherrill said Wednesday.

Officials in both states urged residents to take precautions including being prepared to evacuate if needed.

"The worst of it is expected late [Thursday] afternoon," Hochul wrote on X. "A good time to stay inside, watch some football and sleep off that big meal!"

She urged people to keep an eye on local forecasts and use caution if traveling.

“When we issue a high wind warning like this, that means we have an 80% confidence level at least that these conditions will occur,” said Joe Pollina, a National Weather Service meteorologist. “Don't go outside during the peak of the storm, which is during the afternoon [Thursday].”

High winds will begin to pick up in the late evening Wednesday and continue to increase throughout Thanksgiving morning before peaking in the afternoon, according to the NWS.

“Overall, it’s going to be a very windy day [Thursday],” Pollina said.

Mamdani and NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch are expected to give a storm safety update on Wednesday evening.

During these windy conditions, the National Weather Service highly recommends securing or bringing in any loose outdoor objects, such as holiday decorations, which could become dangerous projectiles. The gusts could bring down power lines and tree limbs. The service also warns paradegoers to stay clear of trees.

Because of the city’s vertical geometry, there could be spots of higher wind.

“You could have higher winds around a certain corner,” Pollina said. “It's very much a microclimate.”

In addition to the gales, weather predictions include moderate to heavy rain with widespread flooding that could be major at some of the city’s typical flood-prone spots.
