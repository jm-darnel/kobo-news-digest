Mamdani says more help is coming to homeless New Yorkers living outside this winter

By Karen Yi · Gothamist · November 9, 2026

Last winter, more than two dozen people died outdoors or in their homes during a brutal stretch of freezing temperatures.

“We have spent the past year preparing for any challenge winter throws our way. We have trained our workers, readied our equipment and strengthened our protocols because keeping New Yorkers safe can’t begin when the first storm hits,” Mayor Zohran Mamdani said in a statement.

“This winter, we’re doing things differently — we’re expanding Code Blue to reach more New Yorkers in need, equipping New York’s Strongest to clear snow and ice faster and leaving no stone unturned when it comes to protecting our neighbors from the cold. No New Yorker should face the dangers of winter alone, and no part of our city should be left behind.”

This winter, street canvassers will reach out to every homeless person they meet, rather than focusing on a list of at-risk individuals. More than 50 mobile vans run by the city’s public hospital system will provide medical care and transportation to warming facilities.

City officials also announced improvements to its snow clearing efforts, putting a priority on keeping sidewalks clear for older adults and New Yorkers with disabilities. The city’s sanitation department will include more snow clearing routes on Staten Island and upgrade snow plows to more effectively clear roads. It also purchased the agency’s first snow blowers to clear bus stops and crosswalks faster.

New Yorkers can sign up for emergency alerts about extreme weather and emergency resources by texting NOTIFYNYC to 692-692 or download the app. Those interested can also sign up to be a snow shoveler earning $30 an hour.
