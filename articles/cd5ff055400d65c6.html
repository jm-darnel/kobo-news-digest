Menin, Council Announces $2.1 Million For Nontraditional Worker Organizing And Education

Harlem World · November 19, 2026

Today, New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin and Council Members announced a $2.1 million investment for a new nontraditional worker initiative, which will support organizing and education efforts among grassroots labor organizations serving contract workers, domestic workers, and other vulnerable workers across the city.

Funding will go towards targeted outreach and the empowerment of nontraditional workers on their legal rights and recently enacted city protections. Through the on-the-ground efforts of partnering organizations, this initiative will ensure nontraditional workers throughout the city know their rights and what to do if those rights are violated.

“As one-third of New York City’s labor force, nontraditional and gig-based workers are an essential part of our city, and we must ensure they are protected,” said Speaker Julie Menin. “Strong worker protection laws can be the difference between someone earning a fair day’s pay and being out on the street. I am grateful to our incredible community partners who are championing workers’ issues and leading this initiative on the ground to ensure our deliveristas, home-care workers, laundry workers, and all our nontraditional workers are not only guaranteed safer and fairer working conditions, but are also properly informed of their rights and the resources available to them.”

In 2019, about 1.3 million people — or roughly one third of NYC’s labor force — could be categorized as freelance or working in independent arrangements, according to data from the Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment, Freelancers Union, and Upwork. This includes gig and project-based employees who lack traditional labor protections and benefits, like health insurance or retirement plans, that come with traditional, long-term work arrangements. These workers are particularly vulnerable to labor and wage exploitation, and may not always know the legal protections that are in place to safeguard their livelihoods.

• Know Your Rights education, including structured classes and workshops with workplace and community-based outreach;

• Labor rights clinics on filing complaints;

• Train-the-Trainer programs for worker-leaders to serve as Know Your Rights Ambassadors;

• Advancing delivery workers’ capacity to monitor algorithms, identify labor violations, and prevent unjust deactivations;

• Additional programming and education on worker protection laws to help workers identify and document potential workplace violations, understand how to engage appropriate government agencies, and develop the skills necessary to share rights information with coworkers and others in their communities.

“Over one third of workers in New York City are non-traditional, which means many of them do not receive the same rights training and protections that others do, all while facing greater uncertainty in their employment,” said Majority Leader Shaun Abreu. “I am proud that the Council has delivered this new funding that will empower our organizational partners to bridge that gap and ensure nontraditional workers know their rights. Legal resources and knowledge for deliveristas, street vendors, day laborers, and others creates safer and more fair workplaces for those who keep our city running. Thank you to Speaker Menin and my colleagues for their commitment to worker rights.”

“I’m proud to have been part of a great collaboration between Speaker Menin, Finance Chair Lee, my colleagues in the Council and a strong coalition of workers justice groups to secure $2.1 Million in funding for organizing and education access for nontraditional workers across the city,” said Council Member Harvey Epstein. “This initiative would not only enable targeted outreach and education on the new rights recently enacted by the NYC Council but also enhance organizations’ ability to act as a pipeline to surface complaints about workers’ rights violations and increase the effectiveness of enforcement, generating additional revenue for the City and improving economic stability for workers, which in turn strengthens New York’s economy.”

“Street vendors, deliveristas, domestic workers, and other nontraditional workers are essential to the fabric of our city and keep New York running every day,” said Council Member Pierina Sanchez. “That is why I am proud to have worked with Speaker Menin and Chair Epstein to secure this $2.1 million investment in organizing, outreach, and education so workers can understand and exercise their rights. I was proud to lead the Council’s passage of landmark street-vending reform—the most comprehensive overhaul of New York City’s vending system in generations. But even the strongest reforms will only succeed when people understand their rights and responsibilities. This investment will help ensure that our historic reforms reach the workers they were designed to serve and translate into meaningful change on the ground.”

“Delivery workers, street vendors, and domestic workers keep this city running, but too often they work outside the protections most New Yorkers take for granted,” said Council Member Linda Lee, Chair of the Committee on Finance. “With $2.1M through the Nontraditional Worker Organizing and Education initiative, the Council is funding trusted community organizations to make sure these workers know their rights, can report violations, and can get help from the agencies charged with enforcing the law.”

“The New York job economy is changing, and with that means we need to provide support to new types of workers,” said Council Member Shekar Krishnan. “Gig or project-based workers often lack basic labor protections, and they make up a third of the workforce. The New York City Council is committed to changing that. I’m proud that we’re investing $2.1 in this new initiative to support grassroots labor organizations and make sure these workers know their rights and have access to services to hold their employers accountable.”

“New York City has become a national leader in advancing protections for workers who have too often been excluded from labor laws and workplace protections,” said Ligia Guallpa, Executive Director of Worker’s Justice Project. “We applaud the City Council and Speaker Menin for investing in this initiative to ensure that these workers know about their legal rights, understand how to exercise them, and have trusted community organizations helping them defend them. The Worker Organizing and Education Initiative is a critical component of making these laws real in workers’ lives, strengthening enforcement by connecting workers to the organizations they trust and ensuring violations do not remain hidden in the shadows.”

“The Worker Rights Organizing and Education Initiative marks a historic first step toward ensuring that workers in New York City, many of whom are low-wage immigrants, can learn and defend their rights,” said Rosanna Rodriguez, Executive Director of Laundry Workers Center. “With these resources, the Laundry Workers Center would organize, educate, and reach workers directly at laundromats and in surrounding communities. We know that change happens through grassroots, transformative work, and these resources serve as a vehicle to continue improving workers’ working and living conditions. As NYC advances its affordability agenda, we urge the Council to expand this investment over time and include more grassroots organizations. Workers’ rights and laws are meaningful only when workers know they have them and are empowered to exercise them.”

“Public investment in worker education and organizing is essential to building lasting worker power,” said Marco A. Carrión, President of the Consortium for Worker Education. “Worker protection laws are only as effective as workers’ ability to understand and exercise their rights. That requires investing in the unions and worker centers that empower workers to advocate for themselves and organize collectively. We thank Speaker Menin, Council Member Epstein, and the City Council for their leadership and commitment to ensuring that all workers, regardless of industry and their employment or classification status, have the tools and resources to build a more equitable economy.”

“The City Council’s historic $2.1M investment in the Worker Rights Organizing and Education Initiative demonstrates its bold commitment to economic justice and worker power,” said Mohamed Attia, Managing Director of the Street Vendor Project. “Following the passage of historic legislation, this investment will ensure that street vendors, delivery workers, and other non-traditional workers know their rights, protections, and responsibilities under the new laws. We thank Council Speaker Julie Menin for her vision and commitment to New York’s smallest small businesses and the tireless advocacy of Council Member Harvey Epstein, Chair of the Committee on Consumer and Worker Protection.”

“We are deeply grateful to the New York City Council for this funding which comes at a critical time as delivery workers face increased enforcement actions, e-bike theft, and other challenges that threaten their safety and livelihoods,” said Maimouna Dieye, New York Chapter Director for African Communities Together. “This funding will allow ACT to strengthen our outreach efforts to African immigrant delivery workers, many of whom face language barriers and economic insecurity, and ensure they have access to accurate information, resources and support.”
