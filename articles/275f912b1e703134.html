Judge Tosses Lawsuit Against Mamdani’s Signature NYC Rent Freeze

By Samantha Maldonado · The City · November 25, 2026

Rent freezes for stabilized tenants across roughly 1 million New York City apartments will remain in place after a Manhattan judge on Wednesday dismissed a lawsuit brought by landlords challenging the policy.

It was not immediately clear if the plaintiffs will appeal. Their attorney, Randy Mastro, said in a statement that they were “disappointed” by the outcome and were “considering next steps.”

In his decision, Manhattan Supreme Court Judge Brendan Lantry called Mamdani’s campaign pledge to freeze rents “reckless and irresponsible” because the mayor cannot unilaterally do so. But Lantry wrote that the plaintiffs failed to establish the freeze enacted by the Rent Guidelines Board in June was “unreasonable, arbitrary or capricious.”

A group of several landlords represented by Mastro, who served as former Mayor Eric Adams’ first deputy, sued in July to try to overturn the freeze. Pointing to Mamdani’s campaign pledge, they alleged the Rent Guidelines Board, which sets allowable rent increases for stabilized units, “ran a sham process with a predetermined outcome.”

Throughout the lawsuit, the board maintained its independence from City Hall and said it voted to freeze rents after examining official data. Three previous rent freezes were in place under Mayor Bill de Blasio.

While Mamdani could not direct a freeze, he appointed six of the board’s nine members when he took office. Adams appointed three of the members, including one who resigned just hours before the board voted on how much stabilized landlords could raise rents.

Tenants and some housing advocates have long said many stabilized tenants cannot afford to keep up with rent increases.

Landlords and housing analysts have argued rents must increase so owners can keep up with rising operating costs, including for insurance and fuel.

Sumathy Kumar, director of the advocacy group New York State Tenant Bloc, celebrated the judge’s decision.

“Today’s decision affirms that tenants won the rent freeze fairly and secures relief for millions of tenants,” she said in a statement.
