Fatal Brooklyn fire renews concerns over e-bike batteries

A deadly fire in Brooklyn that killed six people is renewing concerns about the safety of lithium-ion batteries, as investigators work to determine whether they played a role in the blaze.

Fire Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore told PIX11 that the cause of the Brooklyn fire remains under investigation but emphasized the importance of fire safety education.

“The cause of the fire is still under investigation. But we need to get the message out there that fire safety and education is an important thing to learn for every person, every family and every person,” Bonsignore said.

City Council Member Alexa Avilés, who represents the area, said officials need to revisit how they address battery safety.

“We need to get back to the drawing board, right? We need to make sure we address safety hazards and really want to tell residents over and over again they should not be inside,” Avilés said.

Avilés said some e-bike users bring their batteries indoors because they worry about losing their transportation or source of income. She suggested the city explore ways to provide safe outdoor storage and replace unlicensed batteries with certified ones.

“Folks take them inside because they’re concerned they might lose their form of income or transportation, so they try to safeguard it. So perhaps the question is how can we create safe infrastructure for folks to keep their bikes outside,” she said.

Council member Phil Wong has introduced legislation that would eliminate the current fire code exemption allowing up to five battery-powered mobility devices to be stored and charged inside apartments and other buildings.

“E-bikes should be charged outdoors only and not in any indoor facility, and right now it's in committee,” Wong said.

“We hope to improve our current fire code and have a safer New York and safer community,” Wong said.
