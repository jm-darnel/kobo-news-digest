15 Years After Complaints to Authorities, Lawsuit Accuses Brooklyn Doctor of Sex Abuse

By Reuven Blau · The City · November 19, 2026

More than a decade before three women filed a lawsuit accusing Dr. Richard Grazi, a Brooklyn fertility specialist, of sexually abusing patients, one of the plaintiffs took her allegations to state health officials.

She recalled being interviewed alongside several other women who also had complaints about Grazi, a prominent physician in her tight-knit Jewish community.

“I was deflated,” she recalled, noting she was 19 when she first saw Dr. Grazi. “It’s not something I’d readily share with other people,” she said of her alleged assault. “A person carries a lot of shame.”

Now, she is among three women suing Grazi, Maimonides Medical Center and Genesis Fertility alleging that the doctor sexually abused them under the guise of medical examinations and that the institutions knew or should have known about his alleged misconduct but failed to protect patients.

Doctor Denies Wrongdoing

Grazi, who has practiced in Brooklyn for decades, has not been charged with a crime and denied any wrongdoing.

“Dr. Grazi categorically denies the allegations made against him in this lawsuit,” his lawyer, James Nikraftar, told NBC. “Throughout his career, Dr. Grazi has helped thousands of women build their families. He has treated patients with care and professionalism, and that is exactly what the facts will show.”

Tina Lee, a spokesperson for Maimonides, said, “We cannot comment on active litigation, but can confirm that Dr. Richard Grazi is not a Maimonides employee and does not have privileges here.”

Grazi was previously the director of Reproductive Endocrinology and Fertility at Maimonides Medical Center and founded Genesis Fertility and Reproductive Medicine in Bensonhurst. The clinic, which specializes in IVF and egg freezing, was popular among members of the Jewish community.

Grazi’s medical license remains active, according to state Health Department records.

Patient advocates have long criticized state medical boards for allowing doctors who are under scrutiny to continue to practice while under investigation. The boards usually are heavily made up of doctors nominated by state and local medical societies.

Erin Clary, a Health Department spokesperson, said the state takes “all allegations of medical misconduct seriously” and follows guidelines laid out by state law. The department is prohibited from discussing any investigation, complaint, or prosecution beyond what is published on its website, she added.

A 2025 Newsday investigation found that the state’s system for disciplining doctors had become increasingly lenient, with serious disciplinary actions declining even as the number of physicians practicing in New York grew.

Newsday also found that the rate of serious disciplinary actions fell by roughly half over the decade ending in 2023.

Eileen Corina, founder and president of Pulse Center for Patient Safety Education & Advocacy, said the case highlights how vulnerable patients can be when they seek medical care, especially when they don’t know what to expect during an examination or where to turn if a doctor crosses a line.

More women have come forward alleging similar abuses since the lawsuit was filed, according to Helene Weiss, who is representing the three plaintiffs. They are considering joining the litigation, Weiss said.

“The doctor should not have a medical license,” she said, “and should not be around women and children.”
