NY students will still have to take Regents exams, even as graduation rules change

By Giulia Heyward · Gothamist · November 9, 2026

Students across the state will no longer need to pass Regents exams to graduate, but most students will still need to take them.

State education officials on Monday said high school students will still need to take assessments in four subjects to meet federal testing requirements.

Passing the exams will no longer be required to graduate starting in the 2027-2028 school year. State officials have said the tests can be obstacles to graduation for students with disabilities, different learning styles or limited English proficiency.

But during a Board of Regents meeting on Monday, education officials said students would still need to take Regents exams or approved alternatives in each of the four subjects to satisfy statewide testing requirements under federal law.

Education advocates have raised concerns about the changes. In a June 2024 statement, EdTrust-New York warned that eliminating Regents exams as a graduation requirement could lead to inconsistent academic standards across school districts and make it harder to determine whether students are prepared for college and careers.
