NYC, NJ airports grounded as part of nationwide shutdown, FAA says

By Charles Lane · Gothamist · November 13, 2026

"Every flight has been accounted for and international flights will be handled on a case by case basis," the agency said at 5 p.m. on social media.

U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy wrote in a social media post just after 4 p.m. that the ground stop is “following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers."

This is the first nationwide ground stop since January 2023 when a key FAA computer system malfunctioned, according to the Associated Press. Before that, the last time had been after the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks.

Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said that “everyone is safe.”

"There is no reason to be concerned about your loved ones if they're flying or anything like that, I just wanted to make sure everybody is at ease about that,” he said to reporters Friday, according to the agency’s social media channel.

John F. Kennedy Airport, LaGuardia Airport and Newark Liberty International Airport all advised travelers to confirm their flight status with their airline before heading to the airport.

“We waited about 15 minutes and then they said, ‘Yep, you got to get off,’” said Repe, adding that he’s not optimistic he’ll make it to Florida.

Jason Haber was also traveling from JFK to Fort Lauderdale when flights were grounded.

“Initially the airport looked just as it would on any other day,” he said as he waited for this flight in Terminal 4. “Then all of a sudden you started to see as word got around the help desk line getting very long and people sitting down and lines forming.”

Haber ended up canceling his flight and plans to rebook in the coming days.

By early Friday evening, elected officials in the region began to demand federal aviation and transportation officials to update the public, including New York Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand and New Jersey Sen. Andy Kim.

"This is outrageous,” Kim said in a statement. “Flights grounded nationwide will disrupt travelers, our way of life, and our economy for days. We need answers, and we need this fixed immediately.”
