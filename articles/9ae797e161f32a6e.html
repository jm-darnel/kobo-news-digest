Reported flu cases surge across New York ahead of Thanksgiving

By Caroline Lewis · Gothamist · November 23, 2026

As New Yorkers get ready to gather with family and friends for Thanksgiving this week, an early flu surge is threatening to throw a wrench in holiday plans.

More than 43,000 New Yorkers statewide have caught the flu since early October, compared to about 7,000 cases at this point during flu season last year, state health department data shows.

City health officials are urging New Yorkers to take precautions to avoid spreading the virus to loved ones over the long weekend — including sitting out the festivities if they’re not feeling well.

“We encourage everyone to stay home when they’re sick, to wash their hands often, cover their coughs and sneezes and consider wearing a mask in crowded spaces,” Dr. Andrea Howard, the city’s deputy commissioner of disease control, said during an appearance on WNYC’s All Things Considered.

She added that getting vaccinated is the “best tool to protect ourselves” during flu season.

In a recent notice about the flu, the city health department recommended vaccination for everyone over 6 months old — and urged New Yorkers to get their flu shots sooner rather than later, since it can take up to two weeks for immunity to build up following vaccination.

In New York City, the flu surge has followed a similar pattern. More than 6,000 cases were reported the week ending Nov. 14.

Flu symptoms can last a few days to a couple of weeks and may include a fever, chills, body aches, a sore throat and runny nose, according to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Some cases of the flu can become severe and lead to hospitalization or death.

Hospitalizations are currently on the rise in New York as cases mount. More than 886 New Yorkers statewide were hospitalized the second week of November, state data shows. The rate of hospitalizations for the flu is highest among adults over 65 and children under 4.

“As we gather, let’s look out for our older relatives, young children, and others who may be more vulnerable to serious illness,” Dr. Alister Martin, the city health commissioner said in a statement on the surge.

New York is not alone in grappling with the flu ahead of Thanksgiving this year. National data shows the virus arrived earlier than usual, according to the CDC.

But that doesn’t necessarily mean this year’s surge will be more severe overall, since every flu season looks different, Howard said.
