Flatbush Avenue gets city's first center-running bus lanes

By Alyssa Paolicelli · NY1 · November 18, 2026

A nearly 2-mile stretch of Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn has been redesigned with the city’s first center-running bus lanes, banning passenger vehicles from the lanes around the clock from Grand Army Plaza to Livingston Street.

According to the city's Department of Transportation, the protected center lanes are expected to speed up service for riders on the B41, B45, B67 and B103 routes.

Before the changes were implemented, the DOT said some buses on those routes were moving as slowly as 4 mph.

“Riders are no longer suffering from commutes slowed by double-parked cars or general traffic,” DOT Commissioner Mike Flynn said. “And commuters are not losing time, as buses weave into and out of the curb lane for pickups and drop-offs.”

Some riders said the changes have improved their commutes.

“I love it, because you can get to and from your destination really faster,” said Cynthia Archer, a Brooklyn bus rider.

“It’s much easier — well, I wouldn’t say easier, because I’m not driving a bus, but definitely faster,” added Devante Lountzi, another Brooklyn bus rider. “Less getting caught at traffic lights."

With the new design, general vehicle traffic is limited to one lane in each direction. Emergency vehicles and specialized public transit service vehicles are the only other vehicles allowed to use the bus lanes.

“Used to be like four lanes. Now, they made it only one. It is ridiculous,” one driver said.

“I hate it,” added Shanisha, a driver who was on her way to work. “It makes me late for work every day. I do understand the point of it, but at the same time, it doesn’t make sense.”

Transportation officials hope to expand the design to King’s Plaza.
