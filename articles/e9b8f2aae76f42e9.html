With search for victims over, NYC officials look to what caused fatal building collapse

By Ryan Kost, Catalina Gonella, Brittany Kriegstein, David Brand · Gothamist · November 13, 2026

It was a mild Veterans Day afternoon, and Katie Stevens, a teacher, had the day off. She’d left her windows open to let the cool fall air into her Flatbush apartment as she did her laundry.

At about 4 p.m., she heard what sounded like a car crash. A few minutes later came the sirens and then helicopters overhead.

Just down the block, near the corner of Bedford and Flatbush Avenues, a three-story apartment building had collapsed into a lot that construction workers had been excavating for a new development. Nearly all of the building had caved into the lot, shearing off the side of the neighboring building, exposing a stairway, a closet full of clothes and a bedroom set.

As Stevens approached the scene, she found a family crying on the sidewalk: “We may need everything right now,” they told her.

In the hours that followed, more than 250 firefighters and emergency service providers responded to the scene. Construction workers who had barely escaped themselves turned back to dig victims out of the rubble, Mayor Zohran Mamdani would later say. Councilmember Farah Louis described infants and toddlers being carried out on stretchers.

By Thursday afternoon, eight people were dead, including two children, and 18 others were injured. One person remained in critical condition. The rescue operation continued as a storm soaked the area in rain. By 4 p.m just as skies were clearing again, the FDNY declared the search and rescue operation over.

City officials said they began an investigation immediately after the collapse, one of the worst such disasters in recent city history. Gothamist reported Thursday that the developer of the site had a record for cutting corners on the project, according to records from the city buildings department.

“If there is anyone or any entity operating in this city that bears responsibility for what happened, we will hold them fully accountable,” Mamdani said.

In the immediate aftermath of the collapse, families gathered with their neighbors and loved ones on the sidewalk near the place that was once their home. Stevens, the teacher, said she took one young boy to a nearby clothing store to use the restroom. He kept asking her when he could go back home.

“I don't know, love,” Stevens recalled telling him. “But you are safe. Your family is safe. You're gonna be okay.”

Anderson Velez said he got a panicked phone call from his wife at home after he arrived for a painting job in the Bronx. She was screaming, he said, begging rescue workers to pull their two sons from the rubble of their Brooklyn apartment building, which had collapsed minutes earlier.

All three survived, but his 9-year-old son was in intensive care after undergoing surgery on his skull and his wife has serious injuries to her ribs, spine and collar bone, he said Thursday. Velez provided Gothamist with video of his apartment as the building collapsed.

“I am still in shock,” Velez said in the small lobby of a Brooklyn hotel the city is using to house people displaced by the disaster. “The truth is, I’m trying to be strong for my son, but I feel terrible.”

“And so it was just amazing when his wife was found and taken out alive,” Williams said. “I'm hoping that that blessing, that miracle continues.”

But many were still left waiting.

On Thursday morning, rescuers continued the search as the community looked on. A demolition excavator worked through the rubble, piling the debris on one end of the lot — a mattress, a refrigerator and wood floor panels. Clothing and dozens of music records lay scattered across the site.

One woman collapsed to the ground after learning her husband was among the dead. “My husband, he died,” she yelled in Spanish.

“We’ve been waiting here all night. We went to four hospitals, and they haven’t given us any information,” Luis Siguencia said of his son, 22-year-old construction worker Gabriel Ariolfo Siguencia Yupa, who had recently arrived from Ecuador.

“He wanted to get ahead,” Luis Siguencia said. “Because we all come here with the dream of getting ahead. He had his papers and everything.”

It wasn’t until Thursday afternoon that the Ecuadorean consulate general could confirm Gabriel had died in the collapse.

Kayla Chimborazo, who said she was Gabriel's stepsister, said her family spent the night “jumping from hospital to hospital, showing pictures, telling his name, his date of birth, to see if anybody has him.”

“We didn't even get a chance to see him,” Chimborazo said. “Even if there was that one possibility that he was still alive, we didn’t get to see him.”

Chimborazo described her stepbrother as “a sweet soul,” who was working to be able to provide for his wife and son in Ecuador. Chimborazo said she'd just seen him two weeks ago, at a family church gathering in New Jersey.

“He was really stepping up and starting a new start in life,” she said.

José Luis Caguana Cosquillo, 26, another construction worker from Ecuador, was also among the dead, the consulate reported.

His friend, Segundo Iza, stood vigil at the site of the collapse Thursday. He said he and Caguana Cosquillo had grown up together in Ambato, Ecuador. They’d met playing soccer.

Iza said his friend had moved to the U.S. four or five years ago. He had one son and another child on the way.

“He was someone who came here to achieve many dreams, to find better days for our children and our parents,” Iza said. “So it’s sad that our friend died here in New York.”

Eventually, the storm moved in, and with it, a heavy rain. The mayor spoke to reporters under a tent.

“These were our friends and our neighbors, parents and children, young families building their lives in our city, and immigrants from Mexico, Colombia, Ecuador, Trinidad and beyond,” Mamdani said. “ Let us continue to hold close those who have been plunged into unimaginable grief and pray for a full and rapid recovery for every New Yorker injured.”

An aunt to two of the men confirmed dead, Mamadou Bhoye N’diaye and Mamadou Bhoye Barry, gathered with her family that evening and grieved at the news.

“Right now my emotions are not good,” Kadidiatou Diallo said. “I just lost my nephews.”
