Congestion pricing toll increase remains on track for 2028, Hochul says

By Spectrum News NY1 · NY1 · November 17, 2026

New Yorkers who were hoping the congestion pricing toll would stay at its current rate should think again.

Gov. Kathy Hochul has confirmed to NY1 that the peak daytime toll for passenger vehicles with E-ZPass will rise from $9 to $12 in 2028, as planned.

Congestion pricing took effect on Jan. 5, 2025, charging drivers to enter Manhattan south of 60th Street. Hochul initially paused the program before it went into effect, then announced a revised plan in November 2024 that lowered the initial toll from $15 to $9.

But the lower rate was always scheduled to increase to $12 in 2028 and then $15 in 2031, according to the MTA's toll schedule.

Hochul said changing that schedule could open the MTA up to a federal government challenge. She said the toll increases were part of the conditions the federal government set when it approved the program.

"The challenge is that that was put in place as a prerequisite by the federal government to OK this in the first place. And I don't want to take it to Donald Trump because I think his desire is to abolish it entirely. And so, it'd be risky to open this issue up to them,” Hochul said. “So I believe that the law that's in place now and the conditions that were set in place to allow us to do this, to be able to make these extraordinary changes will continue.”

Hochul discussed the investments during a tour Monday of upgrades at the Chambers Street J and Z station in Lower Manhattan.
