Having a job doesn’t save you from going hungry in NYC, report finds

By Karen Yi · Gothamist · November 16, 2026

More than half of 1.3 million New Yorkers who visited food pantries last year were working but still didn’t earn enough to feed their families, a new report found.

Robin Hood found 39% of pantry users worked full time, 12% worked part-time and 4% had a working spouse or partner. Most of those remaining did not work due to a health reason or were 65 years or older.

“Work really isn't a guarantee against hunger. Many New Yorkers are working. About 55% are either working themselves or living with a family member who works, and they're still having a hard time making ends meet,” said Sarah Oltmans, chief of grant strategy for Robin Hood.

Nearly half the people who visited pantries experienced financial shocks. That could mean needing to cover a major expense, losing income or losing a job, the report said. The report also said food pantries play a critical role to help New Yorkers out of poverty.

“We're seeing that pantries are really filling these gaps that the paychecks aren't able to do,” Oltmans said.

“People will skip meals. They'll rely on friends and relatives. They'll borrow money. We see a lot of people taking on increased amounts of credit card debt,” said Leslie Gordon, CEO of the Food Bank of NYC, a hunger relief group.

“By the time individuals and/or households turn to our food assistance network, they've already exhausted a lot of these other coping strategies.”

Gordon said pantries are also closely monitoring the drop in how many people are applying to SNAP, not just those who already lost their monthly benefit. The thousands of New Yorkers who lost their benefit under the work rules, can reapply to receive at least three more months of assistance.

“It's the new policy and requirements, along with the fact that people are just anxious and fearful,” she said.

Undocumented New Yorkers are not eligible for SNAP but their U.S. citizen children are. But immigration enforcement crackdowns and recent federal changes around how immigration officials weigh a green card and visa applicant’s use of public assistance when deciding whether to approve their cases, known as the public charge rule, is having a chilling effect on New Yorkers wanting to sign up for a benefit they’re entitled to.

But Gordon said pantries “can’t fill the gap” on their own. For every one meal provided by a food pantry, SNAP provides nine.
