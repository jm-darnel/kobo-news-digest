Desirée Elder has worked closely with Joseph and NHTG through her leadership of arts programming at First Corinthian Baptist Church, a vital community hub and worship space in Harlem. Desirée Elder is a cultural strategist, writer, and institution builder who serves as Pastor of Development and Innovation at First Corinthian Baptist Church and Founding Executive Director of the FCBC Community Development Corporation. With more than fifteen years of experience in organizational leadership, arts programming, and community development, Elder has created cultural platforms and artist development initiatives that strengthen Harlem’s creative ecosystem. She is the first woman to serve as Artistic Director in New Heritage Theatre Group’s 62-year history.

Reflecting on her appointment, Desirée Elder said: “It is a profound honor to help steward New Heritage Theatre Group into its next chapter. Voza Rivers and Jamal Joseph have built a cultural legacy rooted in the belief that our stories have the power to shape communities and transform lives. I step into this role with deep reverence for that foundation and a clear commitment to what comes next. Our responsibility is to preserve the richness of our history while creating new pathways for artists, new work, and the next generation of storytellers. Legacy is not something we preserve by holding it still. We honor it by keeping it in motion.”

About New Heritage Theatre Group

Founded in 1964 and based in Harlem, New York, New Heritage Theatre Group is uniquely dedicated to uplifting vital and compelling voices and narratives from Black, Brown, multicultural, LGBTQ+, and other underrepresented communities, rooted in social justice, healing, and love. Honoring the legacies of founder Roger Furman and Emeritus Executive Producer & Founding Member Voza Rivers, NHTG cultivates vibrant, inclusive audiences that reflect the rich diversity of New York City and the world. Through storytelling in theater, dance, music, film, and social justice initiatives, NHTG has fostered transformation and cultural dialogue on local and global stages, building connections between Harlem and communities in South Africa, Canada, the United Kingdom, Nigeria, Zimbabwe, Japan, Cuba, Jamaica, and beyond. As a global connector and catalyst, NHTG empowers multimedia artists and engages multicultural audiences, bridging cultures and continents.
