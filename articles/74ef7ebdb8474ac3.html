Romare Bearden: A Life In Collage Makes Its NYC Premiere At DOC NYC Tomorrow, Fresh Off IDFA

Harlem World · November 16, 2026

Fresh off its acclaimed International Premiere in the prestigious Frontlight section at the International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam (IDFA), Romare Bearden: A Life in Collage—the feature documentary from writer-director Deborah Riley Draper and Coffee Bluff Pictures—makes its New York City Premiere tomorrow, Tuesday, November 17, at DOC NYC.

Driven by overwhelming early demand, advance tickets for both tomorrow’s 6:30 PM premiere and the Wednesday 11:00 AM screening at IFC Center have already sold out to rush-only status, leaving limited advance tickets remaining for the Wednesday, November 18, 8:40 PM screening alongside a nationwide DOC NYC online run through November 29.

One of just 16 titles selected for IDFA’s premiere-only Frontlight program—which spotlights urgent, formally inventive documentaries illuminating history, art, and society—the film launched in Amsterdam this past weekend with packed Q&A screenings and continues its Dutch festival run through November 21. In New York, the film screens in DOC NYC’s Metropolis Competition and is one of 10 nominees in the festival’s inaugural Archival Storytelling Competition (presented with the Archival Producers Alliance).

Romare Bearden: A Life in Collage examines the life and enduring influence of the painter and master collagist whose vibrant, groundbreaking work transformed the rituals, traditions, and textures of Black American life into a singular modernist visual language. Tracing his upbringing in Harlem and Pittsburgh, his early promise as a gifted baseball player, and his decades as a visual artist, jazz-inspired innovator, and civil rights activist, the film weaves together rare archival footage of Bearden inside his New York studios and alongside close collaborators Alvin Ailey and James Baldwin. Featuring commentary from Denzel Washington, Usher Raymond IV, Grant Hill, Titus Kaphar, Mickalene Thomas, Derrick Adams, Elizabeth Alexander, and Dr. Mary Schmidt Campbell, the documentary reintroduces an essential American master whose aesthetic legacy resonates across global contemporary art and culture.

“Romare Bearden spent his life cutting and layering images of Black life until they formed something no one had seen before,” said writer-director Deborah Riley Draper. “Launching internationally at IDFA in Amsterdam—a city deeply connected to the European art history Bearden studied and transformed—and immediately bringing his story home to New York for our DOC NYC premiere is the exact momentum we dreamed of. Bearden’s work was always in conversation with the wider world, and we are thrilled to share his legacy on both sides of the Atlantic.”

About Deborah Riley Draper

Deborah Riley Draper is an award-winning writer, director, producer, and founder of Coffee Bluff Pictures. A 2025 NAACP Image Award nominee for Outstanding Directing in a Documentary for James Brown: Say It Loud, her acclaimed documentary filmography also includes Olympic Pride, American Prejudice, Versailles ’73: American Runway Revolution, Twenty Pearls: The Story of Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, and the NAACP Image Award-nominated The Legacy of Black Wall Street. Expanding into narrative features, Draper is currently in post-production on her narrative directorial debut, Don’t Go Gentle, and has completed her screenplay adaptation of Coffee Will Make You Black, with production slated for late 2027. Before turning to filmmaking, she was an advertising executive on Madison Avenue. Her work centers culturally resonant, character-driven stories about American history and Black life.

About Coffee Bluff Pictures

Founded by filmmaker Deborah Riley Draper, Coffee Bluff Pictures is a storytelling company dedicated to uncovering culturally rooted narratives that illuminate the people, places, and legacies overlooked—whether by accident or by design. Through acclaimed documentaries, narrative features, and mission-driven content, the Atlanta-based studio restores essential voices to the center of the American story.
