NYC Bodegas Get Reprieve From New SNAP Rules After Mamdani-Trump Call, Mayor Says

By Katie Honan · The City · November 13, 2026

Stores across the country will get a 90-day delay on the enforcement of rules requiring shops that accept federal food benefits to stock a wider selection of items, after New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani pleaded the case directly to President Donald Trump, the mayor announced Friday.

“Bodegas are operating on razor-thin margins,” Amr Abozaid, director of small business services at the Yemeni American Merchants Association, said at a City Hall press conference alongside Mamdani on Friday. “If we lose our SNAP, or if these stores are losing their SNAP authorization for six months, that could be the end of their businesses.”

More than 100 bodega owners reached out to Mamdani’s office requesting more time to meet the new requirements, he said, and he brought those concerns to Trump.

“We formally raised those requests to the federal government, and I personally escalated them directly to President Trump,” Mamdani said during the press conference.

SNAP Enrollment Falling

SNAP enrollment has declined in the city since Congress passed the bill affecting SNAP access, to 1.61 million in August 2026 from 1.79 million July 2025. The decrease has occurred even as grocery prices continue to rise.

Lawmakers from both parties had in recent months asked the Trump administration to delay enacting the food stocking rules.

Mamdani and Trump have had a surprisingly cooperative relationship since the mayor was elected last November, meeting most recently at Gracie Mansion in September. He has gone to the president for the release of New Yorkers detained by federal immigration agents and for financial support for the city.

“The president and I have an ongoing conversation about anything that affects our city, and for all of the policy disagreements the two of us have, we do have a shared love of the city,” Mamdani said Friday. “That does not mean that every attempt is successful. But what it does mean, however, is that there is always more than there was the day before working about a working New Yorker’s life and their needs being addressed as a result of what we talk about.”

One in four children in the city do not always know where their next meal will come from, the nonprofit City Harvest said last year. In The Bronx’s 15th Congressional District, 43% of children faced food insecurity in 2024, according to the group Feeding America.
