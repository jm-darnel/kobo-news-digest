Sean Bell’s fiancé reflects on 20 years since Queens shooting

By Ayana Harry · NY1 · November 25, 2026

It has been 20 years since Sean Bell, an unarmed 23-year-old Queens man, was fatally shot by undercover NYPD officers as he left his bachelor party in Jamaica on the day he was set to be married.

Bell was killed before dawn on Nov. 25, 2006, outside Club Kalua. His friends, Joseph Guzman and Trent Benefied, were wounded.

Five undercover officers fired a total of 50 shots at the three men, who were unarmed.

“It truly was a fairy tale that just, you know, ended really tragically,” Nicole Paultre Bell, Bell’s fiancé, said.

Paultre Bell said she later learned how many shots had been fired.

“There was no reason for 50 shots,” she said.

Instead of going to a church to say “I do,” Paultre Bell went to the hospital.

In an interview with NY1, she said she walked out of the emergency room after identifying Bell’s body and was met by news reporters.

“I was in shock,” she said. “But I knew right then that this was something that was way bigger than us.”

Three of the five officers involved were indicted. After a non-jury trial, all three were acquitted of all charges.

“This verdict is one round down, but the fight is far from over,” the Rev. Al Sharpton said after the verdict.

At the time, Paultre Bell was advocating for Bell while raising their two young daughters.

“Sean never got justice,” she said. “Justice would have been him being at every birthday party for Jada and Jordan, and every graduation, and the Sweet Sixteens, and being here to talk them through their disappointments and help them get back up. That would have been justice for Sean.”

Paultre Bell has continued to tell Bell’s story. Her story is now the subject of the 2025 film “Aftershock.”

Twenty years later, Bell’s name remains part of New York City’s conversation about police accountability.

“Sean’s case made everyone, you know, or made our elected officials look at the way policing is done, and realize that a lot needs to be changed,” Paultre Bell said.
