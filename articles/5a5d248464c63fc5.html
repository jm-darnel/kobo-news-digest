Bronx: Healthfirst And SBH Bring Diabetes Education To The Community

Harlem World · November 11, 2026

Healthfirst and SBH Health System brought diabetes education into focus on Friday, November 6, hosting a community workshop at the SBH Health & Wellness Center in recognition of National Diabetes Awareness Month.

A Community Health Challenge Close To Home

Blood sugar management presents another challenge. Separate data from the city’s 2022 A1C Registry showed that 16.5% of Bronx adults with diabetes represented in the registry had a latest A1C result above 9%, compared with 13.9% citywide. A1C tests measure average blood sugar levels over roughly three months; the city’s report classifies results above 9% as very high.

Against that backdrop, the workshop connected clinical expertise with activities designed to make health information easier to understand and apply.

Bringing Clinical Expertise Into The Community

Dr. Alen Sajan, director of the Division of Endocrinology at SBH Health System, delivered a presentation as part of a program that included discussions about diabetes screenings and medication management. Healthfirst health educators also hosted breakout sessions on making more nutritious food choices.

“Diabetes is one of the most important public health issues facing New Yorkers,” said Dr. Paul Amajor, director of Strategic Health Initiatives at Healthfirst. “At Healthfirst, we are focused on connecting our members with quality care and practical resources to help prevent and manage diabetes. Events like the Diabetes Awareness Month Workshop with SBH Health System bring together clinical expertise and health education to give New Yorkers information and tools they can use in their everyday lives to support their health and well-being.”

From Beverage Labels To The Teaching Kitchen

Healthfirst’s interactive “Show Me the Sugar” activity revealed the amounts of added sugar in common beverages. A healthy cooking demonstration from the SBH Health System Culinary Medicine & Teaching Kitchen brought nutrition education into a practical setting.

Together, the activities offered several ways to engage with the day’s central themes: understanding diabetes, navigating treatment, and incorporating healthier habits into daily life.

Supporting Health Beyond The Doctor’s Office

For Sajan, the event reflected an approach that brings medical care together with support for everyday living.

“Managing diabetes takes more than medication and regular doctor visits. Healthy eating, physical activity, and education are just as important in helping people manage their diabetes and prevent complications,” he said. “At the SBH Health and Wellness Center, we bring medical care together with access to healthy food, nutrition education, and exercise programs to help patients make lasting changes in their health. We’re grateful for our collaboration with Healthfirst and the opportunity to work together to help our Bronx community live healthier lives.”

Healthfirst regularly organizes interactive workshops to reach New York City communities with higher rates of diabetes, combining expert-led forums, healthy living activities, and access to preventive screenings.
