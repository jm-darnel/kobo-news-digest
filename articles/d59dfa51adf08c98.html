No bench, no problem: Things to do at Moynihan Train Hall

By Samantha Max · Gothamist · November 25, 2026

Hordes of New Yorkers and out-of-towners are traveling by train over the Thanksgiving holiday amid ongoing air travel headaches, including a rare nationwide ground stop earlier this month.

Thanksgiving is Amtrak's busiest travel season, with more than 380,000 people using the national railroad to travel through Moynihan Train Hall and Penn Station last year, according to a spokesperson. There’s so much demand for train travel to and from New York City over Thanksgiving that Amtrak said it has added extra trips between Penn Station and Washington D.C.

Moynihan Train Hall, which opened Jan. 1, 2021, offers an alternative to Amtrak’s notoriously dingy areas of Penn Station across Eighth Avenue. It’s also a place for Long Island Rail Road riders to board. (NJ Transit doesn't have entrances at the newer station, which means Garden State train commuters are stuck at old Penn.)

With so many people passing through Moynihan and Penn, Amtrak spokesperson Jason Abrams recommends that riders arrive 30-45 minutes before their scheduled departures.

Buy a gift

If you need a gift, you’ve got options.

You can buy a bouquet, a greeting card, a notebook or skincare products. There are also plenty of options for sweet treats, including Magnolia Bakery, known for its banana pudding and its cupcakes, which Carrie and Miranda famously devoured on an episode of Sex and the City.

Grab a bite

There’s basically a full-on food court in the train station. Several local restaurants have stalls inside the train hall, including Jacob’s Pickles and Pastrami Queen. You can also nosh on a salad, sushi, pizza, ramen or a classic H&H bagel. There are even more food stalls on the lower level.

For travelers who need a caffeine fix, there are multiple coffee shops and a Cha Cha Matcha. Or if you’re looking for a different kind of drink, you can sip on a beer at Threes Brewing.

Take a seat (if you have a ticket)

While there are no benches on the main concourse, there are some places to sit. Amtrak and Long Island Rail Road ticket holders can rest their legs in waiting rooms, where there are lightly padded seats and a smattering of outlets to charge your devices.

Amtrak has a lounge on the mezzanine level, but it’s only open to select riders, including those with first-class tickets. You can also buy a day pass for access to the lounge if you’re willing to shell out $50.

If you need to get some work done or want to pass the time with your favorite TV show, you can connect to free Wi-Fi throughout Moynihan Train Hall.

Hit the restroom

If you need to go before you go, you can find public restrooms in the concourse and on the first floor of the station. There are also bathrooms in the ticketed waiting room and the Amtrak lounge.

Beware that the restrooms do occasionally close for cleaning, which can put riders in a pinch if time is running out before boarding. But if you don’t make it before your train arrives, don’t sweat. There are also bathrooms on the train.

Anxiously await your track assignment

For Amtrak customers, boarding is often the most chaotic part of a trip to the train hall. As soon as a track assignment is announced — and sometimes even before — travelers swarm.

Lining up early can help to secure a better spot on a sold-out train, especially if you’re hoping to sit with someone else or have a lot of bags to lug around. But track assignments aren’t announced until moments before departure, making it tricky to find out where to go before a massive queue has already formed.

There are a few clues to look for if you want to get a good spot in line. If you see train staff setting up retractable belts to form a line, it might be a good cue to check if they’re preparing for your train to board. If you see a small line already starting to form, you can ask and see if the other commuters are heading toward the same destination — with the caveat that sometimes people line up without actually knowing if they’re in the right place.

There are boards around the station that list the status of upcoming trains and track numbers once they are available. As your departure time approaches, keep an eye on those boards. You may also be able to catch an announcement in the ticketed waiting area.

Abrams, the Amtrak spokesperson, recommended that travelers download the Amtrak app and follow the railroad operator on social media for updates on their trip, including gate assignments.

Need help? Find a red cap

Amtrak employees in signature red caps are available to help people navigate the station and board the train. The Red Cap service offers assistance for people with disabilities, seniors and big groups, as well as solo commuters. Red Cap employees can also help to carry luggage and load it onto the train.

Red Cap service is free, but tips are welcome, according to Amtrak’s website.
