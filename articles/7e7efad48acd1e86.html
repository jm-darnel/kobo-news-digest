NYC, state sue Trump administration over ‘mixed-status’ federal housing assistance rule

By Ramsey Khalifeh · Gothamist · November 23, 2026

New York Attorney General Letitia James and the city of New York sued the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development Monday over a new rule that would strip federal housing assistance for families with mixed immigration status.

James said the new rule means that HUD will require people in federal housing programs to prove their citizenship through an “unreliable” process or even risk eviction.

“Everyone deserves a safe place to call home. Denying a child that security because who they live with is cruel and indefensible,” James said in a statement. “With our country already in the throes of a housing crisis, it is outrageous that the federal government would push even more families out of their homes. This rule runs counter to our nation’s values and the very purpose of these housing programs.”

And as a result of removing people from their homes supported by federal assistance, the lawsuit argued the new rule would increase homelessness and shift much of these costs to house people to local governments.

In a statement, HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the rule change “ends the exploitation of taxpayer dollars.”

“This rule guarantees only American citizens and certain eligible noncitizens will reside in HUD-funded housing. This change represents a reallocation of up to $218 million which will now go to eligible Americans that are currently on a waitlist,” Turner said.

James also said the rule would make it harder for millions of U.S. citizens to keep the housing they already qualify for because they’d have to provide their Social Security number and authorize a citizenship status check through a database.
