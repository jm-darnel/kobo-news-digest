Judge upholds NYC rent freeze, marking win for Mamdani and his affordability agenda

By David Brand · Gothamist · November 25, 2026

A Manhattan judge has upheld a city board’s decision to enact a two-year rent freeze for roughly 1 million New York City apartments after landlords sued to reverse the first-of-its-kind directive.

In an order Wednesday, Judge Brendan Lantry rejected the bid to block the rent freeze for stabilized apartments approved by the city’s Rent Guidelines Board in June. The pause in rent increases fulfills Mayor Zohran Mamdani's campaign pledges. Lantry wrote that although the freeze “may have detrimental impacts on the New York City residential real estate industry,” the court lacked the authority to overturn the decision.

A group of landlords represented by Randy Mastro — a former first deputy mayor under previous Mayor Eric Adams — argued that the board conducted a “sham” process that ignored owners’ rising expenses to deliver on one of Mamdani’s signature campaign pledges. Mastro asked a judge to order the board to hold a new vote.

Mamdani and city lawyers denied any interference in the process and countered that the board arrived at its decision independently.

Mamdani hailed the ruling in a written statement Wednesday.

“This is a victory for tenants, for affordability and for the principle that working New Yorkers deserve a city they can afford to live in,” he said.

Mastro said he and his clients are “considering next steps” regarding a potential appeal.

“Despite the economic realities and uncontroverted factual record that this unprecedented rent freeze will have devastating consequences for small landlords, the judge deferred to the RGB’s ultimate decision,” he added in a written statement.

Small Property Owners of New York Board President Ann Korchak said the decision was “terribly disappointing” and would have financial consequences for the landlords her group represents.

“The rent freeze will push more small rent-stabilized property owners to the edge of the cliff,” she said. “It denies the resources they need to maintain and operate their rent-stabilized buildings.”

In contrast, Legal Aid Society attorney Ellen Davidson, whose organization intervened on behalf of tenants, said she was pleased with the ruling but not surprised.

“The law is clear that courts do not have the power to override the decisions of the Rent Guidelines Board,” she said.

Mamdani named six board members, but stopped publicly calling for the rent freeze after taking office in January.

To find evidence that City Hall swayed the board’s decision, Mastro requested communications between top city officials, including Mamdani, and board members and staff. Lantry granted the request and ordered officials to turn over emails and texts.

Mastro released roughly a dozen of those exchanges. They largely showed City Hall officials informing new members of their appointments, coordinating meeting dates, and planning a presentation on a new “true cost of living” report the board had not previously considered.

Mastro said the materials were “shocking” in a letter to the judge. Top city lawyer Steven Banks said there was nothing “improper” about the communications.

At an unrelated news conference Oct. 1, Mamdani sought to turn the tables on Mastro and cited Gothamist reporting that revealed how Mastro himself tried to influence the board’s vote during his time as top deputy to Adams in 2025.

Former Rent Guidelines Board Chair Doug Apple told Gothamist that Mastro had spoken with him by phone last year and “advocated for a low vote” when the board was considering an increase up to 4.75%.

Mastro confirmed that he had spoken with Apple, but only to say how he and Adams “disagreed” with the board’s eventual decision to raise rent by 3% that year.

Even as Lantry kept the board’s decision in place, he criticized its potential impact and Mamdani for his campaign pledge.

“This court would be remiss not to state that such comments, promises and guarantees to freeze the rent were reckless and irresponsible,” he wrote.

But he said he found no clear evidence of meddling or of a “sham process” after Mamdani took office, even if landlords suing to reverse the decision disagreed with the mayor and his picks for the board.

“Elections have consequences,” Lantry wrote. “A premise that is the backbone of our democracy.”
