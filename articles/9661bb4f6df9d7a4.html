Incoming NY Rep. Constantino isn’t looking to make friends. What are Republicans to do?

By Jon Campbell · Gothamist · November 19, 2026

This column originally appeared in The Politics Brief, our weekly newsletter on the people, power and policies that shape New Yorkers' lives.

Anthony Constantino has an — ahem — unusual approach to politics.

You might’ve seen his rap video baselessly accusing New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani of associating with terrorists, or his full album honoring President Donald Trump. Perhaps you saw a clip of his election night victory speech, where he cursed at “evil” reporters and called for Diddy’s release. Or maybe you keep up with his very active X account, where he’s settling scores with GOP leaders who failed to back him in a contentious primary and trying to convince Nvidia to reinstate its embattled ex-CEO.

Now, Constantino is trying to put his mark on the state Republican Party before officially joining New York’s congressional delegation in January. It’s left Republican leaders grappling with the same question: What do we do with this guy?

Liz Joy, the Republican Party’s regional chair for New York’s North Country, has clashed with Constantino for more than a year. The feud has spilled out into the open since the election, with Constantino calling her a “stone cold loser and quite evil” and Joy saying Constantino is "mentally unfit for office."

Joy said Republican leaders have already been discussing potential opponents for Constantino in a 2028 Republican primary.

”The conversations about a primary were already taking place before the election,” said Joy.

Constantino didn’t have the backing of the state Republican Party and most GOP leaders in the 21st District during his bruising primary battle with state Assemblymember Robert Smullen in June. But he did have the support of a far more influential Republican in his red-leaning district: Trump, whose endorsement helped propel Constantino to a primary victory.

Constantino was able to survive the blue wave that overtook much of the country — but just barely. The latest count had Constantino up by 1.6 percentage points over Democrat Blake Gendebien. Stefanik won the same district by 24 points two years ago.

“I think that the election results in NY-21 clearly show that the Republican Party has not embraced Anthony Constantino's style of erratic and bombastic behavior and language,” Joy said.

On X, Constantino said he “banned [Joy] from my race” and won. “All Party Bosses who elevated her should be ashamed of themselves,” he wrote.

Other Republicans say Constantino’s win in the midst of a blue wave is reason enough to give him a seat at the table as the party tries to rebuild.

Former Rep. Marc Molinaro, a Republican who previously ran for governor and won a seat in the state Assembly this year, said voters “desperately want authenticity.”

“[Constantino] does speak to a segment of voters — by the way, not all Republicans — who want people to take on the elites, who want candidates who speak and talk and feel the way they do,” Molinaro said. "And is that the orthodox, traditional candidate for office? No. But there are lessons to be learned there.”

Constantino’s camp didn’t respond to requests for comment. But the congressmember-elect has been outspoken on social media about Republican leaders — including state GOP Chair Ed Cox, who’s led the state party since 2023 and, before that, from 2009 to 2019.

After Constantino declared victory on election night, he took to X at 3:15 a.m. to call Cox “dumb as rocks.”

“I am with the people and insist we rebuild the Party,” Constantino posted. “Fire Ed Cox!”

Cox hasn’t addressed Constantino’s criticism directly, at least in public. But he issued a statement following Election Day calling on the party to “regroup,” saying that now is “not the time for accusations or a circular firing squad.”

Last week, the party held a virtual meeting with county chairs across the state to shift the focus to the 2027 elections — when Republicans are bracing for a possible ballot measure that, if approved, could clear the way for Democrats in Albany to redraw the state’s congressional map.

On Wednesday, Constantino again took to X to call out “haters” who have underestimated him and are now “doubting if I’ll perform in Congress for my district and get things done.”

“What will it take for my haters to get tired of being wrong and looking stupid?”
