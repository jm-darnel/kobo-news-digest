Mamdani strikes deal to cap Citi Bike member fares, freeze prices through 2029

By Spectrum News Staff · NY1 · November 23, 2026

New York City has struck a deal with Citi Bike parent company Lyft to cap e-bike fares for members and freeze key prices through the spring of 2029.

Starting Jan. 19 of next year, e-bike rides of up to 45 minutes will be capped at $3.65 for Citi Bike members, including those in the Reduced Fare Bike Share program, Mayor Zohran Mamdani's office said in a release.

"Shorter rides that currently cost less than $3.65 will remain at their current price. The cap will save riders up to $8.50 each trip, reducing maximum fares by more than two-thirds and giving riders up to 31 minutes of riding at no extra cost," the release said.

Citi Bike's annual membership fee, its member per-minute fee and its member overage fees will remain at current rates through May 26, 2029, according to the release.

"Getting around New York City shouldn't come with a price tag that keeps climbing by the minute," Mamdani said in a statement. "For too many working New Yorkers, even a bike ride across the borough can become another expense to worry about."

Citi Bike members currently pay about 27 cents per minute for e-bike rides, or $12.15 for a 45-minute ride, except for rides entering or leaving Manhattan, which are capped at $5.40.

In exchange for the lower cap, Lyft is taking away the 60 free e-bike minutes that it had been giving members each year as a part of their membership.
