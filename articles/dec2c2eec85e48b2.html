The Studio Museum In Harlem’s Thelma Golden To Hand Off Leadership In 2027

Harlem World · November 24, 2026

Kathryn C. Chenault, Chair of the Board of Trustees of the Studio Museum in Harlem, today announced that with profound admiration for Thelma Golden, Ford Foundation Director and Chief Curator, and gratitude for her unparalleled achievements, the Board has accepted her plan to hand off leadership of the Museum in late 2027.

Golden informed the Board of her decision one year after marking her 20th anniversary as the institution’s Director and Chief Curator and celebrating the opening of the historic project she spearheaded, the Museum’s first purpose-built home. Chenault announced that the Board has formed a search committee to identify a successor to Golden, retaining an international executive search firm to assist in the effort. Golden has agreed to continue leading the Museum until a successor is hired, and she will assist in ensuring a smooth transition. Kathryn C. Chenault said, “For more than twenty years, Thelma Golden has defined the vision and identity of the Studio Museum in Harlem. She has elevated and strengthened this institution in every way, helped move Black artists into the center of the cultural landscape, and mentored countless Black museum professionals across the country. She is now passing on the leadership of the museum she helped build, entrusting it to a new generation she helped foster.”

Thelma Golden said, “I know this is the right moment to begin this transition—and as I look ahead to new projects and opportunities, I also look back with gratitude toward the many people whose achievements have been my bedrock. I think of my teachers, mentors, and professional colleagues; the long line of Directors, Trustees, and donors who established the Studio Museum and made it thrive; half a century’s worth of endlessly dedicated staff members, audiences, and beloved Harlem neighbors; and above all the generations of artists who have enlightened us. Together, they are an expansive community I cherish that will continue to inspire and enable the next leader of the Studio Museum.” Now celebrating its 58th year, the Studio Museum in Harlem is realizing possibilities that were enabled by the sweeping changes set off during Golden’s tenure, both at the institution and in the larger culture. The creation of a permanent new home, designed from the ground up to serve the institution’s mission and programs, has confirmed the rise in status and influence of the Studio Museum, and of the nation’s many other culturally specific institutions and mid-size museums. The heightened prominence of Black artists, as witnessed throughout the art world and reflected in the Studio Museum’s dramatically expanded collection, testifies to an aesthetic, intellectual, and political transformation that is ongoing. The nature of institutional leadership has changed as well, as seen in Golden’s far-reaching success in seeding a network of Black museum professionals throughout the country and elevating the work of younger curators, who are responsible for four exhibitions currently on view at the Studio Museum. As the transition at the Studio Museum moves ahead, Golden’s successor will be able to imagine new models for leading the institution. The next director will have the potential to carry forward the equitable new relationships that have been achieved with collegial institutions, elected officials, and the philanthropic community and will contribute to, and benefit from, a cultural landscape in which Black artists and professionals are now recognized as central figures. While being rooted more powerfully than had been imaginable when it was founded in 1968, the Studio Museum will continue to be an agent of change when it soon marks its 60th anniversary, empowered in its work by still-growing resources and guided by the dynamism of new executive leadership.

Thelma Golden

Born and raised in New York City, Thelma Golden began her career as a high-school apprentice at the Metropolitan Museum of Art. She began working at the Studio Museum in Harlem as an intern while studying art history and African American studies as an undergraduate at Smith College, and after graduation she continued at the Studio Museum as a curatorial fellow. In 1988, she joined the Whitney Museum of American Art as a curatorial assistant. After a brief leave to work for Dr. Kellie Jones, the Visual Arts Director at the Jamaica Arts Center, Golden returned to the Whitney in 1991 as Director of the Whitney Museum of American Art at Philip Morris and was appointed Associate Curator in 1993. In her curatorial positions there, she organized numerous innovative exhibitions, including the groundbreaking 1993 Whitney Biennial (with Elisabeth Sussman) and her landmark 1994 exhibition Black Male: Representations of Masculinity in American Art. In 2000, Golden returned to the Studio Museum as Deputy Director for Exhibitions and Programs, working closely with the Director, Dr. Lowery Stokes Sims. She was named Director and Chief Curator in 2005 when Dr. Sims stepped down as Director. Under Golden’s leadership, the Museum has gained increased renown as a global leader in contemporary art exhibitions, a center for innovative education, and a cultural anchor in the Harlem community. Golden’s curation at the Studio Museum includes many exhibitions, including the epoch-making 2001 Freestyle (curated with Christine Y. Kim), the first in the series of signature group exhibitions highlighting emerging Black artists that have become known as the “F” shows. Other exhibitions have included Black Romantic: The Figurative Impulse in Contemporary African-American Art (2002), Chris Ofili: Afro Muses 1995–2005 (2005), and many more. Publications of the Studio Museum undertaken under her leadership, including exhibition catalogues, are recognized as defining histories of the period and its artists. During its 50th anniversary year, 2018, the Studio Museum began site preparation to build the new home that had long been envisioned by the institution’s Trustees and leaders, from the late Nancy Lane through Raymond J. McGuire, now Chair Emeritus of the Board of Trustees. To maintain presence and community engagement while the Studio Museum was closed for construction, Ms. Golden launched a range of initiatives, including the inHarlem collaborations with neighborhood cultural and civic organizations and a multi-year collaborative partnership with the Museum of Modern Art and MoMA PS1.

Exhibitions that Golden organized at MoMA included the first solo US museum exhibition of Michael Armitage (2019), a critical reintroduction to the photography of Ming Smith (2023), and the first solo exhibition in the US of the work of Tadáskía (2024). The annual exhibition for the Artist-in-Residence program—a signature component of the Studio Museum—was held at MoMA PS1, keeping the artists in residence in the public eye. In 2023, Golden played an instrumental role in the Frick Collection’s unprecedented exhibition of portrait paintings by Barkley L. Hendricks, shown side-by-side with iconic, historic portraits from its own holdings. In 2023, Golden also served on the International Jury for the Venice Architecture Biennale. When the new Studio Museum in Harlem opened to the public on November 15, 2025, it did so with a collection that had been greatly expanded under Golden’s leadership through gifts and purchases, including acquisitions from artists, and the landmark Peggy Cooper Cafritz bequest. The museum’s Creating Space campaign had raised more than $307 million by the opening of the Studio Museum’s new home last year, in an effort that to date has secured a total of $322 million and increased the endowment from $5 million when the building project was approved by the Board to the current $95 million. Thelma Golden holds a BA degree from Smith College. She has received honorary doctorates from the New School (2022), Columbia University (2018), Barnard College (2010), the City College of New York (2009), San Francisco Art Institute (2008), and Smith College (2004). In 2010, she received the Audrey Irmas Award for Curatorial Excellence from the Center for Curatorial Studies at Bard College. In 2018, she was the recipient of the J. Paul Getty Medal. In 2023, she became the first arts administrator to be awarded the Dorothy and Lillian Gish Prize. Golden was named a Henry Crown Fellow in 2008 and a Ford Foundation Fellow in 2015. President Obama appointed her to the Committee for the Preservation of the White House, on which she served from 2010 to 2016. She serves on the Board of Directors for the Barack Obama Foundation, the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, and the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art. She is a member of the Advisory Committee for the Goldman Sachs “One Million Black Women” initiative and the Advisory Board for the Black Trustee Alliance for Art.

Founded in 1968 by a diverse group of artists, community activists, and philanthropists, the Studio Museum in Harlem is internationally known for its catalytic role in promoting the work of artists of African descent. The Studio Museum’s new home, situated at its longtime location on West 125th Street, opened to the public on November 15, 2025. Designed by Adjaye Associates, with Corgan (formerly Cooper Robertson) serving as executive architect, the building—the first created expressly for the institution’s program—enables the Studio Museum to better serve a growing and diverse audience, provide additional educational opportunities for people of all ages, expand its program of world-renowned exhibitions, effectively display its singular collection, and strengthen its trailblazing Artist-in-Residence program.
