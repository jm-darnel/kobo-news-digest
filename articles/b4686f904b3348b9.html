New York leaders call on Schumer, Jeffries to enforce 2-year SNAP cost shift delay

By Nicole Neuman · NY1 · November 18, 2026

New York lawmakers and advocates are calling on Congress to take swift action to delay federal cuts from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, commonly known as SNAP.

SNAP is the largest U.S. anti-hunger program.

“Yet despite this affordability crisis, in July of 2025, Congress cut $187 billion from SNAP,” said Andrés Vives, CEO of Hunger Solutions New York. “It’s the largest cut to SNAP in American history.”

Vives and other leaders say these changes will put a $1.2 billion financial burden on state and local budgets in New York.

Specifically, a financial shift that began Oct. 1, 2026, changes the prior 50/50 split of SNAP administrative costs. With the change, the federal government now covers 25% while states and localities are responsible for 75%.

In October 2027, for the first time in the program’s history, most states will be required to pay a portion of benefit costs. The Center on Budget and Policy Priorities estimates that it will be between 5%-15%.

“Hunger knows no party lines,” said Democratic city of Hudson Mayor Joseph Ferris.

Ferris was among the leaders who spoke at the New York State Capitol on Wednesday and said these changes threaten to keep food off the tables of the nearly 3 million New Yorkers, including children, seniors, the disabled and veterans who depend on the program to afford groceries.

It’s after Ferris spearheaded a bipartisan letter earlier this month signed by more than 130 mayors from across New York, writing to New York congressional leaders Chuck Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries to “ensure a two-year SNAP cost-shift delay is provided to all states.”

They say there’s an “opportunity” in the bipartisan end-of-year agreement for Schumer and Jeffries to act. Congress returned for a five-week lame-duck session on Nov. 9. Congress is anticipated to reach a new funding measure since the current one expires on Dec. 11, 2026.

“Both of their careers started in that chamber around the corner from here and they have made careers of showing up for working families,” said Peter Gannon, president and CEO of the United Way of the Greater Capital Region. “We’re asking them one more time to do it again.”

After Democrats won full control of the United States Congress on Election Day, Jeffries, who was nominated by Democrats in a historic move Wednesday to become the next speaker of the House, talked about the SNAP cuts on an appearance of MSNOW’s "The Last Word" on Nov. 9.

“We certainly need to reverse the cuts to SNAP, $186 billion cut to nutritional assistance where Republicans literally rip food away from the mouths of children, seniors and veterans,” said Jeffries.

To illustrate the need, Gannon says some food banks are moving 50 million pounds of food a year. If these SNAP changes are allowed to happen and New Yorkers lose their benefits, he anticipates food banks could move up to 32 million pounds in just one month.
