City Says Pied-à-Terre Tax Will Bring in Half a Billion Dollars, Despite Fewer Bills

By Greg B. Smith · The City · November 19, 2026

Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s administration wound up billing thousands fewer homeowners for his vaunted pied-à-terre tax than they originally believed would be eligible, but he continues to insist it will raise the $500 million that he’d previously predicted.

On Sunday, the Department of Finance mailed out bills to 7,500 households — less than half the 17,000 who got notices last summer saying they were susceptible to the tax because the city believed their properties were not primary residences.

But thousands of targeted owners insisted the city got it wrong, and the administration ultimately granted exemptions to nearly 9,000 properties that were initially pegged for the tax.

Despite the big drop in the number of properties that actually got billed, the administration stuck with the $500 million revenue figure they say will help plug a budget gap.

City officials explained that the predicted revenue will materialize despite the significant drop in billed properties because those that were ultimately confirmed as second homes had higher market values.

“As Mayor Mamdani has said before, owning a second home worth over $5 million should come with a responsibility to the city that makes it valuable,” Finance Commissioner Richard Lee said in a statement, asserting that the agency “will continue to implement the pied-à-terre surcharge fairly and efficiently, ensuring that those most able to contribute do their part to build a city that works for everyone.”

Ken Girardin of the Manhattan Institute noted that the 7,500 billed properties fall short of the 10,000 homes Gov. Kathy Hochul had estimated as subject to the tax when she signed off on it in May.

“The governor’s office expected the final number of properties would land around 10,000 and this is the strongest indication yet that the city will come up short,” Girardin said, predicting that “this will make it tougher to close the FY28 budget gap next spring.”

A spokesperson for the governor, Jennifer Goodman, noted the 10,000 households eligible for the tax was merely an estimate, adding, “It’s up to the city to determine how the tax is implemented. I don’t think ‘coming up short’ is fair but I refer you to the city for specific questions here.”
