AG Letitia James Secures Conviction As Harlem Deed Thief Joseph Makhani Gets Up To 21 Years In Prison

By Ann Brown · Harlem World · November 13, 2026

By HWM

A real estate fraud scheme that left an elderly Harlem homeowner homeless and disrupted the lives of tenants has landed Joseph Makhani behind bars for up to 21 years.

Never miss new stories in Harlem

One of the most disturbing parts of the scheme involved 107 W. 118th St., a brownstone owned by Veronica Palmer, an elderly woman who purchased the property in 1985. Prosecutors said Makhani used fraudulent documents and shell companies beginning in 2012 to take control of the building without her permission.

Palmer, a former corrections officer, was forced out of her home and eventually ended up living in a homeless shelter in Brownsville. According to prosecutors, Makhani later rented out four units in the building for between $3,000 and $3,400 per month after obtaining approval to convert the property into market-rate rentals, The Real Deal reported.

Authorities said Makhani falsely reported purchasing the brownstone for $975,000 and secured a $650,000 construction loan. He later refinanced the property and obtained a $1.2 million mortgage.

Makhani was initially indicted in 2021, but that case was dismissed on a technicality. James brought a second indictment in 2023, leading to the jury conviction following a seven-week trial.

“After years of scheming and deceiving New Yorkers, Joseph Makhani is finally facing justice,” James said.
