3 high-rise towers in Astoria? 'Not the affordable housing' residents have in mind.

By Arya Sundaram · Gothamist · November 18, 2026

Attendees said they worried the new housing would be too pricey for many already living in the neighborhood – echoing concerns frequently heard in communities across the five boroughs where developers are pushing new housing developments.

"This is not the affordable housing that our community is really needing," community board member Christina Chaise said to applause from some attendees.

Based on figures from 2025, rents for those apartments would range from $1,130 to $3,370, depending on the apartment size and tenant's income. And they would be available to individuals making between 40% and 80% of the city's median income, between $45,400 and $90,700 for individuals and up to $140,000 for families, depending on the size.

But angry residents voiced concerns that even the required affordable housing units would be out of reach for working-class Astorians. The neighborhood’s median income as of 2024 was $93,460, about 11% higher than the city median income, according to the NYU Furman Center, a housing policy and research institute.

“Developments that have been pitched here and approved generally are doing more than the minimum,” community board member Frank Fredericks told the developers.

Some board members welcomed the new apartments, nothing their hope that the development could boost business in a struggling section of the commercial corridor.

“I like to see in general the residential being built in this area,” community board member Richard Khuzami said. ”It's going to help support some of the commercial. It's been a challenge to have healthy commercial down there."

But other residents voiced concerns that the new developments would raise rents for the small businesses in the area.

“The only way I can support this development is if it goes way deeper into affordable housing,” said Alfredo Hernandez, a neighborhood resident. “I also would only support it as well if it includes small business spaces with long, affordable leases. Because I’m really tired of walking around Steinway and there’s so many out-of-business stores.”

Representatives from Schuman Properties declined to comment on the criticisms of their project. Jasper Wu, of ZD Jasper Realty, said his firm has already adjusted the proposals, such as the unit count, based on feedback from the community board.

“I understand there’s a lot of strong reactions to it,” said Wu. “I understand people care deeply about the community… We’re definitely open to feedback. I’m sure there’s going to be more feedback from this. And we’ll take it and we’ll process it and we’ll respond.”
