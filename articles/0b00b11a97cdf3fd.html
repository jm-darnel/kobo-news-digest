Trump said congestion pricing would end NYC. Now, his admin wants it in more cities.

By Stephen Nessen · Gothamist · November 18, 2026

President Donald Trump once declared congestion pricing would kill New York City. Now, his administration wants to help other cities set up similar tolling programs for drivers.

Shortly after Gothamist published this article, "congestion" and "cordon" pricing programs were deleted from the Federal Highway Administration's announcement. Cordon tolls are a technical term for a toll imposed on drivers to enter a designated zone, like a central business district.

MTA Chair Janno Lieber reveled in the apparent change of heart.

“ I say they've been hacked or (Transportation Secretary) Sean Duffy's been reading the press on this subject. But either way, that's a pretty remarkable about-face,” Lieber said Wednesday. “And I think it really speaks to the fact that this program has not only survived legally, but has been such a success, in its implementation.”

Last spring, a federal judge declared the Trump administration's efforts to revoke approval for congestion pricing unlawful. The MTA successfully defended numerous other lawsuits seeking to halt the program, which charges drivers $9 during peak periods to drive in Manhattan south of 60th Street. Toll revenue is used to fund public transit.

“Gridlock is costing Americans time with their families and money from their wallets,” FHWA Administrator Sean McMaster wrote in a release. “This program brings much-needed relief to our most congested metropolitan areas. Alongside building new capacity, the program uses cutting-edge technology to maximize the efficiency of our highways.”

New York City's congestion pricing tolls have been in effect for nearly two years. The MTA reports traffic is down 15% in Manhattan since the tolls were turned on. The agency says the tolls have raised hundreds of millions of dollars used to secure billions of dollars in bonds to fund capital projects.

“It may have taken nearly two years of unquestionable results and repeated losses in court to understand, but we are glad that Secretary Duffy has seen the light," Sean Butler, a spokesperson for Gov. Kathy Hochul, said in a statement. "Congestion pricing works and we welcome any attempt to emulate New York’s huge success."

Lieber reflected on the fight to implement congestion pricing during an event Wednesday night at WNYC’s Greene Space. He said the lengthy process to get it approved under the Biden administration wound up helping the MTA protect the program from Trump's legal attacks.

“There are improvements to air quality documented, a reduction in crashes that injure pedestrians and cyclists,” Lieber said. “There's a dramatic reduction in the number of noise complaints to 311. And even the drivers have shifted in polls because they are recognizing the value of the time they're saving, especially on the crossings in, in and out of Manhattan.”
