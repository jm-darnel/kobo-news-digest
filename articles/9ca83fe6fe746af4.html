Chase software bug delays transactions as customers report issues at major banks

By Giulia Heyward · Gothamist · November 17, 2026

Thousands of customers of major banks across the country reported difficulty accessing their online banking accounts Monday.

Customers of Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank and users of payment service Zelle reported technical difficulties, according to Downdetector, which tracks service disruptions.

Users reported being unable to log into their accounts, check balances and transfer money.

JPMorganChase said a software bug affecting its internal hardware systems was delaying some transactions, but customers still had access to their accounts and funds. A spokesperson said there was no evidence of a cyberattack and that the bank was working to resolve the issue by the end of Monday.

It's the latest technology meltdown to affect the nation after flights were grounded for hours at U.S. airports Friday afternoon because of a "temporary telecommunications meltdown."

A spokesperson for U.S. Bank said its customers “are not directly impacted” but that clients may still have issues because of the outage.

“However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays,” U.S. Bank spokesperson Jeffrey D. Shelman said.

A Zelle spokesperson also directed Gothamist to other banking institutions and said its site isn't down either.

“Consumers do not have a separate Zelle account, and Zelle does not hold consumer funds,” Zelle spokesperson Eric Blankenbaker said. “An issue at the financial institutions may be impacting broader services, including Zelle payments, for some of their customers.”

Spokespeople for Bank of America and Wells Fargo did not immediately respond to Gothamist's requests for comment.

This is a developing story and may be updated.
