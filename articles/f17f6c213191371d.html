Menin And Council Announce $12 Million For Senior Home Meal Deliveries

Harlem World · November 20, 2026

Today, New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin and Council Members announced a $12 million investment for Home Delivered Meals, bolstering support for meal providers serving homebound seniors experiencing food insecurity.

“When one in five New Yorkers don’t know where their next meal might come from, we need to be tackling the food insecurity crisis from every single angle, and we must ensure our eldest New Yorkers do not get left behind,” said Speaker Julie Menin. “This $12 million increase in funding for home delivered meals adds to the already $85 million the Council invests each year to make sure every senior New Yorker who needs it can receive a meal delivered right to their door. I’m grateful to my Council colleagues who brought this critical funding to bear, to the DFTA and City Meals on Wheels for overseeing this program, and to our incredible community partners who tirelessly carry out this important work and keep our elder New Yorkers fed.”

“…lacking consistent access to adequate food…”

More than 1.7 million New York City residents suffer from food insecurity, with 20 percent of New Yorkers lacking consistent access to adequate food, and with seniors being particularly vulnerable.

In the FY27 Budget, the Council negotiated this increase in baseline funding for home delivered meals, meaning this $12 million investment is in addition to the existing $85 million baseline budget that the Council allocates for Home Delivered Meals.

Home Delivered Meals are currently available to eligible homebound individuals who are 60 and older, who have functional limitations, and who do not have other available support to assist them. Meals are provided at no cost to eligible participants and are distributed by contracted providers. Meal options include culturally competent meals such as kosher or halal meals.

Following the announcement, Council Members joined Encore Community Services staff to pack and deliver meals to older New Yorkers in need.

As the cost of living continues to rise, the New York City Council is taking an all-hands approach to tackling food insecurity. In addition to this $12 million investment in Home Delivered Meals, the Council has allocated more than $27 million in discretionary funding for Fiscal Year 2027 specifically designed for food assistance programs, supporting food pantries and benefits access.

“Hunger doesn’t take weekends off,” said Jeremy Kaplan, CEO of Encore Community Services. “We thank Speaker Menin and the City Council for this vital $12 million investment in weekend, home-delivered meals. We won’t stop advocating until every older New Yorker who needs support can count on three nutritious meals a day, seven days a week.”

“NYC Aging’s Home Delivered Meals program remains a vital resource for older New Yorkers, allowing them to remain in their homes and the communities they prefer while accessing healthy, delicious meals,” said Dr. Lisa Scott-McKenzie, Commissioner of the NYC Department for the Aging. “We are grateful for this additional funding that ensures this resource is available every day to enhance food security which reflects the administration’s continued commitment to supporting those in need. With the city’s older adult population only growing, I look forward to continuing to make progress on issues that directly impact the ability for older New Yorkers to age in place with dignity, independence and respect.”

“…strengthen the network of providers…”

“For many homebound seniors, food insecurity is an unfortunate reality, with approximately 25,000 New Yorkers relying on food delivery services and partnerships with community-based organizations to keep them from going hungry. This has been a long-standing issue that I have seen firsthand, dating back to my experience in the nonprofit sector,” said Council Member Linda Lee, Chair of the Committee on Finance. “Thanks to advocacy by the Council, $12M in funding has been baselined for the Department for the Aging’s home-delivered meals program to strengthen the network of providers New Yorkers depend on. This funding shows our commitment to organizations like Encore Community Services, which are dedicated to the work on the ground to ensure that meals are delivered for older adults in need.”

“This $12 million investment in home meal delivery is a major step forward to making a change,” said Council Member Susan Zhuang, Chair of the Committee on Aging. “The money will help expand weekend meal delivery and provide culturally appropriate meals that reflect the diverse communities our seniors call home. It’s actually a change I’ve been trying to make for a while now as the Chair of the Aging Committee. Coming from an immigrant family, I was raised to respect our elders and make sure they are cared for. And so as our senior population grows in New York, we must make sure our homebound seniors have access to meals that are culturally competent and nutritious. It is unacceptable to let the people who built our communities go hungry in 2026.”
