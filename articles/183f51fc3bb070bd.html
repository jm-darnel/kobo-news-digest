Developer at Brooklyn collapse site had record of flouting orders from city agency

By David Brand · Gothamist · November 12, 2026

Those documents show that owner Robert Fischman sent a letter to neighboring landlords in June of this year to inform them of an upcoming disturbance: his company would begin excavating the empty lot at 2625 Bedford Ave. to make way for a new apartment building. It was one of many similar projects set to rise in the rapidly gentrifying neighborhood.

Five months later, the excavation site was the scene of a fatal disaster, after one of the apartment buildings next door at 1321 Flatbush Ave. crumbled into the dirt pit below. At least eight people have been pronounced dead as a result of the collapse and many more were injured, including multiple children on a day that public school students had off for the Veterans Day holiday. Rescue workers were still digging through the rubble Thursday morning for survivors.

By the time Fischman had sent the excavation notice, the project team had already ignored commands from the Department of Buildings, according to records from the city buildings department.

Construction workers had been digging in the empty lot Wednesday before the adjacent structure collapsed, Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said at a press conference late Wednesday.

Structural plans filed by engineer Jeiming Wang of the firm LMW Engineering Group call for extensive excavation and bracing work to prevent neighboring buildings from collapsing, including steel, timber and concrete shoring. They also require the excavation contractor to use seismic monitors to track vibration and install crack gauges on surrounding buildings to monitor movements. Visual inspections were required, along with various oversight and reporting requirements under the supervision of a special inspector.

It’s unclear whether these precautions were taken. The Department of Buildings said it is still investigating what caused the collapse. Wang and the special inspector, MD Islam of Queens-based RI Consulting Services, did not respond to calls and emails on Wednesday.

Other problems plagued the three-story apartment at 1321 Flatbush Ave., which is owned by a separate landlord, prior to Wednesday's disaster.

City records show inspectors documented a raft of hazards and that the owner Olufemi Falade had a long track record of deplorable building conditions. A judge sent Falade, to jail for 12 days in 2006 for violations at a different building, including vermin and exposed wiring.

Before its collapse, the building had 98 open housing code violations for unsafe electrical wiring, inadequate water supply to the sprinkler system, a lack of smoke detectors, water outages and a bed bug infestation among other problems, according to city data.

Falade did not respond to a phone message.
