Families remember cousins killed in deadly Brooklyn building collapse

By Ron Lee · NY1 · November 16, 2026

Family members and community members gathered in Crown Heights on Sunday to remember two cousins killed in last week's deadly building collapse in Flatbush.

At a prayer service at a mosque on Utica Avenue, Mamadou Mouctar Balde received handshakes and support as he continued to grieve the loss of his cousins, who were among the eight people killed when a three-story building collapsed Wednesday.

"It's very, very sad. I'm very upset," Balde said.

He remembered his cousins, 23-year-old Mamadou Bhoye Barry and 31-year-old Mamadou Bhoye Diaye, as young men with dreams of building a better life in the United States.

"They have a lot of dreams. A better life. A lot of dreams. Go to college, have a degree and have a career in the United States," Balde said. "Unfortunately, they lost their lives while sleeping in their house."

Balde was not home when the building on Flatbush Avenue collapsed. The tragedy also affected his family in another way: His wife, who was six months pregnant, was injured.

"I'm feeling very, very sad and upset because of the tragedy that happened to my wife and me," he said.

Despite the loss, Balde said he was grateful his wife survived and appreciated the community's support.

"I'm thankful my wife is still alive, and I'm thankful to God my wife is still alive," he said.

"I'm very happy for everybody coming here and supporting us in this tragic moment."

Sunday night's prayer gathering followed a vigil held hours earlier near the collapse site, where people remembered all of the victims.

An aunt of Barry and Diaye said the cousins came to the United States with hopes of a better future.

"They left with that vision in head. And today, whatever we say here is not going to bring them back," she said. "But we stand here, because we don't want something like this to happen again."

She called for justice, accountability and transparency as the investigation into the collapse continues.

Six adults and two children were killed in Wednesday's collapse. More than a dozen other people were injured.
