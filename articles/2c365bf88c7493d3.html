Rain once again coming for NYC's idyllic fall — and maybe Thanksgiving, too

By Ryan Kost · Gothamist · November 21, 2026

New York City’s generational run of perfect fall days is about to wash out — and forecasters are already sizing up a second storm that could complicate Thanksgiving plans.

Sunday afternoon is set to get soggy when a low-pressure system moving up from the Southeast coast drops showers across the region, said Dominic Ramunni, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service's New York office. The rain will come and go throughout Sunday night and may even stick around for the Monday morning commute before things dry out.

Most of the New York City area will see about an inch or two of rain depending on how the storm tracks, Ramunni said, a significant amount for a short period.

“If it deviates just a little bit further east, the city itself could actually escape some of the steadier rain,” he said.

Once the storm has passed, temperatures are expected to drop from this weekend’s unseasonably warm weather to a more typical range in the 40s, and they’ll stay there throughout the week.

This Thanksgiving could be a blustery one. Ramunni said another coastal storm could swing north toward the region, bringing rain and gusty winds just as New Yorkers are getting their turkeys in the oven.

Still, that’s days away and Ramunni emphasized the forecast this far out is likely to change.

There’s “a lot of uncertainty as we go out that far at this point with a lot of different solutions still on the table,” he said. “But I would encourage anyone who is traveling anytime around Thursday to continue to monitor the forecast.”

Wind, of course, could be an issue for the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, which starts at 8:30 a.m. Thursday.

Under city guidelines, balloons cannot fly if winds consistently exceed 23 miles per hour or if gusts are stronger than 34 miles per hour. This dates back to an unfortunate “Cat in the Hat”-related incident in 1997, when wind blew the cat and his stove-pipe hat into a lampost on Central Park West. Part of the post fell into the crowd, and two people were hospitalized with head injuries.
