Goodbye Boxing: The Sweet Science is perhaps taking its last gasp

By Tony Paige · Amsterdam News · November 26, 2026

I was covering a fight card at the old Felt Forum in Madison Square Garden when a PR guy came up to me and asked if I’d like to meet the President.

As the head of the Boxing Writers Association of America, I was honored. I didn’t think President Clinton was in town or knew who I was. When I asked if the Secret Service had to vet me, he said, “No. The President is right here.”

I turned and this huge, hulking Black man stood in front of me. The PR guy said, “Say Hello to ‘The President,’ Ike Ibeabuchi.” He was the rising undefeated heavyweight contender with knockout power in both hands. Yet when I shook his hands and looked into his eyes, I saw nothing.

A blank stare. I hadn’t seen a stare that blank since Lou Duva introduced me to his latest find — Tony Ayala Jr. — at ringside also at the Garden. Now, Ibeabuchi, at age 53 and after a lengthy prison stay, was going to face former heavyweight champ Oliver “The Atomic Bull” McCall. He’s only 61.

So, what happened? The fight was cancelled.

Surprised?

This is where I take my leave from the sport. I will always be concerned for boxers after they retire, but enough is enough. Take the heavyweight clash between former champs Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua scheduled for Dec. 11 in Cardiff, Wales. This fight should have been made years ago, but promoters always drag their feet getting good fights made.

Promoters shouldn’t have taken this long to make the All-Brit battle without tripping over themselves. This contest was just a 12-round fight until the WBA deemed it a “Super” fight which warranted sanctioning fees.

Then there’s Zuffa Boxing, run by Dana White, he of the UFC, trying to “fix” the sport. He kept insisting that he was a promoter of Fury-Joshua until he wasn’t. Don’t forget Riyadh Season, out of Saudi Arabia, promoter of Canelo Álvarez’s recent wipeout against Christian Mbilli on Halloween.

Saudi money man Turki Alalshikh put that fight and Fury-Joshua together. He’s the main man in boxing until he wasn’t. He announced that he’s not long for the sport and, due to health reasons, is moving on.

But not before he sold Ring Magazine, which he bought from Oscar De La Hoya two years ago, to DAZN, the streaming service. The streaming service? And if that isn’t enough, here comes Bare Knuckle Boxing. Now you have all these past-their-prime fighters moving up to a more violent form of the sport.

I will keep my eye on what’s left of the Sweet Science, but I’m not covering any more “fights.” Besides, the AmNews has a fine boxing and combat sports writer in Derrel Johnson. I used to enjoy the sport, the competition, the characters, but now I’ll say ‘Goodbye.’

Hope the younger generation can fix what’s definitely broken.

PS: And when is the Mayweather-Pacquiao rematch?
