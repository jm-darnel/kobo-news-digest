Hudson Valley duck farm suing over NYC foie gras ban destroyed in fire

By Walter Wuthmann · Gothamist · November 19, 2026

A Hudson Valley duck farm at the center of a high-profile lawsuit challenging New York City’s foie gras ban was destroyed in a fire early Thursday morning, according to Sullivan County fire officials.

Hudson Valley Foie Gras is one of only two remaining foie gras farms in the entire country, alongside La Belle Farms, which is also in Sullivan County. The two farms are asking the New York Court of Appeals to overturn the city’s ban on the fatty delicacy made from the livers of force-fed ducks and geese.

County officials said they received a 911 call from Hudson Valley Foie Gras’ facility in Bethel at 1:10 a.m. Thursday. By the time firefighters from the Smallwood-Mongaup Valley Fire Department arrived on scene the largest building was already fully engulfed, with the fire spreading rapidly through other wood-framed structures.

“The fire took off so fast, there was no way to get inside,” said Smallwood-Mongaup Valley Fire Chief Charlie Kalinowski. More than two dozen nearby fire departments responded to the blaze.

Kalinowski said most of the buildings at the Hudson Valley Foie Gras property were completely destroyed. Fire officials said “some” of the ducks and geese were rescued, including hundreds of hatchlings, but others were killed by the flames.

It’s not clear how many ducks and geese were on site. Hudson Valley Foie Gras Manager Marcus Henley told a Gothamist reporter at a previous visit to the farm’s nearby Ferndale facility that they process about 6,000 ducks a week. Reached by phone Thursday, Henley declined an interview but said no employees or first responders were injured.

Henley told Radio Catskill Thursday that the fire is suspected to have started due to an electrical malfunction. The Sullivan County Fire Investigation Team said its investigation is ongoing and no cause has been determined.

Henley’s farm is at the center of an ongoing legal battle pitting the New York City Council, which banned the sale of foie gras in 2019, against the farms and the New York Department of Agriculture, which argue the local measure violates state agricultural policy. Animal rights activists say the process of making foie gras amounts to animal cruelty.

Radio Catskill’s Kimberly Izar contributed to this report.
