Bronx grand jury to weigh charges against teen in fatal shooting of 15-year-old

By Samantha Max · Gothamist · November 12, 2026

A Bronx grand jury will consider Monday whether to indict a teen arrested in connection with the killing of 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, who was shot on his way to school last month, according to the district attorney’s office.

Prosecutors said Thierno Jalloh, 17, and two others were involved in Javits Frimpong's killing. Javits Frimpong was shot in the stomach as he walked to DreamYard Preparatory School around 9:30 a.m. on Oct. 29. The two others are still at large, officials said.

Jalloh briefly appeared in court Thursday on murder, manslaughter and weapons charges. He is being held in detention without bail and is expected back in court Monday.

According to the criminal complaint, someone who knows Jalloh identified him in surveillance footage connected to the crime during an interview with police several days later. Prosecutors said ballistic evidence and information from witnesses also tied Jalloh and two other people to the shooting. Those people had not been arrested as of Thursday afternoon.

Bronx Defenders, which is representing Jalloh, declined to comment after the court appearance.

Family members previously told Gothamist that the 15-year-old recently moved to New York from Ghana, following his father, who immigrated here in 2000. Prominent civil rights advocates, including the Rev. Al Sharpton and attorney Ben Crump, have condemned the killing and rallied around Javits Frimpong’s family.

Dozens of supporters for Frimpong’s family filled the sixth-floor courtroom for Jalloh’s brief appearance Thursday afternoon. Outside the courthouse, they shouted, “Justice for Javits.” Someone held a framed photograph of the 15-year-old with angel wings.

Thomas Frimpong, the boy’s dad, previously described his son as quiet. He said two students fought with his son a few days before the shooting and were suspended. The school told him his son was not to blame, he said.

Crump, who is now representing Frimpong’s family, said after the court appearance Thursday that prosecutors told his clients they do not believe the shooters went to Frimpong’s school or were involved with the bullying incident a few days earlier.

“Young people being able to get access to guns and cause irreparable harm — not only to Javits’ family, but also to themselves — I mean, it is a tragedy upon tragedy that everybody loses in this situation,” the attorney said. “It has to be a teachable moment to lead to a greater discussion about stopping gun violence in our community.”

Police arrested Jalloh Wednesday. Javits Frimpong’s sister, Millicent Frimpong, told Gothamist after the arrest that she was happy a suspect had been apprehended but that it did “not really” bring her any relief.
