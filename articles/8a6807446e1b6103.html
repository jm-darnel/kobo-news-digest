NYC looks to close a 'loophole' in sanctuary protection for immigrants

By Arya Sundaram · Gothamist · November 19, 2026

A bill introduced by Councilmember Elsie Encarnación, the immigration committee chair, would explicitly prohibit city jail officials from notifying federal immigration agents of a detainee’s release unless provided with a judicial warrant.

A similar bill, also introduced by Encarnación and set for review during a Council hearing on Friday, would establish similar restrictions for the NYPD.

Under the city’s sanctuary protections, an immigrant detainee must have been convicted of a serious or violent crime, or come up as a possible match on a terrorist screening database, to warrant being turned over to federal immigration officials. The law also requires that federal officials provide a warrant from a judge.

But city jails have transferred dozens of people to federal immigration authorities in recent years, without receipt of a judicial warrant, jail officials have repeatedly testified in the City Council. From July 1, 2025, to June 30 this year, the city's Department of Correction transferred 24 people to federal immigration authorities, and 19 people during the year prior, according to agency data.

James Conroy, the Department of Correction’s chief lawyer, justified these transfers in a March Council hearing, citing another section of the city’s sanctuary laws. Those provisions under certain circumstances allow jail officials to share information with federal authorities even without a judicial warrant, he said.

Rosa Cohen-Cruz, director of immigration policy at the Bronx Defenders, who helped draft and lobby for the bills, said they are important measures for ensuring due process for immigrant detainees and preventing individuals from ending up in the custody of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, which she called a “rogue, violent, dangerous organization.”

“ We should be protecting all New Yorkers,” Cohen-Cruz said in an interview. “We should not be funneling any New Yorkers into that system, regardless of their criminal legal system contact.”

New York City agreed in December 2024 to pay up to $92.5 million to settle claims it unlawfully detained more than 20,000 undocumented immigrants beyond their scheduled release from city jails, to allow for their transfer into federal immigration custody.
