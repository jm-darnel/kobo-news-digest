NJ Gov. Sherrill announces her new lieutenant governor pick

By Mike Hayes, Michael Sol Warren · Gothamist · November 9, 2026

New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill announced Monday she has selected Shirley Emehelu, a former prosecutor currently serving as New Jersey’s acting secretary of state, as her new lieutenant governor.

“That was pretty impressive,” the governor said. “She worked around the clock.”

Sherrill said selecting a woman for the roll was not a priority for her.

“I did not go into the process thinking we had to have a certain gender in the role,” she said, adding that Emehelu, a Nigerian-American, is the first immigrant to hold the lieutenant governor position.

Emehelu said it was a “profound honor” to make history with Sherrill. She was sworn in as her family and Sherrill looked on.

Her appointment follows the September resignation of former Lieutenant Governor Dale Caldwell.

Sherrill had demanded Caldwell, her running mate in last year’s election, step down after an internal investigation determined he had sexually harassed female staff in his office and committed ethics violations.

Caldwell has challenged the investigation’s findings. He has not been charged with any crime.

State law requires that the Lieutenant Governor hold a cabinet office as well. Caldwell served as secretary of state, making him New Jersey’s top elections official.

Emehelu, who originally joined the Sherrill administration as the acting state comptroller, was tapped to become acting secretary of state on the day he resigned.

Caldwell, a Black pastor who had served as the president of Centenary University, boosted Sherrill’s standing with the Black community during her campaign. On the day he resigned, Sherrill commended Caldwell for his commitment to advancing policies aimed at improving the lives of Black New Jerseyans. Influential Black politicians and community leaders around the state publicly urged Sherrill to continue that commitment after Caldwell’s departure.

Democrats secured an 11-1 advantage in the state’s congressional delegation. The party flipped two Republican seats in New Jersey.

In North Jersey, Bennett defeated Rep. Tom Kean in the state’s 7th Congressional District. In South Jersey, Cape May Mayor Zack Mullock denied Rep. Jeff Van Drew a fifth term in congress. Sherrill and other prominent Democrats in the state hit the campaign trail for both candidates.

This story has been updated with comment from Gov. Mikie Sherrill and Lt. Gov. Shirley Emehelu.
