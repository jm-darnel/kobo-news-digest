New York State and local police out at sobriety checkpoints all Thanksgiving weekend long

By Rosemary Misdary · Gothamist · November 26, 2026

State and local law enforcement are on high alert during the long Thanksgiving holiday weekend.

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul announced a statewide increase in patrols to target drunk and reckless drivers from Thursday through Sunday, Nov. 29. According to the Governor’s office, Thanksgiving in particular has a lethal combination of increased vehicle traffic and alcohol use.

“As we gather with friends and family to celebrate Thanksgiving, I want to ensure that everyone gets where they are going safely and not have what should be happy memories marred by a preventable tragedy,” Hochul said. “I urge drivers to stay alert, stay sober and move over when you see vehicles stopped on the side of the road. If you’re in a vehicle, you should wear seat belts or use proper restraint. I thank our law enforcement for sacrificing their time with loved ones to work on Thanksgiving and throughout the holidays to help keep New York’s roadways safe.”

During the 2025 Thanksgiving weekend, police officers statewide arrested 2,236 drivers under the influence of alcohol or drugs. Over the same period last year, law enforcement issued nearly 12,500 speeding tickets and more than 3,000 tickets for seat belt violations. In total, more than 62,000 tickets were issued over the four-day holiday weekend.

“Please think twice before getting behind the wheel of a vehicle if you’ve been drinking,” said New York State Police Superintendent Steven G. James. “If you have had alcohol, there are several options available that will provide you with a safe ride to your destination.”

State officials said millions of drivers are on the road during the holiday. Police presence will increase statewide, including sobriety checkpoints and unmarked patrols to spot distracted drivers, especially drivers who are texting on the road.

To promote a safer roadway and minimize traffic congestion and delays, lane closures and construction zones for roads and bridges on state highways will be suspended until 6 a.m. Monday, Nov. 30.

All 27 rest stops along the New York State Thruway are open 24 hours a day during the holiday weekend.

Travelers can check road and traffic conditions by visiting the 511 website.
