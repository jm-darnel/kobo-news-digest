New York Ranks Third For Mental Health Spending Per Capita Nationally

Harlem World · November 16, 2026

Rank — State — Total SMHA spending — Spend per resident — Percentage difference from national average — Dollar difference from national average

A new study has revealed that New York ranked third for the amount its state mental health agency spent on mental healthcare per person.

New York ranked third for how much it spent on mental healthcare per person, with its state mental health agency (SMHA) spending a reported $470.73 per person on mental healthcare, for a total of $9.3 billion statewide. This was 150.9% or $283.08 per person above the national average, with the average per person spending at $187.65 nationwide, and totaling $63.6 billion.

Oregon comfortably had the highest reported per capita spending on mental healthcare in SAMHSA’s latest data. Oregon’s SMHA spent $729.36 per resident, 288.7% or $541.71 more than the national average of $187.65. In total, Oregon’s SMHA spent $3.1 billion in 2025.

Vermont was second, with reported mental health spending of $488.77 per person and totaling $316.9 million. Vermont spent 160.5% or $301.12 more per capita than the national average.

Pennsylvania was fourth, with reported mental health spending at $431.87 per resident and totaling $5.6 billion. This was $244.22 above the national average of $187.65.

Iowa spent a reported $401.76 per person, and a total of $1.3 billion, on mental healthcare. This was the fifth-highest spend per person, and was $214.11 more than the national average.

Louisiana was sixth, with a reported per-resident spend of $390.48, and Minnesota was seventh, with per-resident spending at a reported $390.26.

Montana, Connecticut, and Washington rounded out the top 10 states with the highest spending on mental healthcare per resident, with reported per-resident spending of $344.64, $326.91, and $324.13, respectively.

Ash Bhattacharya, CEO and Co-founder of Autism360, commented on the findings:

“It was especially impressive to see Oregon’s per-resident spending on mental healthcare. The state’s spending was nearly four times more than the national average, and this may be because the state has ranked poorly in studies that looked at mental health outcomes over the past few years. It will be interesting to see if this high spend per person helps close the gap between Oregon and states with better rankings for mental health outcomes.

“It’s also important to remember the amounts reported to SAMHSA might not cover all mental health funding in a state. For example, Michigan has the lowest reported spend per capita on mental healthcare, but this is because the state has multiple agencies and organizations responsible for mental healthcare spending, not just the state mental healthcare agency.”
