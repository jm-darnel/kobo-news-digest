Mamdani announces plan to disband NYPD’s Strategic Response Group

By David Giambusso, Walter Wuthmann · Gothamist · November 16, 2026

Mayor Zohran Mamdani said he is disbanding the heavily criticized NYPD unit that responds to protests in the city.

Mamdani announced Monday that the NYPD's Strategic Response Group would be formally disbanded and its core functions split into new and existing divisions. The mayor pledged to shut the group down during his 2025 campaign.

Mamdani said he would create a new unit specially designed to maintain peaceful public demonstrations.

“New Yorkers exercising their constitutional right to protest should not be met with the same police response as a terrorism threat,” he said in a statement. “We are establishing a new, civilian-led approach that prioritizes dialogue, engagement and de-escalation.”

Now, protest response and crowd control will be handled by a new team focused on de-escalation, civilian coordination and peaceful assembly, the mayor’s office said.

Groups like the New York Civil Liberties Union, and even one of the NYPD officials who helped create the unit, have criticized the NYPD for bringing heavily armored vehicles and weaponry to peaceful protests. Now, instead, a Patrol Support Division comprised of closely vetted and trained officers will be on hand to assist other teams. It will only be called on in dangerous situations, the mayor's office said.

“The demise of the SRG is a victory for our First Amendment rights and the advocates who worked tirelessly for this day,” said NYCLU Executive Director Donna Lieberman.

Lieberman urged the City Council also to pass the Communities United to Reject Brutality Act, which closely mirrors the mayor’s executive action, so that future administrations couldn’t reverse course due to “shifting political winds."

City officials announced that some of the practices that have drawn the most criticism from protesters and civil libertarians will no longer be allowed. The mayor’s office and the NYPD will formalize a ban on kettling, or cordoning off protesters, and prohibit the use of long guns, pepper spray and tear gas during “First Amendment activities.”

“ These all seem like reasonable steps to take,” said Liz Glazer, founder of the policy journal Vital City and former head of the Mayor’s Office of Criminal Justice during the de Blasio administration.

Glazer noted that many of the new civilian functions would be housed under the Office of Community Safety, which Mamdani created in March. “ So just a question mark about how it’s all going to work together and how they stand up a unit like that in a department that is itself is trying to create its own mechanisms and its own mission right now,” she said.

Police union officials criticized the mayor’s plan.

“Today is not a good day for New Yorkers who just want a safe and orderly city,” Police Benevolent Association President Patrick Hendry said in a statement. “Mayor Mamdani is showing his true colors: he’s willing to put your safety and police officers’ safety at risk to appease his radical base.”

Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch said in a statement that she endorsed the changes.

“I believe strongly that the NYPD must always be willing to examine how we operate and make improvements,” she said. “I would never support changes that diminish the NYPD's ability to protect New Yorkers, or that put our officers in a position where they lack the resources necessary to confront a serious threat.”
