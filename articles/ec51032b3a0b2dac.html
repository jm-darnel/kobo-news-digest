Ninth Detainee Dies on Rikers Island So Far This Year

By Reuven Blau · The City · November 10, 2026

A 36-year-old Rikers Island detainee awaiting trial on an attempted murder charge was found dead in his bed Tuesday morning, marking the ninth death in city jail custody so far this year, The City Reporter has learned.

Cesar Gabriel Medero Curet was arrested March 7 after allegedly punching a stranger in the face and ear at a subway station and trying to throw him onto the tracks in front of an incoming train, according to the NYPD.

He was found inside the Robert N. Davoren Center and pronounced dead at 6:03 a.m., one of the 10 jails on Rikers Island, according to the Department of Correction.

Nine people have now died in city jail custody this year, following 15 deaths in 2025, five in 2024, nine in 2023 and 19 in 2022.

Correction Commissioner Stanley Richards said the department was mourning the loss of Medero Curet and would investigate the circumstances surrounding his death with city and state authorities.

“The safety and well-being of everyone who lives and works with us will always be our highest priority,” Richards said in a statement. “This profound loss weighs heavily on us all. We extend our sincere condolences to his family and loved ones.”

“Mr. Medero Curet’s death is yet another devastating reminder of the human cost of the City’s dysfunctional jail system,” the organization said, renewing its call to close Rikers Island.

Richards has also talked about restricting physical mail in an effort to reduce drug soaked letters from coming into the system. A similar proposal failed to gain approval from the city’s oversight Board of Correction in 2023.

All deaths in custody are reviewed by the board and several other government agencies.

In September, the board issued a report into four recent deaths citing familiar breakdowns: correction officers failing to conduct meaningful tours of housing areas, inaccurate handwritten logbook entries, officers leaving their posts, and detainees gathering in unassigned areas because of broken cell doors.

In one particularly egregious case, John Price, 49, waited for roughly 3 ½ hours in a clinic holding pen in March without being seen because jail officers didn’t tell medical staff he was having a medical emergency.
