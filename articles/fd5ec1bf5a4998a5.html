Teen arrested for fatal shooting of 15-year-old Bronx boy walking to school, NYPD says

By Hannah Frishberg · Gothamist · November 11, 2026

A teen suspect has been arrested for the murder of 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, who was shot and killed on his way to school in the Bronx, police said.

“He came looking to have an education. He came looking to live a life that he could contribute to his family. His mother and father sent him here to do well,” Sharpton said. “And for him to be in any way harmed is a blight on all of our communities.”

Family said that Javits was a good student, wanted to join the military and eventually become a doctor.

Javits' sister, Millicent Frimpong, said Wednesday morning by telephone that she was glad the suspect was off the street. When asked whether the arrest brought her any relief, she replied “not really.”

Javits was shot in the stomach around 9:30 a.m. on Oct. 29 near Crotona Park as he walked to DreamYard Preparatory School.

Frimpong’s father, Thomas Frimpong, 60, previously told Gothamist his son immigrated from Ghana to live with him last year.

“It’s not easy for me,” Thomas said. “I’m here with him alone.”

Thomas noted that two DreamYard students were fighting with Javits a few days before the shooting, and that both were suspended.

NYPD Chief of Detectives Joseph Kenny said four days after the shooting that the police were investigating bullying as a motive, while also considering that the case may be a case of mistaken identity or robbery.

Sharpton said at the press conference that the suspect’s motive still wasn’t clear, and that more arrests are expected.

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who is representing Frimpong’s family, condemned anti-immigrant rhetoric. Crump likened Frimpong’s killing to that of other Black teens like Trayvon Martin and the questions surrounding the death of Nolan Wells in Mississippi.

“We have a lot of anti-immigrant rhetoric that is not humane. Especially as children of Africa,” Crump said.

DreamYard Preparatory School students held a walkout and a memorial for Javits after his murder.

Soon after the shooting, Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced that President Donald Trump had agreed to grant an immediate travel visa to Javits' mother in Ghana.
