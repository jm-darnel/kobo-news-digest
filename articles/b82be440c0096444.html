Mamdani Officials Defend Response to Deadly Lithium-Ion Battery Fires

By Claudia Irizarry Aponte · The City · November 20, 2026

But the rate of fires has continued a steady climb across the five boroughs since 2022 in spite of local reforms. At the hearing on Friday, officials from the Fire Department defended its record to the palpable frustration of Julie Menin, the Council speaker.

“I asked for an emergency hearing because we are in an emergency. The numbers in terms of fatalities, injuries are just completely unacceptable. This is a crisis of epic proportions,” said Menin. “The Council has passed bill after bill after bill and yet the problem is getting worse, not better.”

Calls for Public Education

Fire Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore said that without curbing illegal sales, there is little officials can do to prevent fires without greater education for the public about how to safely vet, charge and store lithium-ion batteries.

“We do understand how big a problem this is, but this is a technology that more people are now starting to engage with,” said Bonsignore. “So I think we have to accept that this is a changing technology and it’s putting us in a very difficult place as it emerges and standards start to become something that will make it safer. But education is by far, I think, one of the most important things we can do — and we need to do this in every language.”

Fire officials testified alongside representatives from the housing and buildings departments. While previous hearings on the issue of lithium-ion battery safety have attracted large crowds eager to speak directly to lawmakers, on Friday only about two dozen members of the public were in attendance at the Council’s main chambers in City Hall, a handful of app-based delivery workers among them.

Faulty lithium-ion batteries have ignited 408 fires resulting in nine deaths so far this year, including a fire at a South Bronx public housing development that killed four people and another weeks later at a Sunset Park home that killed six. The cause of the latter fire has not yet officially been determined, but fire officials confirmed at the time that two charred e-bikes were removed from the burnt structure.

Stores that sell, store, and maintain e-bikes and related equipment cater mostly to the city’s vast community of app-based delivery workers, many of whom are immigrants living in the outer boroughs. The main group representing these workers, Los Deliveristas Unidos, have long said their own members are the ones most at-risk by the technology, a reality that fire officials have long acknowledged.

On Thursday, Councilmember Shaun Abreu introduced a bill, with the mayor’s support, that would make companies like UberEats and DoorDash responsible for ensuring workers use safe equipment.
