Nine MTA fare enforcement agents face termination after violent incidents with bus riders

By Samantha Liebman · NY1 · November 18, 2026

Nine MTA EAGLE Team fare enforcement agents, including two supervisors, are facing disciplinary charges and possible termination after an investigation into two violent incidents involving bus riders.

A memo to MTA Chair and CEO Janno Lieber from transit president Demetrius Crichlow said that videos show that agents initiated physical contact in both cases, violating policy.

“I don’t understand why they’re not reaching out to me and talking to me and telling me what’s going on in the process,” he said.

“I did want to spend a little time with him,” Lieber said. “And what I said to him was straightforward, which is, you know, number one, you know, I’m really disappointed that you got injured by people who work for the MTA.”

Taylor said he is still suffering from the trauma.

Community activist Rev. Kevin McCall said the public should have input before the teams are redeployed.

“We applaud the MTA for making sure that they are removing them immediately, that they did immediately,” McCall said. “But don’t just be so fast and swift to bring them back without talking to the community.”

Several people at the board meeting called for the MTA to end the EAGLE Team program altogether.

“This year’s crackdown on bus evasion meant delays. It meant harassment, and it meant riders feeling criminalized simply for taking the bus,” Queens Council Member Tiffany Cabán said.

Salma Alam of Riders Alliance also criticized fare enforcement and issues with OMNY.

“Riders are sick of being criminalized at the turnstile, and every time that OMNY glitches, fails to deliver a free transfer or a discount, it’s a slap in the face for riders,” Alam said.

Gov. Kathy Hochul told NY1 exclusively that the EAGLE Teams will remain in place.

“When there’s a situation where there’s any industry, any business where there’s abuse, you fix the abuse. It doesn’t mean that the entire system is flawed,” Hochul said. “So it causes us to step back and say, ‘How can we improve the existing system?’”

According to the MTA and Hochul, EAGLE Team agents are still being trained, and there is no timeline for when they will be redeployed.

At the same board meeting, the MTA said a 4% fare increase is expected in late March or early April, though what that means for transit riders has not been determined.
