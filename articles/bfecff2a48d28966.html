Residents fight plans to rebuild Tompkins Square Park fitness bars

By Carly Haynes · NY1 · November 12, 2026

Now, the city wants to rebuild them, but some neighbors are fighting to keep them just as they are.

Keith McDermott moved to the Lower East Side 45 years ago and has been using the bars since the city installed them around 1990.

"It's a real community and it has developed and it still develops. It doesn't stop," McDermott said.

Those who use the bars say they provide everything from a free workout to therapy.

"Like punk rock. They have the sweat and the energy of so many people who use them," said Salo Coslovsky.

Users pushed back after the Parks Department presented its initial redesign plan, which they said threatened to remove the historic bars and close off the fitness area for close to two years.

“It’s not only threatened to get rid of these historic bars, but also boxes out for close to two years,” said Gino Parisi.

Officials presented a design to a Community Board 3 committee that included rebuilding the bars to make the fitness area safer and more accessible.

“We live in a society now that we have to think about resiliency when we touch any project. The other thing I think is important is that when you touch a project or when you add improvements, you have to think about accessibility, said Trever Holland, chair of the board's parks committee.

He added that the Parks Department planned to use 3D modeling to reconstruct the bars with the same dimensions as the current equipment while adding accessible features.

Holland also said the existing bars have a limited life span.

"The bar is just like any other thing in any other piece of equipment. Have a life span," he said. "I don't remember the exact date. I thought I did. I think it's 1990 where they're installed, so they all have an end of life."

But fans say the chipped paint gives the bars character, and the community that has formed around them is what makes the space special.

"They have some magic elements to them here that people come here and everybody's nice, friendly, everybody understand from all walks of life," Coslovsky said.

Users also have concerns about the possible 18-month construction timeline.

"It means everything to me. It means everything to me. It's my safe space and me and a whole bunch of other people," said Jamal Kerwin.

Meanwhile, those who use the park are holding on to the hope that the bars can remain the way they are.
