Death toll from Brooklyn building collapse continues to rise amid ongoing search

By Brittany Kriegstein, Verónica Del Valle · Gothamist · November 12, 2026

Eight people are dead, and roughly 19 are injured after a three-story apartment building collapsed in Flatbush on Wednesday afternoon, city officials said. More than 250 firefighters and emergency service workers responded to the scene; they continued to search the rubble for survivors Thursday morning.

Families who had spent the night on the sidewalk wrapped in blankets waited for any information about the fate of their loved ones.

One woman collapsed to the ground after learning her husband was among the dead.

“My husband, he died,” she yelled in Spanish.

Assiah Bah, 23, said she and her family were waiting outside all night for news of their two cousins who lived in the collapsed building and were cooking dinner at the time of the disaster.

“We don't know what is going on, if they're still alive or if they passed, we don't know,” Bah said Thursday morning, saying her family immigrated from Guinea.

"We've seen infants, toddlers being pulled out of the rubble on stretchers into ambulances to be rushed to the hospital,” Councilmember Farah Louis said from the scene Wednesday night.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Thursday morning that rescue workers had been searching for survivors all night in very dangerous conditions and the death toll had risen to eight people.

“There are no words for a loss of this magnitude,” he said. “ Rescue operations are ongoing. Please stay clear of the area and keep those affected, and our first responders risking their lives in these efforts, in your thoughts.”

Kat Stevens, a teacher who lives down the block, said she was home doing her laundry when she heard what sounded almost like a car crash. A few minutes later, she heard sirens and helicopters overhead. She said she found a family with a young boy standing on the sidewalk next to the rubble, crying.

“We need everything right now,” Stevens recalled them telling her. She took the young boy to a nearby clothing store to use the restroom. He kept asking her when he could go back home.

”I don't know, love,” Stevens told him. “I don't know. I don't know, but you are safe. Your family is safe. You're gonna be OK."

“ I actually watched it. I was in my room when the building fell down. You could hear it.” Antione Vines said Wednesday night from the scene. “ Before they build a new building and you're connecting it to another building that’s been up since the 1700s, you gotta look at the structure of the building. You can't just build a new building and connect it.”

Images shared by the FDNY appeared to show that nearly all of the building had caved into the adjacent construction site, exposing a stairway and clothes stored in a closet in a neighboring residential building.

“We’ve been waiting here all night. We went to four hospitals, and they haven’t given us any information,” Luis Siguencia said of his son, 22-year-old construction worker Gabriel Siguencia, as he anxiously waited at the scene Thursday morning.

“He wanted to get ahead. Because we all come here with the dream of getting ahead. He had his papers and everything.”

Gabriel, who recently arrived from Ecuador, had been working on the foundation at the site for the last two weeks.

"You see the building next to it is sheared off in part," former FDNY Commissioner Laura Kavanagh told WNYC, warning that Thursday's storm forecast could further complicate the rescue effort and the collapse site. "Rain and wind can cause further instability."

New York City's Office of Emergency Management said it has coordinated with the Red Cross to open a reception center for affected residents at the Flatbush YMCA at 1401 Flatbush Ave. in Brooklyn.

As of 6 a.m., the Red Cross has registered seven households — 14 adults and five children — for emergency assistance, including temporary lodging.

Department of Buildings spokesperson David Maggiotto said buildings officials were at the scene and would investigate the cause of the collapse.

Mamdani said anyone searching for a loved one should call 311 or 212-639-9675.
