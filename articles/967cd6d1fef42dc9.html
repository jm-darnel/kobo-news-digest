Long-lost Georgia O’Keeffe painting found in East Village closet

By Hannah Frishberg · Gothamist · November 11, 2026

“Oh, by the way, there's a Georgia O'Keeffe in the closet,” a woman casually called to her niece, who was over at her East Village home, helping her get her affairs in order shortly before she passed away.

“We have a very large inbox that gets a lot of stuff sent to it, as you can imagine, and the vast majority of those things are…not that compelling,” Tylee Abbott, senior specialist and international director of American Art at Christie's, said in a phone interview. “From time to time we get something pretty good. This was obviously exceptional. As soon as it came in we all kind of immediately jumped to our feet and ran together and huddled around a computer screen and looked at it, and then started pulling books off the shelves and finding it in published literature to confirm that it was the real deal, and then quickly gave them a call and said we'd be right over.”

For years, “Near Abiquiu” hung in the woman’s East Village apartment until, during a bridge game in the mid-1980s, she noticed a guest “eyeing it a bit too closely” and decided to pack it up in its original box and hide it in the closet, where it sat until the niece unearthed it.

It is likely the first in a series of three works depicting New Mexico’s Lobo Peak, the second of which hangs in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and the third of which was formerly in the collection of David and Peggy Rockefeller.

O’Keeffe painted the series in 1930, a year after her first trip to New Mexico, the American Southwest freshly seeping into her mind’s eye.

“This is the Lobo Mountain…I want to paint this mountain,” she wrote at the time in a letter to her husband, Alfred Stieglitz, “but it is quite impossible.”

A 1935 Stieglitz photo of Radio City in the morning light is also part of the sale, which is on view to the public through Nov. 17 at Christie’s New York. The Stieglitz, however, is an iconic print of well-documented provenance far less delightfully unexpected than the depths of one woman’s East Village coat closet.

“You know, we can get a little bit jaded, seeing art so often coming across our desks,” said Abbott of the charming surprise the O’Keeffe’s inclusion has brought to this month’s sale. “It's really kind of re-inspiring to know that there are still treasures hidden away.”
