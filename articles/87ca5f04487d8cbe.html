B62, M42 take home unwanted awards for slow and unreliable NYC bus service

By Emma Barnett · NY1 · November 19, 2026

A group of transit advocates handed out awards to New York City bus routes, but not the kind the MTA would likely be proud to receive.

This year, the B62 won the Schleppie Award, which is given to the most unreliable high-ridership MTA bus route — meaning routes with at least 5,000 daily riders.

In Downtown Brooklyn, riders said delays and inconsistent service are a regular frustration.

“It’s never on time, or when it comes, three buses come at the same time,” one rider said of the B62. “The time on your phone, it tells you the time that it gets here, but it never gets here.”

“It needs a busway. It needs a dedicated busway,” Lisa Daglian, the executive director of the Permanent Citizens Advisory Committee to the MTA, said. “It needs the bus treatments that other boroughs are getting.”

City officials said they are working to improve bus service.

Elizabeth Adams, the mayor’s senior advisor for Fast and Free Buses, said the city and state are partnering on an effort called “Next Stop: Fast Buses, Better Service.”

“We will aggressively deliver Next Stop: Fast Buses, Better Service, a first-of-its-kind partnership between the city and state when it comes to improving our buses,” Adams said. “And that includes 20% faster buses, faster speeds, stopping delays of less than two minutes beyond the schedule wait time on 50 priority corridors around the city.”

Not every award was negative. The Mazal Award, which is given to the most improved bus route, went to the Q27. The route, which runs from Cambria Heights to Flushing, Queens, increased its speed by 0.91 miles per hour compared to last year.
