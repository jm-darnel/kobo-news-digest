Menin Announces Preliminary Council Findings On Prediction Market Platforms’ Marketing Practices

Harlem World · November 24, 2026

Today, Speaker Julie Menin announced the preliminary findings of the New York City Council’s legislative inquiry into the marketing practices of major prediction market platforms.

Council investigators identified instances of deceptive marketing tactics that sought to create “buzz” among young users, as well as highly disproportionate net losses both by young people (relative to older users) and among “individual” user accounts (compared to “institutional” accounts).

“We are done taking chances with prediction market platforms,” said Speaker Julie Menin. “Our investigation found that these companies are targeting young people with deceptive, predatory marketing practices — and tragically, these tactics are working. Nearly 500,000 individual Kalshi accounts are registered to a New York City address, with net losses totaling millions of dollars. And the disproportionate financial and mental tolls on young New Yorkers are simply unacceptable. As a mom and consumer protection attorney, I believe we must end these companies’ predatory marketing tactics. The City Council will hold a hearing to consider first-in-the-nation regulations of these practices, and we expect these companies to testify.”

• Trading outcomes for individual and institutional New York City Kalshi users differed substantially in 2026, with individual users experiencing aggregate net losses and institutional users realizing significant aggregate net gains. According to data provided by Kalshi representatives, from January 1 through August 11, 2026, individual New York City users lost, in aggregate, approximately $17.6 million while institutional users gained more than $210 million. From August 12, 2026 to November 1, 2026, individual users, in aggregate, lost approximately $34.9 million and institutional users gained $130.7 million.

• Polymarket representatives acknowledged issues with the company’s user-generated content program, which ran from January to June 2026 and included social media ads utilizing a mock trading website displaying fictitious profits and account activity. According to representatives, the program sought to create “buzz” among a younger crowd through short-form videos designed to appear organic and was managed by a 19-year-old and four interns. Participants were paid between $20 and a few hundred dollars based on views and received $50 for referrals to the program. Representatives stated that program guidelines were not consistently followed and could not identify who created the mock website used in the advertisements.

• Kalshi has established a substantial user base in New York City, including more than 50,000 account holders between the ages of 18 and 20. According to data provided by Kalshi representatives, as of November 1, 2026, there were approximately 484,000 individual Kalshi accounts associated with New York City addresses. Approximately 51,000 of those accounts belonged to users ages 18 to 20, while an additional 78,600 accounts belonged to users ages 21 to 24.

• Users ages 18 to 20 accounted for about one-sixth of all losses incurred by individual New York City users despite representing only about one-tenth of New York City accounts on Kalshi and approximately 4% of daily contract trading volume in the city. As of November 1, 2026, approximately 51,000 of Kalshi’s 484,000 New York City accounts (10.5%) belonged to users between the ages of 18 and 20. Between January 1 and November 1, 2026, those users incurred approximately $8.9 million in net trading losses, representing approximately 17% of the $52.5 million in losses incurred by individual New York City users during that period. At the same time, these users between 18 and 20 years old traded an average of only approximately 1.3 million contracts per day between November 2025 and November 1, 2026, compared to about 30 million per day traded by users 21 and older.

• Council investigators found that prediction market platforms employ marketing techniques used in gambling marketing that researchers have identified as encouraging participation, increasing betting, and normalizing gambling. These techniques include sign-up incentives, sponsorships and advertising tied to sporting events and celebrities, and marketing that frames participation as a social activity. In a preliminary review of a sample of prediction market advertisements, investigators found ads offering sign-up, deposit, or first-trade bonuses, ads tied to betting, sports viewing, fandom, or socializing, and ads featuring a league, team, broadcaster, or media partner.

• Prediction market platforms have recently introduced new reforms and restrictions intended to address compulsive behavior and age-related risks. In late September 2026, Polymarket announced voluntary self-exclusion and deposit limit features as well as access to mental health services. In late October 2026, Kalshi introduced default $2,500 monthly deposit limits for accounts for 18- to 20-year-olds and new trade alerts.

“The preliminary findings of the Council’s inquiry raise serious concerns about how prediction market platforms are reaching consumers, particularly young New Yorkers who may be especially vulnerable to the risks of these products,” said Deputy Speaker Dr. Nantasha Williams. “When young adults account for a disproportionate share of financial losses, and marketing practices are designed to encourage greater participation and normalize gambling, we have to examine whether existing consumer protections are keeping pace with this rapidly evolving industry. These findings reinforce why the Council’s oversight is so important. As we prepare for the upcoming hearing and consider legislative action, our focus must be on accountability, transparency, and ensuring that New Yorkers have the information and protections they need to make informed decisions.”

“Online prediction markets are surging in popularity, and posing growing risks to the safety and financial wellbeing of New Yorkers, particularly minors and young adults,” said Council Member Harvey Epstein, chair of the Committee on Consumer and Worker Protection. “We have a responsibility to investigate the advertising tactics these companies are using to ensure they are following the law. I look forward to our upcoming hearing alongside the Speaker and my colleagues and hearing testimony from executives of major platforms to assess the threats facing consumers and how we can protect them from predatory advertising and the potentially addictive nature of prediction markets.”

“When prediction market platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket remain largely unregulated and unchecked despite their rapid expansion, government oversight is crucial for keeping consumers safe,” said Council Member Shekar Krishnan, chair of the Committee on Oversight and Investigations. “Too many New Yorkers, especially young adults, have already fallen victim to the deceptive marketing tactics these companies use to pull them in and wring them dry — which is why we have to act fast and we have to act now to hold these corporations responsible. As Chair of the Committee on Oversight and Investigations, I’m extremely concerned by what we’ve been seeing and recognize this issue as a top priority. This upcoming Council hearing is the first of many steps the Council is taking to keep New York consumers safe and end these deceptive practices.”

“Predictive markets are operating like the Wild West, preying on working class people and using tactics they know are addictive to keep people hooked, all with very little regulation,” said Council Member Tiffany Cabán, chair of the Committee on Mental Health and Substance Use. “I’m looking forward to working with my colleagues to look into these exploitative practices and explore ways for the Council to regulate this industry.”

These findings come just days after New York State Attorney General Letitia James’ office filed a response letter in the State’s ongoing lawsuit against Polymarket, alleging that the company has been operating unlawfully by allowing users ages 18 to 20 to place bets, despite the legal minimum age of 21 for sports betting in the state. On Monday, New York Supreme Court Justice Melissa Crane noted this issue as being a particular cause for concern.

In recent months, the Council has taken significant actions to advance consumer protections, including holding an impactful hearing on AI regulations and passing Speaker Menin’s bill to ban surveillance pricing. Speaker Menin is the city’s former commissioner of consumer affairs.
