Kathryn Lewek Brings Mozart And A Little Magic To Madison Avenue

Harlem World · November 6, 2026

Champagne, nearly 70 guests, and a soprano whose voice usually fills an opera house: on Wednesday evening, a Madison Avenue residence became a setting for something considerably more intimate.

Andrew Hawkins and Lisa Walsh-Hawkins, joined by their daughter, Lauren Walsh, welcomed members of the American Mozarteum Society for a chamber concert featuring Kathryn Lewek. The attraction was not simply the opportunity to hear an acclaimed artist up close. It was the chance to catch her at a moment of discovery, trying out a Mozart aria before an audience for the first time.

For Harlem readers looking beyond the familiar cultural itinerary, the evening suggested an appealing proposition: sometimes, the most memorable encounter with music happens outside the grand auditorium.

A Grand Voice, An Intimate Setting

Lewek’s program moved between Verdi and Mozart, pairing “Ah, forse è lui” from La traviata with “Come scoglio” from Così fan tutte and “Martern aller Arten” from Die Entführung aus dem Serail.

Her hosts had first encountered her in another Mozart role entirely.

“Andrew and I first met Kathryn at Mozart Week this past January, where she was singing the Queen of the Night in Mozart’s The Magic Flute,” Lisa told guests. “Needless to say, she absolutely brought the house down.”

Lisa noted that Lewek had sung the role more than 350 times worldwide, including a record 85 performances at the Metropolitan Opera—a formidable résumé for an artist now sharing her voice at close quarters.

For all its vocal ambition, the evening’s most personal theme was connection.

“This career has brought me all over the world, and my journey has been defined by the relationships and friends I have made, Lisa and Andrew among them,” Lewek said. “It’s only been a year, and we’re already family.”

That sentiment gave the gathering its character: less a formal presentation than a meeting of people brought together by a shared enthusiasm.

Among those attending were American Mozarteum Society board members Kimberly Allan and Marta de Radzyminski, along with Allan’s husband, Rob Allan. The society’s work centers on preserving and advancing Mozart’s legacy through performance, education, and cultural exchange, linking American audiences with the International Mozarteum Foundation in Salzburg.

Andrew’s invitation to guests was correspondingly direct.

“We put on events so you get to experience things in a way that is uniquely uplifting, enlightening, informative, and just enjoyable,” he said, encouraging those moved by the performance to attend future events or become members.

From Manhattan To Salzburg

Lewek’s next engagements will carry her from the Manhattan gathering back to Europe. She is scheduled to return to Konstanze in Die Entführung aus dem Serail at Deutsche Oper Berlin in December 2026, followed by her Fiordiligi role debut during Mozart Week in January 2027.

For listeners inspired to make a cultural journey of their own, the American Mozarteum Society has announced member gatherings during Salzburg’s 2027 Mozart Week, whose program comprises 50 events spanning concerts, opera, and discussion.

Back on Madison Avenue, however, the appeal was simpler: a room small enough for an artist’s words to matter alongside her music, and an audience invited to hear not only what she had mastered, but what she was preparing to become.
