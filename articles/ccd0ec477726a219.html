MTA warns of major budget deficit without help from city, state and feds

By Ramsey Khalifeh · Gothamist · November 18, 2026

MTA officials on Wednesday warned the transit agency will face a half-billion-dollar deficit as soon as 2028 if the city, state and federal governments don’t help balance its books.

MTA Chair Janno Lieber said he hoped the incoming Democratic majority in Congress would approve more funding for the agency. He also called on the city to pay for a larger share of the MTA’s paratransit service.

Lieber on Wednesday noted that without new funding, the MTA would need to resort to even higher fare hikes, service cuts and headcount reductions to balance its books.

“Those are the only tools the MTA has at its disposal,” Lieber said. “Those are not tools that we ever want to use.”

Lieber also noted the MTA has not received any new discretionary funds from the U.S. Department of Transportation since President Donald Trump returned to office in 2025. He argued it wasn’t fair that New York carries 43% of the nation’s mass transit riders but only receives 17% of the federal funding annually allocated for mass transit agencies.

“If we get a fairer contribution from Washington, we might have to borrow a little less money,” Lieber said at a news conference. “So if we get a fairer share from Washington, it too will contribute to the solution and that’s why we’re focusing on it so much.”
