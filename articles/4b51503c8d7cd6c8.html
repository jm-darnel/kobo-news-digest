Newark ICE detention center passes fed inspection, despite rising 'deficiencies'

By Arya Sundaram · Gothamist · November 13, 2026

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement inspectors found 22 deficiencies at Delaney Hall during their mid-September inspection, up from 12 during an inspection in late August of last year.

Delaney Hall earlier this year emerged as a flashpoint in the nation’s immigration debates, as hundreds of detainees inside the facility launched a hunger and labor strike, and protesters outside gathered in support, at times clashing with local and federal law enforcement officers.

Recent lawsuits filed by state and local officials seeking entry for fire and health inspectors remain pending. State health inspectors’ review of the food service areas in late May found poor employee hygiene, unclean surfaces, and food stored at unsafe temperatures.

Federal investigators found that facility staff didn’t report sexual assault allegations to appropriate ICE leaders, or conduct necessary reports after sexual assault investigations to determine if any policy changes were needed. It remains unclear who the assault allegations were against.

Investigators also found several cases in which detainees didn’t receive a necessary initial medical screening within 12 hours of arriving at the facility, or the comprehensive health assessment required within 14 days of arriving. In four of the 25 cases reviewed, investigators found detainees’ medications weren’t distributed properly.

Kathy O’Leary, the New Jersey coordinator for the Catholic social justice organization Pax Christi USA, said that she was surprised investigators found “so few” cases of detainees not receiving necessary medication, given that she routinely hears from detainees and their families that they don’t receive them.

“We haven’t heard of anything getting better,” O’Leary said.

She added that transparency has been "reduced significantly since May,” given stricter restrictions on who is allowed to visit detainees and which detainees local government officials are allowed to speak with.
