Hochul does not rule out new taxes to help fund programs like universal childcare

Gov. Kathy Hochul admitted she’s open to raising taxes, especially when it comes to funding the estimated multi-billion dollar universal childcare program, which was also a campaign promise of Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

“When it came to helping the city out in a severe financial crisis that the mayor inherited, I stepped up with a creative way to do that. I’m telling you that I want to find a creative path to make sure we have the resources necessary to be able to have a long-term commitment to childcare,” Hochul said during a press conference in San Juan, Puerto Rico.

While the governor didn’t deny income tax hikes could be on the horizon, she said she’s backed other tax hikes to fund key priorities in the past, such as the pied-à-terre tax on second homes owned by non-primary residents of the city and raising the payroll mobility tax rate to pay for MTA infrastructure upgrades.

Mamdani is likely to have more left-leaning allies in the state Legislature next session on board with wealth tax proposals. In past years, both Democratic majorities in the state Assembly and Senate have also backed raising taxes on high earners.

“I’ve always been clear about where I stand. I continue to hold those positions. And I’m also looking forward to continuing a productive working relationship with our governor to deliver for working people. And that’s something that excited so many New Yorkers over the course of this recent election was that the fruits of this partnership was ones that they could feel in their wallets,” said Mamdani, standing next to Hochul.

Mamdani also attended an anti-ICE rally in San Juan, opposing bill amendments at play in the Puerto Rican Senate that could allow federal immigration officials into public schools.

“The amendments that have been put forward for Senate Bill 1062 will enable ICE to access schools, threatening the lives of Puerto Rican children in the very places where they should be safest,” he said.

“This moment demands solidarity. It demands action, and it demands that we not stay silent while our Puerto Rican neighbors, who are so often treated as second-class citizens in this country, are targeted,” Mamdani added.
