City Council set to approve package of bills to support veterans

By Alyssa Paolicelli · NY1 · November 11, 2026

One bill would provide a full property tax exemption for severely disabled veterans who meet the state's requirements for permanent and total service-connected disabilities. The city currently offers partial property tax exemptions to eligible veterans.

Officials estimate the new exemption would benefit about 4,500 veteran households and cost the city less than $5 million in lost tax revenue.

A second bill would require the Department of Housing Preservation and Development to publish annual reports tracking how many veterans and their families apply for and receive apartments in city-supervised affordable housing. The measure would not create new preferences for veteran applicants but would establish a system to track those applications.

A third bill would create an LGBTQ+ veterans’ liaison within the Department of Veterans' Services to connect LGBTQ+ veterans with resources and services.

City Council Speaker Julie Menin said the legislation is intended to help veterans remain in New York City.

“New York City is home to as many as 200,000 veterans, whose selfless sacrifices have kept us safe, and who now deserve real support in our city,” Menin said.

She also spoke about her father, Robert Jacobs, who was a doctor at Walter Reed Army Medical Center and treated wounded service members and veterans.

“His sense of service inspired my own and taught me about the battles that veterans face after they return home,” Menin said. “The City Council is commemorating this Veterans Day with tangible action, passing a package of legislation that will improve life for veterans across the five boroughs. Every veteran deserves to afford to stay in their home and to live with dignity in the city and country they fought to defend.”

About 20,000 people are expected to participate. Organizers say the parade will also serve as a culmination of America 250 celebrations, with all branches of the military expected to be represented.

This year's grand marshal is Navy veteran Suni Williams, who is known for spending nearly 300 days in space after technical issues with her spacecraft.
