Yankees non-tender Fernando Cruz, Luis Gil

By Peter Brody · Pinstripe Alley · November 20, 2026

Should both parties fail to agree on a number, the case would be submitted to an arbitration hearing. These hearings typically occur in February — subject to change due to the looming expiry of the CBA and expected owner lockout of the players — with the arbitration panel resolving the case by awarding the figure tendered by either the club or the player as opposed to a midpoint of those two figures.

Our own Andrés Chávez identified Gil as a non-tender candidate at the beginning of the month. It has been a precipitous fall from the stratospheric seven-start stretch that ultimately won Gil the 2024 AL Rookie of the Year Award. He has battled lat and shoulder injuries over the past two seasons, limiting him to 20 appearances (15 starts) with a 3.74 ERA, 5.44 FIP, and 58 strikeouts in 84.1 innings. An alarming loss of velocity on his fastball and worsening command has seen his strikeout rate crater from 27.3-percent between 2021-2024 to just 15.8-percent in the last two years, creating an untenable 1.9-percent strikeout-minus-walk ratio. There were question whether Gil might rediscover some effectiveness in a max-effort bullpen role, but ultimately the Yankees decided that the experiment wasn’t worth the effort. In five big league seasons with the Yankees, Gil went 21-11 in 56 appearances (51 starts), posting a 3.61 ERA, 4.54 FIP, 272 strikeouts, and 2.8 fWAR in 269.1 innings.

As for Cruz, the righty reliever became something of a cult hero thanks to his devastating splitter and enthusiastic celebrations. Arriving via trade from the Reds in the 2024-25 offseason, Cruz carved out a late-inning high leverage role by posting a 32-percent strikeout rate thanks to the splitter that allowed him to sit at the very top of the league whiff rate leaderboard. However, his appearances waned across his final two months of action in 2026, and it was subsequently revealed that he had suffered a partial tear of his UCL. The injury ended his campaign despite Cruz opting not to undergo surgery in the hope of making a late-season comeback. He was recently able to begin a throwing program, and GM Brian Cashman expressed optimism about his availability in 2027, but it appears the medicals on his elbow were scary enough to not be worth the risk at that price. In two seasons with the Yankees, Cruz made 110 relief appearances, pitching to a 3.33 ERA and 3.71 FIP with 139 strikeouts and 1.2 fWAR in 102.2 innings.

It is no surprise that the Yankees tendered contracts to the remaining seven arbitration-eligible players. The only other plausible candidate for non-tender was Schmidt, who is projected to earn $4.5 million in his third and final year of arbitration eligibility. The 30-year-old righty has missed significant parts of the last three seasons to injury — a lat strain in 2024, Tommy John surgery in 2025, and a biceps injury shortly after his return from Tommy John rehab in 2026. In total, he has made just 30 starts since the start of 2024, pitching just 164.2 innings after tossing a career-high 159 in 2023. However, Cashman confirmed that Schmidt is fully healthy entering this offseason, and the righty offers a much high floor and ceiling than Gil as starting rotation depth while also representing an intriguing multi-inning relief option.

With Cruz and Gil’s non-tenders, the Yankees have now subtracted two more arms from their already depleted relief corps. David Bednar did choose to return on the qualifying offer while the team has also reunited with Tim Hill and Jonathan Loáisiga in recent days, but there remains a good deal of work to be done to fill out the big-leaguer bullpen. There also remains the possibility that the Yankees could reintegrate Cruz and Gil on minor-league deals in the coming days.
