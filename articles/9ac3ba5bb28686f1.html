Mamdani unveils framework to support horse carriage workers ahead of ban

By Erica Brosnan · NY1 · November 17, 2026

Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced a framework Tuesday to help horse-drawn carriage drivers and stable workers find new jobs as New York City prepares to phase out the industry by June 2028.

"As New York City moves to bring the horse-drawn carriage industry to a close, we have a responsibility to the workers who have built careers in this industry. This administration will not leave them behind,” Mamdani said in a statement. “Our transition plan will provide financial support and pathways to new careers through training, career guidance and education. We will support workers every step of the way."

Mamdani said his administration will partner with NYC Talent and the city's Department of Small Business Services to establish a Horse Carriage Worker Industry Program aimed at helping 200 workers transition into new careers.

Mamdani said he will also direct his administration to study electric carriages and electric pedal-assist bicycles as possible alternatives to horse carriages within Central Park, and electric carriages as a possibility along the park's perimeter.

But the union representing carriage drivers says the city's proposed support does not go far enough.

In a statement, Transport Workers Union International President John Samuelsen called the legislation "horrifically flawed and unconstitutional."

“All of these planned initiatives could quickly be abandoned in a fiscal crisis as the city scrambles to fund all of its other responsibilities and priorities. That would leave hundreds of horse-carriage workers, owners, and family members on the curb with nothing at all,” Samuelsen said.

Samuelsen said the union plans to file a federal lawsuit challenging the law's legality once Mamdani signs it.
