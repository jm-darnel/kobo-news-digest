Brewers Update: Pet Adoptions This Sunday, Free Doughnuts And More

Harlem World · November 13, 2026

From Gales Brewers Update Newsletter

This summer the Council passed my bill, Local Law 143 of 2026, allowing restaurants the option to install high-quality winter vestibules enclosing their licensed sidewalk café areas—spaces for which they already pay the City of New York year-round.

Earlier this week, I testified in support (PDF) of Dept. of Transportation rules allowing for seasonal use of structured enclosures which can be readily removed as needed, and ensuring that implementation will protect pedestrian circulation and accessibility (including for those who are disabled). Testimony at the link.

Never miss new stories in Harlem

A week from Saturday, on 11/21 at 5 pm at the Leonard Nimoy Thalia (2537 Broadway at 95th St.), I’m co-sponsoring a screening of West Side Familia, a documentary directed by Taylor Hosking, which follows the story of a Puerto Rican-led neighborhood outlaw crew and community care group from the ‘70s as they struggle to preserve their public traditions and cultural identity in Manhattan’s Upper West Side. The screening will be followed by a Q&A featuring cast and crew from the film. Please join us! This Sunday, 11/15, 12–4 pm, across from our office at 563 Columbus Ave: Animal Care Centers’ Mobile Pet Adoption Event with cats and dogs looking for new homes. Adoptions are first-come, first-served. Bring a government-issued photo ID and a charged smartphone to complete the online adoption application. Visit ACC’s adoption calendar for more information. (The event may be canceled if there’s heavy rain).

Tuesday, 11/17, 12–1 pm, JASA Club 76 (120 West 76th Street), Medicare Rights Center will be presenting on Fall Open Enrollment, the period from 10/15 through 12/7, when you can change your Medicare Part B, C, and/or D coverage. Learn about the types of changes you can make, how to review your coverage for the coming year, and programs that can help save money on Medicare costs.

Ongoing helpful events at my District Office:

First and third Thursdays, from 10 am–5 pm, a member of the West Side Campaign Against Hunger’s Benefits Access Specialist Team will be at my district office to help people apply for SNAP benefits, submit case changes or appeals, and answer questions. Call my office at (212) 873-0282 to schedule an appointment.

Last Tuesdays, 10 am–3 pm, at my District Office (563 Columbus Ave.): A rep with the Veterans of Foreign Wars will be available to assist veterans applying for VA benefits. Bring military service records and discharge form DD214; no appointment necessary.

Mondays from 2–6 pm, at my District Office (563 Columbus Ave.): Cheryl is our talented volunteer who helps those with SCRIE/DRIE applications and renewals. Reps from the NYC Dept. of Finance, who also assist with these programs (and also SCHE), will be at my district office on second Tuesdays. Call our office (212) 873-0282 for an appointment for Cheryl or DOF.

Reps from the Dept. of Housing Preservation and Development will be in my District Office on 11/17 and can help you prepare to apply for affordable housing, file a housing complaint, finance improvements and repairs, or remedy a violation with their HPD in Your District program.

First Wednesdays (except holidays), 6 pm, Goddard Riverside (593 Columbus @88th St.): Our monthly Housing Clinics. The December topic, Wednesday 12/2, is “Holdovers and Non-pays.” There’ll be a presentation and Q&A and an opportunity for a free consultation with a housing attorney. Co-sponsored by Goddard Riverside and TakeRoot Justice. Please arrive a little early to sign in; capacity is limited. Call (212) 873-0282 for info.

Monday, 11/16, 5:30–7:30 pm, in the Council Chambers in City Hall, my office is cosponsoring Council’s Veterans Day Celebration. To attend, RSVP at the link or call (212)482-6753.

Gale Brewer

Gale Brewer represents Manhattan’s 6th District on the New York City Council. A former Manhattan Borough President, she consistently advocates for affordable housing, worker protections, government transparency, and public safety. She holds a master’s degree in public administration from Harvard’s Kennedy School and has taught at CUNY and Barnard College.
