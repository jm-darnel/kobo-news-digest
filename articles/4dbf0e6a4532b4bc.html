Study links ADHD to NYC’s deadly subway surfing trend

By Ramsey Khalifeh, Stephen Nessen · Gothamist · November 10, 2026

People killed “surfing” on top of New York City subway trains tend to be teen boys diagnosed with ADHD, according to a new study published this week.

“The most striking finding in our series is the overrepresentation of ADHD; 60% of surfers carried the diagnosis versus 6.1% of nonsurfers,” the study said. “This association has not been previously described in the train surfing literature and is among the most notable findings of our study.”

Researchers behind the study acknowledged the sample size is too small to make a sweeping generalization of all young people in New York City who ride on top of trains. The analysis doesn’t include two young girls, 12-year-old Zemfira Mukhtarov and 13-year-old Ebba Morina, who died while riding on top of a J train in Williamsburg in Oct. 2025.

Eveline Teresa Hidalgo, senior author of the study and pediatric neurosurgeon at NYU and Bellevue, said the subway surfers diagnosed with ADHD had also been treated for the disorder.

“We don't know what this means exactly, and it's not that ADHD causes them to subway surf, but it means that it's something that we need to investigate further,” said Hidalgo. ”It could be associated with some attention-seeking behavior. It could be associated with the thrill-seeking behavior, and it's a typical teenage behavior.”

Hidalgo said the study shows how subway surfing can cause life-altering injuries among people who don’t die after falling from trains. Some patients they studied at Bellevue suffered injuries to their brains and spine, and some had their limbs amputated.

Both the MTA and NYPD have attempted to prevent subway surfing in recent years.

NYPD data shows 185 people have been arrested for subway surfing so far this year, a 12% increase compared to the same period last year.
