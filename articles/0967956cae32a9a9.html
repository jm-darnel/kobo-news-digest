DNA, photos could link alleged Central Park serial rapist to 3 assaults, prosecutors say

By Brittany Kriegstein · Gothamist · November 18, 2026

Manhattan assistant district attorney Maxine Rosenthal told the court that DNA swabs from the genital areas of three different women matched that of 30-year-old Dereck Rivera-Deleon, who was extradited from Florida to New York City and arraigned on two counts of predatory sexual assault.

One of the counts stems from the Oct. 24 assault, in which prosecutors said Rivera-Deleon threatened a 39-year-old woman at knifepoint, took her into Central Park near East 67th Street and East Drive, and sexually assaulted her. A witness later identified him in a photo array, Rosenthal said.

According to prosecutors, the other count is connected to a nearly identical incident on July 18, 2021. The victim in that case identified Rivera-Deleon in a photo array “as the man who took her into Central Park and engaged in a forceful sexual encounter,” but police were unable to locate him at the time, Rosenthal said.

Investigators are also working to connect Rivera-Deleon to a rape that was reported in the park in October 2023, where the victim also identified him in a photo array, according to prosecutors. Rosenthal told the court that DNA collected from an anal swab in the 2023 case matched the DNA collected from the victim in the October 2026 case.

Rosenthal said Rivera-Deleon fled south after the most recent incident because he saw himself on the news and realized that police were looking for him. He was taken into custody last week at a Burger King in Miami by NYPD detectives assigned to the U.S. Marshals regional fugitive task force, police said.

Rivera-Deleon did not speak throughout the brief proceeding, and did not enter a plea. He was dressed in a white T-shirt and sneakers, and his feet were shackled and chained together.

Rivera-Deleon’s defense attorney, Cara Mazor, argued for the judge to set “some amount of bail” because he has a limited criminal history, with one arrest from 2015.

But Judge Shirin Zarabi, citing the “seriousness of the allegations,” ordered him held in jail without bail as the case proceeds.

“At this time, the accusations against him are only allegations, and Mr. Rivera is entitled to the full presumption of innocence,” said Lupe Todd-Medina, a spokesperson for New York County Defender Services, which is representing Rivera-Deleon. “We are actively investigating the facts of this case and will vigorously defend Mr. Rivera’s rights as the legal process moves forward.”

Rivera-Deleon will be back in court on Nov. 23.
