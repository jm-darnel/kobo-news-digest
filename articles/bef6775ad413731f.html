Mayor Mamdani among massive crowd at slain Bronx teen Javits Frimpong’s funeral

By Brittany Kriegstein · Gothamist · November 20, 2026

Javits Frimpong lay in a white suit Friday morning as hundreds of mourners, many in traditional Ghanaian dress, filed past his open casket, paying their respects to the 15-year-old boy who was shot and killed while on his way to school in the Bronx last month.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani, state Attorney General Letitia James and the Rev. Al Sharpton were among the officials and activists who joined the boy’s family for the funeral at New Convent Dominion Cathedral in the Bronx.

Javits’ mother, father and sister sat in the front row, tearfully accepting condolences.

“ I cannot explain the emptiness your departure has created in my life,” Thomas Frimpong said through a friend who delivered the eulogy on his behalf. “I keep thinking about the things we were yet to do, the conversations we were yet to have, and the milestones I was supposed to witness.

“I was supposed to celebrate your achievements, not stand beside your grave.”

Frimpong, who had emigrated to New York City from Ghana in October 2025, was killed on Oct. 29 as he walked to DreamYard Preparatory School. His death captured widespread attention after Thomas Frimpong said his son had been bullied by several boys at school a few days earlier.

Prominent civil rights advocates, including Sharpton and attorney Ben Crump, have condemned the killing and rallied around the family. Students at his school also staged a walkout to honor the teen and speak out against the violence that had claimed his life.

Last week, police arrested 17-year-old Thierno Jalloh and charged him with murder, manslaughter and criminal weapons possession in connection with the killing. NYPD officials said they are still looking for two other suspects.

Crump, who is representing Frimpong’s family, said prosecutors told his clients they do not believe the shooters went to Frimpong’s school or were involved with the bullying incident a few days earlier.

Mamdani asked President Donald Trump to grant Frimpong’s mother, who lives in Ghana, an immediate travel visa so she could come to the Bronx to bury her son. The mayor’s Office of Community Safety also funded and coordinated the deployment of grief counselors, outreach specialists and other resources to Frimpong’s neighborhood, according to City Hall spokesperson Sam Raskin.

Frimpong was at least the 19th teenager to be shot and killed in New York City this year, according to police data compiled by Gothamist.
