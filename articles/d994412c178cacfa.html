First Rikers Island Demolition Set for 2027 Under Mamdani Plan

By Reuven Blau · The City · November 18, 2026

On Rikers, the North Infirmary Command, which opened in 1932, is slated to be torn down in 2027. The facility was closed in June, with some detainees moved to a new medical unit at Bellevue Hospital.

Marvin Pines, 65, died there in February 2023 after suffering a seizure. A state review found that Correction Department staff failed to meet minimum supervision standards and that jail medical staff failed to conduct basic assessments and follow-ups. A fire also broke out at the facility in April 2023, prompting an investigation by the city’s Board of Correction that resulted in a dozen recommendations for the department.

Jail Alternatives

It also entails expanding support for families who provide housing for former detainees and assistance to people returning home from jail, according to a source familiar with the plan. That program, known as kinship housing, has been run by the Osborne Association, a nonprofit helping formerly incarcerated people.

“They really are focused on the re-entry side of things,” the source said. “Once you get arrested and charged: How do you divert people as soon as possible?”

Reducing the number of people held in city jails has been one of the biggest obstacles to closing and reforming Rikers, according to multiple jail experts.

It is unclear what the blueprint includes to speed up the processing of criminal cases. Some of those delays are tied to mental health evaluations, dragged-out court cases, and a backlogged legal system.

But expanding supportive housing for former detainees has been a longstanding challenge for City Hall dating back more than four decades. Previous administrations have struggled to fund new facilities, get neighborhood buy-in, and cover yearly operating costs.

As for the close Rikers plan, the Mamdani administration has already put new money behind its effort.

In June, the city transferred the North Infirmary Command and portions of two other vacant jail facilities from the Department of Correction to the Department of Citywide Administrative Services, which will oversee planning for their future use.

On Wednesday, City Hall spokesperson Sam Raskin said the administration remains committed to closing Rikers and moving to a borough-based jail system, but he declined to provide additional details about the planned demolition or broader blueprint.

“As the mayor has said, our administration is fully committed to closing Rikers Island and transitioning to a borough-based jail system that is safer and more humane for both people in custody and staff who work in the jails,” Raskin said in a statement. “We will have more to share soon.”
