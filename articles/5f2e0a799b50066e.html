School bus woes lead parents, lawmakers to seek contract changes

By Dan Rivoli · NY1 · November 18, 2026

Another school year began with thousands of students who did not have a bus driver to take them to school.

Many more face hours-long commutes.

“We saw those students as not having a route, we notified the schools, we notified the families, and we gave a prepaid rideshare and saying, we’re going to work to put you on,” Kevin Moran, deputy chancellor at the city’s Department of Education, told lawmakers.

Students and parents complain about lengthy commutes or inconvenient schedules.

Councilman Eric Dinowitz, who chairs the education committee, recounted a story from a constituent whose child had an inconvenient pickup schedule.

“So the choice that this parent has to make is: do I take my child out of school an hour early, a child with a disability who needs that support and education? Or do we pay for private transport?” Dinowitz said.

About 2,000 families were notified that their child would have no bus route this school year, according to education officials.

Edward Straka was one parent who rallied with lawmakers before the hearing.

Straka told NY1 his 9-year-old son’s commute was taking two and a half hours. His son, Edward, is autistic and non-verbal.

“He’s stressed when he was coming home that late. It’s a long day for him. He would have to wake up at 5:30 a.m. in the dark,” Straka said.

He eventually got his son on a direct route

“It took over a month of screaming and advocating,” Straka said.

Education officials said they’re trying to meet higher demand — a 31% increase in students needing curb-to-school transportation in four years

“Through aggressive hiring by our transportation providers, close collaboration between vendors and our teams, and daily operational adjustments, we have made substantial progress,” Moran said.

Lawmakers say the cause of the problems: school bus contracts that have been in place for decades

“When contracts aren’t renegotiated, things fall through the cracks,” Councilwoman Lynn Schulman said. “You don’t update things. The same bad actors are out there.”

A state law could allow those negotiations with employee protections.

But in a statement, the New York School Bus Contractor Association’s director Tammy Mortier said: “NYSBCA believes school transportation contracts can be modernized and strengthened through existing frameworks, as New York City has demonstrated over the years.”
