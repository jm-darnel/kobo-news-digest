City explores voluntary buyouts for flood-prone homes in Queens

By Noorulain Khawaja · NY1 · November 25, 2026

Homeowners on 183rd Street in Hollis, Queens, are seeking city buyouts after years of flooding they say has damaged homes and disrupted daily life whenever it rains.

And despite the city’s $52 million infrastructure upgrade for Queens to help prevent flooding — including projects to replace water mains that were installed before the 1940s — some residents say they’re ready to leave their neighborhood.

Residents said flooding has repeatedly affected the area around 183rd Street and 90th Avenue.

During a strong rain event in August, water formed a whirlpool in the street after a manhole cover popped off.

Residents on the block say the flooding keeps pulling their life down into the sewer.

George Lee, a 74-year-old homeowner on 183rd Street, said he has lived in a house his father bought 60 years ago. He said he is ready to leave if the city agrees to buy him out.

“I can’t deal with this [anymore]. Every single time it rains, my blood pressure goes up,” Lee said. “Every single time it rains, we have to worry about what’s going on. We can’t sleep at night because we worry about the [streets] getting flooded. My car is parked in front of my house. The buyout means I don’t have to worry about this [anymore]. Once I’m gone, I don’t have to worry about this place [anymore].”

Amit Shivprasad, a homeowner who grew up on the block, said he has been going door to door to talk with neighbors on 183rd Street, 184th Street and part of 182nd Street about a possible city buyout.

Shivprasad, who is on his local community board, said he has been part of negotiations with the city to seek market value for families who want to move.

“I’ve already requested it must be market value or even more,” he said. “Or an incentive to help these folks move to an area that doesn’t flood, so if you give them an extra 30, 40 thousand [dollars] to go do a little bit of homework to look for a new home in an area that doesn’t flood, it helps.”

Shivprasad said he spent about $500,000 to repair his home after Hurricane Ida in 2021, when his basement flooded. Two people who were living in his basement apartment died during that event and are now suing Shivprasad and the city for negligence.

Shivprasad and the city have denied wrongdoing. He said he has lived on the street for more than 30 years and is pushing for a buyout, in part, for older residents and others who need help to leave the flood-prone area.

“I’m doing this more for the seniors in the community, and the people who [need] it the most,” Shivprasad said. “I hate to see everyone [go], including myself, but we need to move outta this area so we don’t deal with this flooding ever, ever again. It’s just not worth it.”

NY1 reached out to the Mayor’s Office of Climate and Environmental Justice, which is leading the buyout efforts, for more information about negotiations between the city and homeowners.

“The City continues to explore opportunities for voluntary buyouts and advance long-term flood resilience in partnership with community stakeholders,” a spokesperson for the agency said in a statement. “We remain committed to evaluating homeowners’ expressions of interest to develop feasible pathways that achieve this goal. These efforts are ongoing, and the City will continue to collaborate with residents and partners on a path forward.”

Shivprasad said that nine families interested in the buyout have filled out a form on the city’s website.

Kamrul Chowdhaury, another homeowner on 183rd Street, said he has spent hundreds of thousands of dollars on repairs to his home after flood damage.

“If they give me good money, offer me, I can sell out. I can go somewhere else,” Chowdhaury said. “It cost money, lot of money for rebuild.”

Once homeowners sell their properties to the city and move to a new location, the city says it will demolish the homes.
