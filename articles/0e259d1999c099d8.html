Seven former Yankees on 2027 Hall of Fame ballot

By Jeremy Nielsen · Pinstripe Alley · November 16, 2026

Fourteen retired players are debuting on the ballot this year: Jake Arrieta, Jay Bruce, Asdrúbal Cabrera, Starlin Castro, Wade Davis, Brett Gardner, Jon Lester, Andrew Miller, Buster Posey, Pablo Sandoval, Ervin Santana, Kyle Seager, Joakim Soria and Ryan Zimmerman. Gardner spent his entire career with the Yankees, while Castro, Miller, and Bruce spent shorter stints in the Bronx.

Pettitte’s voter share leapt from 110 in 2025 to 206 in 2026, for an increase of 20 percentage points. He’ll need a similarly giant step forward this year to have a good chance of eventually entering the Hall. Despite commanding universal respect from the baseball community, Pettitte’s relative lack of individual accolades or dominant individual seasons (as well as PED connections from the Mitchell Report) has prevented his case from truly taking off.

Similarly, Abreu’s case is similarly reliant on how voters view a long, consistent career without any periods of transcendent play. His voter share also saw a jump in 2026, but with only two inductees that year, some of that can likely be chalked up to a lack of stronger candidates for those who filled out a full ballot. He does not appear to be on track to earn induction.

A-Rod remains, as ever, a lightning rod. His links to steroids have probably permanently thwarted a statistical body of work which is just about as Cooperstown-worthy as anybody to have played in the past 50 years. He has garnered a slightly better showing than Manny Ramírez, whose similarly-tarnished case finally fell off the ballot with a 38.8 percent showing in his 10th and final year.

If Yankee fans had exclusive power over who made the Hall of Fame, Brett Gardner would probably be a first-year inductee. The fan-favorite outfielder played all 14 of his Major League seasons with the Yankees, filling the role of top-of-the-order spark plug with outstanding defense. Over the latter half of his career, he perfected his swing for Yankee Stadium, adding a steady diet of homers to his diet of singles, doubles, and oodles of walks. Gardner’s tendency for amusing antics (such as banging the dugout roof with a bat when he disagreed with an umpire’s call) endeared him to Yankees fans in a way few other players have in recent years. Brett doesn’t have a realistic shot at the Hall, but it’s still great to see his name appear on the ballot.

In addition to the annual BBWAA ballot, the Hall of Fame also assembles the Era Committee every year to evaluate other cases which fall outside the standard ballot’s purview. This year’s Era Committee ballot focuses on managers, executives, and other non-players, and includes former Yankee player and fiery manager Lou Piniella, erstwhile Yankee executive Brian Sabean, and Cardinals slugger/AL president Bill White, who broadcasted Yankee games along Phil Rizzuto for almost two decades. The results of the Era Committee election will be announced next month.
