Alexa Gasparotto lands historic triple Axel at the Skate America competition

By Lois Elfman · Amsterdam News · November 19, 2026

It was a moment beyond figure skating. When Alexa Gasparotto landed a beautiful clean triple Axel jump in the short program at Skate America, the international competition held last weekend in Everett, Washington, she became the first Black woman to land the jump in a Grand Prix competition. Her accomplishment was picked up by media outlets around the nation.

“For herself and us as her coaches, it wasn’t a surprise; she’s been landing them daily in practice and she was ready to do it,” said Tommy Steenberg, one of her coaches and her choreographer. “Of course, doing it in the moment has its own special feeling because that’s the moment you’ve trained for. … It’s really cool for her to have that historic element tied with that jump.”

This has been an impactful season for Gasparotto, 23. After placing well at Kinoshita Group Cup in Tokyo in September, she appeared on people’s radars as someone to watch. When a spot came open at Skate America, which is part of the International Skating Union Grand Prix, she was selected.

“Some of my favorite things in terms of her progression have been seeing her lock in and consciously lean into challenges and not let anything get in her way of having her eye on the prize, whatever the prize means,” said Steenberg. “A really good example of that was when she was preparing for [the] Kinoshita Group Cup. It was a big deal for her to have that international experience competing against some Olympians. The week before she was sick, but she navigated it and came into the rink with absolutely no excuses.”

Competing at Skate America gave Gasparotto a bye to the 2027 U.S. Figure Skating Championships in Salt Lake City in January. She will do another international competition, Warsaw Cup, later this month. That gives her the opportunity to refocus her energy after the triple Axel and bring that same skill to the rest of her program.

“It can’t be all about that one jump,” said Steenberg. “Through training the triple Axels in her practice on a daily basis, she has been preparing, but that moment at Skate America in that big arena was a lot. This helped prepare her for Nationals. Having the experience of realizing she did reset but it took longer than she wanted (she doubled her next triple jump), she’ll be more aware for next time. Every time she puts it out in a competition, it’s going to help normalize the jump pass for her and allow her to get it done.”
