Senator Cordell Cleare Invites The Community In For Mammogram Screenings In The Heart AOf Harlem

By Eartha Watts Hicks · Harlem World · November 15, 2026

As Breast Cancer Awareness Month drew to a close, New York State Senator Cordell Cleare brought a powerful message of prevention, early detection, and community care to the heart of Harlem, hosting a Breast Cancer Awareness Mammogram Event on Friday, October 30, 2026, at 215 West 125th Street.

Recognizing how deeply the disease affects families and communities, the senator also announced that she would undergo her own mammogram during the event, reinforcing her message through personal participation.

“As we close out Breast Cancer Awareness Month, I want to encourage members of our community to continue to monitor their breast health, as I see too many members of the community suffering from cancer,” Senator Cleare stated in her announcement.

In partnership with AdvantageCare Physicians (ACPNY) Harlem Medical Office, Lenox Hill Radiology, and EmblemHealth Neighborhood Care Harlem, Senator Cleare’s office extended the experience beyond the medical examination. Attendees who arrived with their insurance information and completed their screenings received refreshments, educational materials, signature pink hats, commemorative mementos, and even complimentary nail services, courtesy of the senator’s office.

These thoughtful gestures reminded everyone that taking care of one’s health warrants recognition and encouragement. Walk-ins were welcomed until the day’s screening capacity was reached. Individuals who could not be accommodated immediately were scheduled for appointments early the following week.

This initiative followed Senator Cleare’s participation in the annual Breast Cancer Awareness Walk. Extending the conversation from public awareness to direct access to screening services, this initiative encouraged residents to make breast health a continuing priority.

According to NYC Health + Hospitals, Black women, Latinas, and other women of color face significant challenges associated with advanced-stage breast cancer diagnoses and mortality. The importance of events such as Senator Cleare’s mammogram initiative becomes even more apparent when considering the disparities in breast cancer outcomes among women of color.

For women ages 40 to 74, NYC Health + Hospitals recommends mammograms at least every two years. Individuals with a family history of breast cancer or other risk factors should speak with their healthcare providers about whether earlier or more frequent screening is appropriate.

Breast Cancer Does Not Discriminate. While breast cancer is most commonly associated with women, men are not immune to the disease. According to the American Cancer Society’s 2026 estimates, approximately 2,670 men will be diagnosed with invasive breast cancer in the United States this year, nearly 1% of all cases. Men who notice unusual breast changes, lumps, or nipple discharge should consult a healthcare professional.

Although routine mammograms are not generally recommended for average-risk men, breast health awareness is important for everyone, regardless of gender. By bringing screening opportunities directly into the community, Senator Cleare and her healthcare partners helped reduce practical barriers that can keep residents from making breast health a priority.

Senator Cordell Cleare’s Breast Cancer Awareness Mammogram Event served as an important reminder that awareness is only the beginning, and taking 20 minutes to care for one’s health can make a meaningful difference. Early detection matters, and breast health deserves attention.

How to Schedule a Mammogram

Harlem residents and other New Yorkers can schedule breast cancer screenings through NYC Health + Hospitals by calling 1-844-NYC-4NYC (1-844-692-4692) or contacting their NYC Health + Hospitals or Gotham Health care team.
