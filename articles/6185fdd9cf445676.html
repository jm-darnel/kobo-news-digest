Raft of New Penalties Eat at Outdoor Dining Owners

By Katie Honan · The City · November 18, 2026

Restaurant owners could face dozens of new fines as part of the city’s new year-round outdoor dining program, adding more than 10 times the number of penalties faced under a previous version of the program.

Despite a vow by Mayor Zohran Mamdani to cut red tape across city agencies, businesses could face 36 individual violations through the program, from improper winterization coverings to blocking clearance. The changes also include moving up closing time from midnight to 11 p.m.

“You never want to see more small business fines,” Andrew Rigie, the executive director of the New York City Hospitality Alliance, said.

He warned against steep penalties — which start at $200 for the first offense and then rise to $500 for those after — that could discourage more restaurants from taking part.

“The city shouldn’t be using small businesses as an ATM,” he added.

“Outdoor dining has become a beloved part of New York City’s streetscape, and our new year-round program will make it easier for more restaurants to participate by reducing costs associated with the seasonal program,” an agency spokesperson, Will Livingston, said in a statement.

“Our goal is not to issue violations but bring restaurants into compliance with standards set to preserve quality of life for New Yorkers — like establishing clear pedestrian paths, requiring clean, and accessible setups — and that’s why we have a 30-day grace period for restaurants to fix issues before any violation is written.”

Year-round outdoor dining was approved by the City Council in August, increasing the pandemic-era program that helped keep bars and restaurants afloat during indoor shutdowns.

Fans and foes of the new fine structure sounded off last week at a hearing on the new change, sharing a large menu of suggestions.

Stacie Johnson, the district manager of Manhattan’s Community Board 4, had concerns over what constitutes a “clear path” around outdoor cafes.

“The intended clear path for pedestrians on the busiest streets and avenues is being severely encroached upon due to changes in interpretation,” she wrote. “The situation would worsen if a new law permits café enclosures without careful guardrails.”

Some welcomed more violations, so long as they were properly enforced — and not added in as the cost of doing business for restaurants.

Other people expressed concerns over the fully-enclosed structures — which are in demand during cold winter months — noting how dangerous it could be for immunocompromised New Yorkers.

“Outdoor seating must be safe and accessible for ALL disabilities, not just physical ones,” Talia Miele, who posted that she’s disabled with immune deficiency issues, shared online.

Sandra Jaquez, the president of the New York State Latino Restaurant Association, said she fields calls all day long from owners struggling to stay open.

But she was concerned with the increased number of potential fines, even if it provides clarity for the program. She was also concerned about the curfew for outdoor dining changing from midnight to 11 p.m.

“That really could hurt a lot of businesses,” she said about the new time limit. “That one hour, it is significant for us when it comes down to making a profit or losing that night.”

She said the fines could scare businesses away.

“We actually advocated very much to make this program permanent, it was a lifesaver — post-Covid we saw it was something that could be beneficial for some restaurants,” she said.

“Now some of these fines that are just attached to this, it makes it kind of irrelevant to even have this outdoor dining.”
