Yankees Birthday of the Day: Luis Tiant

By Andrés Chávez · Pinstripe Alley · November 23, 2026

Underrated ace Luis Tiant pitched for six different MLB franchises, making the most impact for the Cleveland and Boston in the sixties and seventies, respectively. However, in 1979 and 1980, a veteran Tiant made a bit of a splash, putting on the pinstripes near the end of his storied career.

Luis Clemente Tiant y Vega Born: November 23, 1940 (Marianao, Cuba) Died: October 8, 2024 (Wells, ME) Yankees Tenure: 1979-80

Tiant was born on November 23, 1940, in Cuba, a country with an incredibly rich baseball culture. Marianao was his hometown, and he was the son of famous Negro Leagues pitcher Luis Eleuterio Tiant, an All-Star for the 1935 and 1947 New York Cubans. The elder Tiant is now rightfully recognized as a 10-year big leaguer. As one can imagine, pitching was in Luis’ blood. His father used to dominate with a wide array of pitchers and unorthodox mechanics, so Tiant the Younger had someone to look up to both in baseball and in life.

Tiant starred in local youth teams in Cuba, and traveled to Mexico as a 16-year-old as part of an international tournament. That marked the start of a life between the two countries: he would play for the Mexico City Tigers from 1959 to 1961, and return to Cuba to be part of Winter Ball with the Havana Sugar Kings.

Playing in Mexico City, he caught the eye of Cleveland scout Bobby Ávila, who signed him for $35,000. Around this time, he married Maria del Refugio Navarro, whom he met in Mexico City, at a ballpark. With the political tension resulting from Fidel Castro’s rise to power in the sixties, Tiant wouldn’t return home in 1961. Instead, he started his journey with the Indians, with whom he developed until reaching the majors in 1964.

Tiant pitched for Cleveland from 1964 to 1969, and the highest they finished during his tenure there was third, in 1968. That was perhaps Tiant’s career year, as he spun an AL-best 1.60 ERA and 8.5 rWAR in 258.1 innings, though Detroit’s Denny McLain stole the spotlight with 31 wins to take the Cy (and MVP). He didn’t play a single postseason game for Cleveland despite posting a brilliant 2.84 ERA. Then, before the 1970 campaign, Tiant was traded to the Minnesota Twins in the same transaction that took Yankees great Graig Nettles to Cleveland, where he spent three solid seasons before joining New York in 1973.

Tiant got his first taste of postseason baseball with the Twins in 1970, pitching just 0.2 innings as they got swept in the AL Championship Series. Starting in 1971, he would spend most of his prime with the Red Sox until 1978, and posted a 3.36 ERA and a 122-81 record. He went to the 1975 World Series with them, losing to the Cincinnati Reds in seven games.

Tiant was a wrecking crew in that postseason run, limiting the Athletics to an unearned run in a complete game in the ALCS and winning two of the Red Sox’s three games in the Fall Classic, with a 3.60 ERA. He was beloved in Boston and became one of the team’s best stories, particularly when he reunited with his father during the season; they hadn’t seen each other for 14 years due to the strife between Cuba and the U.S. It was poignantly timed, as both the elder Tiant and his wife would pass away a year later, in Massachusetts with Luis nearby.

After the 1978 campaign, Tiant signed with the Yankees as a free agent for two years and $750,000. He made 30 starts for the 1979 Yanks, posting a solid 3.91 ERA and completing 195.2 innings. New York finished fourth in the AL East that year, missing the playoffs and mourning the loss of star catcher Thurman Munson.

That Yankees rotation was very talented and deep, with Tommy John, Ron Guidry, Tom Underwood, Rudy May, and Tiant, plus trade acquisition Gaylord Perry coming over at the deadline. So Tiant did not pitch in the postseason. New York, however, was swept by Kansas City in the ALCS, though between MVP George Brett and playoff series MVP Frank White, he would’ve had his hands full with those Royals.

Tiant would then spend the 1981 campaign with the Pittsburgh Pirates and the 1982 one with the California Angels before calling it quits, wrapping up an incredibly fruitful career.

A 1997 inductee to the Red Sox Hall of Fame, Tiant finished his MLB stint with a 229-172 record and a brilliant 3.30 ERA. He was in the top six of the AL Cy Young vote three times, all with Boston, and struck out 2,416 batters throughout his career.

Tiant led the junior circuit in ERA twice and went to three All-Star Games. Plenty of people say that he should be a member of the National Baseball Hall of Fame, but he is a notable absence from Cooperstown . He peaked at 30.9 percent in the HOF ballot, well short of the 75 percent needed for induction. Subsequent attempts to get in via the Veterans/Golden Era/Golden Days Committee were also unsuccessful.

Tiant is, however, a member of the Venezuelan Baseball Hall of Fame, a league in which he racked up impressive winter stats and earned legendary status.

After retiring, Tiant tried coaching. He became the pitching coach of the Los Angeles Dodgers’ minor-league system from 1992 through 1995, and also worked for the Chicago White Sox in 1997 in a similar role. He also coached at the college level and then with the Red Sox in 2022, down in their farm system.

Tiant died on October 8, 2024 , at age 83. The baseball world will remember his funky delivery, his eccentric cigars, his unconventional facial hair, and his dominance on the mound. His time with the Yankees was short, but his contributions to the game we all love can’t be denied — it’s no small feat to win fans of a rival over with your infectious personality on top of latent talent.

Happy birthday in the great beyond to the similarly great El Tiante.
