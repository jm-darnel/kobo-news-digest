Art Week NYC highlights lesser-known side of 'Caps for Sale' creator

By Roger Clark · NY1 · November 12, 2026

In her 1992 painting "Dreams," Esphyr Slobodkina depicts memories of a recurring dream in four different scenes.

Slobodkina was a pioneer of early American abstract art, and her work is the focus of a new exhibition at Rosenberg & Co. on the Upper East Side.

“Some of her work is purely abstract, but I think there is always kind of a reference to something identifiable or something that as a viewer you might connect with,” said Kadie Ross, director of Rosenberg & Co.

But the exhibition, titled "An Unbound Creativity," explores Slobodkina's full body of work. Her long career continued until her death in 2002 at age 93.

“We've been getting a lot of reaction, people come in and 'Caps for Sale' was such a part of their childhood or their kids' childhood or their grandchildren's childhood,” Ross said. “And I think the show has really let them put together these two images of a really wonderful woman who illustrated these children's books that were so much a part of people's lives but also had this very complete and expansive career as an artist.”

Born in 1908, Slobodkina left Russia with her family and moved to Manchuria, in present-day China. She moved to New York in 1928, at age 20, and became a founding member of the American Abstract Artists group, which helped pave the way for greater acceptance of abstract art in the United States.

An Upper East Side art walk is scheduled for Nov. 14.

“It's open, it's free to the public, we're really here to welcome people,” Ross said of the event, which offers visitors a chance to learn about an artist experiencing renewed interest and appreciation.
