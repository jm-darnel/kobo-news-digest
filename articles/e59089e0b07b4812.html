Travel treachery: The Thanksgiving holiday travel outlook is grim

By Rosemary Misdary · Gothamist · November 26, 2026

Traveling home after a Thanksgiving feast tonight could be a treacherous ordeal.

New York City officials warn travelers to prepare for strong winds up to 65 mph, heavy rain and flooding that could plague the region until late evening. According to the National Weather Service, the worst of the storm will come late afternoon through the evening. The National Weather Service advises waiting until after 10 p.m. or the next day to return home.

“If you're traveling, give yourself extra time, and remember, no destination is worth risking your safety,” said Mayor Mamdani in a public service announcement. “If winds pick up, get indoors. This wind is strong enough to knock down tree limbs and power lines.”

“It’s definitely downhill from here,” said National Weather Service meteorologist Matthew Wunsch. “There will be locally damaging wind gusts which could bring down trees and some power lines as well.”

“Never walk, bike, or drive through flooding,” Madani said. “If you live in a basement apartment or your home floods often, make a plan now. Move to higher ground before flooding starts.”

Even as the storm begins to subside by the late evening, travel conditions will still not be ideal. High wind warnings are in effect past midnight, and rain may persist through Friday.

If traveling on Thanksgiving is unavoidable, city officials said to allow more time, especially for public transportation.
