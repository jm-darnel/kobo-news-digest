The Group Trip Is Four Hours From JFK: Planning A Punta Cana Villa For 14

Harlem World · November 10, 2026

Haute Retreats, the luxury villa and concierge company, books a steady run of group trips to the Dominican Republic: milestone birthdays, bachelor weekends, families bringing three generations under one roof.

Among the more than 80 Haute Retreats Punta Cana villas, the big houses, with six to nine bedrooms for 14 to 18 people, are built for exactly that kind of trip.

About four hours from JFK

Delta, JetBlue and American fly nonstop to Punta Cana from JFK, and United and Arajet from Newark, in about four hours. The airport is roughly ten minutes from the gates of Puntacana Resort & Club, so a crew on a morning flight can be in the pool by mid-afternoon.

Two gated communities, two moods

Most of the company’s Punta Cana houses are inside one of two gated resorts. Puntacana Resort & Club is the larger: two championship golf courses, beach clubs, a spa and the Ojos Indígenas ecological reserve. Within it, Corales lines the Tom Fazio course, Arrecife and Playa Serena sit by the beach, and the Hacienda area is quieter, with lakes and lawns that suit families. Cap Cana, the other, has a marina, the Jack Nicklaus course at Punta Espada and Juanillo beach, and estates of up to 11 bedrooms.

Four houses built for a crew

Villa La Cana H8. Six en-suite bedrooms over two floors for up to 14, children included. Four of the six rooms have two queen beds, which suits cousins and friends who don’t mind sharing. A chef cooks three meals a day plus snacks, to menus agreed before you arrive, with a butler, daily maid service and someone on site around the clock. The pool sits in a walled garden next to a heated jacuzzi, and La Cana Beach Club is about two minutes away by golf cart.

Corales 114. Six bedrooms for 14 on the Corales course, with a butler, private chef, housekeeper, gardener and pool attendant on the team.

Ananda. Eight bedrooms for 16, split between a five-bedroom main villa and a three-bedroom guest house, which helps when two families want a little distance. Cook, butler, maid service and a 24/7 concierge are included, plus a one-way airport transfer for the whole group, and the resort adds two golf courses, two beach clubs, seven restaurants, padel and pickleball.

Villa Guacamaya. The biggest of the four: nine bedrooms for 18 on Playa Juanillo in Cap Cana, with a chef, a sous chef, a butler and 24-hour security.

Settle the money before the flights

Two conversations save a group trip. The first is about what the staff covers: in most Punta Cana villas the chef’s work is included but groceries and drinks are billed separately, so agree a food and bar budget up front. The second is about rooms. The large-groups guide recommends bedrooms of similar quality so nobody feels they paid the same for less, and if the rooms are not equal, decide before booking whether you split by couple, by bedroom or evenly.

New York City’s public schools close for midwinter recess from Feb. 15 through Feb. 19, the Presidents’ Day week. On its Punta Cana page, Haute Retreats lists Presidents’ Day with Christmas and New Year as the first weeks to go in the dry season, so a crew set on that week should choose its house now. February also brings the whales: from mid-January through March, humpbacks crowd into Samaná Bay in the northeast, a day trip from Punta Cana by road or small plane.

Ask for the sleeping arrangements room by room, so the couples, the kids and the light sleepers each land in the right place. Confirm the minimum stay for your dates in writing; outside the holidays, three nights is typical at Ananda and La Cana H8, four at Villa Guacamaya. Ask whether the airport transfer is part of the stay. And have the concierge book the beach club days, tee times and the whale trip before you fly, so nobody spends the first morning on the phone.

“A group trip works when nobody has to be the travel agent,” said Sabrina Piccinin, Founder and CEO of Haute Retreats. “In Punta Cana the house, the chef and the beach are ten minutes from the airport. Decide who sleeps where and who pays for what before you book, and the rest of the week belongs to the people you came with.”
