NYC Thanksgiving travel: Trains, buses, traffic will be busy, so get on the road now

By Catalina Gonella · Gothamist · November 24, 2026

Before the turkey, there’s the travel.

Tuesday and Wednesday will be among the busiest travel days of the year in New York City and around the country as people head to their destinations ahead of Thanksgiving on Thursday. The AAA expects major metro areas like ours could see double the normal traffic congestion, especially in the afternoon and early evenings.

“It's a busy traffic week. It'll be a little lighter than last year, I predict, because gas prices in New York are up by 86 cents from $3.15 a gallon average to $4.01 average,” said Schwartz, a former city traffic commissioner whose claim to fame is popularizing the term “gridlock.” But he said there will still be “plenty of traffic.”

We’ve compiled information from agencies around the metro area to help you get to your destinations as smoothly as possible this week.

Thanksgiving Day Parade Road closures

Whether you’re hoping to watch the parade or avoid it, you’ll want to take note of the route if you’re traveling through Manhattan. Barriers will be going up as early as Wednesday for balloon inflation.

For a full list of closures, check the DOT’s notice here.

Behind the wheel

If you’re not reading this from the road (presumably and hopefully as a passenger), you’re probably leaving the city too late. Schwartz said the best time to leave the city, if you’re driving, is Tuesday before 1 p.m. But you’ll get another lighter traffic window Wednesday morning, when again, “the earlier you leave the better,” according to Schwartz, with heavy traffic starting at 1 p.m. and lasting through 6 or 7 p.m.

“A number of people will try to get out early,” Schwartz said. “We are expecting a lot of traffic after 2 [p.m.], and that'll affect the Van Wyck, the Belt Parkway, the Grand Central, BQE, New Jersey Turnpike.”

Travel won’t be easy for anyone — but at least those heading to JFK will have a smoother time this year because of the recent completion of the Van Wyck Expressway, which included a new HOV lane for multi-occupant vehicles, according to Schwartz.

“Not telling you to leave too late, but there is an extra lane if you're taking a taxi cab or you if you have two or more people, so that's good news,” Schwartz said.

Things will ramp up again on Sunday as everyone heads back into the city — and again, trying to get back sooner rather than later will allow drivers to avoid the worst traffic, according to Schwartz.

In the skies

Wednesday, Sunday, and this coming Monday are projected to be the busiest travel days, according to the agency.

Last-minute travelers whose flights depart Thursday might have the easiest time, according to Schwartz.

“I find the best time to fly is the morning of Thanksgiving, so just 12 hours will make the world of difference, between 8 o’clock in the evening on Wednesday versus 8 o’clock in the morning on Thursday,” he said.

On the subway and PATH

On Wednesday, the subway and the Staten Island Railway will operate on a regular weekday schedule, according to the MTA. On Thursday, they will operate on a Sunday schedule.

Those headed to the Upper West Side for the parade or otherwise should take note of service changes on the B and C lines on both days, because of the Macy's Balloon Inflation Celebration on Wednesday and then the parade on Thursday. Those involve some intermittent closures of the 72nd and 81st Street stations on those lines.

On the buses

MTA buses will also operate on a Sunday schedule on Thanksgiving Day, with many expected to experience delays, affected by reroutes, detours and frozen zones, particularly those on routes operating near the parade route in Midtown Manhattan.

Then on Friday, most buses will operate on a reduced weekday schedule, with the majority of routes running, but coming less often. Limited-stop service will not run on some routes in Manhattan and most routes in Staten Island; for more information, check the MTA’s advisory.

For regional bus travel, bus commuters may experience intermittent departure delays during weekday afternoon peak periods due to new traffic patterns in place because of the construction of the new Midtown Bus Terminal, according to the Port Authority advisory.

NJ Transit is increasing bus capacity to Newark Airport on Wednesday, and then also adding extra buses to the schedule Thursday morning and early afternoon hours for those getting to and from the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade. On Friday, NJ Transit will be providing buses to shopping centers throughout New Jersey. For more information, check the NJ Transit advisory.

On the rails

On Friday, the LIRR will run on a weekday schedule, and the Metro-North will run on a Saturday schedule, and off-peak fares will be valid on both. For more information, check the MTA’s advisory.

Amtrak added two additional Northeast Regional round trips between Washington, D.C. and New York Penn Station for Nov. 24-25 and Nov. 28-29.

NJ Transit is stepping up train routes throughout the holiday weekend as well. On Wednesday, trains will operate on a weekday schedule on all lines, with the additional “early getaway” service from Penn Station, New York, Newark Penn Station and Hoboken Terminal starting at 2 p.m. on the Northeast Corridor, North Jersey Coast, Raritan Valley, Morris & Essex and Port Jervis lines. Newark Light Rail, Hudson-Bergen Light Rail and River Line Light Rail service will operate on a weekday schedule.

On Thursday, NJ Transit trains will operate on a weekend/major holiday schedule, with extra trains on the Northeast Corridor, North Jersey Coast, Raritan Valley, Morris & Essex and Port Jervis lines to accommodate people heading to the parade.

On Friday, NJ Transit trains are on an enhanced weekend/major holiday schedule on all rail lines with additional trains accommodating shoppers during the morning and afternoon peak periods on the Northeast Corridor, North Jersey Coast, Raritan Valley, Morris & Essex, Gladstone, Montclair-Boonton and Port Jervis lines.

For more information, check the NJ Transit advisory.

Oh, and one more thing …

Schwartz really wants to remind all New Yorkers that while Friday might be a work holiday for many, it isn’t a parking holiday.

“Friday is the biggest shopping day of the year, and it's also the day traffic agents write the most parking tickets because people feel like it's a holiday, and it is a holiday for many people. They're off from work, but traffic agents are not off from work, and all rules are in effect on the Friday after Thanksgiving,” he said.
