NYC, State Sue To Block Trump Rule on Families With Undocumented Members in Public Housing

By Samantha Maldonado · The City · November 23, 2026

A coalition of nearly two dozen plaintiffs including New York City sued the Trump administration Monday to stop a rule barring families with undocumented members from living in public housing or receiving certain rental vouchers from taking effect next month.

“We will not stand by while families are put at risk,” Mayor Zohran Mamdani said in a statement. “New York City is proud to join our peers across the country in fighting this cruel and unlawful rule.”

New York Attorney General Letitia James in the same court Monday filed a separate suit on behalf of more than 20 states also seeking to block the rule on similar grounds.

“This rule runs counter to our nation’s values and the very purpose of these housing programs,” she said in a statement, citing a national “housing crisis.”

HUD did not immediately respond to a request for comment. In recent weeks, HUD Secretary Scott Turner has said the rule would prevent “illegal aliens and ineligible noncitizens” from benefiting from government-funded programs.

That’s not how the programs have worked. Mixed-status families have for years been allowed to live in subsidized housing, though only eligible individuals in the household receive a rental subsidy that is prorated based on who is eligible rather than the full household size.

HUD’s new rule — which the Trump administration proposed in February and is set to take effect on Dec. 14 — would no longer allow that to happen.

If implemented, about 20,000 households across the country would be at risk of losing their homes if they live in public housing, receive Section 8 project-based rental assistance or participate in the Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher Program.

Those households consist of nearly 80,000 people, most of whom are eligible for HUD housing assistance, according to the lawsuits, which cite HUD figures.

‘Impossible Choice’

Monday’s lawsuits challenging the rule follow another lawsuit that a group of housing advocates filed Thursday, also in the U.S. District Court in Massachusetts. One of the plaintiffs in that suit is the Alfred E. Smith Houses Resident Association, which represents tenants of a NYCHA development in Lower Manhattan. There are 16 mixed-status families at risk of losing their homes in the Smith Houses, according to the suit.

Another plaintiff, named as Jane Doe #2, is a Honduran woman living in a project-based Section 8 apartment in New York City with her husband, who is a U.S. permanent resident, and their three daughters, all of whom are U.S. citizens. According to the lawsuit, Doe #2 is the primary caregiver for her children, including her middle daughter, who has autism. Doe #2’s husband is the family’s sole provider, as she is unable to work because of pain from an accident on a job.

Lucy Newman, an attorney for the nonprofit Legal Aid Society who is working on the suit filed last week, said mixed-status families should not voluntarily move out of their homes before the rule kicks in. But she said it would force families to make an “impossible choice.”

“If implemented, this rule inhumanely compels mixed-status families to choose between staying together and losing critical rental assistance or separating to allow eligible members to maintain it,” Newman said. “If implemented, this rule will cause the mass displacement of thousands of mixed status families in New York City from their homes.”
