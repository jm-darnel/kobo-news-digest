Greater Harlem Chamber Of Commerce Hosts Festive New York City Marathon Reception

By Keith Forest

All roads led uptown as The Greater Harlem Chamber of Commerce (GHCC), in partnership with New York Road Runners (NYRR), hosted the 2026 Marathon Week in Harlem.

Running from Sunday, October 25, right up to the official kickoff of TCS New York City Marathon Day on Sunday, November 1, the eight-day fall tourism initiative offered international runners, their families, friends, and local patrons an unparalleled opportunity to discover Harlem’s deep cultural roots while supporting local businesses.

A signature centerpiece of Marathon Day in Harlem was the vibrant energy along the Percy Sutton Harlem Miles—a 42-block stretch running down Fifth Avenue from 135th Street to 96th Street. Honoring the visionary civil rights leader and former Manhattan Borough President who was instrumental in transforming the marathon into a five-borough celebration, the course surged with community pride. Gospel choirs greeted runners crossing the 135th Street Bridge, live DJs energized 125th Street, and performers delivered soul, jazz, R&B, and Latin rhythms at Marcus Garvey Park and 116th Street to power distance runners through their crucial three-mile stretch.

At the 22nd mile marker, the official Sounds of Harlem main stage showcased an eclectic mix of Harlem-rooted talent. The lineup featured hypnotic beats from percussionist Baba Don, intergenerational performances from IMPACT Repertory Theatre, powerful spoken word from Harlem Bomb Shelter, and funk-driven grooves from The Jeff Foxx Band. Harlem’s own DJ Mitch was on hand to keep the runners musically motivated as they surged toward the finish line.

Along the course, history was made at the elite level. Thirty-one-year-old Swedish runner Andreas Almgren delivered a stunning performance in his marathon debut, taking the men’s title in 2:06:01 and pulling away in the final mile to win by 13 seconds. On the women’s side, Kenyan star Sheila Chepkirui claimed top honors, earning her second NYC Marathon victory with a picture-perfect finish of 2:24:36.

The celebration reached its peak at the official Marathon Day in Harlem reception. Set to great music by DJ Tall Guy, civic leaders, elected officials, residents, and international visitors gathered to cheer on the runners and enjoy a live performance by the stylish husband-and-wife singing duo Uptown Royalty.

Closing out the reception, guest of honor Keshia Sutton-James reflected on the 50th anniversary of the five-borough race and her grandfather Percy Sutton’s contribution. “Percy Sutton was a true visionary who understood that when you build big, you build a lasting legacy,” shared Sutton-James. “As runners pass through Harlem, we celebrate the legacy of two Black men—Percy Sutton and Ted Corbitt—who ensured Harlem remains forever woven into the history of the New York City Marathon.”

Established in 1896, the Greater Harlem Chamber of Commerce once again demonstrated its historic commitment to driving economic vitality, tourism, and cultural pride throughout the community, wrapping up a landmark week that showed why Harlem remains the beating heart of the NYC Marathon.
