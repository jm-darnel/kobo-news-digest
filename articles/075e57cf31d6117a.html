Newtown High School builds its first football team in 129 years

By Stef Manisero · NY1 · November 10, 2026

“I wake up every morning, and it's like, ‘Man, I coach Newtown football,’” said Wayne Crawford.

“People were telling us, ‘You're never going to be able to do it, this isn't a football school, we don't have kids who play football,’” Crawford said. “And I was like, ‘Well, we got to either find them or make them.’”

We first met Crawford and some of the players last season, when the team was holding its first practices. But this year, things are different.

“Now we're an established football team playing real games, preparing for real games, so there's really nothing like it,” Crawford said.

“I always tell the kids, ‘You don't gotta do it, you GET to do it,’ and the fact that I get to do this is just amazing,” Crawford said.

But being a startup program comes with growing pains. The majority of the team is made up of freshmen and sophomores, and only two players have any prior high school football experience.

“We’re playing established 3A varsity programs with largely a roster comprised of junior varsity players,” Crawford said.

For many students on the team, English is their second language.

“So you'll see me on my phone with a kid showing them what cover three looks like,” Crawford said. “It's part of the fun of being a Newtown coach!”

Then there's the journey just to get to practice. While the field near their high school is being renovated, the team is practicing in Flushing Meadows Corona Park.

“At 3:30 p.m. the bell rings, they all sprint down to my room and grab their bags,” Crawford said.

“We walk to the bus stop, wait for the bus, it’s packed sometimes and we can’t get on,” said Miguel Infant, the team's center and middle linebacker.

“And we have big bags on,” added Newtown quarterback Carmaine Allen. “Sometimes you have to go 10 of us on the bus, 10 of us on that bus, and we have to wait for them to come off the other bus, then walk 10 minutes from the bus.”

All in all, it's a 45-minute trip just to get there.

“We’re not the best team, but at least we put in more effort than a lot of teams and show up,” said running back Zavion Williams.

Showing up, by the way, to a field with no lines.

“Football without lines! I think I'm the originator of that!” Crawford joked.

But the coach chooses to see the obstacles as opportunities.

“It helps spatial awareness. Not having the lines there, now they need to be more cognizant of where they are on the field,” Crawford said.

Right now, it's not about the score, but rather the little wins each week.

“On offense, we want to have an eight-play drive, we want to get two first downs within that time. Defensively, we want to get a couple to three and outs,” Crawford explained.

Because, as any coach knows, those little wins stack up.

“And then when we watch the film — we had great blocking,” Crawford said. “Our left tackle, left guard created a great hole, we had a great lead block, I'm like, ‘Oh my God, look at us, we're looking like a football team!’”

And before you know it, there's the unanimous highlight of the season to date.

“I did score the first touchdown,” Infant said with a smile.

“It was a defensive touchdown, and I'm the offensive coordinator,” Crawford laughed.

“It was a fumble recovery, actually,” Allen said. “I didn't even know we scored.”

“They threw a lateral, a lateral backwards pass, it went in the end zone, we're like, ‘Jump on it, jump on it!’” Crawford said.

“So I picked up the ball, I looked around, the referee put his hands up — touchdown! History right there,” Infant said.

“It's like, ‘Oh my God, we did it, we did it,’” Crawford said.

“It's crazy, because this school is 129 years old and being the first person to ever score a touchdown for this school — it hits a special place in my heart for me,” he said. “I'd relive that moment a hundred times if I could.”

They know there will be plenty more moments ahead.

“We’re out here every day working harder, trying to get that first win in school history, and it'll come eventually,” Infant said.

“Everybody expects us, oh, we're this, we're that, we’re too young, we're too small, and I don't care about none of that, we just go in there and play,” Allen said.

They'll keep playing their way.

“This isn't the end of our story, this is the beginning of our story, and it's going to be better, and that's just what I got to keep preaching to them because I believe it, I believe it in my heart that at some point this program is going to be a program that people respect,” Crawford said.
