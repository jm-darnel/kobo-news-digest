Federal judge in NY denies release of man shot by ICE in the Bronx

By Arya Sundaram · Gothamist · November 21, 2026

A federal judge ruled that Oscar Belgal, the man who was shot in the Bronx last month by federal immigration officers, will remain in custody as the Trump administration pursues his deportation case.

District Judge Hector Gonzalez of New York's Eastern District said U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement had the legal basis to arrest Belgal, whom ICE agents were trying to capture in October when police say federal agents fired seven rounds at Belgal’s SUV, wounding him in the neck.

Gonzalez found that ICE had an independent, credible basis for its arrest warrant, including biometric confirmation of Belgal's identity, prior criminal history, and federal records indicating he lacked legal immigration status, according to the order issued Friday.

Legal Aid lawyers argued that ICE did not properly evaluate whether Belgal should be detained before taking him into custody.

Judge Gonzalez disagreed, citing his prior arrest on a first-degree robbery charge and a conviction for fourth-degree grand larceny, among other things. While ICE officials were late to file proper documents formally initiating immigration removal proceedings for Belgal, Gonzalez ruled the tardiness did not lead to prejudice or legal harm.

Belgal, a citizen of the Dominican Republic, has said that he didn’t know that the men who ambushed and shot him were ICE agents until he later woke up in the hospital. According to an NYPD account, Belgal accelerated his SUV forward as plainclothes federal officers approached him with their guns drawn. His 5-year-old son was nearby when ICE agents opened fire, according to his attorneys and police accounts.

Lawyers had argued that the agents’ use of deadly force violated Belgal’s constitutional rights and agency policies, but the judge said the habeas petition filed by Belgal only covers whether someone’s custody is unlawful or not; it doesn’t extend to alleged police misconduct during an arrest.

Gonzalez did write, however, that Belgal could pursue an excessive-force claim through a separate legal action.

Belgal is currently detained in the Metropolitan Detention Center in Sunset Park, Brooklyn.
