NJ agency eyes housing along the highways in Central Jersey

By Michael Sol Warren · Gothamist · November 26, 2026

“Transit-oriented developments have been happening worldwide for a very long time,” Kolluri said. “But I think it's kind of unusual for a toll authority to look at its property and see how it can basically build live-and-ride facilities close to its asset, and then also tie it into mass transit.”

“To bring down housing costs, we need to build more homes and make better use of property the state already owns,” Sherrill said in a statement. “We’re advancing new housing near transit so families can more easily get around. The Turnpike Authority’s Land Plan builds on that progress, creating opportunities to expand housing and support local businesses while generating revenue for our transportation system.”

Kolluri said developing these properties could create new revenue streams for the Turnpike Authority, and mitigate soaring construction costs due to tariffs and supply chain challenges. He described it as complementary to the 3% toll increases that have hit in each of the past five years and are set to occur again in the new year.

Two of the five properties would be in South Brunswick. The first of those is a roughly 30-acre site on Friendship Road. Under the plan, that property could become 13 single-family houses or multifamily townhomes.

Kolluri had especially high praise for the Exit 8A park-and-ride.

“I can't think of a better site for a development, a housing development or an apartment development,” Kolluri said. “Literally steps away from the development you have a mass transit service to New York and other places.”

Kolluri said more land along the Turnpike and the Parkway in North Jersey may be made available for development in the future.

“There certainly may be, especially on the Parkway as you're going up north and maybe even on the Turnpike, opportunities for us to monetize and put some of these properties into play,” Kolluri said.
