On way to House speakership, Hakeem Jeffries heads back to school in Brooklyn

By Dan Rivoli · NY1 · November 23, 2026

It was a kind of Homecoming Day at Midwood High School to honor alumnus Rep. Hakeem Jeffries — class of 1988 — who is now the Democratic leader nominated to become the U.S. House speaker in the new Congress.

His message to seniors is to learn from failure as others have before them, putting his own twist on a quote often attributed to Winston Churchill that success is not final, failure is not fatal, and what matters is courage.

“A knockdown is different than a knockout. That’s the Hakeem Jeffries remix,” Jeffries told the seniors in the auditorium.

After his remarks, Jeffries met with student reporters and its principal, Robert Quinlan.

“Our students are just so exhilarated at the fact that such an awesome [alumnus] came back today,” he said.

Jeffries also met with seniors in student government, like Zhiqi Li, the mayor of the high school.

“We were just talking about U.S. government and also the midterms in AP Gov, so it’s definitely very exciting and actually refreshing to see the actual potential speaker of the House in person,” Li said.

Later, Jeffries took questions from NY1 in an exclusive interview about priorities in the new Congress.

“One of the first things that we’ll do is House Democrats is move legislation to prohibit members of Congress from trading stock,” he said.

Jeffries said he’s still hearing out his caucus on how to handle the bipartisan deal that passed the Senate to bring down the costs of infrastructure and energy projects.

“There are different perspectives. I’m supportive of the general need to do permitting reform, but we want to make sure it’s actually done in the best possible way in a responsible fashion,” he said.

Even with incoming socialist Democrats from New York opposed to him as speaker during an internal caucus vote, Jeffries praises the democratic socialist in City Hall, Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

“Mayor Mamdani and I have had a very communicative relationship over the last year or so,” he said. “And his success is the city’s success, and so I’m looking forward to continuing to work together with him.”

Even as he is poised to take this national post, Jeffries said his Brooklyn constituents can still expect to see him in their neighborhoods and houses of worship.
