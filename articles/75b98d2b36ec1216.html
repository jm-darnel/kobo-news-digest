MTA chair skeptical of Amtrak's big idea to unify Penn Station's railroads

By Clayton Guse · Gothamist · November 20, 2026

This column originally appeared in On The Way, a weekly newsletter covering everything you need to know about NYC-area transportation.

Amtrak has floated a “through-running” system at Penn Station where commuters can ride a single train between New Jersey and Long Island without transferring, but MTA Chair Janno Lieber gave the idea a reality check this week.

I interviewed Lieber live on stage at WNYC’s The Greene Space on Tuesday, and the conversation unsurprisingly turned to plans to improve Penn Station, including a plan to have LIRR trains travel into New Jersey and allow NJ Transit trains to continue to Long Island. Lieber said he fears the construction needed to implement the plan would be too disruptive to service at the Midtown train hub.

For starters, crews would need to remove columns and tracks to put in a wider platform that allows riders to quickly get on and off trains to speed up service. That all spells major disruptions for the 600,000 daily subway and train riders at Penn Station, Lieber noted.

“You can't just focus on Penn Station and start tearing apart the tracks,” Lieber said. “That’s like we’re going to have to kill the village to save it.”

Lieber also noted that NJ Transit’s infrastructure and trains are far older and less reliable than the Long Island Rail Road’s. He questioned whether there’s even room to run trains from New Jersey to Long Island through Amtrak’s East River tunnels, which the MTA plans to soon use for Metro-North trains into Penn Station through its Penn Access project.

He said he was open to the idea of through-running as a larger transportation concept for New York City’s terminal stations.

“Just talking about tearing up Penn Station to make wider platforms because somebody might want to go from Secaucus to Sunnyside is not the way to answer that question,” he said. “If you want a real through-running system, let's talk about all of the issues and do it in a systematic way.”

As Amtrak pushed ahead on its plans to rebuild Penn Station earlier this year, the federal railroad released a study showing through-running could be a way to expand service at the hub without expanding its footprint. A second report from Amtrak on regional train service in and out of Penn is in the works. That report likely will include further examination of through running.

Through-running is an idea that’s been on transit planners’ minds for decades. The Regional Plan Association has been pushing an idea called the “Trans-Regional Express” that would consolidate parts of the MTA and NJ Transit’s rail operations under a single agency.

Lieber didn’t seem too intrigued by the idea.

“I’m a dyed-in-the-wool-New Yorker,” he said. “I don’t have any regional aspirations at all. I’m not even sure I’m welcome on Staten Island.”

NYC transportation news this week

Meet the man who fixes NYC’s subway mosaics. When the colorful, intricate tile mosaics featuring the names of subway stations like Columbus Circle or Cortlandt Street need to be replaced, the MTA relies on just one man to get the job done. Robert Shenfeld, 73, lives 250 miles north of the city in Syracuse. And he has a monopoly on restoring the MTA’s century-old tile mosaics. Read more about which stations he’s worked on here.

Horse carriages come to an end. Mayor Mamdani signed a bill into law banning the carriage horse industry in New York City by June 2028.

"The whole damn system" will finally get new subway signals. During the event at WNYC’s Greene Space, MTA Chair Janno Lieber shared that the agency plans to upgrade every mile of subway track with new signal technology by 2050.

MTA looks to AI to spot people on subway tracks. The agency plans to spend up to $10 million to test a camera system on the front of a train car that alerts the operator to any hazard on the right of way as well as any erratic movement by passengers on the edge of subway platforms.

Brooklyn's Flatbush Avenue redesign gives bus riders priority. Two new bus lanes have been installed in the center of the road between Grand Army Plaza and Livingston Street, and passenger cars are now banned 24 hours a day.

MTA to pilot body-worn cameras on EAGLE teams. After two altercations with riders, the bus fare inspector teams will be retrained on de-escalation and emotional intelligence, and there will be a dedicated fare inspector customer incident hotline to report bad interactions.

MTA warns of major budget deficit without financial aid. The agency’s leaders proposed a balanced budget for next year, but the financial plan also projects a $585 million deficit in its operating budget for 2028, which could grow to a $979 million deficit in 2030 if the city, state and federal governments do not help balance the books.

Curious Commuter

Have a question for us? Use this form to submit yours and we may answer it in a future newsletter!

This week’s Curious Commuter question comes from an audience member at Tuesday’s event in The Greene Space, which has been edited for clarity:

"The MetroCard for years had a display on the turnstile that gave you the charge, whether you got a free transfer, how much you had left on the card. Now we've got this big OMNY paneI and I have no idea whether I've been charged a new fare, whether I got a free transfer, what's left on my card, whether my card is bad, whether it's out of money, whether it's low. Why? Any plans to improve that?"

Lieber's answer

“ I'm not going to make excuses. This is a system that has been designed for well more than 10 years. We’re going to make it deliver the features and the information that you want. It’s not a secret that we have struggled with this contractor who's responsible for all the software, including the features that you just enumerated so well. We have to give that kind of information. In the meantime, if you get online and do an OMNY account, you can confirm all the stuff that you said you want to know, but that's not good enough in the future. You're absolutely right. We have to have that type of information visible at the point of sale or transaction, at the turnstile, and on those validation machines. So point taken, and we're working on it. I can tell you it's coming. Probably another six months to a year before we start to see that kind of data be available as you tap.”
