Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: Ryan McMahon

By Nick Power · Pinstripe Alley · November 23, 2026

When Ryan McMahon joined the Yankees at the 2025 Trade Deadline, it looked like a match made in heaven. After suffering through subpar defense at third from the likes of Oswald Peraza and Oswaldo Cabrera for most of the season — as well as an ill-advised stint at the position by Jazz Chisholm Jr. — the Bombers finally had someone who could man the hot corner. He did so with aplomb, putting on a fielding clinic that recalled the glory days of Graig Nettles. And, while his bat fell off a bit from the consistent 20-homer, 20-double pop he’d showcased the prior six and a half seasons in Colorado, McMahon looked poised to bring consistency and a high floor to what had been something of a revolving door position for the Yankees.

It didn’t turn out quite that way.

Grade: F

2026 Statistics: 124 games, .213/.279/.357, 76 OPS+, 78 wRC+, 1 OAA, 0.5 fWAR

2027 Contract Status: Under contract for $16 million

Ryan McMahon’s 2026 was a true dud. Despite holding down his starting job for pretty much the whole season, he never got any momentum going with the bat and reportedly remained quite obstinate to any feedback that included analytics. The 31-year-0ld put up a 78 wRC+, third-worst among the 41 third basemen who logged at least 350 plate appearances (narrowly edging out Peraza) and second-worst among all Yankees third baseman since 1970 who met the same PA requirement (it turns out Scott Brosius’ 1998 was a lot more fun than his 2000).

McMahon actually squared the ball up fairly well when he did make contact, hitting the ball hard 44.8 percent of the time he did connect, but goodness, was that few and far between. He struck out in nearly 30 percent of his at-bats, an untenable figure for a player who only managed 11 home runs in 360 plate appearances. The only players in Yankees history to come to the plate 350 times and strike out as frequently as McMahon are Aaron Judge and Giancarlo Stanton — who have made up for their punchouts with their power — and 2026 Chisholm , who had a similarly disappointing season of his own.

Perhaps even more alarming, what had been superlative defense fell off. The numbers bear that out — McMahon was worth 0 OAA and -3 DRS at third — but the eye test does as well. He would occasionally make exemplary plays, albeit less frequently than down the stretch in ‘25.

However, the veteran seemed to lose focus on routine plays, failing to attack the ball with the same confidence and intentionality he had showcased throughout his career. Most memorably, he kicked off his team’s defensive unraveling in Game 2 of the ALDS, booting an easy grounder and setting the tone for what would be one of the most humiliating Yankees performances in recent memory.

For a team with World Series aspirations in 2027, none of this points to a likely return. When asked whether he planned to upgrade at third this offseason, Brian Cashman offered an endorsement of his incumbent that was pointedly tepid. “We’ll look at it, without a doubt,” the Yankees’ front-office head at the GM Meetings on November 10th. “We struggled there this year a little bit more than I would’ve expected. That doesn’t mean Ryan McMahon’s not capable of a lot more, too.”

McMahon is still owed $16 million next season, meaning his ouster from the Bronx is not a fait accompli. The Yankees could give him another shot to rebound next year, offload part of his contract in a trade, or cut bait entirely and release him; all options are on the table. A major factor will be whether the team can aquire a suitable alternative — they’ve been linked to options including free agent Alec Bohm, Houston’s Isaac Paredes, Texas’ Corey Seager, and Teruaki Sato, who’s been posted to MLB by the Hanshin Tigers of NPB.

There are many reasons the Yankees failed to reach their expectations this year, and it would be unfair to put their early exit primarily on McMahon’s shoulders. Still, he failed to contribute meaningfully, performing at replacement level and leaving a black hole at third base which made that position a liability all season. And for that, you get an F.
