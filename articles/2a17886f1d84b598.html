Mayor Mamdani, Chancellor Samuels Launch Schools Technology Coalition Following AI Ban

Harlem World · November 17, 2026

Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani and New York City Public Schools (NYCPS) Chancellor Kamar Samuels today announced a continued pivot away from reliance on technology-based instruction with the launch of the Technology in Schools Coalition.

“A strong education is about human connection, curiosity and the ability to work through difficult problems independently. That’s exactly what we want to foster in classrooms across New York City. That is why, in September, we implemented a moratorium on generative AI for students in grades 2-K through eighth grade. As technology companies increasingly push the idea that AI can solve every challenge in our classrooms, we must remember that there is no substitute for a great teacher or the friendships that help young people learn and grow. The Technology in Schools Coalition will help guide our approach to these questions and ensure that decisions about our students’ education are shaped by the people who know our schools best. We look forward to working alongside teachers, parents, unions, education experts and others on the frontlines to build classrooms that put the needs of New York City students first,” said Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

“In September, we set a new standard for protecting our students’ data, learning, and critical thinking skills with our 2026-2027 AI and screen time policy. Today, with the announcement of the Technology in Schools Coalition, we continue to raise the bar for what responsive, community engagement looks like on this topic,” said Schools Chancellor Kamar Samuels. “Through the Coalition, NYC families, educators, and partners will directly shape our AI and screen time policy for next school year and beyond, ensuring their experiences and perspectives are accounted for. I look forward to working in partnership with the Coalition to keep our students safe and to protect their learning through responsible technology guardrails, and I’m grateful for their willingness to share their time and expertise on behalf of our students.”

This group is a response to concerns from parents, educators, and elected officials, as well as a growing body of research on the dangers of excessive screen use and the cognitive offloading allowed by AI that affects negatively children’s learning. Additionally, the coalition will explore whether research supports some targeted use of AI, such as learning a second language.

“As a former teacher, I know the safety and education of our children is paramount, and any technology in our classrooms must be backed by peer-reviewed research. Convening this coalition is a good step in the right direction, and I look forward to working with the chancellor to keep our kids safe,” said Councilman Eric Dinowitz, Education Chair.

“Technology cannot be blindly implemented into the education of our most impressionable minds. We must take a serious, informed approach before integrating evolving tech into our classrooms. A coalition comprised of local leaders and advocates is the community-centered approach we have called for. We look forward to the recommendations put forth by the coalition that ensures NYC remains a tech stronghold while not jeopardizing the educational experience of the largest public school system in the country,” said Council Member Carmen De La Rosa, Chair of the Committee on Technology.

“Technology in classrooms should be in service of advancing educational achievement and increasing students’ understanding of the world around them. If these tools are not properly stewarded, they can replicate systems of bias, exacerbate inequality, and lead to unknown risks for data privacy and learning outcomes. The use of educational technology must be guided by students, educators, and families to ensure they are serving our public school communities and not the profit-margins of tech corporations. I applaud Mayor Zohran Mamdani and NYC Public Schools Chancellor Kamar Samuels for implementing a moratorium on AI in schools through 8th Grade and establishing a task force that includes the voices of impacted community members to evaluate the use of technology in schools.” – NYS Senator Kristen Gonzalez (D-59), Chair of the Committee on Internet & Technology

“I spent more than two decades in the classroom. Teachers know what learning looks like. The wonderful look in a child’s eye that follows necessary, productive struggle. It cannot be replaced by a screen,” said Council Member Rita Joseph, Chair of the Higher Education Committee. “Our young people deserve classrooms that build critical thinking, curiosity, and real human connection. I applaud Mayor Mamdani and Chancellor Samuels for putting educators, families, and students at the table, because the people who know our classrooms best should be the ones deciding what belongs in them.”

“Students build strong educational foundations through dedicated teachers, evidence-based curriculum, meaningful human connection and the opportunity to learn and grow alongside their peers. AI must not undermine these essential parts of the classroom experience. Today’s announcement reflects Mayor Mamdani and Chancellor Samuels’ commitment to putting students’ education first. By bringing together experts who understand the day-to-day realities of our schools, they are ensuring that the people closest to our classrooms have a meaningful role in shaping how technology is used in our schools and how we educate the next generation.” – Assemblyman Bobby Carroll, Chair of the Committee on Libraries and Education Technology

“The appropriate use of technology in our schools has been one of the main areas of concern and feedback raised by parents and school communities to the Panel for Educational Policy,” said Alan Ong. “As Chair of the Panel, I applaud the opportunity to bring together stakeholders to engage in meaningful dialogue about how we can move our schools forward in this rapidly evolving technological landscape. I look forward to the Panel’s role in this coalition and to working collaboratively with the Mayor, the Chancellor, and our school communities on this important topic.”

This builds on previous work by Mayor Mamdani and Chancellor Samuels to increase academic rigor in the school system including:

• Developing and instituting the nation’s most aggressive policy on Artificial Intelligence

• Launching free, high-quality 2-K for more than 2,000 families; expanding to 10,000 additional families next year

• Continued investments in high-quality 3-K and pre-K, saving families thousands of dollars per year

• Continued progress on reducing class size through focused planning and a $1.5B billion investment

• Raising teacher pay

• Dramatically improving procurement practices

• Investing nearly $70 million into preschool education for students with disabilities

• Expanding New York City Reads and New York City Solves
