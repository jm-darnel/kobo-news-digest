These singles gathered in Central Park to swap their exes’ stuff

By James Ramsay · Gothamist · November 19, 2026

This column originally appeared in the Looped In NYC newsletter. Sign up here.

One of the hardest parts of breaking up, especially in this town, is dealing with your ex’s stuff. Most newly-eligible New Yorkers simply don’t have the space for it — in their apartments nor in their hearts.

But of course, one person’s baggage can be another’s treasure, so the upstart dating network Park Bench recently hosted “The Ex-Change,” a mixer of sorts for people to swap mementos.

“The singles that we've talked to are always looking for ways to meet other singles, but in a not explicitly singles-coded way,” said Kasey Luo, a co-founder of Park Bench. This idea had a convenient value proposition: Whether or not you came away with a phone number, you wouldn’t leave empty-handed.

“New Yorkers love free stuff,” Luo said.

Arihant Barajatya — who, for what it’s worth, was one of the few male attendees and the only one game enough to talk to us — was giving away a pair of unisex sweatpants he’d shared with his ex.

“ I don't want anything that reminds me of her now,” he said.

While everything was gratis, the few dozen items people brought, laid out on picnic blankets, covered a range of monetary values. A paperback copy of “2666.” A pack of Marlboro Golds. A navy blue Burberry raincoat with the tags still on.

“I got this Le Labo clutch,” said Brianne Adiola, who was parting with some denim outfits. “I honestly only picked it up because I know Le Labo's expensive.”

But for the people discarding some of these big-ticket items, the benefits were clearly priceless.

When I asked Eliwa Onanga why she was parting with a chic pair of saddle shoes, she said they had nothing to do with an ex and everything to do with her own personal growth.

“ I think there was a time where I felt like I really had to show up super ‘professionally’ at work,” she said. “And I don't necessarily feel that way anymore.”

Margo Hera, who’d recently broken up with her partner of eight years, was giving away a steel sword from the series “One Piece” that could likely fetch three figures.

“ I bought [my ex] this as a present, and she left it,” she said. “It's an anime thing. I've never watched anime. I watched two episodes of the live action with her and I couldn't get into it.”

Read into this what you will, but that wasn’t the only sword-as-a-gift on offer.

“He told me he wanted dangly earrings, so I actually bought these for him,” said Julia Cooper, holding up a pair of sword earrings. “Then he never wore them, and he kinda, like, gifted them back, and I just decided I didn't want anything that reminded me of him at all.”

And that wasn’t the only thing Cooper was giving away for free — she also had some dating advice to offer: “ If you're feeling any sort of negative type of feelings, just break up. If you're spending more of your days unhappy with someone, it's not gonna get better, and the relief that you feel, it's really great.”
