Andrea Stewart-Cousins, New York’s first woman Senate leader, dies at 76

By AmNews Staff Reports · Amsterdam News · November 27, 2026

Andrea Stewart-Cousins, the longtime New York state Senate majority leader who made history as the first woman and first Black woman to lead the chamber, has died. She was 76.

Senate Majority Communications Director Mike Murphy confirmed her death in a statement released Friday:

“It is with profound sadness that I announce the passing of New York State Senate Temporary President and Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins, who died on November 27, 2026, at the age of 76.

Serving the people of the 35th Senate District was one of the greatest honors of her life. Throughout her decades in public service, Stewart-Cousins made a profound impact on the lives of New Yorkers. She was a groundbreaking leader who shattered one of New York’s highest glass ceilings, becoming the first woman to lead the New York State Senate.

She is survived by her brother Robert Stewart Jr and her three children, Kevin, Steven, and Candice, and her six grandchildren. She was preceded in death by her husband, Thomas Cousins.

We ask that you keep Majority Leader Stewart-Cousins’ family, friends, and staff in your thoughts and prayers during this difficult time. Information regarding memorial services and public tributes will be shared in the coming days. We will have further information about the legislative succession in the coming weeks.”

No cause of death has been announced. Funeral arrangements are pending.
