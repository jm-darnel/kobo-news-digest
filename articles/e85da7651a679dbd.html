Yankees 2026 Roster Report Cards: George Lombard Jr.

By Jonathan Farrar · Pinstripe Alley · November 20, 2026

Few positions, or the players manning them, have been debated as intensely as shortstop for the New York Yankees over the last several seasons. Heading into 2026, many fans hoped the club would find a way to move on from the Anthony Volpe experience. However, the club made no external moves and started the season with José Caballero slotted in at the position as the team waited for a rehabbing Volpe to return.

Along the way, injuries and performance, both struggles by the incumbents and red-hot play in the minors, led the Yankees to hand the reins at shortstop over to 21-year-old George Lombard Jr. The rookie would spend August, September, and October at shortstop for the Yankees in what really is the throne of NYC.

In terms of how it went, the staff here at PSA averaged out to a B-minus. In academic speak, that would mean Lombard understood the core material and performed above average, but did not master the assignment.

Grade: B-

2026 Stats: 42 G, 166 PA, 23 R, 36 H, 4 2B, 1 3B, 7 HR, 22 RBI, 2 SB, .237/.285/.414, 92 OPS+, 93 wRC+, 0.4 fWAR, 1.1 bWAR.

2027 Contract Status: Pre-arbitration eligible.

Before reaching the show, Lombard was on an absolute tear in the minors. Starting the season in Double-A, he hit .312 with a .971 OPS in 20 games, which earned him a ticket to Scranton. Lombard’s season looked like it might be coming off the rails after an adjustment period, and then he sprained two fingers, which sidelined him for a month and caused him to miss the MLB Futures Game. However, he came back in mid-July and forced the Yankees’ hand right before the Trade Deadline, going on a 14-game heater to the tune of a .377 average, .477 OBP, and 1.081 OPS.

On August 4th, after surviving the Trade Deadline himself, and with the Yankees not acquiring an outside option at shortstop, the club called Lombard up to the show. The kid finished the minor league season slashing .284/.411/.498 with 12 home runs. This performance led Baseball America to name him the Yankees’ Minor League Player of the Year for 2026.

Lombard’s 2026 season at the professional level, and the basis for our grading, really is a story of three parts. The first was a euphoric honeymoon stage. Lombard committed just one error in his first 14 games and racked up eight Defensive Runs Saved (DRS) in his first 100 innings, looking like a potential Platinum Glove winner. This all came as he homered in his first pro at-bat and slashed .320/.393/.560 with a .953 OPS across his first 25 at-bats. It was too good to be true.

After the rise came the fall, as Lombard slumped with the glove and bat. In late August, Lombard made the wrong kind of history, committing an error in six straight games and tying a 105-year-old franchise record. With the stick, big league pitchers started getting him to chase and roll over breaking balls, causing his average to sink to .217 by mid-September. The vibes from some fans really went from “next” to “next Volpe.”

To the kid’s credit, he righted the ship down the stretch and saved himself in the third act. From September 9th through the end of the regular season, Lombard’s defense stabilized to league average, and he hit five home runs in his final 16 games. He carried that confidence into the postseason, going 3-for-4 in the Wild Card opener against Boston and finishing October batting .333 (6-for-18), albeit with an error in the ALDS.

I’ll save the hype train for future preview articles. However, despite only having an 86 mph average exit velocity, Lombard finished the season pacing for 27 home runs over 162 games. There’s also a lot of room for growth, as Lombard had a 17.3 percent walk rate at Triple-A and only a six percent rate in the majors through his first 42 games, or essentially a quarter of a season. The defense, especially when playing beside a big league-quality third baseman, was fine for someone who was just a few months into being able to buy an adult beverage.

Lombard’s minor league season was an A+, while his rookie season comes in at a B-. With some growing pains behind us and a few more likely ahead, here’s to hoping Lombard can continue to improve and develop into a difference-maker.
