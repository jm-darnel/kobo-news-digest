100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade steps off in Manhattan after postponement

By Rosemary Misdary · Gothamist · November 28, 2026

Compared to Thanksgiving Day, Saturday’s weather conditions are much more favorable – clear, sunny, not too cold and only a little windy. The mayor’s office said it expected more than 3.5 million spectators along the 2.5 mile parade route, which runs from the Upper West Side to Herald Square.

“Honestly, I feel like people are even more energetic after the delay,” said spectator Adam Turner, adding that this was his fourth or fifth parade. “People have been waiting and waiting and waiting for this, so it’s really exciting to see everyone showing up.”

This year’s parade will showcase 28 floats, including a display of marionettes dedicated to Tony Sarg, the event's creator. The event will also feature performances from around 10 marching bands, appearances from the casts of “Hamilton” and “Chicago,” and performances from Smash Mouth, LL Cool J and Keke Palmer, among others.

Mike Meyer came from Granger, Indiana, and secured a spot around 4 a.m. Saturday to watch his daughter perform with the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign’s marching band, the Marching Illini.

“Being able to go with our daughter goes with the Marching Illini, they always bring the energy and it’s really fun to see all of the bands come out with all the energy that they have,” he said.

As part of the celebration, 100 inflatables will glide through New York City’s streets, including the iconic giant character balloons. Ten of the balloons, including Bluey, Hello Kitty, and Joe Cool and Woodstock, are new additions to this year’s parade. Another new addition, the Blue Hippo, is returning to the parade for the first time since 1931 – when the original Blue Hippo was lost at sea but never found. The Blue Hippo’s handlers are wearing special purple patches in memory of Fiona, the hippo who died at the Cincinnati Zoo earlier this month.

Spectator Rebecca Lee Daniels, said she drove all the way from Asheville, North Carolina, on Friday for the parade.

“It’s a dream come true,” she said. “I love the parade more than anything in the world because it’s the one day everyone agrees on everything. [They’re] just so nice to each other and it’s the best of humanity, so I’m just really happy.”
