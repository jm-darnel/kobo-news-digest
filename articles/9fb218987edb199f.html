NYC inspection blitz halts construction at sites tied to fatal Brooklyn building collapse

By David Brand, Joe Hong · Gothamist · November 17, 2026

New York City buildings inspectors have halted construction completely or in part for at least five projects tied to a pair of engineers in the wake of a fatal Brooklyn building collapse, a Gothamist analysis shows.

City records show the Department of Buildings visited projects handled by engineer Jieming Wang and his New Jersey-based LMW Engineering Group. They also inspected projects where MD Ariful Islam and his Queens-based RI Consulting Services drafted city-approved structural plans or performed safety monitoring.

Wang was the licensed engineer who submitted structural drawings and plans for supporting the adjacent buildings during excavation of the Flatbush project involved in last week’s building collapse. Wang’s drawings, which were approved by city plan examiners, did not call for steel bracing or foundational support known as “underpinning” at the site of the collapse, as Gothamist reported on Friday.

Islam was hired to conduct monitoring of the excavation and shoring work at the site as special inspector, according to city documents. Islam applied to withdraw from the project on Nov. 12, a day after the collapse, but Department of Buildings spokesperson Andrew Rudansky said the agency has not yet approved his request.

Wang and Islam did not respond to multiple requests for comment. A person who answered the phone at Wang’s office extension last Friday immediately hung up after a Gothamist reporter identified himself.

City building inspectors issued partial stop-work orders at four of the job sites, including the conversion of a Greenwich Village parking garage into a luxury condo complex and the development of a new six-story apartment building next to the 7 train tracks in Woodside, Queens.

Just one of the stop work orders involves excavation – the focus of the investigation into the cause of the building collapse in Flatbush.

In the case of a planned four-story apartment building in the Bronx, inspectors halted all construction after first finding broken safety fencing around an excavation site on Sunday, according to complaint data on the buildings department website.

An inspector returned Tuesday and issued a full stop-work order after the project’s general contractor “refused inspection of active site and ordered me to [leave] the premises with threats of locking me on the inside,” according to another complaint posted to the agency site.

Rudansky declined to provide more details about the safety blitz after Gothamist shared findings on the inspection locations and stop-work orders.

“To protect the integrity of our ongoing investigation and the related safety sweeps, we are not providing additional information about the targets, scope, or results of the sweeps at this time,” Rudansky said.

He repeated previous written statements he provided to Gothamist in which he said it was the responsibility of the project engineer — in this case, Wang — to determine “whether the plans have considered factors relating to their site-specific design and ensuring those plans are code compliant and safe for construction.”

It does not appear that any of the inspection sweeps sites identified by Gothamist are linked to the same owners of the Flatbush project. A limited liability company tied to Robert Fischman and Judy Swimer received approval to construct a three-story apartment building on the lot in 2025.

They applied to build a nine-story complex with a ground-floor grocery store in September but the city has yet to approve or reject the application.

Fischman and Swimer did not respond to phone calls or visits to their Brooklyn homes.

Engineer Jieming Wang

Building department records show city inspectors visited at least 17 projects that list Wang and LMW Engineering as the engineer who submitted structural and excavation plans, or other site-safety designs. Those projects include the high-profile conversion of a Greenwich Village parking garage at 332 West 11th St. into a luxury, six-story condo complex. The city issued a partial stop-work order to halt all exterior work at the site after finding unsafe scaffolding on Nov. 15, records indicate.

Developer Charles Dunne did not respond to an email seeking comment about the order to pause work at the garage conversion site.

Inspectors also halted some interior work at a new six-story apartment building at 56-12 Roosevelt Ave. in Woodside, Queens, on Nov. 16 after seeing a construction worker standing on an interior scaffolding without a safety harness, buildings department records show. Building documents list Wang as structural engineer for the project.

Reached by phone Tuesday, Green JJ Management administrator Helena See said the company took over the plan from another firm.

“We did not choose that engineer. We purchased the property with that plan,” See said.

Property records show Chang’s company acquired the site in November 2024. Documents filed with the Department of Buildings and signed by Chang show his company began notifying neighboring owners of upcoming demolition work in February 2025.

See said she found Wang to be a competent engineer. But the collapse, she said, “is a very unpleasant thing to hear.”

Inspectors visited another Wang project at 1021 Summit Ave. in the Highbridge section of the Bronx twice in the wake of the disaster. The address is the site of a proposed new four-story apartment building. The buildings department halted all exterior work at the construction site on Nov. 12 after an inspector noted that scaffolding did not meet city code requirements, records show.

In a brief phone interview, building owner Jeton Rugovaj said the partial stop-work order was “no big deal” and that city inspectors were focusing on small problems rather than “structural stuff” in their safety sweeps.

Officials also inspected a job site at 54-47 Little Neck Parkway in Douglaston, Queens, three times since the collapse. The department halted all facade and exterior work after finding safety hazards, like loose netting above a neighboring school playground and missing guardrails, on Nov. 13, according to agency data.

Building records list Wang as the engineer for foundation and earthwork for that project, which is owned by Harvest Church of New York on the border of Queens and Nassau County.

Harvest Church did not respond to emails about the stop-work order.

Special Inspector MD Ariful Islam and RI Consulting Services

Cityapproved documents list Islam as the engineer who designed the excavation safety plans for a planned four-story apartment building at 58 West 184th St. in the Bronx.

Records show the buildings department issued a partial stop work at the project following a construction enforcement sweep on Nov. 15.

An inspector returned on Tuesday and issued a full stop-work order after the project’s general contractor blocked the inspector from entering the site, according to a complaint on the building department website.

Inspectors visited another Brooklyn site where Islam designed the excavation support plan on Nov. 13, records show. The buildings department did not stop construction during the visit.
