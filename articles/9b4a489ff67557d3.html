Schools could explore 'other options' for search due to Google AI tools

By Jillian Jorgensen · NY1 · November 24, 2026

Google says that starting next year, it will be impossible for schools to separate artificial intelligence tools from Google Search.

“What they’re doing is giving us an ultimatum, and they’re saying, look, either you are going to get generative AI or not use our search engine,” Schools Chancellor Kamar Samuels said in an interview with NY1 on Tuesday.

Samuels says that would run afoul of the city’s policy banning the use of generative AI for the vast majority of students. The city currently blocks the use of AI mode and Google’s AI chatbot, Gemini, on devices issued by the school system.

Google has told the city that next year, students using Google Search would either have access to the search engine’s AI mode, or could use a pilot AI technology called Guided Learning.

“I don’t want guided learning from an AI chatbot. I want real learning from a teacher. And so we are going to continue to push back against this,” Samuels said.

In a statement, Google said that the search, along with Gemini and any tools using AI, will be off by default until schools decide to turn it on. The company said Guided Learning would offer step-by-step help with complex problems.

“We are actively building these experiences in partnership with educators, listening closely to ensure our products support students and teachers,” a spokesperson said.

Other cities, including Los Angeles and Chicago, are also pushing back. Samuels says he is willing to look elsewhere for search functions.

“We will look at other options that are out there. Because, you know, this is — this is a path we don’t want to go down,” he said.

But Google is deeply enmeshed in classrooms: Hundreds of thousands of children use Google’s Chromebook laptop, and submit work using the Google Classroom app.

Kelly Clancy, a parent of three who is part of the AI Moratorium Coalition, says that her children have been able to access the company’s chatbot even when logged into their school accounts, when trying from a non-school device.

“I think this is a great time for us to say, what does it look like to wean our school district off of Google so that they can’t blackmail us? And I’ve been really heartened that the chancellor and the mayor both seem really angry about this, and I hope that what it results in is classrooms where teachers and administrators are calling the shots, not Google tech bros,” she said.

Samuels says the conversations with Google are ongoing.

“I’m hopeful, but I’m also surprised at how tone deaf they’ve been, quite frankly,” Samuels said. “We have to move with caution and make sure that we are very clear that we’re going to do everything within our power to not succumb and not be — and not have them push this, these AI products down our throats.”
