Exclusive: Mamdani administration will refer chronic housing discrimination to the state

By Tandy Lau · Amsterdam News · November 23, 2026

For years, the city has levied steep fines against brokers who illegally turn away apartment seekers because they use housing vouchers. But some brokers have dodged accountability by moving from one brokerage firm to the next while continuing to reject applicants who receive rental assistance. Now Mayor Zohran Mamdani is expected to announce on Monday that the city will refer repeat offenders to the state, which can suspend or revoke their real estate licenses.

“When a New Yorker is denied an apartment because they use a housing voucher, that isn’t just an obstacle in their housing search — it is illegal discrimination,” said Mamdani. “Brokers should not be allowed to evade accountability by moving from one firm to another. We’re changing that. If you repeatedly discriminate against New Yorkers, you shouldn’t be able to do business as usual. We will use every tool available to protect renters and hold repeat offenders accountable.”

Housing subsidies help lower-income New Yorkers pay rent at a time when the state’s housing cost burdens are among the highest in the nation . But voucher holders are regularly stigmatized by landlords and brokers. Some voucher programs, such as CityFHEPS, are used disproportionately in majority Black and Brown communities, where residents face higher rates of homelessness and housing insecurity. As a result, source-of-income discrimination can overlap with racial discrimination.

According to the Mamdani administration, more than 1,500 real estate brokers and firms were reported over the past five years for unlawful source-of-income discrimination. That includes advertisements that discourage voucher holders from applying or that express a preference for applicants without vouchers. Almost one in six of those brokers and firms were repeat offenders, and together they accounted for roughly 37% of all complaints. One alone was the subject of 30 complaints.

“Discrimination in rental housing is one of the most common and harmful forms of discrimination that New Yorkers face in this City,” said Christine Clarke, chair and commissioner of the city Commission on Human Rights. “For too long brokers have been able to avoid accountability for their discriminatory behavior, which will no longer be tolerated. Finding an affordable apartment in this city is hard enough, discrimination should not make it harder.”
