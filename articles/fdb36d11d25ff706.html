New York City Council Introduces Legislation To Return Vacant Rent-Stabilized Apartments Back to the Rental Market

Harlem World · November 12, 2026

Today, New York City Council Member Farah Louis introduced legislation at the Council’s Stated Meeting to address the ongoing issue of vacant rent-stabilized apartments citywide that have remained off of the rental market.

Introduction 1165 would require the Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) to conduct an analysis of the city’s stock of vacant rent-stabilized units and develop a plan to ensure those units return to the rental market. According to a review of state vacant rent-stabilized unit data by The City Reporter, building owners reported 57,000 vacant units in 2025. Queens led all boroughs with a vacancy rate of 6.6%. Speaker Julie Menin previously held the bill prior to becoming Council Speaker.

“In the face of a housing crisis, it is unconscionable that over 50,000 rent-stabilized units are sitting vacant and off the market while so many New Yorkers struggle to find an affordable place to live,” said Speaker Julie Menin. “Every unit that remains unavailable represents a missed opportunity to house a New Yorker who needs it. I am grateful to Council Member Louis’ leadership in advancing solutions that could finally address this longstanding issue.

“New York City cannot afford to leave tens of thousands of rent-stabilized apartments sitting vacant while families across our communities struggle to find an affordable place to call home,” said Council Member Farah N. Louis. “With approximately 57,000 reported vacant rent-stabilized units citywide and nearly 279,000 rent-stabilized apartments in Brooklyn alone, we need a clearer understanding of why these homes remain unavailable and what it will take to return them to the rental market. This legislation will require HPD to examine the financial barriers, physical conditions, habitability issues, and existing incentives that contribute to these vacancies, and develop actionable recommendations, from renovation assistance and financial support to stronger enforcement. Every vacant apartment represents a potential home for a New Yorker who needs one, and we must pursue every available opportunity to expand access to affordable housing.”

Last year, the average rent-stabilized apartment rented for $2,593, not including Staten Island. New York City currently has nearly one million rent-stabilized units, a majority of which were built before 1974. The Price Index of Operating Costs (PIOC) for rent-stabilized buildings, which measures price changes in building costs, including fuel, insurance, labor, taxes, and maintenance, increased by 5.3% this year and is expected to increase 4.1% in 2027. Building owners in every borough except The Bronx saw increases in their net operating incomes.
