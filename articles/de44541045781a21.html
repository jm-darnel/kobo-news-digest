Yankees Potential Free Agent Target: Hiromi Itoh

By Peter Brody · Pinstripe Alley · November 24, 2026

With the looming expiration of the CBA creating an artificial deadline crunch for posted NPB players to sign with an MLB club, much of the attention in the wake of the Tarik Skubal signing turns to the trio of stars heading stateside. Teruaki Sato grabs the headlines, but he is accompanied by a pair of starting pitchers poised to make an instant impact in Kaima Taira and Hiromi Itoh. Yesterday, we dove into Taira’s unique profile and today we’ll look at Itoh, who as it happens presents a convenient foil to his fellow countryman.

2026 Statistics (NPB): 26 starts, 172.2 IP, 10-10, 3.23 ERA, 2.81 FIP, 2.98 xFIP, 21.8% K%, 4.7% BB%, 1.26 WHIP

2027 FanGraphs Depths Charts Projections: 26 games started, 104 IP, 4.48 ERA, 4.43 FIP, 22.3% K%, 9.3% BB%, 1.31 WHIP, 1.1 fWAR

In many ways, Itoh is the opposite side of the coin from Taira. Whereas Taira is a pure power pitcher with tantalizing stuff but questions about his durability, Itoh relies on command and finesse to overcome his underwhelming raw stuff but is a workhorse when it comes to posting. While Taira represents the new wave of Japanese pitchers with premium stuff looking to test themselves in MLB — think Shohei Ohtani, Yoshinobu Yamamoto, Roki Sasaki, Tatsuya Imai, Kodai Senga, and Yusei Kikuchi — Itoh is something of a throwback to the previous generation of Japanese starters more in the mould of a Masahiro Tanaka, Hiroki Kuroda, or Kenta Maeda.

Itoh is a six-year starter for the Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters of NPB. He won the Eiji Sawamura Award (NPB’s Cy Young equivalent) in 2025 after going 14-8 in 27 starts with a 2.52 ERA, 2.27 FIP, and 195 strikeouts in 196.2 innings and was named an All-Star in 2022. He is incredibly durable, throwing at least 146 innings in each of his six years in professional ball, and has tossed at least 170 innings in each of the last three seasons. Across his six seasons in NPB, Itoh has posted a 65-51 record in 149 starts with a 2.92 ERA and 902 strikeouts across 1,000.2 innings. The 29-year-old righty appeared for Japan in the 2023 and 2026 editions of the WBC and memorably was the losing pitcher of record after giving up the game-winning home run to Wilyer Abreu as Japan bowed out in the quarterfinals against eventual winners Venezuela.

In terms of comps in MLB, Itoh is practically a mirror-image of lefty starter Shota Imanaga. Both pitchers are supremely talented at limiting walks and inducing chase and whiff. They both possess fastballs in the low-90s that get a surprising amount of carry thanks to high spin rate and spin efficiency. However, those type of fastballs thrown in the 90-92 range walk a razor thin line between effective and disastrous, both Imanaga and Itoh possessing concerning profiles as extreme fly ball pitchers.

Another good comp for Itoh would be Tomoyuki Sugano based on their lower velo fastballs and expansive pitch mix. I do fear that Itoh would suffer a similar fate as that suffered by Imanaga and Sugano. Over the last two seasons, Sugano and Imanaga posted the second-worst and third-worst home run per nine rate of any pitcher with at least 300 innings. However, he might be able to mitigate that thanks to his Yu Darvish-like ability to learn new pitches at will, Itoh experimenting with close to two dozen different variations over his career. I would expect interested teams to recommend a scaling back of four-seamer usage exactly as we have seen with Imanaga in his three seasons in MLB.

Without the tantalizing stuff of Taira and two years his elder, Itoh is expected to command less money and years. The consensus across several projections forecasts a two-year, $20 million deal for the righty starter. Given his consistent ability to post and the need across the league for innings, that feels like a more than reasonable price for a plug-and-play number five. The Yankees have ample depth for that role, while Itoh is probably even less likely than Taira to accept a deal that moves him into the bullpen. It is for all these reasons that we have not heard reports linking the Yankees to Itoh. A righty fly ball pitcher with a slow, crushable four-seamer is a terrifying prospect in Yankee Stadium and probably the chief reason why I would not expect the Yankees to pursue Itoh.
