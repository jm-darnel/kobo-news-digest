Mayor Mamdani Mobilizes Citywide Winter Response, Expands Code Blue And Supercharges Snow And Ice Removal

Harlem World · November 9, 2026

Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani today announced a sweeping, citywide mobilization to prepare New York City for winter weather, including a $25 million investment in an expanded Code Blue system and a supercharged snow and ice clearing operation.

Across City agencies, thousands of workers, new equipment and enhanced emergency protocols are being readied to protect New Yorkers from extreme cold and keep the city moving through the winter months.

According to the National Weather Service (NWS), a strong El Niño is expected to bring a warmer, wetter winter, increasing the likelihood of rain, sleet and ice, as well as potentially heavy, wet snow during major storms.

“We have spent the past year preparing for any challenge winter throws our way. We have trained our workers, readied our equipment and strengthened our protocols because keeping New Yorkers safe can’t begin when the first storm hits,” said Mayor Mamdani. “This winter, we’re doing things differently — we’re expanding Code Blue to reach more New Yorkers in need, equipping New York’s Strongest to clear snow and ice faster and leaving no stone unturned when it comes to protecting our neighbors from the cold. No New Yorker should face the dangers of winter alone, and no part of our city should be left behind.”

“Throughout 2026, we have been working closely with our partner agencies, nonprofit agencies, elected officials and other stakeholders to update our winter plans and include all that we learned during the extreme cold temperatures and snowstorms that New York City experienced earlier this year,” said NYCEM Commissioner Christina Farrell. “Winter weather can be dangerous for everyone, but particularly for vulnerable populations. With just a few weeks until the official start of the winter, we encourage New Yorkers to review and update their winter heating safety and emergency plans. Visit nyc.gov/beready to get started.”

Investing $25 Million in an Upgraded Code Blue System

Last winter, Mayor Mamdani coordinated dozens of agencies during the city’s first-ever 24-hour Enhanced Code Blue activation. The administration opened new warming shelters, deployed warming buses and activated the City’s Strengthening Communities partner network in person for the first time.

This winter, Mayor Mamdani is building on those efforts with a new, upgraded Code Blue system that will put more outreach teams, vehicles and warming sites in the field as cold weather intensifies.

Backed by $25 million in new City funding, the system will scale up resources according to the severity and duration of cold, snowy and icy conditions. New measures include:

· Reaching New Yorkers wherever they are. More than 300 additional canvassers will follow designated walking and driving routes through high-impact areas in all five boroughs, covering 75% of locations where homeless New Yorkers are known to stay. Teams will engage every person they encounter, rather than relying on predetermined lists. This approach builds on the City’s success with Code Red operations this summer, which engaged 20 times more people than previous efforts.

· Bringing medical care directly to New Yorkers in need. Up to 55 NYC Health + Hospitals (H+H) WARM mobile units will provide medical support and transportation to warming centers, medical facilities and other warm locations. Each team will include a registered nurse or nurse practitioner.

· Opening more places to get warm. H+H will open at least three hospitals as warming centers and will open all 11 facilities during the most severe weather. The Department of Homeless Services (DHS) will activate eight drop-in centers, four new warming centers and 20 warming buses at peak level, scaling resources according to Code Blue level. The Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH) will extend hours at four syringe service providers to give clients overnight access to warm spaces.

· Adding more low-barrier shelter options. The City is adding more than 600 single- and double-occupancy rooms, creating 800 beds, giving homeless New Yorkers more options for safe, warm shelter during the coldest days of the year.

· Hiring additional nurses. Nurses will partner with outreach teams during extreme weather to check on people’s health and safety, provide on-site clinical care and competency assessments and escalate to a higher level of care when appropriate.

These new measures will build on existing Code Blue practices, including wellness checks on older adults by NYC Aging and open-door policies across the DHS shelter system.

DOHMH will also activate health-focused outreach teams to expand operations in key areas of concern, including major transit hubs.

Supercharging Snow and Ice Clearing Operations

New York’s Strongest are preparing to work even faster this winter. The Department of Sanitation (DSNY) has spent the past year strengthening its snow and ice clearing operations, with a particular focus on pedestrian infrastructure and ensuring streets and sidewalks are accessible to older adults and people with disabilities.

New this year, DSNY has:

· Mapped pedestrian infrastructure routes into snow removal routes. DSNY has formally incorporated every bus stop, sidewalk and crosswalk into its internal route maps, allowing the agency to better deploy snow clearing resources more effectively during and after snowstorms.

· Established new forecast triggers. New protocols will allow the agency to pre-position contractor equipment to clear pedestrian infrastructure before and after snowfall.

· Expanded coverage on Staten Island. Eight new plow sector routes have been added in the borough’s hilliest areas, bringing more equipment to its steepest and narrowest streets.

· Upgraded its plows. A total of 678 plows and spreaders have been outfitted with new composite plow blades that adapt to the contours of roadways, clear snow more effectively and reduce damage to roads, bridges and highways.

· Restored its existing equipment. All 705 large and small salt spreaders and other snow removal equipment have been serviced and restored following extensive use last winter.

· Acquired its first snow blowers. The agency has acquired 120 snow blowers to clear bus stops and crosswalks faster, along with an expanded fleet of pickup trucks to transport equipment where it’s needed.

· Stockpiled salt. DSNY has secured 500 million pounds of salt to deploy across the city.

· Overhauled the Emergency Snow Shoveler program. DSNY has already registered more than 3,000 Emergency Snow Shovelers, compared with only a few hundred at this point last year.

City agencies are also working together to enhance DSNY’s snow and ice clearing capacity:

· The Department of Transportation (NYC DOT) purchased $2 million in small snow clearing equipment like tractors and gators with plow attachments and established new protocols with JCDeceaux and Citi Bike to clear bus stops, bikeshare infrastructure and other priority transit areas faster.

· NYC Public Schools (NYCPS) has acquired 10 all-terrain vehicles (ATVs) equipped with snowplows, 650 snow blowers and approximately 3,000 shovels, ice choppers and other tools. The department has already upgraded existing equipment with 190 new snowplow and blade attachments.

· The Parks Department (NYC Parks) is preparing nearly 400 vehicles operating out of 11 garages across the five boroughs to clear more than 2,000 miles of pathways following snowfall, prioritizing park perimeters, commuter hubs and other high-traffic areas.

· The Department of Citywide Administrative Services (DCAS) is stocking the City’s Central Storehouse with up to six months’ worth of calcium chloride and rock salt.

Prepositioning Emergency Resources

NYCEM and the City’s first responders are constantly monitoring conditions and preparing to activate emergency operations centers to coordinate agencies and deploy resources when needed.

As New York City readies for a wetter, heavier winter, NYC Parks and City agencies are preparing to activate the Downed Tree Task Force to respond to fallen branches and trees. The New York City Police Department (NYPD) is preparing to deploy tow trucks during storms to clear roads, assist stranded motorists and keep snowplow routes open.

NYPD will also assist in downed tree operations, escort DSNY snowplows, transport DHS employees providing services for at-risk New Yorkers and deploy officers at high-volume traffic locations to help emergency vehicles reach their destinations safely.

NYCEM is also updating mutual aid agreements to ensure the City can access additional resources if needed during a winter emergency.

Activating Outreach Networks

NYCEM will lead citywide communications about cold, snow, ice and other changing weather conditions through Notify NYC, which now reaches more than 1.6 million subscribers, as well as through other communication networks.

NYC Aging will work with its network of service providers to prepare older adult centers, adjust home-delivered meal schedules and coordinate wellness checks for vulnerable clients ahead of, during and following extreme weather.

Readying New Yorkers’ Homes for the Cold

To ensure reliable heat and hot water, the Authority conducted preventative maintenance, inspections and necessary repairs on every single piece of its heating infrastructure: 965 boilers, 1,472 hot water systems and 1,507 heat distribution systems.

Last month, NYCHA cut the ribbon on a new, state-of-the-art, 12,271-square-foot heating training facility. The facility will provide hands-on instruction for all 654 active frontline heating employees, as well as future hires.

Since last winter, NYCHA has invested more than $121 million in heating infrastructure upgrades at four developments, with another $728 million across 20 developments nearing completion this winter.

NYCHA maintains a reserve of mobile boilers that can be deployed during a severe heating emergency. Twenty-two mobile boilers are currently connected to developments, with four more additional units available for deployment if needed.

For tenants in privately managed housing without working heat, the Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) will conduct tens of thousands of inspections in response to complaints. This winter, for the first time, HPD will respond to every nonanonymous heat complaint, rather than grouping together complaints from multiple apartments in the same building and closing all complaints when the first is resolved.

In extreme cases where a landlord fails to correct a heating issue, HPD will conduct emergency repairs, assessing issues with heat and hot water, restoring services and billing the landlord. For buildings that repeatedly fail to provide heat, HPD’s Housing Litigation Division will take cases to Housing Court to obtain court orders for repairs and, where appropriate, civil penalties.

HPD is also improving monitoring and emergency response protocols at Mitchell-Lama and Tenant Interim Lease (TIL) buildings, including improved communication with tenants and building managers, proactive storm preparation and heated buses in the event of a heating emergency at a privately owned Mitchell-Lama development.

Protecting New Yorkers From Rising Utility Costs

Fuel oil prices have risen by nearly 70% over the past year and continue to rise. The Mayor’s Office of Climate and Environmental Justice (MOCEJ) and the Mayor’s Public Engagement Unit (PEU) are canvassing all five boroughs to help New Yorkers lower their energy bills.

HPD will proactively assist New Yorkers who receive HPD rental subsidy with signing up for the Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP), which can help cover the cost of utilities.

NYCEM is also coordinating with Con Edison, National Grid and PSEG-Long Island to prepare for potential natural gas and electrical outages, including how outages will be tracked and how utilities will respond and expedite restoring power. This year, NYCEM will receive utility notifications filed with the New York State Public Service Commission, allowing the agency to identify possible gas service problems sooner.

NYCPS has tripled its emergency fuel delivery contracts, and DCAS is overseeing fuel quality and supply at approximately 5,500 City locations and conducting interagency exercises to streamline fuel delivery during emergencies.

Preparing Schools and Students for Winter

NYCPS is working with students, families and school leaders to ensure schools are ready to transition to remote instruction during a weather emergency.

Preparations include rapid device distribution, three load tests of digital learning platforms during the school year and a practice remote learning assignment this fall to help every student prepare to learn remotely if needed.

Readying Rikers Island for Winter

In preparation for the 2026-27 winter season, the Department of Correction (DOC) has completed extensive repairs and upgrades to Rikers Island’s heating system.

Crews replaced nearly 80 sections of heating equipment, repaired dozens of leaks and restored lighting, pumps and ventilation in underground steam tunnels. These improvements will help ensure reliable heat and hot water throughout the winter, with backup capacity if part of the system goes down.

In addition to daily temperature readings, people in custody have been provided with warmer clothing.

How New Yorkers Can Prepare Now

New Yorkers can take the following steps to protect themselves, their families and their neighbors this winter:

· SIGN UP FOR EMERGENCY ALERTS: Stay informed about extreme weather, power outages, emergency resources and more by texting NOTIFYNYC to 692-692 or download the mobile app.

· REPORT HEAT AND HOT WATER ISSUES: During heat season, which runs from Oct. 1 through May 31, every New York City tenant is legally entitled to heat and hot water. Apartments must maintain a temperature of at least 62 degrees Fahrenheit overnight, regardless of the outdoor temperature, and at least 68 degrees Fahrenheit during the day when the temperature falls below 55 degrees. If your apartment does not have adequate heat or hot water, contact your landlord. If the problem is not addressed promptly, call 311 to request an HPD inspection. Uniformed inspectors can issue violations and, when warranted, coordinate emergency repairs.

· ACCESS AFFORDABLE ENERGY: Energy costs are a significant household expense — you can learn about programs to help lower your energy bills by visiting nyc.gov/utility-advocacy

· PRACTICE FIRE SAFETY: Fires and fire-related fatalities increase during the cold winter months. Protect yourself, your family and your community by following the FDNY’s winter fire safety guidance at www.fdnysmart.org

· PREPARE YOUR SNOW CLEARING EQUIPMENT: Property owners are responsible for clearing a four-foot-wide path on their sidewalks to allow safe passage for wheelchairs and strollers. Make sure your shovels and other equipment are ready before the first storm.

· PROTECT YOUR PIPES: Homeowners are responsible for protecting their pipes and water meters from freezing. Follow these simple steps to protect your property: on.nyc.gov/485DQ8O
