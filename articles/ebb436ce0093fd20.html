Family, friends gather to mourn Brooklyn runner Katie Suarez

By Eric Feldman · NY1 · November 13, 2026

Family and friends gathered Thursday morning at Sts. Simon & Jude Roman Catholic Church in Gravesend, Brooklyn, to remember Katie Suarez, the 28-year-old woman who was fatally stabbed while jogging last month.

On a rainy morning, a casket carrying Suarez was brought into the church, less than two weeks after an early evening run ended in her killing.

"Will we remember Katie simply because of how she passed or will we remember her and honor her in the way she treated others?" one speaker asked during the service.

Suarez was stabbed to death while jogging near Shore Parkway and Bay 17th Street on Oct. 30. Her father found her after she missed plans with him that evening.

Suarez worked as a teller at TD Bank in Caesar's Bay, where customers said she made an impact simply by being herself.

"If we learn nothing else, let us simply remember how Katie treated others and how she treated us," said the Rev. Edwin Ortiz. "She didn't treat us as co-workers. She didn't treat us as customers. She treated us as family."

Police arrested 27-year-old Sayed Mohassib, who worked as a teacher's aide at P.S. 200, just blocks from where Suarez was killed.

New York City Public Schools said Mohassib has since been fired.

He is being held without bail after being charged with murder. He pleaded not guilty.

As the family grieved, loved ones released balloons into the rainy sky, mourning the loss of a daughter, a sister and a friend.
