Yankees news: Yanks eyeing more bullpen upgrades

By Kento Mizuno · Pinstripe Alley · November 26, 2026

Morejon is clearly the better pitcher, having exceeded the 2.0 WAR mark in each of his past two seasons, but he’ll come with a higher price tag. Vesia is a step or two below Morejon, but his track record is still solid, he’s won championships with the similarly big-market Dodgers, and he would come vastly cheaper. With the Yankees’ projected 2027 payroll already pushing $300 million, I think Vesia is the more likely signing. I just hope that their remaining funds go towards a solution at third base.

MLB Trade Rumors | Steve Adams : As Matt detailed yesterday , the Yankees have a new bench coach in former Orioles manager Brandon Hyde. The 2023 AL Manager of the Year was dismissed with Baltimore’s rebuild failing mid-2025, and he spent the 2026 campaign as a senior advisor to the division rival Rays, who beat the Yankees and Guardians to win the AL pennant. Now, he’ll succeed new Astros manager Brad Ausmus as Aaron Boone’s No. 2 in the dugout.

MLB.com | Sam Dykstra : An unheralded Yankees farmhand showed up multiple times in the AFL StatCast leaderboards. Kyle West, a 2025 draftee out of West Virginia, hit the longest home run of this year’s AFL campaign at 462 feet, while ranking 4th in average exit velocity with a 92.4 MPH mark. West, who led the AFL in homers with 6, went unranked in FanGraphs’ recent Yankees top prospects piece but received an honorable mention in the “Power Hitters” section. He’s still got a ways to go before becoming a truly interesting prospect – this year, in his age-23 season, he posted an 81 wRC+ with a K rate of 29.8% in High-A – but you can’t teach power, and West appears to have that in spades.

MLB.com | Shanthi Sepe-Chepuru : Finally, an update about an old friend: Gary Sánchez is headed to the Marlins on a one-year deal. El Gary earned his first World Series ring with the Brewers this year, enjoying somewhat of a renaissance with his bat – a career-high 16.5-percent walk rate and a career-low 21.5-percent strikeout rate led to him posting a potent 117 wRC+ over 275 plate appearances. The Marlins have a good young primary catcher in Joe Mack, but while he’s elite defensively, his bat is below average. With backup catcher (and erstwhile Yankees prospect) Agustín Ramírez failing to provide much offensive value in 2026, it seems like the Marlins are banking on Gary to help boost run production from the catcher spot.

Best of luck, Gary! We’ll always have a soft spot for you.
