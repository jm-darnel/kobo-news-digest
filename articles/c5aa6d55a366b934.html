Ramones music lives on through Marky Ramone's live shows

By Roger Clark · NY1 · November 10, 2026

It's a familiar drumbeat to fans of Ramones: the opening to their classic tune "Rock and Roll High School" from the 1979 film of the same name.

"There's no talking, it's just 1,2,3,4 into each song, and kids sing along, they sing along to it which is great," said Ramone, who joined the band in 1978 after replacing original drummer Tommy Ramone, who left to focus on producing and writing. All four of the original members have since passed away.

"I feel they died too young, Johnny, Joey, Dee Dee and Tommy and their music should live on, and the songs are too good not to be played," he said.

Ramone, a Rock and Roll Hall of Famer, inducted alongside the original band members in 2002, says he still loves playing, and it keeps him in pretty good shape too.

"I ride my bike. I make sure I play the drums two hours a day, three times a week, and that's all I need. I don't need to go to a gym, I just need to do what I do."

Ramone attended public schools growing up in Brooklyn and benefitted from their music education. Now, Marky Ramone's Bowery Beat is a partnership with Dark Matter Coffee on the Bowery, with a portion of the proceeds from sales going to music programs in New York City public schools.

"A lot of the music programs, they don't exist because of money situations, so they can't buy instruments. Some of them exist, but they need help," he said.

Marky Ramone was also a member of another influential band — Richard Hell and the Voidoids, who released the groundbreaking record Blank Generation in 1977.

Ramone's band is also playing the Stone Pony in Asbury Park, New Jersey, on Nov. 21.

After performing more than 1,700 shows with Ramones, Marky says he plans on being behind the drum kit until his body says no more.
