The Growing Interest In Professional Beauty And Grooming Treatments

Harlem World · November 23, 2026

A few years ago, a facial in Harlem was something people saved for a birthday or the week before a wedding. Today the brow bar on Lenox Avenue has a waitlist, and the barbershop down the block now sells skin treatments next to fades and lineups.

People still love a good deal at the pharmacy, but many of them have decided that some jobs belong in the hands of a trained professional.

When The Bathroom Drawer Gives Up

Most of us own a collection of tweezers, razors, and wax strips that never quite delivered. The hair comes back within days, often with bumps and irritation, and on deeper skin tones, that irritation can leave dark spots that stay for months. After enough rounds of this, people start to look for a fix that actually lasts.

Facial hair is where that search usually begins. Laser gets most of the attention, yet it depends on the contrast between hair and skin, so it struggles with gray, blond, or red hair and needs a careful hand on darker complexions. Many people end up with electrolysis for facial hair because it works on every hair color and skin tone.

Women with stubborn chin hair from hormonal conditions swear by it, and so do men who are tired of razor bumps along the jaw, though everyone should expect several months of short sessions before the job is done.

More Hands for a Busier Chair

As appointments fill up, the field has grown to keep up with them. Jobs for skincare specialists are expected to grow 7% over the next decade, much faster than most other occupations.

Pick A Provider With Care

A pretty studio and a busy Instagram page say very little about skill. Ask any provider where they trained, how long they have practiced, and whether they hold a current license, and then ask to see results on skin similar to yours.

A consultation should come before any treatment, and a good provider will insist on one. It is your chance to learn how many sessions you will likely need and what side effects are normal.

For electrolysis, confirm that the probes are sterile and thrown away after your visit, and for peels or laser work, ask for a patch test on a small spot behind the ear or along the jaw.

If someone promises perfect skin after one appointment or pushes a big package before you have even sat down, thank them and leave. Honest providers talk about realistic timelines because they want you to come back happy and tell your friends where you went.

Spend Where It Counts

You do not need a spa membership to see a difference. Choose the treatment that fixes the problem that bothers you most and keep the rest of your routine simple and cheap at home.

Esthetics schools often run student clinics where supervised trainees do facials for far less than a salon charges. Local spas also tend to cut prices on slow weekdays, so a Tuesday visit can cost much less than a Saturday one.

Finally, jot down what you had done at each visit and how your skin felt in the days after. Your provider can use those notes to adjust the products or settings next time, and you will have a clear record of what was worth the money.
