Flatbush turns grief into a fight for justice after deadly building collapse in Brooklyn

By Ariama C. Long · Amsterdam News · November 19, 2026

Last week’s tragic collapse of a three-story building in Flatbush, which cost multiple lives and caused numerous injuries, has spurred the surrounding community to action.

Elected officials, community leaders, and residents on-the-ground have called for a thorough investigation and stronger protections that will help avoid future disasters.

On Nov. 11, the sudden collapse was reportedly due to demolition work at the construction site adjacent to 1321 Flatbush Ave. resulted in eight deaths, including two children and two Ecuadorian construction workers. The collapse also injured 18 others and has left multiple families displaced, said officials.

Additionally, the New York State’s Scaffold Safety Law holds property owners, general contractors, and their agents strictly liable for gravity or elevation-related injuries.

“All of us here take building safety very seriously and we are committed to making sure [workers] have the training and equipment they need. It’s important that owners and builders follow safety rules and that workers can speak up when they see something wrong,” said New York State Attorney General Letitia James at one of the Brooklyn vigils held on Nov. 13, 2026.

Walter Sinche, a construction safety instructor, said “developers, real estate and construction companies make a lot of money with the blood of construction workers. That is unacceptable.”

Rev. Al Sharpton, president of the National Action Network and a Brooklyn native, added his frustrations about ICE officers targeting migrant construction workers.

“How do you ride past buildings looking for migrants that are not here wrong, but you can’t find builders and those who work for developers that have buildings that can collapse in the middle of the night?” said Sharpton. “This is a human catastrophe that most likely could have been avoided had we been as vigilant about protecting the lives of those lost as we are about grabbing people and telling them to prove that they are in the country legally.”

In the meantime, state legislators including Assemblymember Brian Cunningham intend on examining the building-safety laws and drafting additional protections and possibly an early warning system for residents living near active construction and excavation sites.

He also wants to review penalties and enforcement procedures for contractors and developers who continue dangerous operations after receiving stop-work orders.

To help the victims of the tragedy, you can donate to the Emergency Relief Fund through the Mayor’s Fund to Advance New York City .

For those impacted, a reception center was opened at Flatbush YMCA at 1401 Flatbush Ave . by the office of Emergency Management and the Red Cross.
