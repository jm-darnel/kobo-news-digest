New York Senate Aims To Support Local Media

By Eartha Watts Hicks · Harlem World · November 28, 2026

Harlem World Magazine

From payroll tax credits to AI guardrails, our leadership in Albany has spent three sessions trying to keep community media alive.

Here is what has passed, what is pending, and what it means for Harlem and local and ethnic community media throughout New York City.

Never miss new stories in Harlem

New York State has become the national test case for saving local journalism with policy changes. As niche reporting was impacted by the global shutdown, New York became the first state in 2024 to offer a payroll tax credit for hiring and retaining local journalists and photographers. State Senators have since sponsored a wave of follow-up bills on advertising, nonprofit newsrooms, and artificial intelligence. In Albany, there is a respect for the function of local independent press and its importance to democracy.

For Harlem, a neighborhood whose story has long been told best by its own press, the question is simple: will these measures reach the small, independent and Black-owned outlets that cover the community every day?

Now called the Empire State Newspaper and Broadcast Media Jobs Program, it is run by Empire State Development. Applications opened February 24, 2026, through the state’s Consolidated Funding Application, on a first-come, first-served basis. Key terms:

• Who qualifies: newspaper and broadcast businesses operating in New York, including print, digital and broadcast outlets with full-time W2 employees.

• Existing jobs: 50% of a retained journalist’s wages, up to $25,000 per employee and $300,000 per business.

• How Small Outlets are defined: the money is reserved for New York businesses with 100 or fewer full-time, salaried employees with W-2s.

Ad Dollars

Two Senate bills target the other half of the business model: advertising.

State agency ads for community and ethnic media. Senator Jessica Ramos of Queens sponsors the Local, Community and Ethnic Media Commitment Act (S.2129). It would require every state agency to place at least 50% of its print and digital ad spending with local, community and ethnic outlets by December 31, 2027. Legally required notices are exempt. Co-sponsors include Senators Leroy Comrie, John C. Liu, Anthony Palumbo and Kevin Parker.

A tax credit for small businesses that advertise locally. Senator Monica Martinez of Long Island sponsors the Lift Our Communities, Advertise Locally (LOCAL) Act (S.1865). It would give minority-, women- and veteran-owned businesses, and firms with 10 or fewer employees, a refundable credit of 80% of their first $5,000 in local media advertising. That is up to $4,000 a year per business, capped at $10 million statewide.

Business groups, including the National Federation of Independent Business, rallied for it in Albany in March 2025. The bill was re-referred to committee at the start of the 2026 session and has not passed. Not everyone is convinced: the New York State Broadcasters Association has noted that advertising is already fully deductible as a business expense.

Closing the Nonprofit Gap

Senator Jamaal T. Bailey of the Bronx and Assemblyman Jeffrey Dinowitz have pushed a Nonprofit News Media Jobs Grant Program (S.9285 / A.10330). It would give eligible nonprofit and public media outlets grants that mirror the local media employee tax credits. Because The Nonprofit News Media Jobs Grant Program requires funding, it must ultimately be won in the state budget.

Guarding Newsrooms Against AI

Key Provisions

• Transparency Requirements: News media must inform the public about the use of generative AI in their content creation.

• Legislative Status: The act passed both chambers of the New York legislature in June 2026 but has not yet been delivered to the Governor for final approval.

• Public Disclosure: It addresses the need for public awareness regarding AI’s role in news, aiming to maintain trust in media.

This act represents a significant step towards regulating AI’s impact on journalism, ensuring that audiences are aware of the origins of the news they consume. It also requires newsrooms to tell their own staff how AI is used, and to shield confidential source material from AI tools. It passed the Senate 53-7. (http://nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A8962/amendment/B)

News guilds, SAG-AFTRA and the Writers Guild of America East back it. The New York News Publishers Association opposes it, warning of First Amendment problems and unclear standards. Governor Kathy Hochul must sign or veto it by December 31; if signed, it takes effect 60 days later.

What This Means for Harlem

Harlem’s media ecosystem is mostly small: independent digital publications, legacy Black newspapers, neighborhood blogs, youth media programs and community radio. Each of the Senate’s efforts touches that ecosystem differently. As even legacy independent press has shut down operations, Harlem World Magazine understands that local media must now aggressively downsize, reduce staff, and reorganize to protect its infrastructure and continue operating and fact-checking, even under financial strain.

What’s Next

Readers who want a say can look up any bill and register support or opposition at nysenate.gov, or contact their State Senator. Local publishers can find program details through Empire State Development.

Harlem World Magazine will continue to follow these bills as they move through Albany.
