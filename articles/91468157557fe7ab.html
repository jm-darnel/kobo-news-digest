NYC Battles Early Flu Surge as Thanksgiving Approaches

By Reuven Blau · The City · November 19, 2026

New York City is seeing a sharp early-season surge in flu cases with infections more than doubling from the same time last year as officials urge residents to get vaccinated ahead of Thanksgiving gatherings.

There were 6,467 reported flu cases last week, up from 2,489 at the same time last year, according to Health Commissioner Dr. Alister Martin. Nearly 12% of emergency room visits last week were related to respiratory viral illnesses, he added.

“Flu season in New York City started earlier than it has in the past few years,” Martin told reporters. “And now, while we don’t have enough information yet to make judgments about why that might be, we are tracking these developments closely.”

More than half of the city’s reported cases during last year’s flu season involved children under 18. City officials have said the surge contributed to major delays in emergency room treatment, including an average 27-hour wait for a hospital bed at Kings County Hospital, as The City Reporter previously detailed.

Health officials aren’t sure why there has been an early spike in flu cases.

More than 360,000 children between six months and 18 years have received their flu shots in New York City this season, according to Dr. Andrea Howard, the deputy commissioner for disease control.

“That number is very similar to this time last year,” Howard said, noting the city doesn’t have complete data on adult vaccination because healthcare providers are not required to report those jabs.

Health officials are particularly concerned because the flu, COVID-19, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) typically spread during indoor family gatherings and plane travel, especially when people are bunched together.

They are urging everyone six months and older to get the annual flu vaccine. Additionally, most New Yorkers six months and older should receive an updated COVID-19 vaccine and eligible New Yorkers should receive RSV immunization.

“We’re asking everyone to do their best,” Martin said.
