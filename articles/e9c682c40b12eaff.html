New Astoria roundabout draws complaints from some residents

By Alyssa Paolicelli · NY1 · November 10, 2026

A new roundabout at 31st Avenue and Newtown Road in Astoria is drawing complaints from some residents as the city Department of Transportation continues work on safety improvements along 31st Avenue.

For about a month, residents have been adjusting to the new traffic pattern. Some said the change has created confusion and made the intersection harder to navigate.

“It's a disaster, personally it is a disaster,” said Leonard Divittorio, a lifelong Astoria resident.

Olga Richter called the change “horrible.”

Before the redesign, Newtown Road cut diagonally through the neighborhood, creating a large gap between corners at the intersection. The DOT said the new layout is intended to improve visibility for pedestrians and cyclists and shorten pedestrian crossings.

Some residents said they believe the intersection is worse than it was before.

“I think they just made a mess,” Astoria resident Diane Jacobs said. “There was no problem with it before. You have school children. Maybe put up a light, make better access but this for no reason.”

Residents also raised concerns about the width of the new configuration. A garbage truck was seen driving onto the curb to reach 43rd Street for trash pickup, and a tractor-trailer got stuck earlier in the week while making the same turn.

“I kept thinking when they were doing this, what's going to happen when a fire engine has to go through? An ambulance has got to go through?” Divittorio said. “This is a New England thing, not a New York thing.”

Edirin Akpoforayen, an Astoria resident, said the new layout has disrupted the usual flow of traffic but could have some benefits once drivers adjust.

“No one really asked for it,” Akpoforayen said. “It kind of messed up the traffic flow of everything but it does have its benefits, I just think a lot of us are not used to a roundabout.”

A DOT spokesperson said in a statement that the roundabout under construction at 31st Avenue and 43rd Street will improve safety at a complex, six-leg intersection by simplifying driver movements, improving visibility and shortening pedestrian crossings. The spokesperson urged New Yorkers to exercise caution and patience when traveling through active work zones.

Additional roadway markings and signs are expected to be installed over the next few weeks to help clarify the new traffic pattern.
