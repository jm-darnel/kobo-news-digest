New York approves National Grid rate freeze, but customers could see higher bills soon

According to National Grid’s own filings, the utility estimated roughly $1.7 billion in capital investments during the freeze that it would seek to recover from customers after the rate freeze concludes. The move was unusual because utility firms typically ask for rate increases through a public process to pay for expenditures such as new infrastructure and taxes.

“ The proposal is reasonable as it will allow the companies to continue to make safety and reliability investments to their natural gas systems without creating an immediate rate impact for customers,” said Aric Rider, deputy director at the New York State Department of Public Service, at the commission’s meeting on Thursday.

National Grid did not respond to questions about whether its proposal is circumventing the rate hike process.

"We're pleased the Public Service Commission has approved our rate stabilization proposal that will provide immediate meaningful financial relief into 2028 for more than 1.9 million customers in New York City and on Long Island,” National Grid New York president, Matt Barnett, said in a statement. “This decision, along with the recent improved winter heating supply cost forecast, means customers can expect stable costs for the safe and reliable delivery of energy that heats their homes and powers businesses.”

Typically, companies request increases through an extensive and open public process. Utility companies are responsible for providing details on why they need to raise rates and how they will use the money. The state weighs testimony from regular customers, unions, interest groups and city leaders.

Advocates say the National Grid proposal lacks transparency, clarity and could leave customers vulnerable to hefty future increases.

“The commission is asking us to take it on faith that the companies have justified the investments they make with New Yorkers’ money,” said Hillary Aidun, senior attorney at Earth Justice. “The public has the right to participate, ask questions and hire experts to achieve an outcome that's best for ratepayers and we were deprived of that process here.”

A review of the utility's proposal by Synapse Energy Economics, a research consultant, revealed that the surcharge could add more than $50 to customers’ annual bills, capped at 2.5% of operating revenue, after the freeze ends. The surcharge would be on top of any rate hikes the commission approves in the future, the report said.

“What about next winter and the winter after that?” said Kim Fraczek, director of the advocacy group Sane Energy. “Nothing more than a fake headline for a fake rate freeze. This is all a windfall for National Grid.”
