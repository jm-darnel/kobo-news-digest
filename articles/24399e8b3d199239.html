John Legend, Performing Arts Luminaries Turn Out for National Black Theatre Fall Fete

By Nadine Matthews · Amsterdam News · November 19, 2026

Superstars like John Legend, along with stage and screen celebs like Susan Kelechi Watson, Susan Fales-Hill, Nicole Ari Parker, Boris Kodjoe and others were on hand Nov. 11 at Current at Chelsea Piers for the National Black Theatre’s (NBT) inaugural Fall Fete.

They and other supporters of the performing arts community celebrated the storied institution while honoring legendary Afro-futurist rocker Nona Hendryx, Wall Street financier Margaret Anadu, award-winning artist Sanford Biggers, and EGOT winner and superstar John Legend along with business partner and co-producer Mike Jackson and their Get Lifted Film production company, for being instrumental in advancing NBT’s mission.

“We used to do a gala in our old space called the Teer Spirit Awards, which we retired when we decided to redevelop the property,” NBT’s CEO Sade Lythcott told the AmNews . “It’s been about 10 years and I thought, New York doesn’t need another gala. What it needs is space for Black joy and Black celebration.”

It was also a night to celebrate recent milestones for the pioneering organization; co-producing the new musical “ Imitation of Life ,” and major redevelopment of its property which will, according to its website, “occupy 25,000 square feet of space across the ground floor, basement, 3rd, 4th and 5th floors of a 21-story residential, commercial, and cultural mixed-use development.”

Key to structuring the theater’s redevelopment deal was honoree Margaret Anadu, who revealed to a surprised audience she was once an aspiring rapper while growing up. She told the AmNews she became familiar with NBT during her youth.

“I love everything it represents. I am so excited about the beautiful space that’s about to open. It’s important to continue to do everything we can to really honor the legacy of [NBT founder] Dr. Barbara Ann Teer and her far-reaching legacy. I grew up going to the Ensemble Theatre in Texas, which was actually founded eight years after [the] National Black Theatre, as part of the Black Arts Movement. She was certainly a leader and guiding light.”

“Gilded Age” actress Jessica Frances Dukes revealed her own special history with NBT.

“They gave me my first job in New York,” she explained. “I came up from D.C. and they just wrapped me up. They define culture and art in New York City.” Dukes also shared her thoughts on Legend, “We all have learned so much from his songs, from his person, and I’m just excited to celebrate him and everybody else tonight.”

Honoree Legend discussed his ongoing creative collaboration with NBT with the Amsterdam News . “We’ve been partnering with them on the musical ‘ Imitation of Life ’ for a couple years now. They’ve been wonderful partners, nourishing our artistry and helping us get it to the world.”

Nona Hendryx noted NBT’s role, as a cultural landmark, in preventing erasure of Black cultural institutions in Harlem. She also described the evening as “one of the highest honors” she has received in her life.

“Dr. Teer was a friend,” she continued, “and I’m being honored by an institution she founded in the ’60s, that has thrived and been so important to our community, Harlem, to the world.” Later on from the stage Hendryx discussed her own work with NBT – “Land of Vision 2.0,” an immersive theatrical installation inspired by Teer’s final manifesto.

TV and film producer Debra Martin Chase pointed to her longtime friendship and business relationship with Legend and his Get Lifted co-founder Mike Jackson, with whom she is currently working on a number of projects including the upcoming adaptation of Kennedy Ryan’s novel “Before I Let Go.” Martin Chase also expressed deep regard for National Black Theatre’s mission saying, “I think that National Black Theatre is so needed. It’s essential. It is at the epicenter of Black theater in America.”

She encouraged those interested in NBT’s long-term survival to support it. “Go to the events, buy tickets. Turn out, spread the word and keep the excitement going and, if you can, donate money.”
