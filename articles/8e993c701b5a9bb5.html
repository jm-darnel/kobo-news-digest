Jazmine 'Jazz' Szympruch: Climbing like a brown girl

By Pat Kiernan, Chelsea Katz · NY1 · November 23, 2026

There’s no wall too steep, no mountain too tall to keep Jasmine "Jazz" Syzmpruch from their passion.

“Climbing is important to me because it’s a way that I can connect intuitively with the parts of myself that I don’t get to tap into in the day to day,” Syzmpruch said.

Syzmpruch, who uses they/them pronouns, is a mentor with Brown Girls Climb, also known as BGC. The national nonprofit shows women and nonbinary people of color the ropes of their namesake sport.

“It was very gratifying to be a part of spreading, spreading the idea of who can be a climber, of who can enjoy the outdoors,” they said.

Syzmpruch has been climbing since childhood in Colorado.

“I felt just free when I was in the outdoors,” Syzmpruch said. “I felt able to explore and be myself.”

Syzmpruch discovered Brown Girls Climb in the city — shortly before the world went into lockdown. So when they moved back to Colorado to ride out the pandemic, Syzmpruch found a climbing buddy through BGC to keep active.

“So it was very special to have Maggie, to have a consistent place to, a consistent place to reconnect with my body,” they said.

Syzmpruch reconnected with BGC when they moved back to the city.

They served as a local leader from 2025 to 2026, organizing meet-ups and facilitating scholarship and climbing opportunities.

“It’s nice to again see someone over and over again and build that relationship and know that it’s grounded in something that you both enjoy,” Victoria Wanjohi, a member of Brown Girls Climb, said.

“There’s, like, a calm wave of confidence as I’m climbing up the wall, and I know that they got me,” Emma Hoang, another member of Brown Girls Climb, said.

Syzmpruch has continued to advocate for more diversity in the sport through their work. They are currently producing a documentary about a group of women and nonbinary people of color climbing Mount Kenya.

“Share their story of being guides and alpinists because we don’t often see folks that look like them in the outdoors doing high committing, very challenging ascents,” they said.

“Climbing has offered me a community that I didn’t know I would gain such support and love from, and has allowed me to transfer, you know, the confidence that it takes to climb the fearlessness that it takes the climb, the, whimsy that comes with climbing into everything else that I do in the world,” Syzmpruch said.

For helping others ascend to new heights, Jasmine “Jazz” Szympruch is our New Yorker of the Week.
