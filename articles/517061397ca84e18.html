New exhibit sheds light on New York City after dark

By Roger Clark · NY1 · November 20, 2026

High society social events. Parties. Diners. Work in the harbor. They're all part of a world that exists after the sun goes down.

It's the theme of the Photography Triennial at the Museum of the City of New York. "After Dark" features 33 artists showcasing contemporary photography.

It was curated by Sean Corcoran, senior curator of prints and photographs at the museum in East Harlem, along with Marina Chao.

"All these things quietly happening as we rest our heads, these are all happening in the city," Corcoran said. "And that's what's most, kind of, interesting to me, is to expose all of these activities that people are completely unaware of."

"Together we, with an advisory committee, scoured what was happening with contemporary photographers and how they were picturing life in the city at night," Corcoran added. "Over the course of about two years, looked at hundreds, literally hundreds of photographers, and slowly kind of found the right chemistry, the right mix of work, that kind of told as complete a story as possible to all the various things that happen in the city."

There are images from the parks, Governors Island, of the streets, the runners, and yes, the rats, from wildlife photographer Charlie Hamilton James.

"There's a few of them that are kind of tender, and they look a little charming, actually," Corcoran said. "And those are sprinkled around the galleries, just like the rats are kind of everywhere in our city. You will encounter them throughout the gallery as you walk through."

Only in photos, thankfully, using all types of cameras and methods to make the night come to life.
