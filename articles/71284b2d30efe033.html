Brooklyn body shop owner charged with bilking drivers in illegal tow truck scheme

By Liam Quigley · Gothamist · November 24, 2026

A Brooklyn bodyshop owner preyed on car crash victims for years, using a fleet of unlicensed tow trucks to haul wrecked vehicles that he held hostage while charging thousands of dollars in bogus storage fees, prosecutors charged Tuesday.

When one of the company’s drivers would find a crash victim, the tow truck operator would allegedly urge the car’s owner to send their vehicle to the Atlantic Avenue shop for repairs. Once there, Ventura and his team would hold onto the car and illegally demand the vehicle’s owner pay $150 in daily storage fees that would often add up to more than $10,000, prosecutors said.

Ventura was arrested in Osceola County, Florida, on Nov. 13. He and his business face 29 counts, including grand larceny, scheming to defraud and illegally equipping a motor vehicle with a radio that scans frequencies used by police. Prosecutors allege at least 12 people fell victim to the scheme.

Ventura pleaded not guilty and was released with orders to come back in February. He smiled at a TV camera as he left the building.

His arrest is law enforcement's latest effort to clamp down on the growing ranks of illegal tow truck drivers in New York City.

A Gothamist investigation published in February identified more than 700 unlicensed tow trucks operating on city streets. In 2024, the owner of another illegal tow truck company, Christian Lugo, ordered a shooting that led to the death of a rival tower. Lugo is serving 19 years in prison. Last month, the driver of a tow truck that lacked a medallion or company information, which is required by law, ran a red light and crashed into two children crossing a Queens street. The children survived the crash, according to police.

It’s not clear how many unlicensed tow truck drivers worked for Ventura, though Gothamist routinely spotted unlicensed tow trucks and other vehicles with Florida license plates coming and going from State to State Automotive.

Sara-Lee Ramsawak, one of the victims identified in a criminal complaint against the shop, alleged Ventura towed her car after she crashed on Pacific Street in 2024. He promised to repair her ride within three weeks, according to prosecutors. Instead, he stalled for more than six months while charging $150 a day in storage fees, leading to a bill of $30,000.

Ramsawak confronted Ventura at the shop as police radio frequencies blared from the speakers.

"I wasn't really that scared,” she said. “My preference to try to get my vehicle back and then try to get justice probably overtook any sort of fears that I may have had.”

Brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez said more tow operations could face criminal penalties for towing schemes like Ventura’s.

"This case is indicative of wider lawlessness in the towing industry and should put unscrupulous operators on notice: the NYPD and my office are watching and attempts to defraud motorists will lead to consequences," he said in a statement.

Councilmember Justin Sanchez of the Bronx, who chairs the city's sanitation committee, said that unlicensed towers are also openly operating in his borough.

"Just as it's felt in Brooklyn, we see dirty work by towing companies every day in the Bronx. It's time for our city to get it under control and clean it up," Sanchez said.

Sanchez said he plans to introduce legislation stripping towing oversight from the city’s Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, which employs a small team of civilian inspectors. He wants to turn over control to the Business Integrity Commission, which has law enforcement authority and was formed to regulate the once notoriously corrupt and deadly private waste-hauling industry.

A spokesperson for the commission said it would review Sanchez’s legislation once it’s introduced.
